# The Truth of Intelligence and Knowledge
**Details**
Emerson notes the way to discover oneness and to break down the walls of academia is to venture forth into the world, to exit the hallways of colleges and institutions, to find a more honest intelligence. We must act. We must put to work our hands and our senses beyond the safe confines of formal scholarly pursuits. We are whole, in a way, that is why some art pieces that may seem so different can resonate with us so deeply, it is because it represents the very thought that we have kept silent for so long, in the modern world, a word to represent this has been "authenticity".

Often, Emerson said, man thinking will play the role of the idiot. As he learns and discovers new things in the real world, he will be confronted by his own ignorance. It will be embarrassing, and it will result in admonishment rather than praise. The learned men of yesterday will look at him derisively as he stammers and stutters. Man thinking’s language will take the form of questions rather than answers. He will stand off in society, but that is fine. Instead of fetishizing appearances and decorum, the role of the intelligent man is to look inward. He competes only for his own respect, which is the highest honor that man can achieve in this world. In doing so, he learns not what the world appears to be, he learns not what academics have proclaimed it to be; instead, he gains an intimate understanding of the world as he experiences it, as it is. He feels the salt of the sea not by reading about it in a book, but by plunging himself into the ocean.
