# The effects of Neurofeedback
**Details**
Building upon the real-time feedback mechanism, neurofeedback aims to induce lasting changes in brain function for individuals with PTSD. When the feedback system signals that the brain's activity is moving towards a more regulated state (e.g., reduced hyper-arousal patterns, increased calm-alert patterns), this acts as a form of reinforcement.  

The brain, even operating below conscious awareness, tends to repeat actions or states that lead to positive or rewarding outcomes. This process mirrors principles of operant conditioning. By consistently receiving positive feedback for producing healthier EEG patterns, the brain gradually learns how to generate these states more readily and reliably.  

This learning is not superficial; it engages the brain's neuroplasticity – its fundamental ability to modify its own structure and function in response to experience. Repeated neurofeedback sessions act like targeted exercise, strengthening the neural pathways associated with self-regulation, calm, and focus, while potentially weakening those associated with the persistent trauma response.  

The ultimate therapeutic goal is to enhance the brain's intrinsic capacity for self-regulation. The aim is for the individual to maintain these more stable and adaptive neurological states naturally, outside of the training sessions and without reliance on the feedback device, leading to a reduction in core PTSD symptoms related to nervous system dysregulation.
