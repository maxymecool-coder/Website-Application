# Readings of the Project
**Details**
Karl Ameriks identifies four possible readings of what Kant is doing in the First Critique:

1. **Systematic metaphysics** — building a proper metaphysical system at a time when metaphysics was considered wreckage.
2. **Refutation of skepticism** — specifically Humean empiricist skepticism, which showed that if all knowledge comes through the senses, and senses only give us mediated access to reality, we can never be sure our reports are faithful. You can't check the accuracy of your senses without using your senses — "that's the box you can't get out of."
3. **Ontology** — answering "what is there?" against Locke's claim that we only ever know _nominal_ essences (how we label things based on experience) never _real_ essences (the ultimate nature of things, which on Newton's corpuscular theory lies beyond perception).
4. **The transcendental option** (what Ameriks and the lecturer favor) — uncovering the _necessary conditions_ for both scientific knowledge and ordinary experience to be possible at all. Not defending common sense, not doing psychology, but asking: what must already be in place for any experience or knowledge claim to get off the ground?

Kant recognizes the stunning success of Newton, Galileo, the whole 17th-century scientific project — and insists this cannot rest on the "stunningly chancy" deliverances of fallible human senses. There must be a deeper foundation. The transcendental argument asks: given that science _works_, what does that tell us about the necessary structure of experience and reason?
