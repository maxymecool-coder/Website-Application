# The Harmful Dysfunction Model
**Details**
Aggression poses a unique challenge to the "Harmful Dysfunction" model because some level of aggression is normative, and perhaps even adaptive, in humans (and primates). Toddlers (ages 1-2) are actually the most physically aggressive age group, frequently hitting to assert "mine." Therefore, aggression only becomes a disorder when it harms others _and_ represents a breakdown of internal mechanisms.

The lecture distinguishes between "Externalizing" (the broad umbrella of acting out), "Antisocial Behavior" (breaking social norms), and "Delinquency" (breaking legal codes). Within aggression, there is a crucial distinction between "Reactive Aggression" (explosive, retaliatory, often linked to ADHD and hostile attribution bias) and "Proactive Aggression" (calculated, instrumental bullying). While reactive aggressors are often rejected by peers, proactive bullies can be "controversial"—feared but respected for their social dominance.
