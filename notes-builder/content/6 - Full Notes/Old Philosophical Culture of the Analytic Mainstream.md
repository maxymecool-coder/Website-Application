# Old Philosophical Culture of the Analytic Mainstream
**Details**
The dominant strand of modern analytic philosophy is a direct descendant of what Marx would categorize as the "old philosophical culture" of the 18th-century Enlightenment. Championed by thinkers like Descartes and Kant, and later revived by Frege and Russell, this paradigm is fundamentally individualistic, ontological, and ahistorical. It conceptualizes the human being primarily as a cognitive entity, a _res cogitans_ operating within an internal theater of the mind that processes propositions or psychological states. In this framework, society is viewed merely as a secondary aggregate of these pre-existing, isolated individuals. Furthermore, this tradition assumes that human cognition and logical structures can be studied in a vacuum, entirely abstracted from the historical, linguistic, and social conditions in which the thinker is embedded.
