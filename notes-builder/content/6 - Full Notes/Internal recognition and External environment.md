# Internal recognition and External environment
**Details**
The area around our lives are familiar enough for us to act accordingly to how it is. For example, stopping at red lights, going other routes when one is crowded,... We are, though, less aware of the internal organization of signals that correspond to these external signals. Our self-regulating system interpret and process all these external signals into comprehensive data to instruct, prohibit,... us.

But these two systems can mesh with each other, they can shut down or twist each other so we are completely out of phase with what is exactly happening, which can in turn, culminate in psychological disorders. As talked about before, they are very puzzling and defy common-sense because of the lack of information. When that data is supplied, the therapist can make sense of the patient's behavior and treat them accordingly.

This leads to the conscious thought between an event and an emotional response in the patient. This is though ignored by the major schools of psychotherapy, this is understandable because they often ignore or don't investigate the patient's conscious ideation. But we can still train patients to do this, to focus on their introspections in various situations, consequently coming to the conclusion that a thought is leading to that response. When a person suddenly experience an emotion when no event is occurring, this may mean that there is an integral "cognitive event" which produced it. Another situation when we are acting out of place for what most should react to, it can be explained with behavior theory, which asserts that these responses are based on accidental past conditionings and set off by the events that occurred similarly and had been associated to in the past.

In most emotional disorders, a person's appraisal (basically everything we just talked about) is extremely overexaggerated and become dominant, controlling the patient's reaction to certain events.
