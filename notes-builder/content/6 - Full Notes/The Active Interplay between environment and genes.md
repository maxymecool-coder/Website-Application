# The Active Interplay
**Details**
The relationship between genes and environment is not a one-way street where a static environment acts upon a passive set of genes. Instead, it is a highly dynamic interplay where our genetic predispositions actively influence the very environments we encounter. This is known as **gene-environment correlation**, and it occurs in several distinct ways. A **passive** correlation happens when children receive both genes and a corresponding environment from their parents; for example, musically gifted parents are likely to pass on musical genes _and_ create a household rich with musical influence.

The correlation can also be **evocative** (or reactive), where a child's inherited traits evoke specific responses from their environment. A child who shows a natural talent for singing is more likely to be noticed by parents and teachers, who then provide music lessons, thereby creating an environment that nurtures the genetic talent. Finally, an **active** correlation occurs when individuals, partly because of their genotype, consciously or unconsciously seek out environments that align with their genetic tendencies. A child with an innate musical talent might actively choose to join the school band or spend their time with other musicians. This active shaping of our own worlds makes it practically impossible to ever fully disentangle the effects of nature from nurture, as our nature fundamentally influences the nurture we receive.
