# The Biology of Fermentation
**Details**
To produce ethyl ethanol (alcohol),  we have to turn to look at the single-celled fungus Saccharomyces cerevisiae (yeast). Metabolizing sugars found in fruit (for wine) or malted grains (for beer), yeast excretes ethanol and carbon dioxide as waste products. This process has a natural ceiling, however, due to the ethanol killing the yeast, acting as an antiseptic.

And so because of that, the process cannot produce alcohol with higher concentration than approximately 15% to 20% as the yeast will die and not produce more. So to bypass this area, producers must first "malt" the barley—sprouting it to activate enzymes that convert stored starches into fermentable sugars.
