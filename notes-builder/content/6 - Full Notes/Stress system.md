# Stress system
**Details**
The endocrinology system of the human being is not just simply the hypothalamus sending a signal to the pituitary gland and to other glands to release stress hormones. There are different stress hormones and are mixed and combined depending on the situation of them being stressed. For example, having an existential crisis is different from being in front of a tiger, so those two situations would make the system release different stress hormones. 

One key aspect of this complexity is the use of multiple releasing hormones to trigger a single pituitary hormone. The release of the stress hormone ACTH, for instance, is not solely dependent on CRH. It can also be stimulated by vasopressin, oxytocin, and norepinephrine. This is not a redundant system; rather, different types of stressors evoke different combinations, or "orchestrations," of these releasing factors, creating what can be thought of as a unique "stress signature." This arrangement provides two distinct advantages: it allows for precise control over the shape and duration of the ACTH secretory curve, and it couples ACTH release with other necessary physiological adjustments that are specific to the nature of the stressor. Furthermore, this regulation is bidirectional, with inhibitory factors like Delta Sleep-Inducing Factor providing a "brake" on the system to ensure precise control. Furthermore, these cells may react differently due to their neighborhood cells due to sensitivity and so on.
