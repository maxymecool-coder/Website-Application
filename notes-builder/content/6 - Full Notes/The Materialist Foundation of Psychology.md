# The Materialist Foundation of Psychology
**Details**
The core ideological assumption of modern psychology and neuroscience is _materialism_: the principle that the mind is what the brain does. This framework posits that all mental phenomena—thoughts, feelings, memories, consciousness—are the direct result of physical and chemical processes within the brain. It stands in stark contrast to _dualism_, an older philosophical position most famously articulated by René Descartes. Dualism argues that the mind (or soul) is an immaterial, non-physical entity that is separate from the physical body and brain. Descartes, for instance, searched for a single, non-symmetrical point in the brain (settling on the pineal gland) where this immaterial soul could interact with the physical body.

Materialism rejects this separation. It asserts that to understand the mind, we must understand the brain's structure and function. If a thought exists, it exists as a pattern of neural firing. If a memory is stored, it is stored as a physical change in the connections between neurons. This foundation is not just a philosophical stance; it is the practical basis for all modern methods of brain study. It is the reason why studying lesions, stimulating brain tissue with TMS, or recording neural activity with fMRI can provide profound insights into our mental lives.
