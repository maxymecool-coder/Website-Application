# The categories of stress
**Details**
The first is stress that comes from the kinds of overwhelming situations that occur under battling situations. Stressful situations like these are so powerful and realistic when one is in it that it is unavoidable for the person to feel anxious. Although this is the case, we have talked about the battle-seasoned soldiers before, but even though they are more resistant, they are not immune, after a certain amount of missions or time, they will eventually develop emotional or psychophysiological symptoms. Other types of situations are things like impending academic or business difficulties that may threaten a person's social or economic status, posing a risk to health or wellbeing,...

The second includes chronic insidious pressures. Which means the kinds of stress that is accumulative from a number of subtle strains, none as much as the first category. A long series of adverse conditions like frustrations, rejection, and fear-inducing situations will eventually lead to a high level of emotional arousal and undermines a person's ability to adapt.

The final kind is is highly specific for each individual, the situation must impinge on his own vulnerabilities, so that it will affect his domain heavily. Which means conditions that normally may not invoke such reactions and effects on someone may be detrimental to someone else. This all of course depends on the personal meanings the individual gives to that situation.

**Supporting Content**
Research has tried to find correlations between stressful situations and psychophysiological conditions, which they do. However, clinical evidences have suggest that stressful life situations are less important than the way they are interpreted in causing stress. Also those who are more physically susceptible to being ill perceive situations more stressfully than those who aren't.
