# Philosophical methodologies
**Details**
Philosophy is not just “pure” speculation, which means thinking without experience. There are stringent tests of the validity of these answers. Although these tests are based on common experience alone - the experience you have because that you are a human being, not a philosopher.

The only difference is that philosopher has thought about these questions extremely well and formulated the most penetrating questions that could be asked about and took their time developing answers. And they’ve done so by thinking more profoundly about the experience than the rest of us.

Understanding this is not enough. We must also realize that not all of the questions that philosophers have asked and tried to answer are truly philosophical. They themselves were not always aware of this, and their ignorance or mistake in this crucial respect can cause unperceptive readers considerable difficulty. To avoid such difficulties, it is necessary to be able to distinguish the truly philosophical questions from the other questions that a philosopher may deal with, but that he should have waived and left for later scientific investigation to answer. The philosopher was misled by failing to see that such questions can be answered by scientific investigation, though he probably could not have known this at the time of his writing.

And so to distinguish between these things we merely have to differentiate between problems that can be tested and do in our normal lives and those that we must only think about to do so.

A good theoretical book in philosophy is as free from oratory and propaganda as a good scientific treatise. So that you will not have to worry about the author’s personality, or investigate his background. Though it may be of great use still so see other points of view of people who have tried to tackle the same problems as they will usually converse.

It should not trouble you if philosophers disagree, for two reasons. First is that even if they disagree, they persistently do so, may point to a great unsolved or even insolvable problem. (Which is good). Secondly, the disagreements of others are relatively unimportant, your responsibility is to make up your on mind, so don’t get swayed by others, make up your own mind.

The grounds of philosophy is that everyone must answer for himself. Taking side with the opinions of others (without evidence), is evading the question. And so your answers must be solidly grounded, with arguments to back them up. This is different from science as you cannot rely on experts, only yourselves.

The reason is that these questions are more important than the questions of anyone else, except children.
