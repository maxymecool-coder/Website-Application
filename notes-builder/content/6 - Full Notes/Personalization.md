# Personalization
**Details**
In the argument of humanity in philosophy, the inevitable egocentricity of man has been debated for ages. In a sense, everybody seems to have a private world of which he is the axis. Nonetheless, people are still generally capable of making judgements that are separate, objective from their relevancy and emotions in the event. In psychiatric disorders though, these thoughts become unusually compelling and may completely displace the objective judgements. That is what we call "personalization."

This process can be illustrated by a few extreme examples: specifically, patients who fall into the loose category of "psychotic.". A paranoid schizophrenic patient believed that the images he saw on the television screen were talking directly to him, and he did so to them. A psychotic depressive heard about an epidemic of an infectious disease in a distant land and blamed himself for having caused it. In conclusion, psychotic patients consistently interpret events totally unrelated to them as though it was caused by them or at least directed at them.

Neurotic patients on the other hand experience less extreme forms of self-reference. They tend to overestimate and be excessively absorbed in it. For example, a mother blames herself for every single error that is apparent in her children, a man seeing another expressing disgust for him interprets that everyone will feel the same way as well.
