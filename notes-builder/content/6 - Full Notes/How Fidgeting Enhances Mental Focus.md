# How Fidgeting Enhances Mental Focus
**Details**
The constant need to move, fidget, and shift—a hallmark of hyperactivity in ADHD—is not just random restlessness. It stems from a high level of "reverberatory activity" in the brain's premotor circuits, which are constantly sending out commands to move. Trying to suppress this powerful neural drive requires immense mental effort, which then depletes the resources available for cognitive focus. This creates a paradox where sitting perfectly still can make it _harder_ to concentrate.

A highly effective behavioral tool is to provide a controlled outlet for this motor activity. Engaging in a small, repetitive, and often subconscious physical action—like tapping a foot, bouncing a knee, pacing, or using a fidget toy—channels some of this excess premotor signaling. By giving the restless circuits a simple task to perform, it liberates the rest of the brain's cognitive resources to be directed toward the primary task at hand, whether that's listening, reading, or problem-solving. This is why surgeons learn to tap a foot to steady their hands, and why pacing can help with public speaking. It's not about "getting energy out"; it's about giving the brain's motor system a simple, rhythmic job to do so that the mind can achieve a state of stillness and clarity.
