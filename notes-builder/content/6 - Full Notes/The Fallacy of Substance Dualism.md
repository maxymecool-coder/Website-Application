# The Fallacy of Substance Dualism
**Details**
When faced with the difficulty of explaining consciousness in a physical world, a common reaction is to propose **Substance Dualism**: the idea that there is a physical world of atoms and a separate "mental world" made of "soul stuff" or "ectoplasm."

Campbell argues this is a philosophical dead end. He uses the analogy of Martians investigating a television. If Martians understood the electronics but couldn't explain the picture, postulating a magical "TV Stuff" that generates images doesn't solve the problem; it just names the mystery.

1. It doesn't explain _how_ the "stuff" works.
    
2. It doesn't explain how the "stuff" interacts with the physical parts.
    

Postulating a soul or "golden dust" (a la _The Amber Spyglass_) merely pushes the problem back one step. We are still left with the question: How does this substance sustain a mind? Therefore, we must solve the problem within the context of the brain and the physical world, not by fleeing from it.
