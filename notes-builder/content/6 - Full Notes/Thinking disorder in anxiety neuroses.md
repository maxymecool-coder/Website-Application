# Thinking disorder in anxiety neuroses
**Details**
Repetitive thoughts about danger. The patient has continuous verbal or pictorial cognitions about the occurrence of harmful events (“false alarms”).

Reduced ability to “reason” with the fearful thoughts. The patient may suspect that his anxiety-producing thoughts are not reasonable; however, his capacity for objectively evaluating and reappraising is impaired. Even though he may be able to question the reasonableness of his anxiety-producing thoughts, he believes predominantly in their validity.

“Stimulus generalization.” The range of anxiety-evoking stimuli increases so that almost any sound, movement, or other environmental change may be perceived as a danger. For example, a woman in an acute anxiety attack had these experiences: She heard the siren of a fire engine and thought, “My house may be on fire.” At the same time, she visualized her family trapped at home in the fire. Then she heard an airplane flying overhead and had a pictorial image of herself in the airplane and the airplane’s crashing. As she imagined the scene, she experienced anxiety.

Some other characteristics of people suffering from anxiety neuroses may be a decrease in attention and recall due to the patient constantly being hypervigilant for signs of threats rather than the information being given to him. This whole process is involuntary and he has no control whatsoever to diverting his attention to other things, which can make him believe he is going crazy and therefore enhancing his anxiety.

Another characteristic is the tendency to "catastrophize". In any situation where there is a possibility of an unpleasant outcome, the patient will dwell on the worst situation possible. He ignores any and every sign as he equates the hypothesis with a fact. Even further, these patients usually stay highly anxious with symptoms maybe even going further with each encounter whilst the normal patient will habituate and adapt to calm themselves down.

Also their response to external stimuli is different, as the noxious stimuli is adapted and determined fairly rapidly by someone normal, the anxious patient cannot discriminate this, and he sticks with the meaning and concept of danger that has been assigned to that stimuli.
