# Retinal Processing and Bipolar Cells
**Details**
The journey of a visual signal does not end at the photoreceptor. Once a rod or cone transduces light into an electrical impulse, the signal begins a complex process of refinement within the retina itself, starting with the **bipolar cells**. The wiring between photoreceptors and bipolar cells is a masterpiece of biological engineering that explains their different functions. For rods, many converge their signals onto a single bipolar cell. This 'pooling' of information creates a system highly sensitive to dim light, as weak signals can combine to trigger a response. The trade-off is a loss of spatial detail. For cones, especially in the fovea, the connection is much more direct, with very few (or just one) connecting to a bipolar cell. This preserves the precise location of the stimulus, enabling the high-acuity vision for which cones are known. From the bipolar cells, the signal continues to other retinal neurons before being sent to the brain via the **optic nerve**.
