# Action Identification Theory
**Details**
Relative to [[Automaticity and Donald]] and [[Pre-Reflective and Reflective Consciousness]], this theory basically identifies that there is a hierarchy to how we word and understand classes of actions and behaviors. Picking up a pen can be "contracting finger muscles," "picking up my pen," "preparing to write," or "pursuing my teaching career." Asking "how?" moves down the hierarchy; asking "why?" moves up. And so whenever we learn a new skill, we must move the habit of doing so (though I think this is a delicate process that may move something up and down) further up so that we are conscious when we are doing it and notice our imperfections before refining it into automaticity.

Pushing these activities up in the hierarchy of our behavior also allows us to become more self-aware of it, as suddenly it is now significant. This also leads to another point about how morals can be tied to this theory, as low-level action identifications may classify one act as easily dismissible morally: At low levels, the same behavior is neutral mechanics — "I am reaching out and grasping." But this doesn't eliminate reflection, rather just prevents the [[Generalized Other]] from gaining traction.
