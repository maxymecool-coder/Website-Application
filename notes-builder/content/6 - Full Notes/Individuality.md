# Individuality
**Details**
Individuality, at least in Mill's sense, is "the permanent interests of a man as a progressive being." Individuality combines two elements:

- **The Greek ideal of self-development**: Cultivating all one's natural powers — especially the higher faculties of intellect, imagination, feeling, and moral capacity — into some harmonious whole. Mill draws this from Humboldt's work published around 1795. The idea is the full unfolding and cultivation of human powers rather than their suppression.
    
- **The Christian ideal of self-government**: Recognizing and acting within the limits imposed by the equal basic rights of others. Mill insists in paragraph nine that recognizing others' equal rights is not incompatible with individuality — it is essential to the self-government component of individuality. You cannot genuinely govern yourself while violating the equal standing of others.

Furthermore, he posits this as a psychological fact, as if people will, if they have the necessary faculties, try to achieve individuality and improve themselves.
