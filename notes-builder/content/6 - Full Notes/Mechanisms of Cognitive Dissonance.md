# Mechanisms of Cognitive Dissonance
**Details**
Festinger proposed a specific chain of events inside the "black box" of dissonance reduction. Firstly, the conflict between two cognitions triggers physiological arousal, which includes your autonomic nervous system activating. Second, you become consciously aware of this discomfort. Thirdly, you start attributing that discomfort to the cognitive conflict and then lastly you change your attitude to eliminate the inconsistency and discomfort.

Zanna and Cooper tested this model with a placebo study. Before going through a counter-attitudinal essay paradigm, subjects received one of three pills, all of which were placebos. One group was told that it was a pill that would make them aroused, another that it was relaxed and a control group which received none.

The no-pill group showed standard dissonance effects. The Relaxin group showed _amplified_ effects — they expected to feel calm, found themselves aroused instead, and concluded the conflict must be even more intense than usual, warranting greater attitude change. But the arousal-pill group showed **no attitude change at all**. When they noticed their physiological arousal, they had a ready explanation: "That's just the pill." With the arousal attributed to a pharmacological source rather than a psychological conflict, there was no motivation to change attitudes.

This study effectively proves the attribution step inclusion, as it does not just require conflicting cognitions but also the person connecting their discomfort to the conflict.
