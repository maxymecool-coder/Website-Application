# Unrealism of Acute Neurosis
**Details**
Why can he suddenly lose all these abilities ? An explanation comes to light, it is that his mind is overly focusing on his inner thoughts, internal processes and so his attention are fixed to thoughts, and feelings, which becomes vivid. This can be comparable to someone under realistically threatening situations. Like a student taking a test. They both will be overly alert to stimuli relevant to danger; any change can draw their attention as they are hyper-reactive in this state, losing attention over the things that does not scream danger.

Despite these similarities, the patient with acute anxiety reaction differs significantly still as he is responding to a danger that is nonexistent or is blown wide out of proportion. And so the stimuli that he is recognizing as danger may not being danger at all.

With that we come to the conclusion of the problem, which is the meaning that the patient attach to the stimuli. For example, a sound of a siren can make a person think that their house is on fire. The cumulative effect of indiscriminately interpreting events as danger can lead to a warped view of the world and escalating anxiety, which can range from mild inaccuracy to gross misinterpretation.

**Supporting Content**
Though we are only talking about acute anxiety neuroses, this can appear in other acute neuroses such as depression, hypomania, and paranoid state. These states differ in the kind of emotion experienced: sadness, euphoria, anger. And so it differs in emotions and meanings assigned. Thinking disorders are also at the core of other neuroses such as hysteria, phobia, and obsessive-compulsive neurosis.
