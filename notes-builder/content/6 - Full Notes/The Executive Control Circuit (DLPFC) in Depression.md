# The Executive Control Circuit (DLPFC) in Depression
**Details**
The **Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC)** is a key part of the brain's executive control system. It functions like the brain's "brakes," responsible for cognitive flexibility, problem-solving, and, most importantly, suppressing unwanted thoughts and regulating emotional responses.

A critical feature of major depression is that the **DLPFC becomes underactive**. It loses its strength and can even atrophy with chronic depression.

This weakening of the DLPFC is what allows rumination to run rampant. The overactive Default Mode Network (DMN) generates endless negative thoughts, and the underactive DLPFC fails in its job to inhibit them. It cannot apply the brakes to the runaway train of negativity. This failure of top-down control is a core reason why individuals with depression feel powerless to stop their own painful thought processes.
