# Evolutionary mismatch of Delayed Gratification
**Details**
As humans, we did not evolve for the delayed-return environment that characterizes the modern world. For thousands of years, our ancestors operated in an immediate-return environment. Hunting and gathering required immediate gratification to push humans to continue performing tasks necessary to sustain vitality and survive. However, as society progressed—starting with the agricultural revolution (farming) and evolving into modern economic structures (bi-weekly salaries, long-term investments)—delayed gratification became the norm. Because our brains are still wired for immediate returns, we naturally feel dissatisfied with delayed tasks. Our biology fights our modern goals. To counter this evolutionary mismatch and sustain motivation for long-term projects, we must artificially inject immediate stimuli and short-term rewards into our routines to appease our ancient neurology.
