# Feature Detectors as the Building Blocks of Vision
**Details**
The brain begins to process visual information by breaking it down into simple components. This is accomplished by **feature detectors**—specialized neurons in the visual cortex that respond to specific, simple features such as lines, angles, or movement in a particular direction. One neuron might fire in response to a horizontal line, while another fires for a vertical line.

Evidence for feature detectors comes from two primary sources. First, neurophysiological studies (notably by Hubel and Wiesel) recorded activity from individual neurons in the visual cortex of animals, showing that cells responded selectively to specific patterns. Second, psychological phenomena like the "waterfall illusion" demonstrate sensory fatigue. Staring at a consistent motion (like a waterfall) fatigues the feature detectors for that motion. When you look away, the unfatigued neurons that detect the opposite motion create an aftereffect illusion.
