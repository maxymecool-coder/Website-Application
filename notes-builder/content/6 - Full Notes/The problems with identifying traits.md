# The problems with identifying traits
**Details**
One of the fundamental challenges in trait psychology is how to accurately and reliably measure personality. While traits are defined as consistent patterns of behavior across situations, observing a person in enough situations to get a true measure is practically impossible.

Because of this, researchers primarily rely on **self-report questionnaires**. However, this method comes with significant problems that can affect the validity of the research:

- **Self-Enhancement Bias:** People are not always objective when rating themselves. Most people, particularly in Western cultures like the United States, tend to rate themselves as "above average" on almost any desirable trait, from intelligence to sense of humor. This means that someone who provides an honest, average self-assessment may be incorrectly scored as having low self-esteem or a negative personality.
    
- **Cultural Differences:** This self-enhancement bias is not universal. Studies show that people from British or Asian cultures tend to be more modest in their self-ratings, which can complicate cross-cultural comparisons.
    
- **Questionable Validity:** When the results of personality questionnaires are checked against more detailed records, like daily diaries of behavior, the accuracy is only moderate. In one study, questionnaire results for aggressiveness correlated about .4 with daily reports of aggressive acts. This indicates that while questionnaires provide some useful information, they are an imperfect tool for capturing the true complexity of an individual's personality.
