# Fear and Phobia
**Details**
In our daily lives, it is clear that there are many things in our environment that are logically a threat to us. Accidents consistently happen - automobile crashes, fires, explosions - or illnesses that result in death. A person who avoids interacting with things that clearly are dangerous can hardly be accused of being neurotic.

But that raises the question, how do we distinguish between normal fears and phobias ? This can simply be explained and tackled in basic terms of psychology: "Nearly always, excessive fear of some particular type of object or situation; fear that is persistent and without sound grounds, or without grounds accepted as reasonable by the sufferer" (English and English, 1958).

Some important components of a phobia are the urgent desire to avoid the situation and, as the patient approaches it, he may accumulate more anxiety and try to escape. Frequently, it is observed that even though the phobia can interfere greatly with his life, the phobic patient has no problem navigating and maintaining one. It is significant that even though he acknowledges that his phobia is unreasonable, he cannot erase his fear or desire to avoid it.
