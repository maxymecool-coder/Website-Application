# John Stuart Mill's Life
**Details**
John Stuart Mill (1806–1873) stands alongside Marx as one of the 19th century's most significant philosopher-economist-social theorists. Unlike Marx, who devoted himself to producing rigorous academic works like _Capital_, Mill cast himself in a fundamentally different role: educator of advanced public opinion. Writing primarily around 1830, Mill believed he lived in an age of transition toward modern democratic-industrial society, and saw his vocation as conveying philosophical and political principles that ought to govern this emerging form of civilization. This explains why his texts appear loosely written with imprecise terminology — he was not attempting systematic academic philosophy but rather persuading educated, politically engaged readers.

Mill's unusual educational background also shaped his intellectual character. Tutored exclusively by his father James Mill, he mastered Ricardo's economics by age 12 through rigorous drilling and recitation, never attending school or university. This created a psychological constraint: he could not openly reject his father's ideas or Bentham's utilitarian framework. Throughout his work, Mill consistently represses his originality, never claiming novel ideas as his own, always presenting changes as clarifications rather than corrections. This makes reading Mill require active interpretive reconstruction — identifying the drift of his arguments rather than taking his official statements at face value.
