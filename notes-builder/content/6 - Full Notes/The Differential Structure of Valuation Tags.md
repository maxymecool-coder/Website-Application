# The Differential Structure of Valuation Tags
**Details**
Nietzsche posits a structural theory of psychology: to value something is inherently an act of **differentiation**. Valuation is not a passive, one-place predicate (e.g., "I like tea"); it is a relational hierarchy (e.g., "I prefer tea to coffee"). To affirm "X" necessarily implies the subordination or rejection of "Y." Therefore, valuation is fundamentally discriminatory.

A state of universal acceptance, or an inability to discriminate, represents a failure of valuation-a form of nihilism where everything is equal and therefore nothing matters. Nietzsche argue that the human soul can only find the world "good" if it is in a state of tension, constantly ranking experiences. This means that "preference" is the fundamental unit of value. Even if the comparison is tacit, every "Yes" to a value involves a "No" to its opposite. This psychological mechanism explains why Nietzsche is so hostile to egalitarianism; if you remove the hierarchy and the ability to distinguish "better" from "worse," you destroy the very mechanism by which human beings find meaning and value in their existence.

Moreover, there exists a difference of level between passive valuation and active valuation. Nietzsche argue that the capability for someone to appreciate something needs them to be able to create something, the future. This is illustrated by an anecdote involving Richard Wagner and the Louvre during the Franco-Prussian War. Upon hearing a false rumor that the Louvre had burned down, Wagner supposedly remarked that if the French could not paint new pictures as good as or better than the old ones, they did not deserve the originals anyway. And so a pianist who plays Beethoven cannot truly value his work, only someone who creates a different kind/type of music.

Furthermore, this would even be used to justify how high culture functions, how it requires a hierarchical society, potentially even slavery. Unlike Aristotle, who defended slavery on economic grounds, Nietzsche defends it on psychological grounds about how Masters have to distinguish themselves from slaves to actually function and experience...Vitality.
