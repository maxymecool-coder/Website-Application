# Rhetoric as an Approach
**Details**
Rhetoric, as mentioned in [[Debating as a Sport]], is a radically different approach from any other kind of approaches in any other subject because of its uniqueness to be able to approach ANY subject at all. When someone asks "Are you for or against abortion?", the rhetorician's first instinct is not to answer but to interrogate the question itself. The question has already done enormous work before you even open your mouth: it has framed the issue as binary (for/against), it has named the act as "abortion" (a termination rather than, say, a restoration of menstrual flow, as 19th-century medicine once framed it), and it has positioned you as someone who must choose a side within those prefabricated terms. The rhetorician refuses this positioning. The approach of the rhetorician is to question any and everything rather than accept the premises and go on.

Rhetoric, because of its very nature, has no home, it is merely an approach that applies [[Karl Popper and the Myth of Deductive Science]]. It can challenge a field and question its validity and purpose but that's its only function, which may not be productive overall. This may extend to the proof of arguments, as historical documents may be questioned by rhetorician if the evidence are real, not compelling, but rather real. This has profound methodological consequences. If you can never get behind the document to the thing itself, then what you are always doing is reading documents — not recovering history, but interpreting texts. The lecturer's undergraduate thesis argued essentially that history, as traditionally conceived, is impossible. Each text is a particular moment, and the leap from individual texts to a unified historical event ("all these texts equal the War of 1812") is never justified — it is an assumption smuggled in before the research even begins. This is why the course focuses on learning to read texts carefully, as singular objects, rather than treating them as transparent evidence of something beyond themselves.
