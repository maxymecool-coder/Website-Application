# The Model of Schizophrenia
**Details**
This model posits that schizophrenia is not a sudden-onset degenerative disease, but the result of a chain of events starting before birth. 

The foundation is a silent, static brain lesion or wiring abnormality that occurs during prenatal or perinatal development. This initial "hit" does not cause symptoms. It can be caused by genetic predisposition, maternal viral infections (e.g., influenza), malnutrition, or obstetric complications (e.g., hypoxia at birth). This results in subtle disruptions in neural migration and connectivity, particularly in regions like the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus. The individual is born with a brain that is quietly vulnerable. 

Throughout childhood, although people would not notice anything faulty or inherently wrong about the person who has these vulnerabilities, when checking back from an adult stance, they can clearly see the symptoms more, like how undeveloped their cognitive abilities are sometimes, their inability to express emotion, lack of empathy, so on and so forth.

This combination of a vulnerable brain undergoing faulty adolescent reorganization, often under environmental stress, "unmasks" the initial lesion. The brain's circuits can no longer compensate, leading to the dysregulation of neurotransmitter systems (especially dopamine) and the emergence of positive (hallucinations, delusions) and negative (avolition, anhedonia) symptoms of schizophrenia.
