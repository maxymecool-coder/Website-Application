# Helping and Acceptance
**Details**
When trying to help a loved one with BPD, you have to go through the process of asking them and all about what they want you to do or if they want your help. Here is where acceptance comes into hand, if they do then you can start talking about your role, as a cheerleader, a second opinion or something along those lines. If they don’t, well you have to go back to validating and understanding them, saying warm and compassionate words.

For some people, that is difficult, trying to accept the fact that a person that is hurting refuses to accept your help, but you must understand they have their own thoughts and opinions and may not want to accept help in that way. Either way, you must be able to continue assisting them one way or the other. A pitfall that people commonly fall into whilst doing this is assuming the future rather, they would generalize it to their loved ones never wanting to be helped and that its hopeless and worthless to continue. This may only proceed to push patients to not want to be helped even more as they are treated that way, so try to sustain a mindset of accepting day by day, at the moment they don’t want help and continue fighting with and for them.
