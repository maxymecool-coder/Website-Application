# The Definition and Double Crisis of Nihilism
**Details**
Nietzsche moved away from the naive definition of Nihilism: instead of positing it as "believing in nothing," he redefined it as the state where "the highest values devalue themselves." It is not the absence of values, but the collapse of the specific values that previously gave life meaning.

Nietzsche's view of the 19th century posited that Christian values are inherently nihilistic due to how it rejects the objective, "real" world in favor of a fictional, poetic one with the afterlife, soul, God, and so on. He also posited that Christianity in his era was nihilistic due to how it is failing. Without sufficient Vitality, he posits that we don't have a necessary goal, nothing to do.
