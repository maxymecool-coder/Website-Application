# Clouded judgement goes both ways
**Details**
The objection that is perhaps closely related to psychology the most (that I've seen). Basically it states that someone in the conditions for death would be clouded, and since that is the case their judgement is untrustworthy. So anyone in said situation cannot warrant that their situation is actually the second one.

The biggest counter here is that it goes both ways. Take the same patient, same pain, same distress, and offer a surgery that cures in the overwhelming majority of cases with a small chance of death under anaesthesia. Everyone agrees consenting is rational. But the objection's principle applies verbatim: the consent is formed under the same cloud, so it cannot be trusted, so the patient must never agree to the surgery. Nobody accepts that. The principle therefore proves too much.
