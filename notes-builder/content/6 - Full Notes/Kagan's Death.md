# Kagan's Death
**Details**
Not in the literal sense of course, but rather in the sense of the belief-cluster that he has been dismantling. As it runs: we have souls, so immortality is possible; desirable; death is a catastrophe; therefore fear and dread are fitting responses; etc...

He replaces said premises with his own: there is no soul, we are machines. Special machines, but still machines, and when the machine breaks it stops, no more mysteriously than a lamp does. Death is bad when life still had something to offer. Immortality would nonetheless be bad. Fear is the wrong response, but not because death is nothing. Sadness at going too soon is appropriate; but what is meant to offset it is how improbably it is to have existed.

Nonetheless, that doesn't mean being lucky to have lived entail being lucky to keep living, sometimes holding on isn't the thing to do and can be justified.
