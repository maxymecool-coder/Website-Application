# Justification for the Difference Principle
**Details**
Using the [[The Original Position]], Rawls believes that this proves the two principles of justice as superior to the pure principle of average utility. However, that does not fully justify [[The Difference Principle]]. And so, to rigorously test it, Rawls sets up a "Second Fundamental Comparison." Here, the Two Principles are pitted against a much stronger alternative: a system where the First Principle (Equal Liberties) and Fair Equality of Opportunity are guaranteed, but the Difference Principle is replaced by a "Principle of Constrained Average Utility" operating above a guaranteed social minimum.

In this situation, as basic liberties and catastrophic outcomes are already protected from the citizens, one must evaluate the resulting "[[The Well-Ordered Society]]" that is inherently more desirable and stable. To effectively determine this, we must look to the Publicity Condition and the concept of Reciprocity. The Publicity Condition demands that a political conception of justice must function smoothly even when its foundational rules are fully known, publicly recognized, and internalized by all citizens. The Difference Principle inherently embodies _reciprocity_—it guarantees that those who gain more wealth only do so on terms that directly benefit those who have the least. When this principle is deeply understood and publicly recognized, it actively generates social harmony, trust, and civic friendship, while mitigating destructive forces like envy. On the other hand, a society operating on the Constrained Average Utility lacks the reciprocity, which breeds resentment and then instability in exchange for slightly higher aggregate wealth with the public knowledge of the principle out there.
