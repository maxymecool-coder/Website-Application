# Master Morality
**Details**
Nietzsche further extends his philosophical ideas to apply to his times. He believed that, alike [[Problem of Vitality and Decadence]], the masters of the slaves are inherently healthy and vital people because they have self-affirmation. He believed that them comparing themselves to the slaves causes them to look inward and says, "I am powerful, I am beautiful, I am happy, I am beloved of the gods; therefore, I am Good." This is basically an auto-valuation of their own vitality, they seize the right to define reality based on their own experience.

In contrast to this are the slaves, who doesn't have any of the aforementioned power, and so in this system, they are viewed with a "Pathos of Distance." This basically entails that people are at different hierarchical positions in the chain of power and so when looking at each other, or from the top down, the master would obviously feel a sense of superiority over the slaves and dismiss them as "Bad" (schlecht) which would equate to being a "loser" in today's terms. It indicates that the slave failed to thrive and such, but not that they ought to be a master. The Master acknowledges this difference and feel no guilt or resentment towards those below him, as long as they stay that way.

Furthermore, Nietzsche compares this to the power of the priests and warriors at the time. Originally, these roles might overlap, but as society differentiates, a specific group becomes dedicated to ritual purity. Nietzsche cites the Roman example of the _Flamen Dialis_, the high priest of Jupiter, who was surrounded by extreme taboos: he could not touch the ground, he could not see a corpse, he could not touch iron.

And so this difference in how the two attribute power creates a sever psychological problem for the Priests. Nietzsche believes that because of their physical "Impotence" their Will to Power turns inward, causing them to become distinctively "evil" in a new sense, making them resent the warriors. This would then make them side with the slaves to overthrow everyone else.
