# Shaping Behaviors
**Details**
Mastery is the goal of shaping, and is something that is complex and unlikely to be understood easily or all at once. And so a process need to be derived, a process that slowly but surely shapes into the act that we want to achieve, in this case mastery. This can also be applied to reinforcing and shaping the approximate actions of animals for us to achieve a behavior that we want from them.

The process begins by identifying a behavior the subject already does naturally. If the goal is for the pigeon to peck a key, the trainer might first reward the pigeon simply for turning toward the wall where the key is located. Once that behavior is established, the reinforcement criteria are raised: the pigeon is now only rewarded for moving closer to the wall. This continues incrementally—rewarding for looking at the key, then for touching it, and finally, only for pecking it.

This same principle applies to human behavior. A professor's jokes are shaped by the audience's laughter; jokes that get a positive response are repeated and refined, while those that fall flat are dropped. By rewarding small steps toward a desired outcome, shaping makes it possible to cultivate complex and precise behaviors that would be highly unlikely to occur spontaneously.
