# Capitalism is not Unjust
**Details**
Marx does not actually critique the system of capitalism in a moral sense. His critiques of the "utopian socialists" reveals that Marx deliberately avoids moralizing. For Marx, "justice" is merely a juridical and political conception that belongs to the ideological superstructure (which exists to regulate and support the [[Mode of Production]]), it is entirely relative to its historical epoch.

Therefore, under capitalism, the extraction of surplus value is perfectly "just." When a capitalist buys a worker's labor-power at its true market value (the socially necessary labor time required to sustain and reproduce the worker) and then extracts more value from the worker than was paid in wages, an exact exchange of equivalents has formally taken place. Marx is reasonable enough to recognize that there is no inherent value in applying a moral framework to capitalism as every moral framework does not apply universally to every epoch.
