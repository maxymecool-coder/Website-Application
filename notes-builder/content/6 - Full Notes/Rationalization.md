# Rationalization
**Details**
Fritz Heider's **Balance Theory** established that people are uncomfortable when their beliefs conflict with someone else's, as if you like Jim but Jim does or like something you find morally objectionable, the triad is unbalanced and psychologically distressing. But, another breakthrough happened when Festinger removed the second person and only left you yourself. Two of your own cognitions can clash, producing cognitive dissonance, an aversive internal state analogous to dissonant musical notes. Three paths exist for reduction: change an attitude, change a behavior (often impossible since the act already happened), or justify and minimize the conflict. This third path is what everyone recognizes as _rationalization_.

Festinger's most famous demonstration, and best, is the insufficient justification paradigm, which made the mechanism viscerally clear. Subjects did an excruciatingly boring task for an hour, then were asked to tell the next subject it was fun. Half were paid $1 for this lie, half were paid $20. Counterintuitively, the $1 group later reported enjoying the task _significantly more_. The $20 group had sufficient external justification — "I lied for a large sum, that's reasonable, I can live with that." The dissonance between "lying is bad" and "I lied" was resolved by the payment itself. The $1 group had no such escape. A dollar doesn't justify lying to someone's face. And so, with no justification available, the only remaining route was internal, you have to change how you feel about the task.
