# Neurobiology of reward
**Details**
This is where the behaviors of us doing stuff that makes us feel good and stuff that makes us feel bad gets explained. Basically, as we evolved, as human beings, our brain developed systems for pleasure and rewards, but more than that, it also encoded information into our brain about stuff that we SHOULD feel rewarded after doing it.

These systems are called "pleasure centers" or, more accurately, reward pathways in the brain. These pathways are intrinsically linked to fundamental survival drives. Further research has shown that different areas correspond to different rewards; stimulating one spot might be as rewarding as eating when hungry, while another might be linked to the reward of mating. This suggests that we are motivated to act in order to activate these specific neural circuits. The satisfaction we get from eating, for instance, is not just about the food, but about the activation of this pre-programmed, hardwired system that tells us "this is good for survival." In this sense, our motivations are a direct extension of our biology.
