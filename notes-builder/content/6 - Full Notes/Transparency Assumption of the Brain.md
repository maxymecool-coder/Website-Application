# Transparency Assumption of the Brain
**Details**
Some researchers argue that individuals born with underdeveloped brain regions can still be valuable subjects for studying cognitive architecture. In fact, selectively impaired developmental cases may reveal which parts of the brain correlate with specific cognitive functions.

However, this approach challenges a key assumption: that the rest of the brain operates “transparently” (i.e., normally) aside from the impaired part. Critics point to the brain’s high plasticity during development, which may allow other regions to “re-wire” and take over lost functions. If such reorganization occurs, it undermines the transparency assumption and makes it harder to interpret deficits cleanly.

As a result, some argue that **lesion studies in adults**—where brain systems were fully developed before injury—offer more reliable insights.
