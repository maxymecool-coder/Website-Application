# Fodor’s mind model
**Details**
The model of the mind that Fodor thinks of is one that is also an information-processing device, but rather it operates on the premise that they operate on specific classes of information, are mandatory, and are fast. They also allow no to little inference from other modules, and produce shallow outputs. The development of modules exhibits a characteristic pace and sequencing, and they have
regular patterns of breakdown or malfunction (Weiskopf, 2002). Finally, according to Fodor, modules are associated with a stable neural architecture (for a more extensive review of Fodorian modularity, see Fodor, 1983).

Moreover, only input systems (responsible for perception and language processing) and output systems (responsible for actions) are plausible for modularity. In contrast, central systems (responsible for “higher” cognitive processes such as reasoning, problem solving and belief-fixation) are likely to be nonmodular. It should also be mentioned that the modular components are peripheral parts of the mind.
