# How, Nature
**Details**
Kant was born in a time where Newton has just published a world-changing work on physics and such, to which Hume ([[Hume's Fork]]) came back with a response that basically attempted to discredit all of physics in the sense that it was simply inductive reasoning and that it couldn't be... explanatory completely. Kant heard this and so he wanted to offer up his own interpretation and his own arguments against Hume for it, with his main question being: "how is nature possible?" Now, in this time, Newtonian physics basically explained that our world is chaotic, its random, and that the variables that constituted it shouldn't have birthed a functional universe in the first place, and so Kant's question here could be interpreted as follow: how does a law-governed, ordered world emerge from sensory chaos? His answer is that we _bring_ the order. The knowledge we have reflects on this world rational and perceptual principles we impose on experience as we confront it.

But this kinds of circles back to the point of it being inductive... a circular reasoning chain, so to say. And so Kant believes that even if we can interpret something different from another species, something objective is still being traced; and that our own personal stamp on things contribute to the objectivity of it, not separate from its objectivity.
