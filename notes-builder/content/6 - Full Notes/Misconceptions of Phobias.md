# Dual Belief System
**Details**
Technical and logical assertions have long clouded the meaning of phobias. For example, Friedman (1959) states that a phobia is a fear "which becomes attached to objects or situations which objectively are not a source of danger-or, more precisely, are known by the individual not to be a source of danger." This raises questions about phobias like: Is there really no danger involved in the situation? Or is the phobic convinced that there is no danger ?

When we tilt our head over to the patients themselves, we find that their fears are rarely bizarre or irrational. Meaning that in the situations that they do imagine themselves in, it is not false to say that they shouldn't feel fear, it is merely their interpretation to the potential of such results in those situations. Similarly to psychosocial situations, like when someone is talking on stage or in front of a lot of people, they may fear they will "make a fool of myself."
