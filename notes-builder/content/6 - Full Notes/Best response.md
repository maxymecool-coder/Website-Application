# Best response
**Details**
Only using dominance would get you nowhere most of the time, because in situations nothing is totally dominated by either player.

	Left	Right
Up	5, 1	0, 2
Middle	1, 3	4, 1
Down	4, 2	2, 3

For Player I, compare the first numbers within each column. Down loses to Up against Left (4 vs 5) but beats it against Right (2 vs 0). Down loses to Middle against Right (2 vs 4) but beats it against Left (4 vs 1). Up and Middle also each win one column. So no row is better in every column.

For Player II, compare the second numbers within each row. Right is better against Up (2 vs 1) and against Down (3 vs 2). Left is better against Middle (3 vs 1). So neither column dominates either.

So your new best friend here is best response: what is your best choice assuming what the other person will do.

So Down is the best response to that belief.

A student defended Down as safer, and it is: its worst outcome is 2, against 0 for Up and 1 for Middle. But Polak's point is stronger. Under a 50–50 belief, Down isn't just safe; it earns the most on average. Those are different reasons that happen to agree here. Lecture 1's angels faced the same split between a safety rule and a belief, and there too the belief settled it.
