# States of Socialization
**Details**
Milgram argued that we have two states of working, the first is autonomous, when we can do our actions for ourselves and we are sure, in this state we usually work quite quickly and automatically as we can clarify and check our actions by ourselves. Another is the agentic state, which is a psychological state where an individual no longer sees themselves as responsible for their actions. Instead, they see themselves as an **agent** for carrying out the wishes of an authority figure.

In these states, we can switch in between them quite effectively, Milgram argued that this is because from a small age we are already trained to be an agent for our parents, teachers, and so on, which prepares us to be an agent for an authority figure later on.

Lastly, the point about unity in the authority, meaning that, the order and the hierarchy of the person of authority must be clear or else we will have problems understanding and accepting/following their requests. For example, back to the Milgram Shock Experiment, if a person of authority, an "Experimenter" is tied up in the chair, another is commanding the Teacher to shock the other, the other is commanding them to stop, then 100% of the time, the participants will just give up.
