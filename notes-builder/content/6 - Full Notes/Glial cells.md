# Glial cells
**Details**
These cells fill the nervous system, in some areas they even outnumber the neurons ten to one. These things function are crucial to neuronal communication. They act as guidewires for growing neurons, and later, they provide a scaffolding for neurons to assist in the repair process.

But more than even that, they increase the speed at which neurons can communicate. The specialized glial cells that can do this is known as myelin. Shortly after birth, they wrap and spiral themselves around neurons that have long axons, which eventually they will cover the whole axon. The uncoated gaps between the wrappers, called the nodes of Ranvier, are also crucial in speeding up nerve impulses along these type of axons.

Myelin is white, which is why the brain is made up of both white and gray matter. Gray matter are cell bodies, dendrites and unmyelinated axons and the interneurons that comprise of the nervous system's circuitry.
