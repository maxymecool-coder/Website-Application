# Dissociative Disorder
**Details**
There are three dissociative disorders including dissociative amnesia, depersonalization/derealization disorder, and dissociative identity disorder. These disorders are all presumed to be caused by dissociation, resulting in some aspects of memories and cognitive ability becoming inaccessible. And this means that our consciousness is failing to integrate the information in our awareness. Both psychodynamic and behavioral theorists consider that dissociation is a method for the person to avoid overwhelming events. Which can also be seen in PTSD.

Dissociative amnesia is when a person completely loses their memories of being someone (perhaps even their sense of self) and then proceed to assume a new identity with new everything. These amnesia periods can last from several hours to years, but they generally do not reoccur after the period is done. Depersonalization/derealization disorders patients usually retain their memories, but they lose their sense of self, wanting to be someone else.

Lastly is dissociative identity disorder (DID). This diagnosis usually requires someone to have at least two separate personalities, or alters - different modes of being, thinking, feeling that exist independently and comes out at certain times. These personalities usually have one primary one who seeks out therapy to gain self-control back, although they generally do not have memories of the other actions.
