# Molecular Shorthand and Structural Similarity
**Details**
To have an effect on the brain, substances usually try to mimic the shape and function of neurotransmitters. For example, by comparing the visual structure of **Amphetamine** to **Dopamine**, one can see they share a distinct hexagonal ring and carbon tail. This structural similarity allows the drug to interact with the same biological machinery (receptors and transporters) as the natural neurotransmitter. Small structural changes can then result in a completely different substance altogether: adding a methyl group ($CH_3$) to Amphetamine creates **Methamphetamine**, a more potent psychostimulant.

This elicits a line in between medicine and drugs, even a singular miniscule change can turn a medicine into a substance that has insanely high drug abuse potential. **Methamphetamine** (a potent, illegal stimulant) and **Ephedrine** (or Pseudoephedrine, a decongestant) are nearly identical in molecular structure. The only difference is the presence of a single Oxygen atom on the Ephedrine molecule.
