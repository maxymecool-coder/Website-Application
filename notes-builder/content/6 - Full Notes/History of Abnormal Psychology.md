# History of Abnormal Psychology
**Details**
The earliest instance of abnormal psychology have been demons and ghosts that affected humans in negative ways which we nowadays call schizophrenia and other mental illnesses. The solutions to these problems at those times were literally drilling holes into their skulls, this obviously sometimes resulted in brain damage and would make people change personalities. 

Continuing, Hippocrates, who was considered the father of medicine, also took a dive into these illnesses and came up with terms such as melancholia, and mania, which we now call depression and mania, respectively. Furthermore, he also suggested about how the brain might be involved in these mental disorders. 

With the coming of the dark ages, the dominance of Christian religions came, and instead of evil spirits now, the people with mental illnesses were thought to be possessed by Satan and sent to be baptized. After that, came the age of witches, which followed with hundreds of years of unstable economy, together with illnesses like the black plague. This of course led to mental illnesses.

Some interesting developments came in Switzerland when Paracelsus had a theory that when the moon misaligned with the stars, it made people go crazy and mentally ill. These tests in lunacy were conducted to see if it truly caused all of the illnesses, or if the person was truly mentally ill to take care of them. All of the explanations of these trials were about things like "having children, brain injuries,..." the other explanation was TRAUMA.

At about the 18th century, neurological illnesses such as hysteria popped up, this prompted an Austrian physician named Mesmer to come up with ways to treat with it. He theorized that this was caused by a universal magnetic fluid inside the person... and used magnets all over their body to help them with this. Since this was quite a hassle, he turned to hypnotism, mesmerizing such patients, and a lot of them got better. Moreover though, he set the basis for what is also known as hypnosis therapy nowadays.

Freud was an apprentice of Mesmer, and he was interested in treating people with hysteria. In fact, he was so interested that he ditched hypnosis and developed his own methodology, known as psychoanalysis.

Continuing with the times came John Watson with behaviorism, Pavlov, and so on... Until the 19th century when people came to genetics studying and the rise of psychological models and methodologies for therapy, neuroscience came roaring back, culture came into consideration in these factors.
