# Establishing Relationships
**Details**
In therapy, it is necessary to almost “market” the treatment and also the person doing it. Clients must understand/trust that the person has the expertise and ability to guide them through to solve their problems.

Expertise, credibility, and efficacy can be conveyed in a variety of ways. Skills trainers’ neatness, professionalism, interest, comfort, self-­ confidence, speech style, and preparation for therapy sessions are no less useful in skills training than in individual psychotherapy. It is especially important in conducting groups to have the group room prepared before the group members arrive: Handouts and worksheets should be distributed; chairs should be in place; and the refreshments should be made and available (if refreshments are provided). The key to the credibility problem, in my experience, is that many clients—­ particularly those with severe and chronic disorders—simply do not believe that learning the skills presented will in fact be helpful. This disbelief detracts from any positive motivation to learn the skills, and unless clients learn the skills and obtain positive rewards, it is difficult to change this attitude. Indeed, this dynamic can become a vicious circle.

In fact, DBT is quite like a shotgun, some pellets may hit, some may not, depending on the target. It is good for people to know about this inherent nature, as people can become distrusted in the therapist and question their credibility.
