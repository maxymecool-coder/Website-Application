# The Landscape of the German Philosophy in the 19th Century
**Details**
German Idealism, particularly in its Hegelian form, was intimately tied to a progressive view of history. It posited that history was rational and that the ideals of the French Revolution—specifically constitutional monarchy and liberal reform—would inevitably realize themselves in Central Europe, which means it was trying to stabilize and keep the status of Europe or the world as it is. However, this philosophical optimism faced a catastrophic reality check with the failure of revolutions of 1848-1849. And so because of this, Idealism lost its philosophical reputation, but not only that, it dragged the reputation of philosophy down to the dumps as well, being viewed as a failed enterprise that could not deliver on its promises of social reconstruction.

Nietzsche lived and wrote during the "interregnum" period, between the collapse of Hegelian Idealism and the rise of Neo-Kantianism. During this period, there was no unified framework of philosophy in Germany, it was a pariah discipline, mocked by established scholars to be magic rather than science. The intellectual landscape was instead populated by history of philosophy (scholarship rather than original thought), reductive materialism (e.g., Lange), and the belated discovery of Arthur Schopenhauer. Nietzsche is thus a product of a fragmented era, an autodidact who did not have to engage with a dominant academic orthodoxy because none existed at the time.

Unlike other great philosophers, Nietzsche had no formal academic training in philosophy. He was a classical philologist-an expert in the history of Greek and Latin languages and literature. He only knew Kant through the distorted lens of Schopenhauer, and of course pre-Socratics. Nietzsche method was strictly philological, taking ancient texts, or corrupt versions of them and using genealogical techniques to trace the errors back to the original source. And this was also his way of contributing to philosophy, by introducing the truth-finding factor into it, as people have to consider the cultural and societal context of the time and how certain versions of the text might have been altered from the original.
