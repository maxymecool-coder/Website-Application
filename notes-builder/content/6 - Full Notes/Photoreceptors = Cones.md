# Photoreceptors = Cones
**Details**
In stark contrast to rods, cones are the photoreceptors that give our visual world its vibrancy and sharpness. They are responsible for our vision in bright light, allowing us to perceive both fine detail and the full spectrum of color. Though they are far less numerous than rods, with only about 6 million in each retina, their influence is immense. Cones are densely concentrated in the center of the retina, particularly in the fovea. Their ability to perceive color comes from three distinct types, each tuned to a different wavelength of light, corresponding roughly to blue, green, and red. This specialization for detail and color makes them essential for tasks like reading and recognizing faces.
