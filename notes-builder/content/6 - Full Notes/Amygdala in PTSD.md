# The brain structure
**Details**
The rational, cognitive part of the brain is the youngest and occupies about 30% of the area inside the skull. It is concerned with the world around us, trying to understand how things and people work and figuring out how to accomplish the goals that we want to. 

Beneath it are two evolutionarily older and somewhat separate, brains, which are in charge of everything else, such as registering what is happening to the body and management of our physiology and the identification of comfort, pain, safety,...

The brain is built from the bottom up, just like in the course of evolution. The most primitive part is the reptilian brain, which is in the brain stem, just above the spinal cord entering the skull. It is responsible for all the things that is necessary for an infant to survive: eat, sleep, wake, cry, breathe,... The hypothalamus (sits directly above this) control the energy levels of the body, they coordinate the function of organs like the heart and lungs, endocrine and immune systems, ensuring that we have a stable internal balance known as homeostasis.

Not having one of these functions or one of them being wrong can throw us into disequilibrium, which can psychologically affect a lot of parts of us. Any effective treatment for trauma must deal with these body housekeeping functions.

Above the reptilian brain is the limbic system which is where emotions, danger, and such are managed, it is the arbiter of which things are necessary for our survival. It is also a head to help us cope with the challenges of living in society's complex social networks. It is shaped in term of experience, with the genetic makeup and inborn temperament. Whatever happens to a baby will contribute to developing this brain, this makes it use-dependent, adapting to whichever the host needs.

These two systems makes up the "emotional brain", it is the heart of the central nervous system and it is to look out for the host's welfare. If it detects danger or an opportunity, it will release hormones. This can have a huge influence on the decisions humans make throughout our lives.

The neocortex, on the other hand, is rational and assesses incoming info more globally, unlike the emotional brain, which jumps to conclusions based on similarities and initiates preprogrammed escape plans, such as fight-or-flight. These reactions are automatic, is done immediately without any thought or planning at least until later, when the rational brain has caught up.
