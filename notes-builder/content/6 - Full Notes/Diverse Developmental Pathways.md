# Diverse Developmental Pathways
**Details**
Developmental psychopathology rejects simplistic, single-cause explanations for mental disorders, instead embracing the concepts of equifinality and multifinality to explain the complex relationship between risk factors and outcomes. These principles acknowledge that developmental pathways are rarely linear or predictable.

Equifinality explains that a single end may have many ways or methods of getting to it. Depression, as an example, the first time a person experience this may be due to their genetics and a bad day, the second time maybe because of childhood neglect, third is severe trauma, and fourth is due to a consistent negative cognitive style. This makes it so that there is not an all-encompassing method to deal with every single depressive case and rather a tailored approach would be more effective ([[Tailored approach to mental illness]]).

Multifinality, on the other hand explains how individuals with the same risk factors can have radically different outcomes depending on their other factors. For example, two people both having grown up with a difficult temperament may all have different futures. The first one may have conduct disorder and become inducted into the juvenile system whilst the second can transform it into a drive and go on to become a successful entrepreneur. The factors like protective factors and environmental differences made these two outcomes possible, and so all of that must be taken into account.
