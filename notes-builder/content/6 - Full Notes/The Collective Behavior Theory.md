# The Collective Behavior Theory
**Details**
Drawing from Durkheim's "collective effervescence," the earliest theory on social movements was about how people become irrational beings and formed mobs of crowds that would just destroy everything. Of course, all of this has largely been disproved with scientific evidence from modern day sociologists.

The basis of this theory worked upon how Durkheim view people who were disconnected from the whole, and so they can easily get swept up and join a "movement." This "mass society" argument suggested that a lack of intermediate associations (like churches or unions) made a population vulnerable to mobilization. Furthermore, there was Marxist echoes in this, believing that those who were at the bottom of society and basically had nothing to lose but their chains were the most likely to revolt.
