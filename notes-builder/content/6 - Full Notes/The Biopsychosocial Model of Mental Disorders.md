# The Biopsychosocial Model of Mental Disorders
**Details**
In modern times, the prevailing view has moved on from single-cause explanations for mental illnesses (e.g., purely biological or purely environmental). The current dominant framework for understanding the causes of abnormal behavior is the **biopsychosocial model**. This model proposes that, rather than just one or two factors affecting the upcoming of mental illness in individuals, there are three factors that may altogether contribute to it.

1. **Biological Factors:** This includes an individual's genetic makeup, brain chemistry, neurotransmitter levels, and overall physiological health. A biological predisposition might make someone more vulnerable to developing a certain disorder.
    
2. **Psychological Factors:** This encompasses an individual's thoughts, interpretations, past traumas, learned behaviors, and coping mechanisms. Stress, low self-esteem, and negative thinking patterns can all contribute to the development of a disorder.
    
3. **Social-Cultural Factors:** This involves the influence of one's environment, including cultural norms, societal expectations, family dynamics, and socioeconomic status. The roles we are expected to play and the support systems we have can either protect against or contribute to psychological distress.
    

A therapist using this model doesn't look for a single "cause" but rather tries to understand how these three areas interact to produce and maintain a person's specific symptoms or if it is truly a combination between the three factors at all.
