# Photoreceptors = Rods
**Details**
Rods are the workhorses of our visual system, responsible for our ability to see in low-light conditions. These photoreceptors, numbering around 120 million and making up about 95% of all receptors in the retina, are incredibly sensitive to faint light. They are most abundant in the periphery of our vision, which is why we can often detect a dim star at night by looking slightly to the side of it. However, this sensitivity comes at a cost: rods do not detect color and provide poor detail. Their strength lies in their collective action, as many rods pool their signals to a single downstream neuron to detect even the faintest glimmer of light.
