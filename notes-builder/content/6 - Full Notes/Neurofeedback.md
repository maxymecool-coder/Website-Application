# Neurofeedback
**Details**
The core mechanism of neurofeedback involves monitoring the brain's electrical activity and providing immediate feedback based on specific parameters. It begins with placing non-invasive sensors on the individual's scalp. These sensors detect the faint electrical signals produced by neuronal activity, collectively known as the electroencephalogram (EEG) or brainwaves.  

These real-time EEG signals are then amplified and processed by computer software. The software analyzes the incoming brainwave data, identifying patterns associated with different states of arousal, focus, or relaxation (e.g., the prevalence of specific frequencies like alpha, beta, theta, SMR. These waves correspond to certain activities like, sleep, drowsiness, focus,...) 

Based on predetermined therapeutic goals for the individual (often aimed at reducing patterns linked to PTSD hyperarousal or instability), the system translates the relevant EEG activity into a perceivable feedback signal. This feedback might be visual (e.g., a video game that progresses, a bar graph that changes) or auditory (e.g., tones that sound, music that plays smoothly). Crucially, this feedback is contingent – it changes directly in response to the fluctuations in the targeted brainwave patterns, signaling to the brain how its current activity aligns with the desired state.
