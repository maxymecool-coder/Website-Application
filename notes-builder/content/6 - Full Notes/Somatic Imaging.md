# Somatic Imaging
**Details**
In a person's domain, some other essential components of the self-concept is the "bodily self," "somatic self," or "body image". This conception of the self may dictate someone (as normally the personal domain does) whether he feels pleased or pained, strong or weak, energetic or listless. This may be even more important than the true physical conditions that the person finds himself in.

Visual stimulation is something that could affect the body image and thus lead to multiple physical sensations. Like how a curved screen with motion pictures may lead the person feeling that they are moving very fast on a vehicle of some sorts. Same with one who is watching gore, they may feel pain or anxiety. These feelings have been documented by Sir Francis Galton (1883). Some of his subjects experienced somatic sensations that were so vivid that they often wondered if it was imagination or reality.

Moving on to fantasies or daydreams (without any external visual stimuli as mentioned above) may also do the same thing. Psychiatric patients usually report this kind of somatic imaging. For example, a patient reported seeing a needle and imagining it being injected into his penis (which I am also experiencing the emotional repercussions of writing that). 

The thought of a physical injury may also do this. A young adolescent reacted with anxiety to blood, as he was sensitive to any stimulus that suggested bones breaking. When his therapist showed him a bone, he thought, "My leg could be broken" and felt pain in his leg and the sensation of the bone protruding through his skin.
