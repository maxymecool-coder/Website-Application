# Weberian Class Conflict in the Welfare State
**Details**
A Weberian framework, which defines class by shared life chances in a market, can be adapted to understand a central political conflict in contemporary societies. Beyond the market, the modern state itself creates new forms of class division by distributing benefits and levying taxes. This gives rise to a conflict between two major groups whose economic life chances are defined by their relationship to the state.

The first group can be described as "welfare state liberals," a broad class of people who are, in one way or another, beneficiaries of public spending. This class does not just involve people who receive welfare but also those in the military, those in universities doing research and such, they all depend on the healthiness of the economy.

On the other hand we have the group who are actively paying taxes to fund these people. They are constantly having to face with the fact that they have to worry about how their own money is being spent, over or under what actually matters and benefits them as a society.
