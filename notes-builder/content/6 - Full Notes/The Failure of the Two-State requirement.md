# The Failure of the Two-State requirement
**Details**
Firstly, I will reconstruct the argument promptly. Basically, to compare whether death is a rational choice or not, or any two "states" so to say, you must exist to be able to comprehend that. This argument takes advantage of the fact that you are simply not there after death. And so "he would be better off dead" would allegedly have a logical defect built into it and the comparison never gets going.

Kagan does not attack the premises, but rather points out the symmetry of the conclusions it allows. Not only does it block being better off dead, it equally blocks better off alive as well. Think of a situation where you save someone in a truck's path, why would he say thank you? Because the situation presupposes gratitude, which presupposes a benefit, and that presupposes that continuing was better. The requirement would go in this situation.

But rather what replace is the whole lives of people, insane amounts of agony followed by some light good years. Or a life that is just pure suffering, dying would be warranted there then. Of course evaluation here still needs a subject and that cannot be inherently agreed on still.
