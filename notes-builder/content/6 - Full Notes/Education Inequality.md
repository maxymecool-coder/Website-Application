# Education matters
**Details**
Depending on the perspective and the range that we are talking about, education can have completely different effects and results. Seeing the big picture (macro), education generally reduces inequality. Those who get an education are going to have more chances at a job and have higher salaries than those who don't. Consider the examples in works such as *Inequality by Design*. During the post-WWII period until 1973, the U.S. aggressively expanded its higher education system, building new universities and creating more college slots than population growth demanded. This massive public investment led to a decline in overall social inequality as more people gained access to the advantages of a college degree. After 1973, when public investment in higher education slowed, the number of available places shrank relative to the population. This reduced overall opportunity, widened the wage gap between college-educated and non-college-educated workers, and contributed to rising inequality.

But on the other hand, viewing the lives of the primary and secondary school students, we can see that the difference in education (private or public school) and so on does not actually matter as much. In a school, there is more inequality than anywhere depending on their results, those who are good are grouped up there and those who get bad grades are in another group. These grades though depend little on the quality of these teachings, instead it is about the child's background, family, for example.
