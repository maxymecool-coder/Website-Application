# Institutional Theory
**Details**
Another view that is totally different from the others. When other theories such as the [[Pluralist Theory]] or the [[Power Elite Theory]] argued that power is like a pie and is a fixed value basically, this argues that instead it can grow or shrink (Parsons), like how love functions, when you are loved less that does not mean that the other person is loved more but rather the overall amount of love is reduced. And so our goal as a society is to make the pie bigger for everyone, not distribute it in a certain way or whatever.

The real-world meaning of this concept is **institutional capacity**. The key question is not just "who has power _within_ the system?" but "how much power does the system _itself_ have?" In powerful and developed countries like the US or China, they have enormous power that allows their decisions to be mattered more, them to host international events like the Olympics, and so on.

On the opposite side of the spectrum are the third world countries, where power is divided and even if someone has a zero-sum power, meaning they hold practically all the power in the country, it does not mean much when they face a power like the US.
