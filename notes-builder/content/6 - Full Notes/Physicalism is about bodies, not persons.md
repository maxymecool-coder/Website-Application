# Physicalism is about bodies, not persons
**Details**
This clarification that carries all the weight: "just a body" is not "just any old body." Chalk, a phone, and a person are all material objects with wildly unequal repertoires. The physicalist is not saying persons are as inert as chalk; he is betting that nothing in the human repertoire requires a second kind of stuff. So the thesis is ambitious about matter and what it can do, not deflationary about persons.

This matters because the instinctive resistance — _I am not just meat_ — is aimed at a deflationary reading nobody defends. Whatever is genuinely hard about physicalism (consciousness, the felt quality of experience) has to be stated as a claim that some specific capacity outruns what any physical system could do. For whatever reason something that we possess are different, basically.

Two branches Kagan names and sets aside: idealism (minds and their ideas exist; matter is a way of talking about them) and the dual-aspect view (mind and body are two views of an underlying reality that is neither). Worth registering that this is a pragmatic narrowing, not a refutation. Both of these can be true, its just not argued in the course.
