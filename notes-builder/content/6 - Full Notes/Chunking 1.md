# Chunking
**Details**
**Chunking** is a powerful encoding strategy that helps overcome the natural limitations of short-term/working memory. While working memory can typically only hold a small number of items (around 7 +/- 2), chunking allows us to effectively increase this capacity by grouping individual elements into larger, meaningful units known as "chunks."

This process works by organizing disparate items based on patterns, similarities, or meanings drawn from our long-term memory. For example, the 10-digit sequence `0912199010` is difficult to hold in memory. However, by chunking it into a familiar format like a date and a number, `09-12-1990-10`, it becomes four manageable chunks. Similarly, the letter string `FBICIAIRS` becomes easy to recall when chunked into three familiar acronyms: `FBI`, `CIA`, `IRS`. By reducing the number of discrete items to be stored, chunking makes complex information easier to encode and recall.
