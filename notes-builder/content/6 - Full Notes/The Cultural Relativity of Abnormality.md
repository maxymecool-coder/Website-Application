# The Cultural Relativity of Abnormality
**Details**
What is considered normal or abnormal behavior varies dramatically across different cultures. This concept of **cultural relativity** is a major challenge for creating a universal system of diagnosis.

**Behaviors Normal in One Culture, Abnormal in Another:**

- **Professional Wrestling (USA):** In American culture, men in tight underwear simulating violence for entertainment is a multi-million dollar industry. In many other cultures, this behavior would be seen as bizarre and pathological.
    
- **"Demon Possession" (Sudan):** In parts of Sudan, it is considered a normal and understandable reaction for a woman under extreme stress to become "possessed by demons," acting violently and screaming obscenities. The "cure" is not medication, but a brief holiday away from her family.
    
- **"Brain Fag" Syndrome (Sub-Saharan Africa):** In some African cultures, it is believed that studying too hard can cause debilitating headaches in children. The accepted treatment is to be excused from schoolwork.
    

**Culture-Bound Syndromes:** Some disorders appear to be created by or are unique to specific cultures.

- **Multiple Personality Disorder (USA):** This disorder became rampant in the United States in the 1960s-80s but was virtually nonexistent in other countries. It has since almost completely disappeared, suggesting it may have been a culturally-created phenomenon.
    
- **"Running Amok" (China):** A few years ago, there was a spate of incidents where overworked men in China would grab weapons and attack children in elementary schools. This specific expression of psychological breakdown was a temporary, culture-specific phenomenon that has since faded.
    

These examples demonstrate that culture not only defines what is abnormal but can also create unique forms of mental distress.
