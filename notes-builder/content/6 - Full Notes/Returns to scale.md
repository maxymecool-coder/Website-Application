# Returns to scale
**Details**
The first wrinkle is returns to scale: what happens to output when you scale all inputs up by the same factor. That's what we mean by a firm getting bigger.

- **Constant returns:** f(2L, 2K) = 2f(L, K). Double the inputs and you double the output.
- **Decreasing returns:** f(2L, 2K) < 2f(L, K). Gruber says this is the usual assumption: two people with two shovels on one hole get in each other's way.
- **Increasing returns:** f(2L, 2K) > 2f(L, K). A bigger firm can specialize and get better at what it does.

Returns to scale sit at the core of every model of economic growth in macroeconomics. Economists usually think returns increase at first, as a firm specializes, then decrease as it matures. If they increased forever, one firm would end up producing everything. Gruber suggests search engines might come close. He points to the then-ongoing antitrust trial, in which Google might be broken up.
