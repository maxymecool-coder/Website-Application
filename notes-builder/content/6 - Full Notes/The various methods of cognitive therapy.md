# The various methods of cognitive therapy
**Details**
There exists many methods which can make a patient become more realistic about his appraisals and his world's as well. The "Intellectual" approach consists of identifying such misconceptions, testing their validity, and substituting more appropriate concepts. More often than not, the fuel for broad attitudinal change comes from the patient's own recognition that the rules he has instated on his unconscious has guided his thinking and behavior to deceive and make him miserable.

The second is the "experiential" approach, which exposes the patient to experiences that are in themselves powerful enough to change these misconceptions. This consists of interactions with other individuals in certain organized situations, which can help patients the same as the first method, to modify his inappropriate maladaptive responses to these situations. Similarly, a patient who receives unyielding warmth and acceptance from his therapist, often changes his view on authority figures. Such a change has been labeled "corrective emotional experience" (Alexander, 1950).

The "behavioral" approach is the last, and it encourages the development of specific forms of behavior that leads to more general changes in the way the patient views himself and the real world. As he practices methods to deal with people who frighten him or his fears, which can also be called "assertive training," it enables him to increase his confidence and deal with such situations better.
