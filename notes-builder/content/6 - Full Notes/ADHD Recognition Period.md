# ADHD Recognition Period
**Details**
Although the DSM-5 recognizes that ADHD symptoms develop around 12 years old and symptoms must already exist since then, it is probable that people do not recognize their symptoms from back when they were children as they were occupied with other tasks such as studying and so on.

A person can have ADHD symptoms when they are far older than 12 as ADHD is a mental disorder that persists throughout the course of a person's life. Symptoms can pop up latter in life when a routinely schedule gets ruined or a person is going through a tough time, which can throw them off-course and allow ADHD to take root, preventing them from focusing effectively again.

In fact, statistics support this fact as Approximately **14.3% of adolescents aged 12–17** in the U.S. have been diagnosed with ADHD, according to data from the CDC. [CHADD](https://chadd.org/about-adhd/general-prevalence-children/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) This rate is higher than the 8.6% prevalence among children aged 5–11. These conditions may be recognized much later in life rather than within the time period of developing the disorder.

Furthermore, the delay of getting a formal diagnosis can also stem from a lack of information on the subject as ADHD is not common knowledge.
