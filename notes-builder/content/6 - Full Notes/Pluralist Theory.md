# Pluralist Theory
**Details**
As a direct critique to the [[Power Elite Theory]], this argues that the power is distributed amongst groups as they compete against each other, with different views, needs, and so on. 

Pioneered by Robert Dahl in his study of New Haven (_Who Governs_), the research methods start from the political decisions then tracing back to those who make such decisions and understanding them. Dahl found out that different groups had parts in different kinds of politics, for example, parents and teachers mattered in school politics; real estate developers and banks were central to urban renewal; and party activists were key in selecting political candidates. There was no single group that dominated across all issue areas.

Most people don't care about most political issues. People and groups mobilize around issues that directly affect their interests ("whose ox gets gored"). The group that cares the most about a specific issue (e.g., gun rights advocates, abortion opponents) will have a disproportionate influence on that issue because of their intense focus and mobilization.

Same with [[Power Elite Theory]], there are drawbacks. Changes cannot be made easily as acceptance needs to be gained from many groups, but they can also be stopped easily because of the same nature. These people also only care about what really matters to them and mobilize to do so, and then so would vote for those who are going to mobilize and promise to manage their causes.
