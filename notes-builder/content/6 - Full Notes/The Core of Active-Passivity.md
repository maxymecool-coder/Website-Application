# The Core of Active-Passivity
**Details**
People with BPD usually actually thinks that they are problem solving, just in a different way. They’re thinking that they are merely utilizing the tools at their disposal by requesting your assistance on help. But as always, BPD makes this get lost in translation, instead of “can you help me” it comes out as “YOU NEED TO HELP ME WITH THIS PROBLEM”. This makes it sounds very passive-aggressive but they’re not doing it intentionally, and they genuinely believe that they were actually reasonably requesting.

And because of that, you shouldn’t treat them harshly, and rather with empathy as you now understand why they act that way. Then, you can start helping them to develop said skills by basically assessing their problems with the current situation. The problems fall into the existing questions:

What is the problem, what needs to happen. To help them with this, work with them on clarifying the problem definition and helping her to problem solve along the way, make sure to validate her.

Are you afraid, or too angry to be effective, or too embarrassed of having to do it. If this is the problem, check the facts of situation, see if they genuinely will react in the way your loved one thinks they will, and proceed.

And if they do not believe in themselves being able to solve the problem. Try to role-play the situation with them to train their confidence on solving the problem (this is a method used in DBT therapy to help patients develop said skills).
