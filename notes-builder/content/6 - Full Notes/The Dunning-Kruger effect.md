# The Dunning-Kruger effect
**Details**
The definition for this phenomenon is basically that incompetent people overestimate their own competence and fail to sense a mismatch between their actual level of competence and their actual skill level. But these people have been shown to be able to raise their competence by improving their metacognition and skills that they are bad at.

Many experiments have shown this, testing students of their logic and rating their own performance, so on and so forth. Even after being shown others' competence and answers they still fail to judge their own questions accurately. The last experiment which explored whether they could improve their judgement. Receiving training after the tests and such, and pitched against a part of the same former group who did something else rather than training, they did perform better than the group that did nothing.

There are quite a few theories on why these people fail to see their own unskillfulness. One is that people seldomly receive negative feedback on their own incompetence because others are too afraid of delivering bad news, and even if they do receive it, they still need to understand why it is that bad.

**Supporting Content**
At times, this incompetence in certain fields can be frank, like sports, where people who are clearly athletic are picked and those who are not are left out. And so students may have a bad view of their own capabilities from the start, preventing them from trying to improve in that field.
