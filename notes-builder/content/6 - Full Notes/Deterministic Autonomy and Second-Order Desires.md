# Deterministic Autonomy and Second-Order Desires
**Details**
To understand second-order desires, we must understand the former "first-order desires" which are immediate impulses or cravings. However, as we have autonomy, it implies our capacity for "second-order desires," which are desires about what desires one wants to have. A smoker may have a first-order desire for nicotine but a second-order desire to be healthy and free from addiction. Agency wouldn't exist when someone fails to control their "first-order desires." Conversely, "deterministic autonomy" states when an agent is able to reflect critically on their commitments and modify their first-order desires to align with their higher-order volitions.

And again, even if it can be explained that our "deterministic autonomy" states that we are inherently choiceless in reviewing our thought process and thoughts in general, it does not strip away the fact that we can still rationally act through our values, logic, and purposes.
