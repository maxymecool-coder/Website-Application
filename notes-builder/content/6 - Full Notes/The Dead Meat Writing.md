# The Dead Meat Writing
**Details**
Dead Meat as a Youtube channel started out with their "kill count" series as the main premise, it was basically just narrating films and counting the kills of said films. The first few episodes were particularly built on indie horror films that usually had their main killer a figure corresponding to an American holiday of some sorts. As the years go by, the team started growing and writing better, now including behind the scenes segments and just an improvement to the writing overall, with better jokes, skits, and narration.

This is where Dead Meat diverges from the norm of random AI voiceover film recap channels who just try to publish as much slop as possible. The host of the show, James A Janisse (cool ass name by the way), include his personal stories, behind the scenes and experiences with people involve in said movies (as he is somewhat of a figure of the horror genre now as Dead Meat is such a popular show) which keeps the episodes always fresh and entertaining.

Though the focus has some what divulged, Dead Meat still tries to keep the integrity of their show (the kills) as much as possible by basically detailing/making them more interesting. By narrating, joking, and talking about the process of making such kills behind the scenes, these segments are still the main and most interesting part of the series, together with the overall commentary of the movies of course. Most of the time, they try to preserve the "feel" of these kills by making their content uncensored, which means they have to rely on their patreons and sponsors.

Moreover, they have started to expand their content, ranging into the horror games territory with Zoris hosting the show. Zoris was an editor and a part of the writer team of Dead Meat, he is extremely funny (his sense of humor gets me) and overall is now a part of the people narrating Dead Meat (like how MatPat left it to his own team members). They also have a yearly Dead Meat awards for hosting their own best kills, horror movies, and so on.

Overall, Dead Meat is straight up gas and is probably the easiest way to get into horror for many people who don't want to watch horror movies with its excellent commentary, writing, editing, and hosting.
