# Analytical Behaviorism
**Details**
From Ryle's argument against Descartes about how the latter mistaken expression and states that we found ourselves in to be different from the mind itself develops Analytical Behaviorism. This view posits that all the psychological terms are not referring to our inner, hidden world but rather logical constructions to classify complex social behaviors.

For example, Wittgenstein’s analysis of "expecting tea at 4 o'clock" involves putting on the kettle, looking at the watch, and glancing out the window. There is no special "expectation pang" inside the soul that constitutes the expecting; the set of behaviors _is_ the expecting. Psychology, therefore, is not the search for the hidden causes of behavior, but the analysis and classification of behavior itself. We are not "ghosts in machines"; we are organisms that behave intelligently.

To dismantle Logical Behaviorism, Hilary Putnam relies on the linguistic distinction between _analytic_ and _synthetic_ truths. An **analytic truth** is true by definition; one cannot understand the words without acknowledging the truth (e.g., "Bachelors are unmarried" or "Tuesday comes after Monday").
