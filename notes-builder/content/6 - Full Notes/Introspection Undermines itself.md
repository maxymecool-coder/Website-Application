# Introspection Undermines itself
**Details**
The question: can thinking about what you like actually lead you to choose things you'll like _less_?

**Nisbett & Wilson (1977)** — the paper the lecturer calls arguably the most important in social psychology over the last 40 years. People primed with "ocean" and "moon" preferentially choose Tide detergent. Not surprising given everything about priming. But when asked _why_ they chose Tide, they confidently generate completely fabricated reasons: "my mom uses Tide," "I like the box." They aren't lying or covering up — they genuinely believe these are their reasons. They have no access to the actual cause of their preference and don't know they lack that access.

**Wilson's art print study** — the lecturer's centerpiece. People choose between a Monet print and a Garfield cartoon. Without introspection, people prefer the Monet (unsurprisingly). But when asked to **write out reasons** for their preferences before choosing, the preference shifts dramatically — the cartoon jumps in popularity. People take their print home. Two weeks later, those who chose the Monet without introspecting still like it. Those who introspected and chose the cartoon now say they chose badly and wish they'd taken the Monet.

But why does this happen ? Nonetheless, the thing is we are verbally suppressing our gut feelings.
