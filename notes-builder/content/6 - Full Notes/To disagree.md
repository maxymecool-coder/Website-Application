# To disagree
**Details**
If the reader disagrees with the author then he must be able to show where the author is wrong, what he is wrong about and so on. They are not merely voicing prejudices or expressing emotions, but fulfilling these conditions:

The first is this. Since men are animals as well as rational, it is necessary to acknowledge the emotions you bring to a dispute, or those that arise in the course of it. Otherwise you are likely to be giving vent to feelings, not stating reasons. You may even think you have reasons, when all you have are strong feelings.

Second, you must make your own assumptions explicit. You must know what your prejudices—that is, your prejudgments—are. Otherwise you are not likely to admit that your opponent may be equally entitled to different assumptions. Good controversy should not be a quarrel about assumptions. If an author, for example, explicitly asks you to take something for granted, the fact that the opposite can also be done so does not prevent you from honoring his request. You must give him a fair hearing.

Third and finally, try to give more light, less heat in the argument, try to take the other point of view, you must also read sympathetically, so that your disagreement will be civil, not contentious.

These are the ideal rules for disagreeing with an author, but we usually won’t achieve this state… so we should not expect the author to do so as well. Do not expect him to be perfect as we are no different, and so we should not confine ourselves to such rules, but rather be more vigilant of the points that it is asking of us.
