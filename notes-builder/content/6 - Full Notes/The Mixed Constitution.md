# The Mixed Constitution
**Details**
As stated before, Locke is strongly against the way of forcing a person to serve under a political obligation merely due to the biological and geographical factors that they are born under. And so he instead structurally divides the concept of social compact into two totally distinct criteria: the fundamental legitimacy of the regime's form itself, and the precise, individual conditions under which a person becomes morally obligated to obey it. But how does one manage its supreme legitimacy ? Well we can look to a country like England, where it is split between the Crown and Parliament. Locke designates these as coordinate, mutually dependent powers—neither is fully supreme over the other in a strictly commanding sense. Parliament cannot enact laws without the King's consent, and the King cannot raise taxes or enforce laws without Parliament. And yet this proposes a terrifying vulnerability: if there is a chronic, systematic conflict between these two figures, then there is absolutely no mechanism to resolve it. From this, Locke argues that when such a persistent constitutional crisis occurs, there is an "appeal to heaven" for a justifiable righteous civil war.
