# Consumer Choice
**Details**
This is the result that we derive from as we combine [[Budget Constraint]] with the [[Indifference curves and tradeoffs]], or in particular the tangency. As the indifference curves represent the customer's preferences and the budget constraint represents their ability to fulfill these preferences, bundled with the fact that higher indifferences curves are preferred, the best affordable bundle is the point where they touch each other at the highest point.

The tangency represents the equality between these two different slopes, and mathematically, they are perfect when they are equal. In the pizza-cookie example, the MRS is the negative ratio of marginal utilities, while the MRT is the negative ratio of prices. Setting them equal gives the central rule of consumer utility maximization: the ratio of marginal utilities equals the ratio of prices. This means the consumer chooses a bundle where the subjective value of another cookie relative to another pizza slice matches the relative cost of cookies and pizza in the market. If the consumer values cookies more than their market cost, they should buy more cookies and less pizza. If they value pizza more than its market cost, they should buy more pizza and fewer cookies.
