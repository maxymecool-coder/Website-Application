# Superstitious Behavior
**Details**
This concept arises from the incorrect assumption from an accidental or non-contingent reinforcement, making the subject believe whatever they were doing was the cause of that result, and subsequently trying to repeat it to achieve such results. The reason for this is that our brains are powerful pattern recognizers, it craves patterns, even in things where patterns do not exist, it will hallucinate one.

B.F. Skinner first demonstrated this by providing food to pigeons at random intervals, regardless of what they were doing. The pigeons began to repeat whatever specific action they happened to be performing just before the food arrived—one might turn in a circle, another might bob its head. They had developed a personal ritual that they falsely associated with the reward.

This phenomenon is readily observable in humans. A baseball player who hits a home run after coincidentally tapping his shoes a certain way may develop a pre-batting ritual of tapping his shoes. A student who scores well on an exam after arranging their pencils in a specific way may feel compelled to repeat that arrangement for future exams. The behavior doesn't actually cause the outcome, but the accidental reinforcement was strong enough to create a powerful, albeit irrational, learned association.
