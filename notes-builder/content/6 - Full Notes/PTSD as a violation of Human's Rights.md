# PTSD as a violation of Human's Rights
**Details**
Dr. Darren Richiter’s lecture reframes Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) not merely as a medical diagnosis but as a direct, measurable _damage_ resulting from a crime. Unlike most psychiatric disorders in the DSM, PTSD is one of the few that requires a specific external cause (Criterion A). In the context of Dr. Richiter's work with refugees and survivors, this cause is invariably a human rights violation such as torture, genocide, or other war crimes.

This crucial link between the crime (the cause) and the psychological injury (the effect) allows for a powerful shift in legal strategy. It enables the use of psychiatric epidemiology—population-level data on mental health—as formal evidence in international criminal tribunals. By demonstrating a statistically significant increase in PTSD prevalence in a post-conflict population, such as in Cambodia after the Khmer Rouge regime, prosecutors can quantify the immense psychological harm inflicted upon an entire people. This approach transforms the abstract concept of "suffering" into concrete, scientific evidence that can influence judgments, strengthen the case for crimes against humanity, and contribute to sentencing, as was successfully done in the Khmer Rouge tribunal.
