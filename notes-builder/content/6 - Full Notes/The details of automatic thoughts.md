# The details of automatic thoughts
**Details**
Guessing and asking about what one “must be thinking” made it possible to define with great precision what he was actually thinking. 

These automatic thoughts actually had a great number of similar characteristics. They generally were not vague and unformulated, but specific and discrete. They were formed with only essential words in a sentence. Moreover, they did not occur as a result of thinking about an event or opinion, it just appeared, as if by reflex. And when they appeared, usually without effort and difficult to get out of one’s head. 

In addition, the patient often considered these thoughts as reasonable, although others would say it doesn’t make sense. Most of the time there is no resistance in accepting the validity of these thoughts. And even if they did evaluate it all in a therapy session and eventually coming to the conclusion that it is illogical, later when they come again, they will revert to accepting the thoughts without second thoughts. And even when they external evidence directly object these thoughts, they will persist until the person stops having depression. 

Also, the thoughts that were repetitive and seemed to be the most powerful, are idiosyncratic. They were closer to the patients’ problems, and not peculiar to a singular patient. They also involved more distortion of reality than other types of thinking. 

All of this information of how someone thinks, functions to themselves will shed light on the inner workings of their emotional disorders. These thoughts prevailed more in depressed patients and less in ones who weren't.
