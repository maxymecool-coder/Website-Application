# High Self-Esteem and Failure
**Details**
High self-esteem is quite a defensive trait as it allows people to buffer against failure. Dodgson and Wood showed that immediately after failure, high self-esteem individuals become _faster_ at recognizing their strengths and _slower_ at recognizing their weaknesses, while the opposite is true for low self-esteem individuals. Moreover, they also are better at mood-repairing, selecting funny videos over neutral ones after failure, and they increase volunteering. However, high self-esteem creates vulnerability when threatened. Baumeister and Heatherton showed that ego-threatened high self-esteem individuals take reckless gambling risks (losing everything) and administer more aggressive noise blasts to others. The higher the standard is, the more desperate the response is.

When their ego is threatened, these individuals become the most dangerous people in the room - more aggressive, more reckless, more willing to harm others if it provides relative advantage. Baumeister's gambling studies show them losing everything through risky strategies; his aggression studies show them delivering the most intense noise blasts to opponents.

The baseline of self-regard that self-esteemed people create is so high that, when disrupted, produces an urgent need for restoration, and so they will do anything for that, even if it includes the suffering of others. This inherently challenges the assumption that self-esteem is a prosocial concept, as people are willing to act to be estimable, to look as if they have high self-esteem rather than actually having it.
