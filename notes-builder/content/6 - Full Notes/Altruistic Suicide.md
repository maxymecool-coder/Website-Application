# The Soldier's Suicide
**Details**
Émile Durkheim identifies the soldier's suicide as a primary example of **altruistic suicide**. This type is not driven by personal despair but by an excessive degree of social integration. The individual's identity is so completely absorbed into the group—the army, the nation—that the collective honor and goals are perceived as being of a higher value than their own life.

Consequently, the act of self-sacrifice, such as a soldier taking their own life to avoid capture or to complete a mission, is not experienced as a tragedy. Instead, it is seen as the ultimate fulfillment of one's duty. In Durkheim's view, the soldier is not melancholic or fearful but is often calm, resolute, and finds a sense of honor and purpose in dying for the cause. The basis for existence is situated entirely beyond individual life itself, making the act of dying for the collective a logical and even fulfilling choice.
