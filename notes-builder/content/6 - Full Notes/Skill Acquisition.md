# Skill Acquisition
**Details**
Depending on the difficulty of the skills, we can try to teach anywhere from 1-3, although it is of course better to acquire mastery in one skill over the others. Presenting many skills may overwhelm the client and suggests that the skills are easy. Secondly, teaching a large amount of skills then saying nothing works can make the client deflated as their resistance and motivation would run out before the amount of skills does.

Moreover, knowing what the task it, the person's role, and what one can expect from the other person facilitates learning enormously. This is orientation, and it called for each specific skill and each homework assignment. Some clients though, may have skill deficits and fear acquiring new ones. It can be useful to point out that learning it doesn't mean using it. A person can acquire then choose whether to use it or not. Also, it may help to reinforce them that the skill will work, but also to say that not every skill is gonna be useful.

Another point to bring up is about assessing the client's abilities. When assessing a client's ability, it is most difficult to determine if they are incapable of doing something or emotionally inhibited or constrained by environmental factors. These people don't have the metacognitive ability to assess their own abilities so it might be more difficult. For instance, they may confuse the inability to do something than being afraid to do it. Not only that, this stems from their own dissociation with themselves, being unable to tell their own thoughts/emotions, or rather, being afraid to do so. They don't wanna be vulnerable, and also their family have completely destroyed that part of them down to the ground. To more effectively see this, trainers can orchestrate situations or role play situations, emotional regulation can be assessed by interrupting (ragebaiting) then seeing if the client can change their state, and so on.

Instructions, this can vary anywhere from general guidelines to specific suggestions of the client's actions. Try to make it as clear and easy to understand as possible, for adolescents, it might help to make them read the instructions out loud. You may even get those who have done it before to make examples. Furthermore, they should try to explain all dimensions of it, when, why, and what. This helps so that they can deal with problems before clients encounter it in practical situations. Lastly, it is also important to get the client to understand that the immediate relief of pain is not the goal but rather the long-term relief and all.

Modeling can be provided by trainers, or other clients. It is basically supporting materials for the people who hasn't understand how to do it yet, and it can also be fitted into how people perceive it all. There are also many ways to model and help the person further understand.
