# Treatments of Conduct Disorder
**Details**
The treatments that seem to have the most effectiveness are ones that addresses the multiple systems that are involved in the life of a child.

Starting with family interventions, which is the most logical treatment of course, due to how maltreatment has shown to correlate to conduct disorder probabilities highly. As an example, in an RCT (Shaw, Dishion, Supplee, et al., 2006), researchers compared what is called the family checkup (FCU) treatment to none at all. FCU involves three meetings, to get to know, assess, and provide feedback to parents about their education and raising methods. FCU was offered to families with toddlers who had a high risk factor for conduct problems. The result was effective, not just short-term, but even 2 years after the intervention.

Another method is a behavioral program called parent management training (PMT), in which they are taught to reward their children for prosocial behaviors rather than anti-social. Of course this treatment can be modified, but, in general, it is the most efficacious intervention to prevent conduct disorder and oppositional defiant disorder. It can be effective anywhere from 1-3 years.

Lastly, multisystemic treatment (MST) has also proven to be promising. It is basically giving intensive and comprehensive therapy services in the community, targeting adolescents, family, school, even the peer group. They basically work on the premise that all these things combine to incite the appearance of conduct disorder. The methods of applying can vary widely, behavioral, cognitive, family, and case-management techniques depending on the situation. It has the highest effective rate, which can range up to 4 years.
