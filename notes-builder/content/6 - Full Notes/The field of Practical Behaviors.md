# Ethology
**Details**
Instead of the view of the [[Behaviorism Psychology]] psychologists, there were people in Europe that followed the philosophy, as Niko Tinbergen put it, was to "interview an animal in its own language. This would mean they had to leave the lab, the control environment and rather go out to the animals' habitat to investigate what was actually happening there.

**Supporting Content**
The reason for studying these kind of things in nature is that a lot of variables are lost in a lab, like the language and communication between these beings. For example, a male elk's bellow doesn't just echo through a valley; it travels to a female and triggers her to ovulate. Rats communicate in ultrasonic giggles that signal a "party" to other rats. The smell of a terrified person's sweat can prime our own brains to perceive fear in ambiguous faces. This hidden world of communication extends into modalities we barely possess. Electric fish sing territorial songs and jam each other's frequencies. Elephants send seismic messages to one another through the soles of their feet. To study an animal in a cage is to study a dolphin in a bathtub; you miss the vast, unseen ocean of sensory information that shapes its existence.
