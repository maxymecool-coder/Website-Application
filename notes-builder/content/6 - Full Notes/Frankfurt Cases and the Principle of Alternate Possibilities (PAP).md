# Frankfurt Cases and the Principle of Alternate Possibilities (PAP)
**Details**
The concept of PAP, or Principle of Alternate Possibilities asserts that an agent is morally responsible for a situation if there existed a world where they could have acted otherwise. Harry Frankfurt challenged this via thought experiments now known as "Frankfurt Cases," which attempt to separate responsibility from the ability to do otherwise. In a typical scenario, an agent (Jones) chooses to perform an action $A$. However, a manipulator (Black) is monitoring Jones’s brain and would have intervened to _force_ Jones to do $A$ had Jones shown any sign of hesitating. Jones does $A$ on his own, so we intuitively hold him responsible, yet he could not have done otherwise due to Black's presence. This suggests moral responsibility stems from the action flowing from the agent's own will, not from the metaphysical possibility of an alternative. But, there still exists an argument against this, being if Jones were to choose $B$ but then diverted to $A$, then he still showed a flicker of freedom, and that itself shows some nuance about the responsibility that he has.
