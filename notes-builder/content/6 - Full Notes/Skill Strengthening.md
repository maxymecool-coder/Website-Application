# Skills Strengthening
**Details**
Happening after [[Skill Acquisition]], then comes skill strengthening as it helps us to shape, refine, and increase the likelihood of its use. We have to have reinforced practice so that we can learn the skills, as it includes effortful behavior which counteracts the passive behavior of the emotionally-dysregulated patients.

Practice of skills can be "overt" or "covert". Covert is practicing the requisite response in the imagination whilst overt is behavioral rehearsal, like roleplaying. Covert response may be more effective than overt methods for teaching more complex skills and also useful for when clients refuse to engage in overt rehearsal. To practice emotional behavior, clients have to practice specific components of emotions, like their faces, inner systems, muscles, and so on. When clients refuse to engage in roleplay/behavioral rehearsal, the trainers would then have to try to talk them through a dialogue or something of the sorts.

The next step is response reinforcement, which is like [[Classical conditioning]], meaning they have to be positive reinforced for their actions and growth. Many individuals have lived in environments that overuse punishment, this makes them expect negative, punishing feedback from the world (including their therapists) which also makes them punish themselves to shape their own behavior. Over the long run though, this reinforcement can increase their self image in a positive way, which enhances the sense of control they have over their lives. A reinforcer though can also be negative, meaning it can take away or discourage the response of people, and so one must be careful to not accidentally negatively reinforce the clients. Lastly, trainers also have to completely be sure about their decisions and reinforcing, not skipping over analyzing failures and so on.

Lastly, bringing our views to feedback, which is necessary for these people whose actions and feedback that they get are so uncontaminated with interpretations about their presumed motives and intent. By trying to judge their motives and getting it wrong, this would discourage the clients from listening and absorbing the feedback. So instead, one should focus on their behaviors, not thinking about their intent. Also, the amount of feedback has to be optimal so that it won't lead to stimulus overload and/or discouragement. But again, these feedback may be interpreted as negative and so people need to surrounding it with positivity like talking about people's own inability to do it, as it isn't just them. In addition, there is "coaching", which is combining feedback with instructions.
