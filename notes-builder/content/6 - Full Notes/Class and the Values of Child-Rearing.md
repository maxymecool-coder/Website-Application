# Class and the Values of Child-Rearing
**Details**
The pioneering research of sociologist Melvin Kohn showed us a consistent pattern of the alignment of parents' values and their own children.

Middle-class parents, whose professional careers often demand autonomy, creativity, and self-direction, consistently value **independence** and self-reliance in their children. They encourage them to question authority, negotiate for themselves, and develop their unique talents. In contrast, working-class parents, whose jobs historically required following orders, adhering to strict procedures, and showing deference to supervisors, tend to value **obedience** and conformity more highly. This is not because they are inherently more authoritarian, but because they are engaging in a rational adaptation. They are equipping their children with the cultural tools they believe are necessary to survive and thrive in the world they know. Kohn’s research further showed that when working-class parents had jobs with more autonomy and protection—such as those in unionized factories—their values shifted to more closely resemble those of the middle class, reinforcing the direct link between one's position in the economic structure and the cultural values one transmits.
