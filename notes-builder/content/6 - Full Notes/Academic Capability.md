# Academic Capability
**Details**
To be admitted into college, an individual generally must be able to thrive in formal academic settings. This highly focused and specific ability can be called "academic capability." But institutions like colleges have taken this academic capability to be the same thing as intelligence. Certainly, anyone who has gone into the world would realize how incorrect this is. A person doesn’t need to have academic capability to offer wisdom, but institutions have locked this entire genre of thought out of their halls. So, with their vast expanse of knowledge, academic institutions offer only one segment of the wisdom that the world has on offer. These places are full of the academically capable, not necessarily the intelligent.

So what is this genre of intelligence that institutions find so uninteresting? It is tempting to consider this idea of going out into the world as traveling to faraway places and distant locales. That would be a mistake. Instead, as Emerson notes, we must sit at the feet of the familiar. We must learn the insight of the commonplace—of songs in the streets, of the breeze through the meadows—of things which are not found in the fortress of academic pursuit. Only then can we understand the world in which we live, rather than the picture of it we have chosen to accept. Simple things have meaning. As Emerson says, a drop is an ocean, the pleasing is the sublime, the decent is the beautiful.

Perhaps one may be able to live their whole life studying academia and even making a decent salary for the work. Perhaps they can live happily in the world created by these academic pursuits. But when that individual lies on their deathbed, they must realize the world they have lived in is a fiction. It is missing pieces. It is a cabin that is missing a wall. And then, with death, it is of course far too late to build that wall.
