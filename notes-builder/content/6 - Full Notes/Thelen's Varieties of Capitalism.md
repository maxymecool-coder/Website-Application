# Thelen's Varieties of Capitalism
**Details**
Kathleen Thelen's theory explains how advanced capitalist countries respond to globalization in distinct ways, challenging the idea that all are forced into a single, deregulated model. She identifies three divergent paths:

1. **Deregulation (Liberal Market Economies):** Countries like the U.S. and Britain, which already had market-driven systems, have responded to global pressures by doubling down on deregulation. This involves weakening unions, increasing competition, and reducing social protections, creating a highly flexible but insecure labor market.
    
2. **Dualization (Coordinated Market Economies):** Germany exemplifies this path. The core of its coordinated economy—centralized wage bargaining between powerful unions and employers—remains intact for "insider" workers in major industries. However, a growing secondary tier of "outsider" workers in the service sector is not covered by these protections. This creates a dual system: stable, high-wage jobs for some, and flexible, less secure work for others. This pattern is maintained by a political coalition (Christian Democrats) historically built around protecting the "male breadwinner" wage.
    
3. **Embedded Flexibilization (Scandinavian Countries):** These nations have also embraced market flexibility, but they have "embedded" it within a strong public safety net. The government, not the employer, guarantees worker security through universal healthcare, pensions that move with the worker from job to job, extensive job retraining programs, and subsidized childcare. This allows firms to be highly competitive and dynamic, while workers remain secure. This model is supported by a political coalition (Social Democrats) that includes women and is built on broad support for a universal welfare state.
    

Thelen's key insight is that these different paths are durable because powerful **political coalitions** form around them, reinforcing the institutional arrangements that serve their interests.
