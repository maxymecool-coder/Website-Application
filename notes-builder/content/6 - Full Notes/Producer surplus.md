# Producer surplus
**Details**
Producer surplus is basically the gap between the price and the producer's willingness to supply, which is its marginal cost. One gas producer facing $3 and making 100 gallons (Figure 9-10) earns surplus on every gallon whose marginal cost is below $3. That's the area above its supply curve and below the price. Gruber says this is basically profit, which makes producer surplus easier to grasp than consumer surplus: it's just dollars.

For the whole market (Figure 9-11), the supply curve slopes up because firms differ in cost. It's Lecture 8's cotton example, smoothed into a straight line. The most efficient producer has nearly zero marginal cost, so it keeps almost the full $3 on each gallon. Moving right along the curve, costs rise, and profit per gallon shrinks. At the marginal producer, A, price = marginal cost = average cost, so its profit is zero, and nobody further right enters. In this market, producer surplus is exactly the profit of the lower-cost firms.
