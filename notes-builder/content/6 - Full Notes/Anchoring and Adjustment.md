# Anchoring and Adjustment
**Details**
When estimating unknown quantities, people seize on whatever number is possible as a mental anchor, and then adjust insufficiently on it. Groups asked "Is the Mississippi River longer or shorter than 750 miles?" then estimate its length cluster around 1,200–1,300 miles. Groups anchored at 3,700 miles estimate around 2,500. Same as for the birthday problem: knowing 366 people guarantee a shared birthday, people anchor on 366 and estimate 182 (half) for 50% probability. The actual answer is 23—but reaching it requires genuine computation, while anchoring requires only lazy arithmetic. This connects directly to Gilbert's sequential operations model of attribution: the initial dispositional inference serves as an anchor, and situational correction is the (insufficient) adjustment. In both domains, the starting point exerts disproportionate influence because adjusting away from it requires cognitive effort, and effort is the scarce resource that cognition perpetually conserves.
