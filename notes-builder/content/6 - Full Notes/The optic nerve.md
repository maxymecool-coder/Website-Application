# The optic nerve
**Details**
The intricate processing that begins in the retina culminates in a grand exit. After signals pass from photoreceptors to bipolar cells, they reach the **ganglion cells**. The long axons of these ganglion cells bundle together to form the **optic nerve**, the primary cable carrying all visual information from the eye to the brain. In a curious quirk of biological design, the optic nerve turns around and exits from the back of the eye. At a structure called the **optic chiasm**, about half of the axons from each eye cross over to the opposite side of the brain. The main destination for this information is the thalamus, a central relay station, which then sends the signals onward to the primary visual cortex in the occipital lobe, where conscious perception finally begins. And so this also corresponds with contra-lateralization, which means the left eye information is processed by the right and the opposite is true to, to be more effective and quick.
