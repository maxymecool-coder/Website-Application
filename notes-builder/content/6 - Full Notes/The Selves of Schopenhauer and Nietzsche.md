# The Selves of Schopenhauer and Nietzsche
**Details**
These two basically had the same internal division but assign opposite moral valuations to each side, and so they have different visions of human flourishing. Schopenhauer split the self into Will (animalistic drive, selfish, degrading) and Idea (reflective thought enabling transcendence). This meant that he believed that we were monsters, brutal, but the idea makes us noble. The idea is quite similar to Freud's Id and Superego — unsurprisingly, since Freud denied reading Schopenhauer but was found posthumously to have annotated copies of his works hidden under his bed. Nietzsche, on the other hand, recast the duality as Dionysian (losing oneself, merging with the world, raw experience) and Apollonian (light, individuation, regulation, categorical thinking). The crucial difference is that he had a more balanced view, in that the Dionysian was required as it is the moments that we truly experience happiness and what we want (similar to when in a concert we may dance with everyone or such like that). The Apollonian, while necessary for civilization, produces an artificial, emotionally sterile existence when it dominates. It tells you what _should_ make you happy through analysis and categories, but what we analytically decide should satisfy us rarely does — an insight echoed by Tim Wilson's modern research on introspection (resembling the Spectacle as well).
