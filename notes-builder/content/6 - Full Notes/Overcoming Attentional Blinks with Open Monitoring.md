# Overcoming Attentional Blinks with Open Monitoring
**Details**
A significant barrier to sustained focus is a cognitive glitch known as an "attentional blink." This occurs when, after successfully identifying a target of our focus, our brain momentarily shuts down its attention, causing us to miss information that appears immediately afterward. This phenomenon is a key reason why we feel distractible; our focus isn't just wandering, it's actively blinking off. Individuals with ADHD may experience these blinks more frequently, but it is a universal aspect of how our attention system works. It's as if our brain celebrates finding what it's looking for with a brief pause, during which we become blind to new information.

A powerful, non-pharmacological method to counteract this is to consciously shift our mode of visual processing. Typically, we focus with a narrow, "soda straw" view of the world. The alternative is to engage in **open monitoring**, also known as **panoramic vision**. This involves deliberately dilating your gaze to take in your entire visual field at once—the walls, the ceiling, the floor—without focusing on any single point. This wide-angle view activates a different neural stream in the brain that is better at processing information over time, effectively increasing its "frame rate." By practicing this open-gaze state, you can train your brain to reduce the frequency and duration of attentional blinks, leading to a more stable and continuous state of focus.
