# Syntax and the Structural Hierarchy of Logic
**Details**
[[Propositional Logic]] functions as another language to analyze the structure of truth preservation, independent of specific content. At its core, the syntax distinguishes between simple sentences (propositional constants) and compound sentences. A propositional constant acts as an abstract variable representing a single condition of the world that is binary, right or wrong, which is unlike natural language, which depends on so many other factors.

And so we have five fundamental operators to relate between these constants: negation, conjunction, disjunction, implication, and the biconditional. And to avoid ambiguity inherent in linear text, there's a hierarchy in this logic. Negation binds most tightly, followed by conjunction, disjunction, and implication, with the biconditional being the loosest.
