# Problems with Empiricism, Rationalism
**Details**
When arguing against Hume, there are two arguments: empiricist and rationalism, which both fail to come to a complete conclusion - something that Kant is trying to resolve.

For the empiricist, the problem is that you will get a thickening book of observations with nothing to pull together, as you have to depend on your own posteriori again to act a certain way and feel a certain way. For example, a man bleeding out in front of a bank where a robbery has clearly occurred. Do you feel like it’s a wrong thing because of life and because it’s an objective thing or it is internally, of yourself ? Kant argues here that even though it’s the latter, there has to be something that constitutes that feeling in the first place, so what is it ?

For rationalist, Leibniz and Wolff try to render experience intelligible through reason alone. Leibniz argues that with fully developed knowledge, all propositions — even factual ones — would turn out to be analytic, everything is what it is necessarily. This produces the conclusion (mocked by Voltaire in Candide) that this is "the best of all possible worlds." Rationalism overreaches — it deduces content it has no business claiming.

Leibniz attributed to Locke scholastic maxim “nihil est in intellectu quod non prius fuerit in sensu” which means that nothing is in the mind that wasn’t in the senses…but Leibniz added “nisi intellectus ipse” - except the intellect itself. The mind is not just a blank state, it interprets the senses in a certain way that makes it intelligible, the question is so: what ?
