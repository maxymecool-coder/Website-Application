# The Formality Problem
**Details**
A persistent objection to liberal political theory is that the basic liberties, even formally equal, are substantively unequal in practice. Even if citizens appear to be equal under the two principles of justice mentioned, because of background social and economic inequality, the wealthy can dominate legislation and social policy, thus rendering them as merely formal for everyone else.

Rawls's response is to distinguish between the worth and extent of a basic liberty. The extent may be the same for everyone but the worth differs depending on the person it is affecting, which is a problem reserved for the [[The Difference Principle]].

The solution introduced is the fair value of political liberties — the idea that whatever one's economic or social position, everyone must have a sufficiently equal opportunity to hold political office and influence political outcomes. This parallels fair equality of opportunity in the second principle but applies specifically to the political domain.

**Supporting Content**
On the other hand, forcing fairness upon everyone may be irrational, superfluous and socially divisive in this situation.
