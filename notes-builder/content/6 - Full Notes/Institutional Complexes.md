# Institutional Complexes
**Details**
If individual institutions like hospitals or law firms are the distinct organs of social life, then institutional complexes are the major systems—the anatomy—that connect these organs into a functioning ecosystem. These complexes represent the large-scale machinery that provides structure and order to modern society.

The primary function of these complexes is to establish a system of impersonal, predictable rules, which stands in contrast to societies governed by personal, arbitrary authority. To exemplify this, consider a community where rights and benefits are ensured by a single leader rather than by a systemic rule of law. In such a place, if the leader dislikes a resident, they can withdraw protection and access to resources at will. The resident's security is based on a personal relationship, not on guaranteed rights.

However, this ideal of a purely impersonal system is not absolute. Traces of personal rule persist even within modern institutional complexes, often through networking. The ability to leverage personal relationships—"knowing someone"—can grant access to services or opportunities that are not available through formal channels alone, revealing a lingering tension between systemic rules and personal influence.
