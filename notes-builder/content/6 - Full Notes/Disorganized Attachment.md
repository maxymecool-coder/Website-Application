# Disorganized Attachment
**Details**
But there is also a third group that was found out later, one that is less stably adapted and makes up the bulk in children clinics and a substantial portion of the adults in psychiatric clinics. Basically these group have caregivers who are both the source of distress and terror to the children but also their source of comfort. They always face a dilemma; their mothers are simultaneously necessary for survival and also their source of fear. They cannot utilize any techniques to get their way out of this situation.  This is "disorganized attachment", children with this exudes PTSD symptoms and also are intensely affectionate with strangers or may trust nobody. They don't have a sense of safety towards anyone or belong to anyone. 

Parental abuse is not the only reason that this can happen: Parents who are preoccupied with their own trauma, such as domestic abuse or rape can also be too unstable and inconsistent to provide comfort and protection. While all parents need all the help they can get to raise secure children, these would need help to attune to their child's needs (because they are severely already unattached). Research also shows that most factors wouldn't affect a child to become this way, it is the family's social economic state.

**Supporting Content**
To some degree these problems overlap with those of combat soldiers (PTSD), but they are also very different in that their childhood trauma has prevented them from developing some of the mental capacities that adult soldier possessed before their traumas.

These children usually get themselves into vicious cycles, like at school, lacking empathy for others and social skills would lead to them becoming unpopular and provoking rejection and punishment from their teachers and peers alike.
