# The argument of Psychological Egoism and Retorts
**Details**
When discussing about the moralities of a person, the question of why people do things come up quite often. One viewpoint that is perhaps cynical is the stance of psychological egoism. Psychological egoism posits that basically everything that humans do are for their own self-interests, regardless of how altruistic it may seem. An argument in this stance is the observation that whenever a person gets what they want, they feel pleasure. Since helping someone makes you feel good, that is what one is chasing, not altruism. The philosopher Joel Feinberg proposes multiple arguments against this claim.

Feinberg first challenges the assumption that what we chasing is pleasure. Feinberg basically posits that even if pleasure accompanies successful action, positing it as the purpose of all action is a non sequitur. Feinberg thinks that pleasure is only something that goes with the desire-fulfillment, not because we are inherently and selfishly chasing it. The main feeling when we are finished with doing anything is satisfaction, regardless of the pleasure or nothingness that it might bring us. And so when a situation where we accomplish something and feel nothing, that would go against the psychological egoist's argument.

The next argument is the argument of disinterested benevolence, where Feinberg turns the egoist's own evidence against him. He brings this up with the example of Lincoln, when Lincoln says that his saving of a the pigs is only the result of his own pleasure for the peace of his mind. Feinberg argues that the only reason we have pleasure from the activity is because we desired the wellness for the person or the being in the first place, effectively turning the point that the psychological egoists made for themselves into one for altruism. In other words, the pleasure was, and always had been the result of concern for the pigs, not the selfish need of peace of mind after. He also argues the same for malevolence, in the sense that the person is ready to be benevolent to others, the same can be said for malevolence, as in both cases the person can also sacrifices his own wellbeing to achieve his malevolent goal.
