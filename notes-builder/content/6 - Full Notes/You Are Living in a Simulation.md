# You Are Living in a Simulation
**Details**
Imagine a mad cartographer who crafts a map so vast and detailed it mirrors an empire perfectly—every mountain, river, and tree replicated at full scale. Then, he lays it over the empire itself, and soon, people can’t tell the map from the land beneath. What happens when the map becomes more real than the reality it covers? This is the heart of Jean Baudrillard’s _Simulacra and Simulation_, and it’s a chilling lens for understanding our digital world today.

Baudrillard’s parable starts simple: the map matches the empire one-to-one. But the cartographer doesn’t stop there—he tweaks it, making trees greener, mountains grander, until the map outshines the empire. People start living on this enhanced version, ignoring the crumbling reality below. Eventually, the empire fades, and the map stands alone. This shift—from a reflection of reality to a replacement of it—is what Baudrillard calls the move from simulation to simulacra, a copy with no original. Sound familiar? Think about your phone screen: curated feeds, filtered photos, and AI-generated art that dazzle us while the unpolished world outside dims.

Today, technology acts like that map. Social media doesn’t just reflect life—it polishes it, amplifies it, and often fakes it outright. Fake news sways elections, influencers sell impossible lifestyles, and virtual reality promises to erase geography itself. We’re not waiting for a metaverse; we’re already in it, layered over the physical world like a glossy veneer. The catch? The more we live on this map, the more the empire—our tangible reality—decays, neglected and forgotten.

We are strangers to our own world, and we are further strangers to each other. We cannot be with or for our humans we must be with or for the Spectacle. Achievements are becoming harder to be measured by connections, happiness. The Spectacle does not reward the happiness man or the closest of friends, but to achieve is now to get diplomas, to get high scores, go abroad. All of this is our Metaverse, our society.
