# Differences in contractualism
**Details**
Of course, when evaluating contractualism, one cannot help but to attempt to compare it to other social contract traditions: Contractarianism, Utilitarianism.

Contractarianism has its roots in Hobbes, which bases his theory on mutual self-interest. And so his argument is grounded upon the equal self-interest, one would seek to maximize their own interests to bargain with others. While under contractualism, one seeks to pursue their interests to justify others who have their own to do so.

Next is Kant, who bases his principles off of which that all rational agents would agree, under certain idealized conditions. Which one could argue is too disconnected and abstract from our moral lives. Furthermore, Kant seeks principles that people would agree too, rather than what no one could reasonably reject.
