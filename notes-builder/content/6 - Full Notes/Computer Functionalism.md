# Computer Functionalism
**Details**
The dominant view in psychology and artificial intelligence (AI) for the last several decades has been the **Computer Model of the Mind**. For the AI, its rules (algorithms) are the states and its physical symbols (syntaxes) are the inputs and outputs.

The litmus test for this model is the **Turing Test**, proposed by Alan Turing in 1950. Turing argued that if a computer could converse with a human (e.g., in English) so convincingly that the human could not distinguish it from another person, we must grant that the machine is intelligent. This relies on a behaviorist assumption: if the _output_ is indistinguishable from human intelligence, the internal process must be equivalent. John Searle challenges this by arguing that a machine can pass the Turing Test—simulating intelligence perfectly—without having any genuine understanding or mental life, or the Chinese test.
