# The Epistemic Explosion of The 16th Century
**Details**
When the 1500s came, there were many factors that contributed to the doubting of the people of religion and subsequently encouraged them to attempt to find out the truth. This included: the exploration of the New World, proving people wrong about the shape of the Earth and the maps of the Bible and such. 

Simultaneously, the Protestant Reformation initiated by Martin Luther in 1517 destroyed the idea of a unified theological truth. If the Church could be wrong about salvation, and the ancients could be wrong about geography, how could anyone be certain about anything? This intellectual chaos was compounded by the Thirty Years' War, a brutal conflict driven by religious disagreement that ended in a "peace of exhaustion" rather than a discovery of truth. Descartes and his contemporaries were writing in a world where the old foundations—Aristotle and the Church—had cracked, creating an urgent need for a new method of acquiring knowledge that didn't rely on trust. Their debates and such would cast even further doubt into believers, resulting in the churches weakening status.
