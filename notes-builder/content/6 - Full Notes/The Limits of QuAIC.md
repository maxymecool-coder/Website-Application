# The Limits of QuAIC
**Details**
The study concludes that the "QuAIC provided a useful standardized framework to screen reported concerns" and recommends it as a "quick and simple tool to guide case prioritization and intervention," particularly during the peak reporting periods that align with key semester activities. Its primary value lies in its ability to bring consistency and structure to the often-chaotic front end of the reporting process.  

However, the analysis also reveals a critical and nuanced limitation. Despite the tool's innovative design, which intentionally balances aggression and intrusion, the study found that the assessed level of concern "was more strongly related to overtly aggressive behavior than to intrusion". This suggests a form of systemic inertia. While the tool itself has evolved to formally recognize the significant harm caused by intrusive behaviors like stalking and harassment, the human and policy systems using the tool may still be preferentially wired to react more strongly to overt, physical threats.  

This discrepancy between the tool's design and its practical application points to a gap between technological innovation and cultural adaptation. The QuAIC provides the means to properly weight the impact of intrusion, but this finding suggests that practitioners may still be conditioned by training, experience, or policy to prioritize aggression. University disciplinary codes, for example, often have clearer, more decisive, and more punitive pathways for responding to a physical altercation than for a persistent pattern of harassing emails. Therefore, the "aggression bias" observed in the results is likely not a flaw in the tool itself, but a symptom of this larger systemic context. It highlights that implementing an innovative tool is only the first step. The second, and arguably more difficult, step is to evolve the surrounding organizational culture, policies, and training to fully leverage the tool's capabilities. This "aggression bias" identifies a critical area for future development and training for campus threat management teams.
