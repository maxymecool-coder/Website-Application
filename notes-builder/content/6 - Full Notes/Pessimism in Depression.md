# Pessimism in Depression
**Details**
In one way or another, we as humans, are living in the future. We interpret our experience not just by what the event means in the current state but as well as its possible results/consequences. For example, a young man just received a compliment from his girl, he would look forward to receiving more, might even think "she likes me," and he foresees a more intimate relationship with her. But if he is disappointed or rejected, he will likely anticipate future relationships alike this one.

Depressed patients have this even worse, as they tend to overgeneralize and be extreme. Since the patients regard the future as an extension of the present, they expect a deprivation to continue permanently, as it is always in absolute statements. Another way depression can develop pessimism is in the presumed deficiency of the self, which he also likely regards as a permanent defect.

Pessimism can engulf not only the future, but also the present of the patient. As they already expect themselves to not have the ability to do it before even indulging in it. It may even become so strong that the patient, even experiencing the success in something, still thinks they cannot do it the next time, ignoring the small "wins."
