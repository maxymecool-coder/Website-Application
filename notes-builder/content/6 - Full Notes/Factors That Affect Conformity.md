# Factors That Affect Conformity
**Details**
Asch's research and subsequent studies have identified key factors that influence the likelihood of conformity:

- **Group Size:** Conformity increases as the size of the majority grows, but it levels off quickly. The pressure exerted by a group of 3 or 4 people is just as strong as the pressure from a much larger group.
    
- **The Presence of an Ally:** The most significant factor in reducing conformity is having an ally. If just one other person in the group gives the correct answer, the pressure on the participant is dramatically reduced, and they are far less likely to conform. Being in a minority of two is much easier than being a minority of one.
    
- **Anonymity:** When participants can give their answers privately (e.g., by writing them down) instead of publicly, conformity drops sharply. This removes the fear of social ridicule.
    
- **Task Difficulty and Ambiguity:** Conformity increases when the task is difficult or the correct answer is ambiguous. When we are uncertain, we are more likely to look to others for information and assume they are correct.
    
- **Group Status:** People are more likely to conform to groups they perceive as having high status or being attractive. The desire to be accepted by or associated with such a group increases the pressure to align with their views.
    
- **Culture:** Rates of conformity can vary across cultures. Studies have shown higher rates in collectivist cultures (e.g., in many Asian countries), where maintaining group harmony may be valued more highly than individual expression. In contrast, rates have generally decreased in individualist cultures like the United States since the 1950s.
