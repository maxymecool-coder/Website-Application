# Negative Coercion
**Details**
This concept comes from **Gerald Patterson’s** work with aggressive children. Now, negative reinforcement doesn't necessarily mean "bad" reinforcement but rather "taking something away." Negative reinforcement is when a behavior is strengthened because the forces making that situation that the person is in go away.

To exemplify this in the parent-child relationship, we can take when a parent is demanding their children to do homework. Now because the child refuses to do said homework, the parent instead changes to shouting, insulting them, which results in the child eventually complying because they're scared. After that, the parents think that if they simply scream loud enough, the child will do their work, they will continue to do that the next time. Now this is a failure in reasoning because they fail to account for the pent up feelings the child is likely experiencing and their plan to rebel the next time.

This cycle continues on and on, escalates further and further which morphs it into a strong indicator of future aggression due to how children might bring this to other environments like school or in their relationships.
