# The Rumination Circuit (Default Mode Network) in Depression
**Details**
The **Default Mode Network (DMN)** is a collection of brain regions that is active when the mind is at rest and wandering, engaged in self-referential thought. It's the network responsible for your sense of self and autobiography.

In major depression, this network is hijacked by negativity. Instead of neutral mind-wandering, the DMN becomes the engine of **rumination**, getting stuck in a persistent loop of sad memories, self-criticism, and feelings of guilt.

This pathological state is driven by the overactive **amygdala** and **Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC)**, which constantly feed the DMN with distressing emotional information. The DMN then processes this information in a self-referential way, creating the inescapable internal narrative of failure and sadness that characterizes depressive rumination.
