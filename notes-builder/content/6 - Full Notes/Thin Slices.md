# Thin Slices
**Details**
For the majority of social psychology has supported that we are terrible evaluators and terrible at understanding or predicting any social situations, ever. Yet, somehow, some way, we possess a remarkable automatic capacity for reading others through nonverbal cues. A photo demonstration reveals that people can reliably identify romantic couples versus strangers, arm-wrestling winners, and a mother playing with her baby—all from still images with no contextual information. Accuracy rates reach 80%+ even though participants feel like they're guessing and cannot articulate how they know. Ambati and Rosenthal's "thin slice" research formalized this: teachers were videotaped lecturing, and strangers who watched just 6 seconds of silent footage—no sound, no knowledge of the subject or the teacher's reputation—produced ratings that correlated at r ≈ .70 with end-of-semester student evaluations from students who had spent months in the class. This correlation held even under cognitive load, confirming the process is automatic and pre-reflective. Controlling statistically for smile frequency, hand gestures, and physical attractiveness did not diminish the effect—something holistic about the person's nonverbal presence carries the signal.
