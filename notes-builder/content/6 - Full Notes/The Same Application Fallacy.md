# The Same Application Fallacy
**Details**
The claim that synthetic a priori knowledge is impossible is in of itself: a synthetic proposition. Those who believe it is true and regard it as such is implicitly treating it as a priori.

More importantly, Kant argues, the existence of mathematics and Newtonian physics already demonstrates that synthetic a priori knowledge is actual. The propositions of geometry are necessarily valid and extend our knowledge of spatial relations — they are both synthetic and a priori. The laws of physics express necessary connections between events that go beyond mere observation. The question is therefore not whether such knowledge exists — it manifestly does — but how it is possible. What cognitive resources make it the case that we can know necessary truths about the structure of experience before any particular experience occurs?
