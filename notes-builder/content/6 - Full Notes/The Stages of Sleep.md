# The Stages of Sleep
**Details**
The process of sleep is studied by using things such as EEGs to elaborate the details and such of it. Upon falling asleep, a person will enter stage 1, this is when the eyes are nearly motionless and the EEG would show choppy, short waves. These waves indicate brain activity of some kind. Because these brain cells fire out of synchrony, they cancel each other out. In this stage, it is easy to wake someone up and they would say something like "I wasn't sleeping."

As sleep continues, a person would go into stages 2 through 4, which differs in the number of long, slow waves. Stage 2 has the fewest and stage 4 had the most. These waves indicated synchrony, which meant that there was decreased brain activity.

After this, they progress back through stages 3 and 2, then to REM sleep instead of stage 1 sleep. At the end of REM sleep, the sleeper again goes through 2,3,4 and the back through 3,2, and then REM. These cycles last for about 90 to 100 minutes on average in a healthy young adult.
