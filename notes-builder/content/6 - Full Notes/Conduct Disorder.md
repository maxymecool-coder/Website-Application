# Conduct Disorder
**Details**
Another externalizing disorder where the symptoms are usually presented by actions. DSM has a criteria for this, basically it focuses on behaviors that violate the basic rights of others and violate major societal norms. These "symptoms" have to go beyond just mischief and pranks that are common among children and adolescents, it can become cruelty toward people or animals, damaging property, lying, and stealing. Along with these behaviors, the perpetrators will not have remorse, which points to the fact that they do not have much empathy or are just overall unemotional. Familial characteristics usually come with these problems according to research.

These children usually exhibit relationship problems with everyone around them, including friends, family. Moreover, they also suffer from problems such as substance abuse and internalizing disorders. For example, among seventh graders who reported having tried marijuana, more than 30 percent had attacked someone with a weapon and 43 percent admitted breaking and entering; fewer than 5 percent of children who reported no substance use had committed these acts. Some research suggests that conduct disorder precedes substance use problems (Nock, Kazdin, Hiripi, et al., 2006), but other findings suggest that conduct disorder and substance use occur concomitantly, with the two conditions exacerbating each other (Loeber et al., 2000). Further, some evidence indicates that comorbid conduct disorder and substance use portend a more severe outcome for boys than for girls (Whitmore, Mikulich, Thompson, et al., 1997).
