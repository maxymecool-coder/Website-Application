# Encoding memories
**Details**
**Encoding** is the initial process of learning, where sensory information from the environment is translated into a construct that can be stored within the brain. It is the crucial first step that determines whether information will be remembered. The effectiveness of encoding often depends on the level of processing applied to the information.

**Shallow processing** involves superficial characteristics, like the visual appearance of a word, and leads to weak, short-lived memories. In contrast, **deep processing** involves semantic analysis—focusing on the meaning of the information and how it relates to existing knowledge. Deeper, more meaningful encoding results in more durable and easily retrievable memory traces. For example, simply repeating a phone number (shallow) is less effective than associating the numbers with a memorable date (deep).
