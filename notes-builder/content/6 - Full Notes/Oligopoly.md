# Oligopoly
**Details**
Most real markets are **oligopolies**: a small number of firms supply the good and compete against each other. That's more than one firm, but too few for entry to push profits to zero. Cars are the standard example. Most cars sold in America come from fewer than 20 companies. That's probably not enough competition to drive profits to zero, but it's enough that no company can simply pick its point on the demand curve.

With only a few firms, there are two ways to behave:

- **Cooperatively**, by forming a **cartel**: otherwise competing firms agree to act as one monopoly, maximize the joint profit and split it. OPEC, the oil exporters' cartel, is the classic case.
- **Non-cooperatively**: each firm does what's best for itself. This is the more realistic case, and Lecture 14 shows why cartels rarely last.

Since there still needs to be competition between the firms, we need something else to understand how these guys will compete when the game is this seated and set. That is called game theory.
