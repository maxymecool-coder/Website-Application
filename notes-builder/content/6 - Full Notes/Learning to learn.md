# Learning to learn
**Details**
As we cannot, and as it is not necessary for us to resolve every single problem that troubles a patient. We need to teach him to develop new ways to learn from his experiences and to solve problems. In a sense... he is "learning to learn." This process has been labeled deutero-learning (Bateson, 1942).

This removes much responsibility from the therapist and instead placing it onto the patient, making him engage more in actively working on his difficulties. This approach increases the student's self-esteem due to him having more control over his own process. As well as yielding more information than the therapist just psychoanalyzing the patient.

The method involves much more than just applying a couple methods to situations, it is rather removing the obstacles that have prevented the patient from profiting from his experience and then help him develop the ways for him to deal with external and internal problems. Most of the patients have been blocked in their psychosocial development by certain maladaptive attitudes and patterns of behavior. For instance, a woman with numerous problems at work and at home had a characteristic response when she was confronted with sensitive interpersonal relations or new practical problems. And so therapy helped her to erode this negative attitude.
