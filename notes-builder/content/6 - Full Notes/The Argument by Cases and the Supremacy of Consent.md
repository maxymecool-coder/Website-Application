# The Argument by Cases and the Supremacy of Consent
**Details**
But if [[The Architecture and Implications of Natural Law]] states that everyone begins with equal jurisdiction over themselves, how does anyone gain authority over anyone ? Locke proves that mutual consent is the only valid mechanism by systematically dismantling every other alternative in what we can call an argument by cases. There are certain moral duties alike being a father, to honor one's parents (though that is debatable [[On Being A Father]]) and though I personally consider these sociological factors only, these do not require consent to perform (and I also believe there's a better way to classify these obligations rather). But political power, which Locke specifically defines as the right to make laws with the penalty of death for the regulation of property and defense of the commonwealth, is a completely different category of authority that cannot be inherited or seized.

He basically does this to argue against frameworks that inherently allow royalties to have control over the people completely by arguing that even stuff like being a parent comes to a certain point and what not rather than inherent control.
