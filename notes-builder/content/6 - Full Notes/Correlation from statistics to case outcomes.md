# Correlation from statistics to case outcomes
**Details**
Beyond simply aligning with professional opinion, a truly effective triage tool should have predictive utility—its initial assessment ought to correlate with future case developments. The study tested this through a series of chi-square analyses, examining the statistical relationship between the QuAIC-assessed level of concern and two key real-world outcomes: the length of time a case remained active and the severity of its final resolution. The results were highly significant.  

First, the analysis revealed a significant relationship between the QuAIC level and case length, finding that "the higher the level of concern, the lengthier the case" (x2=6.64, df=8, p<.001). This suggests that the QuAIC score at intake serves as a reliable indicator of the complexity and resource-intensiveness of a case.  

Second, an even stronger relationship was found between the QuAIC level and the severity of the case outcome. The analysis showed that "the higher the level of concern, the more punitive the outcome" (x2=127.17, df=8, p<.001). The specific patterns were clear:  

- **High/highest risk cases** were most likely to result in an "external referral or a misconduct sanction."
    
- **Moderate risk cases** were most likely to lead to an "informal resolution or a misconduct sanction."
    
- **Low risk/not applicable cases** were overwhelmingly likely to be resolved through "informal resolution."
    

These statistical correlations elevate the QuAIC from a simple triage instrument to a powerful prognostic tool. Its score is not just a label for the present but a statistically valid forecast of a case's likely trajectory. A simple triage tool tells a case manager which file to open first. A prognostic tool, however, provides insight into what that case is likely to _become_. The chi-square results demonstrate that the QuAIC functions as the latter. A "High" concern rating at intake is a data-backed predictor of a complex, time-consuming case that is significantly more likely to require formal disciplinary proceedings or referral to external agencies like the police.

This has profound implications for strategic resource management. A university administrator can leverage this data to move from a purely reactive stance to a data-informed, proactive management model. By tracking the aggregate QuAIC scores of incoming cases over a semester, leadership can forecast demand on the SCU, student misconduct offices, legal counsel, and other support services. If a trend of higher-scoring cases emerges, they can proactively allocate additional staff time, budget for external assessments, or implement targeted preventative programming aimed at the specific behaviors being reported. The QuAIC thus provides the foundational data needed to manage institutional risk at a strategic, rather than purely case-by-case, level.
