# The Nature of Thinking Disorders
**Details**
A thinking disorder has generally been considered a feature of schizophrenia. Whilst the opposite, depression, mania, and anxiety are generally considered emotional disorders due to the excessive emotions evoked in them. However, there is now considerable evidence that a disorder in thinking is less gross and more circumscribed than that in schizophrenia, which is an important component of the common psychiatric syndromes.

This symptom is apparent in the systematically misconstrued specific kinds of experiences. These can range from subtle inaccuracies in the mild neurotics to the fully grotesque misinterpretations of the psychotics. As said before, these thoughts appear in subjects that are relative to the patient's problems.

**Supporting Content**
These thoughts and ideas have the characteristics of automatic thoughts, meaning they appear as if by reflex, without any apparent antecedent reflection or reasoning, being familiar and true to the patient even though implausible to others. And finally, they were less amenable to change by reason or contradictory evidence than other forms associated with the specific form of psychopathology
