# Economic Anomy
**Details**
Anomy is a state in which things exist without direction, without a start or an end, and it is argued by Emile Durkheim that those in this state are more prone to suicidal behaviors. He also provides the argument that it is the most prevalent and destructive in the economic world. Industrial and commercial functions, which throw the door "wide to every sort of adventure," furnish a high number of suicides because they are characterized by this lack of regulation. This condition is exacerbated by a modern belief that suffering is life itself and it should be accepted and integrated into our lives. In fact, he argues that it has become the ideal of swift, ruthless progress has become an article of faith. The data confirms this, showing higher suicide rates in industry and trade compared to agriculture, where older regulative forces persist. Furthermore, the high rate among those with independent means shows that the possessors of comfort suffer most, as those with "only empty space above them are almost inevitably lost in it."
