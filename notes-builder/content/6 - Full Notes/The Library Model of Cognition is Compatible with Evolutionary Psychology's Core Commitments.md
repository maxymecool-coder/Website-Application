# The Library Model of Cognition is Compatible with Evolutionary Psychology's Core Commitments
**Details**
Given the failure of arguments for the Massive Modularity Hypothesis (MMH) to rule out the Library Model of Cognition (LMC), and the empirical stalemate between them, the strong adherence to MMH within evolutionary psychology seems unmotivated. This is especially true because LMC is perfectly consistent with the central theoretical and methodological commitments of the evolutionary psychology research program [cite: Samuels_1998.pdf].

1. **Conceptual Integration:** Evolutionary psychology requires that psychological theories be consistent with the natural sciences, especially evolutionary biology. LMC, which posits innate, evolved structures, does not conflict with any established biological principles and thus meets this standard [cite: Samuels_1998.pdf].
    
2. **Universal Human Nature:** A central tenet of evolutionary psychology is that there is a universal, species-typical cognitive architecture. LMC is compatible with this claim; the "library" of innate, domain-specific knowledge can be posited as a universal feature of the human mind [cite: Samuels_1998.pdf].
    
3. **Psychological Structures as Adaptations:** Evolutionary psychology views our mental architecture as a collection of adaptations built by natural selection. Where MMH identifies modules as these adaptations, LMC simply identifies the innate bodies of information as the adaptations. Both are evolved solutions to recurrent ancestral problems [cite: Samuels_1998.pdf].
    
4. **The Utility of Evolutionary Analysis:** A core methodological claim is that analyzing ancestral adaptive problems can help generate hypotheses about cognitive specializations. This method is equally useful for generating hypotheses about innate bodies of information (for LMC) as it is for innate computational mechanisms (for MMH). For example, analyzing the problem of social exchange could lead one to hypothesize an innate "social contract" body of knowledge just as easily as a "social contract" module [cite: Samuels_1998.pdf].
    

Since LMC can accommodate all the foundational principles of evolutionary psychology, the field's insistence on MMH specifically is not a logical necessity of adopting an evolutionary approach to the mind.
