# Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) in Brain Research
**Details**
- **Methodology**: Uses magnetic fields and radio waves to align and detect hydrogen protons in brain tissue (e.g., water-rich areas). Protons realign after radio bursts, emitting signals that map tissue locations and types. Produces static images of brain structures. 
- **Advantages**: Excellent spatial resolution. Distinguishes tissue types based on proton alignment speeds. 
- **Disadvantages**: Patient discomfort (must lie still for long periods). Shows only static structures, no brain activity.

**Supporting Content**
In PTSD, people are put in MRIs and then asked to think about their traumatic event to see the neurobiological information.
