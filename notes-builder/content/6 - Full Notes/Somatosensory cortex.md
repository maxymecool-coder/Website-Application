# Somatosensory cortex
**Details**
The **primary somatosensory cortex** (S1) is the main receptive area in the cerebral cortex for the sense of touch and other bodily sensations. Located in the postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe, it receives sensory information from the skin, muscles, joints, and internal organs.

Its primary functions include processing tactile information such as pressure, vibration, texture, temperature, and pain. It is also crucial for **proprioception**, the sense of the relative position of one's own body parts and the effort being employed in movement.

The somatosensory cortex is organized somatotopically, meaning that adjacent areas of the skin are represented by adjacent areas in the cortex. This organization is famously depicted as the **sensory homunculus**, a distorted representation of the human body where more sensitive areas (like the lips and hands) have disproportionately larger regions of cortical tissue devoted to them.
