# Methodological Breakthrough of Stratification
**Details**
Peter Blau and Otis Dudley Duncan's book, The American Occupational Structure, revolutionized the study of social mobility by overcoming the flaws of earlier research. Their approach had three key innovations:

 * Massive, Continuous Data: They used a national sample of over 20,000 men, which allowed them to move beyond discrete class boxes to a continuous scale of occupational prestige. This solved the problem of categorizing individuals who didn't fit neatly into predefined classes.

 * Synthetic Cohorts: Instead of trying to track families over generations, they asked men of all ages about their father's occupation when the son was 16. This created "synthetic cohorts" of men who started from the same point (e.g., all men whose fathers were laborers). By comparing the eventual outcomes of these different cohorts, they could measure the advantage or disadvantage conferred by one's starting point.

 * Isolating "Circulation" Mobility: This method effectively controls for "structural" mobility—changes caused by economic shifts (e.g., the decline of farming, the rise of white-collar work) or demographic events. It isolates the degree to which the system allows individuals to move up or down, independent of these larger structural changes.
