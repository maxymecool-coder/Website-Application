# Recency Effect
**Details**
The **recency effect** is the tendency to have superior recall for items at the end of a list compared to those in the middle. This is the second component of the serial position effect, complementing the primacy effect.

This effect is typically explained by the items' presence in **short-term or working memory**. When recall is tested immediately after the list is presented, the last few items are still actively being held in this limited-capacity store, making them readily accessible. Unlike the first items, they haven't been displaced by subsequent information. If a delay or a distracting task is introduced between the end of the list and the recall test, the recency effect often disappears because the contents of short-term memory decay or are interfered with.
