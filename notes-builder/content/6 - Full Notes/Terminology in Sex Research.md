# Terminology in Sex Research
**Details**
To move beyond colloquialisms like "libido," behavioral scientists employ a more precise lexicon to deconstruct sexual behavior. A foundational framework consists of a triad of terms: **attractivity** refers to how appealing an individual is to others; **proceptivity** describes the active, soliciting behaviors used to initiate a sexual encounter; and **receptivity** is the readiness to engage in copulation in response to another's advances. This triad allows for a detailed analysis of the dynamic, interactive process of courtship.

In parallel, particularly within clinical settings, researchers distinguish between **motivation** and **performance**. Motivation encompasses the psychological drive, desire, and arousal for sex, whereas performance relates to the physiological capacity to carry out the sexual act, such as erectile function. Recognizing that these two aspects can be dissociated is fundamental to diagnosing and understanding various forms of sexual dysfunction.
