# Disorganized Attachment
**Details**
Children have a biological instinct to attach, whether their parents are loving or not, they have to find one way or another to stick to them, and develop a coping style based on their attempt to get at least some of their needs met. When their caregivers cannot give what they need, the babies "learns to become the mother's idea of what a baby is" to make the job easier, it adjusts to its caregiver, thinking that something is wrong with itself, which means they would shut down their own physical reactions inside their bodies that may be necessary. 

These children learn to deal with their anxiety when caregivers are unresponsive in two distinct ways. Firstly, "avoidant attachment", in this, the infants would look like nothing is really bothering them, they don't cry when their mother goes and ignores her when she comes back. But their physiology does, their heart rates increase which means they're in a constant state of hyperarousal. Most mothers who have children with this usually have problems, or dislike touching their children, they don't use face expressions or anything of sorts to create a pleasurable back-and-forth with their baby. The second is "anxious" or "ambivalent" attachment, which means they will constantly try to draw attention to themselves, by crying or throwing a tantrum, they seem to have come to the conclusion that unless they make a spectacle, nobody is gonna care. But even when somebody does care, they don't seem to enjoy that company, they would stay passively or angrily focused on them. Although all of this is bad, it can be changed in the stages of life that these children goes through. 

**Supporting Content**
Most parents who read this thinks that a mere misunderstanding or bad action can cause their children to become like this. But more often than not, mistakes are gonna happen, and when they do its actually teaching the child about tolerance, not ruining them entirely. they will learn that broken connections can be repaired and to handle frustrations and disappointments. The issue is if they can "feel safe" with their parents/caregivers.
