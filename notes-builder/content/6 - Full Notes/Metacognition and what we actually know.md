# Metacognition and what we actually know
**Details**
Metacognition is basically the act of monitoring our own thinking process as well as thoughts. Being good at this activity helps us to improve our own thinking significantly as we get more effective and efficient at it, for example noticing our blind spots which allows us to make good decisions and so on. This can actually impact us a lot as we often do not recognize when we have wrong information which can lead to us doing so many things wrong, the sheer scope of such error.

Our brain tricks us into thinking this way because it is always hungry for connections, for a narrative, for something that makes sense with our mind as it is discomforted by that not existing. In a study to test people to see how much distracted their brains get, participants were put into a room to do the test, but in the background there was chatter. The participants, not knowing that it was a part of the test, tried listening into it. Conclusively, it was found out that those who listened to one side of the conversation was significantly more distracted than those who listened to both sides because they had to imagine and forced to think about a narrative for what the other person was saying.

This can have a huge impact on our lives because partially or much of it we do live in this narrative that we ourselves create. Like how we strive to fit the events of our lives into a cohesive narrative to make sense of it all. Credibility of learning methods and so on also largely depends on a person's personal experience with it, some may wholeheartedly find themselves in a mess or unable to understand/use the method whilst others have different results. The objective data and information matters much less than a person's subjective feeling about the matter.
