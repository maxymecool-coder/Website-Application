# Random Student Drug-Testing
**Details**
Whilst random drug testing has been mostly reserved for student-athletes due to their participation in competitions and so on, it has been widen to also include students who merely participate in extracurricular activities (like acting or debating). This represents a significant intersection of drug policy, constitutional law, and civil liberties.

The proponents argue that testing serves as a deterrent, potentially preventing students from initiating drug use. However, critics argue this creates a "slippery slope" that infringes on student privacy and Fourth Amendment rights against unreasonable search and seizure. Furthermore, there is a counter-intuitive risk: by barring students who refuse testing (or test positive) from extracurricular activities, schools may isolate the very students who benefit most from the structure and engagement those activities provide, potentially increasing their risk of substance abuse.
