# The Role of Inflammation in Depression
**Details**
Chronic inflammation is another critical biological factor that has a bidirectional relationship with depression. This connection reveals that depression is not just a neurological disorder, but one that involves the body's immune system.

The link works in two directions, creating a vicious cycle:

1. **Inflammation Increases Depression Risk:** Individuals with chronic inflammatory diseases (like autoimmune disorders) have a significantly higher risk of developing major depression. Furthermore, giving healthy individuals drugs that mimic an inflammatory response can induce a severe depressive episode.
    
2. **Depression Increases Inflammation:** The state of major depression itself acts as a stressor that activates the body's inflammatory systems. Post-mortem studies of the brains of people with depression show clear markers of chronic neuroinflammation.
    

This feedback loop is similar to the one seen with stress hormones. An initial inflammatory state can trigger or worsen depression, and the resulting depressive state then fuels more inflammation, further entrenching the illness. This interplay between the immune system and the brain provides another powerful piece of evidence for the biological nature of depression and helps explain its systemic, whole-body effects.
