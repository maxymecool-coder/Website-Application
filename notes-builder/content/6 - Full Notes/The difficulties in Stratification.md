# The difficulties in Stratification
**Details**
Collecting data for the theory of stratification in the US was quite hard in the mid 20th century due to the methodological limits of the time. The most prevalent difficulties were:

- Elite surveys: surveying top professionals and determining their backgrounds to see their class and such. This showed that 20% of these top professionals came from wealthy families. But this was useless because there was no baseline. If only 1% were rich, then a 20% representation among elites indicates a massive advantage, not an open system. The method lacked a proper comparison group

- Crude Class Categories: Studies like Hollingshead’s in the 1950s. This created two problems. First, people do not fit neatly into classes. Second, the categories distort the scale of mobility. For example, a son who moves from the bottom of tue upper-middle class to the top of middle class might not be much, but on the mobility scale, its significant.
