# Calibrating Judgement
**Details**
To recognize and be in tune with one’s knowledge, they must verify what they truly know by testing themselves frequently with low-stakes quizzes or consolidating learning where one uses the materials from one stage and connect with concepts and such learnt later. An important point here is to not stop practicing just because one has gotten it but rather continue the process to consolidate it even further.

Next is peer instruction, developed by Eric Mazur, which incorporates many of the former mentioned principles. The material is covered before class and then interspersed in the learning section with quick tests and such. It can also be improved upon so that one can understand and remember the knowledge better.

To exemplify this, we will see the cues to judge how much we have learnt and the level to which we have learnt it. But we cannot depend on our intuition to indicate that level, though there is an exception to this which is the ease of retrieval after delay. It is quite far better to create a mental model and connect them to each other (like how this Zettelkasten is working !). As well as elaborating, which is reinterpreting in your own words. It is also advisable that instructors or people who overview the process to give corrective feedback. It is quite helpful in a multitude of fields, such as medical, aviation and many more.
