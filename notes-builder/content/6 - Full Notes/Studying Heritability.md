# Studying Heritability
**Details**
To untangle the influences of genes and environment, researchers rely on clever natural experiments, most notably twin and adoption studies. **Heritability** is a statistical estimate of how much of the variation we see in a particular trait within a population can be attributed to genetic differences among individuals. These studies provide a direct route to estimating this value. The logic behind **twin studies** is straightforward: by comparing monozygotic (identical) twins, who share 100% of their genes, with dizygotic (fraternal) twins, who share on average 50%, researchers can infer the strength of genetic influence. Any differences observed between identical twins are generally assumed to be the result of environmental factors, as their genetic makeup is the same.

**Adoption studies** offer another powerful angle. When children are adopted and raised separately from their biological relatives, similarities between the child and their biological parents point strongly toward shared heredity. Conversely, similarities between adopted, non-biological siblings raised in the same household are believed to be the result of their shared environment. Together, these research designs have been fundamental in demonstrating that nearly every human trait has both a genetic and an environmental component, allowing scientists to move from philosophical debate to empirical investigation.
