# Variations of Hypothesis Testing
**Details**
There's two variables in this matter. First is direction, which is basically predicting which way, so you look in one tail and reject past z = 1.645. A non-directional only claims different, so you would have to look in both tails. Two-tailed catches effects either way but they demand a stronger result. Second, what you know. The z-test assumes you know the population, which you almost never do. When n is small and population is unknown, stick to t-test.
