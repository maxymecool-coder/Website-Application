# Historical Rise of Standards
**Details**
Nietzsche observes a progressive tendency in the history of morality: he viewed that as society became safer and moral responsibility became easier to fulfill, we did not just let it be like that, we tightened our responsibilities further. He cites a particular transition in the Old Testament to the New Testament (specifically the Sermon on the Mount) as the archetype of this shift. As the old law commanded, "Do not kill," Jesus now turned it into "Do not even be angry."

This raise of the bar subsequently becomes too difficult to satisfy for people, for society, which of course, further increase internalization, and as that happens, the self-torment goes even further, and then repeats.
