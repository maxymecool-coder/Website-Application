# Kant’s Copernican Revolution
**Details**
The historical context matters. Copernicus was contacted by Pope Leo X because the Julian calendar, in use since Caesar's time, had drifted so far from astronomical reality that Easter Sunday was wandering by two to three weeks. Mathematicians could not agree on the length of the year. Copernicus's insight was to ask: what happens if we change the position of the observer? What if, instead of assuming the earth is stationary and everything revolves around it, we place the observer on a moving earth orbiting the sun? The result was not subjectivism — Jack and Jill observing from the sun would agree with each other — but a more accurate model of reality achieved by recognizing that the observer's position is itself a variable that must be accounted for.

Kant proposes "another trial of the same sort." Instead of assuming that our cognition passively conforms to objects, suppose that objects must conform to the conditions of our cognition. The pure categories of the understanding — causality, substance, unity — are not extracted from experience but are the framework through which experience becomes intelligible in the first place. Just as Copernicus's repositioning of the observer produced better astronomy, Kant's repositioning of the cognitive subject is meant to produce genuine metaphysics. The observer is not a passive recipient of stimuli but an active participant whose cognitive constitution shapes what counts as an objective observation. Crucially, this does not lead to subjectivism, because the cognitive framework is shared by all rational beings. Astronomy developed as a science on exactly this basis — accounting for the observer's position made the science more objective, not less.
