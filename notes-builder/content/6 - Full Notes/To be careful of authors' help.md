# To be careful of author’s help
**Details**
Sometimes the author will tell you the unity of his plan in his preface. In this aspect fiction writers are completely different from expository writers, as they would want to keep you in the least amount of suspense as possible. 

This is where the balance comes in, do not be too proud to accept the author’s help but neither rely too completely on what he says. Be guided by what the author gives, but always remember the finding of unity is a task of the reader, as having one belongs to the writer. 

But note these points before proceeding. First is how frequently one can expect the author to help them state the plan of his book. Second is to not take the sample summaries as if it were a final and absolute formation of the book’s unity, because it can be variously stated. There is no one right way to do it. One may be better than the other, in proportion as it is brief, accurate and comprehensive. 

Similarly with Rule 3, do not ignore the headings and sectional divisions of the author. Although we should not ignore them, neither should we slavishly follow them. They are here to help us, just like the titles and prefaces. But they are merely guides, helping us to look into and below the book for its structure.
