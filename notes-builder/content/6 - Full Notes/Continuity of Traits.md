# Continuity of Traits
**Details**
This continuity refers to how persistent an underlying trait or problem is, as they can change or stay stable throughout the stages of our lives. There are two types of continuity depending on if the behaviors are stable with the traits or not as traits can be stable whilst behavior changes.

For example, the _underlying trait_ or tendency is stable, but its behavioral expression changes over time in developmentally appropriate ways. This is a core concept in developmental psychopathology. And so a kid at 2 may throw tantrums, then at 8 they may fight with their peers, and so on they start doing even more aggressive activities. This is called heterotypic continuity.

A trait that persists with the behavior throughout life is called a homotypic continuity, which is basically the same kid throwing tantrums from 2 to 8.
