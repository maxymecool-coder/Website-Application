# Dealing with Stigma
**Details**
Similar to therapy, there are many ways that society can deal with stigma. The first and perhaps easiest to see is "Top-down", which basically is the government implementing laws so that discrimination towards the stigmatized groups are essentially illegal. This approach, though straightforward, has not demonstrate much effectiveness. For example, the ADA legislation in 1990, which makes discrimination illegal. The efficacy has been limited for those with mental illness as exposing the fact that they have these things may pose a threat to their societal reputation and their life projection, making these individuals refrain from opening up. Perhaps it is also difficult to keep track of due to how mental health help may be extra hours or help with work rather than physical renovations like ramps for disabled people.

The other two approaches are "bottom-up" and "middle-out", these are more suggestive of changing how these groups are perceived. Bottom up relies on the "contact hypothesis" which states how an individual who reveals their conditions to the world and well, act how they act, the perception people have on the group as a whole can change, to be more normalized, like the LGBTQ+ community (what a fucking hassle to type). Simultaneously, the middle out approach is for the media to have a hand in. Instead of reporting news and making it in a way to condemn and even judge people with mental illnesses, they can instead change how they present these images to be more understanding and fair.

Ultimately, the goal is to humanize these individuals and their differences from society. The "top down" approach and the education of the person's condition may reduce blame yet it can dehumanize them, make them subhuman. To combat this, we need to focus perhaps on the other two approaches to make people understand these people rather than just their conditions.
