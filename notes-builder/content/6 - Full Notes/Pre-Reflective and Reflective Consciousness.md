# Pre-Reflective and Reflective Consciousness
**Details**
When referring to consciousness, there are usually two definitions that people can come to that results in them disagreeing with each other, those being the pre-reflective and reflective stream of consciousnesses (as William James named them). Pre-reflective is what we share with many other animals, as if life was a film that is constantly rolling and showing. In contrast, reflective consciousness is an ability that we possess in tandem with some other animals having a weaker level of it that is strictly characterized by "aboutness" or intentionality; thoughts are always _about_ an object, memory, or concept. Reflective thought requires effort, interferes with other cognitive tasks, relies heavily on linguistic symbols, and operates like a movie critic actively analyzing the film rather than merely experiencing its projection.

**Supporting Content**
The lecturer pushes back against Freud's conscious/unconscious divide. He thinks the important split isn't between conscious and unconscious but between **pre-reflective and reflective** — both conscious and non-conscious processes operate under similar principles on one side, while reflective processing operates under fundamentally different principles on the other.
