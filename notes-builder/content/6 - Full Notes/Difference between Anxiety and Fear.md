# Difference between Anxiety and Fear
**Details**
These two words' definitions have been confounded with each other a lot throughout the times which vitiates the advantages of using two distinct words to designate related but separable phenomena. And so we will define it simply: Fear is a particular kind of ideation; anxiety is an emotion.

This definition will derive from dictionaries mostly, as they all emphasize the meaning of fear as appraisal of an actual or potential danger. And so fear becomes a cognitive function in which people identify something undesirable that may occur.

A clear example of this may come when someone says "I have a fear of thunderstorms". Which he is referring to a set of circumstances that are not present yet may occur some time in the future. In this sense, it is clear that fear is a predisposition to perceive a certain set of conditions as a threat and anxiety being the reaction if exposed to these conditions.

And so when a thunderstorm does happen, the fear is activated. The person reacts in words and actions, indicating the appraisal of potential harm, which is fear. The concept evolves from the possible consequences of the thunderstorm; namely, the possibility that he may be killed. And so "latent fear" which is the inner fear that is not showed to the outsider, is activated and anxiety is aroused, resulting in shakiness, jitteriness, and so on.

Fear is activated when the person gets closer to the dangerous situation. Or in some cases, merely talking about it may do the same as he may think and imagine it. Dwelling in such thoughts may make the threatening situation more salient and imminent as the person brings it into his "here and now", and so he projects himself into it.

**Supporting Content**
This helps us have a clear indicator on how we define the irrational and rational anxiety. When the fear is realistic, it would become rational anxiety, when it is not, the opposite is true.
