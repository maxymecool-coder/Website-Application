# The Modern Toolkit of Cognitive Psychology
**Details**
To make the invisible processes of the mind visible, modern cognitive psychology employs a diverse toolkit that moves beyond simple observation. The most traditional method involves measuring **behavioral outcomes**, such as recording the reaction time between a stimulus and a response, or tracking the accuracy of a participant's performance on a task. Another powerful behavioral approach comes from neuropsychology, which examines the loss of a specific ability following brain damage from events like a stroke or injury, allowing researchers to infer the function of the damaged brain region.

However, recent technological advances have opened a direct window into the working brain. Tools like **Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)** allow researchers to temporarily and non-invasively stimulate or inhibit specific parts of the brain to observe the effect on behavior. Furthermore, sophisticated imaging techniques let us record brain activity in real-time. **Electroencephalography (EEG)** captures the rapid electrical rhythms of neurons, making it ideal for studying the timing of cognitive processes like language or attention. For mapping _where_ activity happens, researchers use methods that track blood flow, such as **Positron Emission Tomography (PET)** and, most commonly, **Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI)**. These tools allow us to see which brain areas become more active when a person is engaged in a specific mental task, giving us a geographical map of cognition.
