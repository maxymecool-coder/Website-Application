# The Lipid Bilayer Membrane
**Details**
From ancient bacterial to braincells, they are all encased in a lipid bilayer membrane, which are formed from phospholipids, unique molecules that have a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tail.

Because the environment inside and outside the cell is water-based, these molecules spontaneously arrange themselves into a "sandwich": the heads face outward toward the water, and the tails tuck inward to hide from the water. This creates a stable, oily barrier that defines the cell's boundary. For a drug to enter a cell passively, it usually needs to be lipophilic (hydrophobic) enough to dissolve through this oily interior layer.
