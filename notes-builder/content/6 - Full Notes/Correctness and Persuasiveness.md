# Correctness and Persuasiveness
**Details**
Logic is a different discipline from rhetoric, which is the art of being persuasive. Now, logic works on premises, meaning if the premises are correct, then it would sufficiently be able to lead propositionally from said premises to a conclusion. For example, a murder case, if the premises (evidences) are true and hold up, then logic is able to take said premises to the conclusion.

The measure of an argument's "correctness" is objective and structural: an argument is correct if, and only if, accepting the premises compels one to accept the conclusion. This relationship holds regardless of whether the premises are actually true in the real world or whether the audience is psychologically convinced. Persuasiveness on the other hand is if it relies on emotional appeals or prejudices to convince the jury and judge. Conversely, a logically correct argument might fail to persuade if it is too complex or dry.
