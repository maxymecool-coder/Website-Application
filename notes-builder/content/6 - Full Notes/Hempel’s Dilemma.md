# Hempel’s Dilemma
**Details**
The trajectory of physicalism leads us to “Hempel’s Dilemma.” In Hobbes' day, "Materialism" meant believing everything is made of solid little rocks (atoms). Today, we use the term "Physicalism" because our physics is full of things that aren't "matter" in the old sense: massless particles, probability waves, spacetime curvature, and forces. But still, our interpretation is incomplete, and so we do not and cannot say that “everything is physical.” Same is for ideal physics, if “everything acts according to a future, perfect physics” then any physics can be perfect, its a vacuous truth, and a useless one.

Rolling back, we can further understand Hobb’s philosophy about how “matter” exists and everything is made up of it, which then can extend even further to Boyle’s Atomism which is the basis of our understanding of atoms today, how they exist with space as its opposite and how different arrangements of it can show different things.
