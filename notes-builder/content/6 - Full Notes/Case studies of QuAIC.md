# Case studies of QuAIC
**Details**
The three case studies presented in the paper provide a vivid, qualitative illustration of this data in action, serving as a proof-of-concept for the entire tiered response system. They demonstrate how the initial risk level, informed by the QuAIC, is not an arbitrary label but a meaningful category that triggers a distinct, defensible, and proportionate level of institutional intervention.  

- **Case 1 (Low Risk):** A staff member reported a welfare concern about a student's declining hygiene. The triage risk level was assessed as "Low." The SCU's response was efficient and minimalist: they provided advice and referral information to the reporting staff member, who then conducted a welfare check. The case was closed after the student was successfully referred to internal support services. This demonstrates a "light touch," low-intensity response appropriate for low-risk matters.  
    
- **Case 2 (Moderate Risk):** A student reported stalking by an ex-partner, involving persistent and intrusive communication. The triage risk level was assessed as "Moderate." This triggered a more intensive and sustained response. The SCU provided ongoing case management, including specialist safety planning and psychoeducation for the target. As the stalking behavior continued despite these initial interventions, the institutional response escalated systematically: from an informal warning, to a formal no-contact direction, to a temporary suspension under misconduct provisions, and ultimately to a referral for the target to report the matter to local police. This case perfectly illustrates a managed, persistent, and escalating response pathway for a moderate-risk situation.  
    
- **Case 3 (High Risk):** A student with a known history of multiple problem behaviors, including threats and stalking, was referred for assessment. The triage risk level was assessed as "High." The response was immediate and coercive. The case was escalated directly to the multi-disciplinary SAT team, which issued a temporary suspension from the university. The student's return was made contingent upon the completion of a mandatory psychological assessment with an external threat assessment specialist and compliance with all recommended safety measures. This demonstrates a rapid, high-level, and authoritative response reserved for the most serious cases.  
    

Taken together, these cases provide the narrative evidence that the entire system—from QuAIC-informed triage to final case resolution—functions as a coherent and proportionate whole. The principle of proportionality, a cornerstone of fair and effective risk management, requires that the institutional response match the level of risk. The case studies show a near-perfect alignment: a low-risk case received a low-intensity response; a moderate-risk case received a moderate and escalating response; and a high-risk case received a high-intensity response. Because the QuAIC is a key input into determining that initial risk level, these qualitative examples serve as powerful validation of the tool's foundational role in initiating this proportionate response pathway.
