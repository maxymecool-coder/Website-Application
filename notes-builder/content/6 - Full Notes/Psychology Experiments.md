# Psychology Experiments
**Details**
Diving deeper into experiments, we can see things such as the **internal validity** which refers to the extent that the experimental effect (the difference between the control group and the group manipulated by the independent variable) to the independent variable. Having a control group does not ensure internal validity, the control group has to be truly random as well. For example, a group of people with the same base characteristics (like all being this age, or all doing this certain thing), and then a coin is flipped so that they can be sent to one group or another, and then we can choose which group is the control which is the influenced group.

The other characteristic is the **external validity** which is how generalized this study can be compared to our daily lives. For example, knowing the result of a treatment tested in an experiment like this does not guarantee it will work with other groups in the outside world. Sometimes psychologists will use groups from the outside to perform experiments on them, this instead sacrificed the **internal validity**, its a tradeoff, basically.
