# Inspectional Reading
**Details**
- There are two steps to this activity, performed simultaneously but they will be described as two different things in this Zettel. So first assume that the reader doesn't know if they want to analytically read but suspects that there maybe valuable information in it. This is when they must skim the book, to know what kind of book the author is writing and what his main contention may be.
- To skim, try to read every piece of info that is not the main content, the preface, title page, index, blurb, and of course the table of contents, as the author most of the time puts a lot of effort into it to allow a more aided approach to reading the book.
- Only after these steps then the reader should look for the pivotal chapters of the book, then going to those chapters, reading the closing/opening statements, summary statements as well. Try to read the 2-3 most important pages of the chapters to get a gist of what is important in the book.
- Try to be as active as possible and spend less than an hour reading all of it.

## After determining
This is when the difficulty of the book has been determined and the reader has deemed it worthy. If the book is hard, the reader should read it through without stopping at any difficult to understand concepts or words. This works because the author would want the main points of the book most clearly to the readers without having to analyze the book fully. 
This skimming also gives the reader a better view of the book's structure and altogether meaning as well (which is the first layer of comprehension to a book).

**Supporting Content**
- After reading, ask yourself
  What kind of book is it ?
  What is it about as a whole ?
  What is the structural order of the work whereby the author develops his conception or understanding of that general subject matter ?
  (Make sure to make notes concerning the answers to these questions, especially if you know that it may be days or months before you return to the book for an analytical read. Best on the contents page or title page.)
