# The Traumatized Parent
**Details**
Not only has research mostly shown that parents who are traumatized have a higher chance of getting hyper-aggressive or have emotional problems (which can affect their spouses as well), but they can also affect their children a lot from their actions and feelings.

A research done by Karlen Lyons-Ruth, observing children from "high-risk families" (poverty, single parents,...) showed that these parents either seem to be too preoccupied to their own problems to think or care about their children whilst the other often was helpless and fearful. Though both of these had something in common, they both wanted the comfort and the response to their *needs*, but through their own children. These mothers didn't seem to be doing these things deliberately-they simply didn't know how to be attuned to their kids and thus failing to respond to their cues and comforting, taking care of them. The hostile ones usually had a history of domestic violence and the dependent ones were more likely to have histories of sexual abuse.

When these kids eventually grow up, them developing mechanisms to cope, they would become more inconsolable, sullen or resistant to their misattuned mothers. This in turn would cause the mothers to see the child as not a part of her family, her love but as a stranger, and she gets mad, frustrated and sets the stage for subsequent abuse on the child.

Of course, these kids when they become adults all most likely cope with self-damaging impulsivity due to their unstable sense of self, inappropriate and intense anger, and recurrent suicidal behavior.

**Supporting Content**
Karlen and her colleagues thought that the behavior of the parents, the hostile part would be affecting the most in these children, but the fact was that the emotional withdrawal had the most profound impact in them.
