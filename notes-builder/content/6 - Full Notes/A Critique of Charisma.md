# A Critique of Charisma
**Details**
_"The spectacle is not a collection of images; it is a social relation between people that is mediated by images." - Guy Debord, The Society of The Spectacle_

Imagine this, a teenage girl comes to high school for the first time. It is a novel experience for her, and so, naturally, she is quiet, perhaps a little awkward. Suddenly, a random dude comes up to her and charismatically engages with her, which makes her feel that he is inherently attractive. Subsequently, they become friends. Some time after, the girl feels like the more that she engages with him, the more differently the girl feels from her original conception - when she attempts to be emotionally vulnerable about her upbringing, he makes a completely inappropriate joke about how her parents “just couldn’t keep their hands off her”, even putting his arms around her to close off the moment fully. Eventually, she has enough of it and disconnects herself away from him, wondering about why she had even made the decision to be with him in the first place. Perhaps it is because she was never engaging with the actual person but rather a mere projection of said person in a social setting. This is precisely what Debord’s framework illuminates - the fact that a social image that someone constructs of themselves inherently has nothing underneath when genuine relation is demanded.

And yet, the charismatic individual is rarely the one accountable. Virtually in every social context, charisma is an admirable quality, a sign of social intelligence and something that should be trained. For the quiet individuals, they are instead told to “come out of their shell” or “work on their social skills.” Never once are their needs and personality actually understood and validated.

To fully engage ourselves into the discussion and critique on charisma, we must first understand what charisma actually stands for. From my point of view, charisma is not a trait, but a skill based in behavioral mechanisms that is treated as a simulation of a person rather than expression of it. And so, it is a set of skills rooted in managing expressions and impressions, eliciting certain responses from others. The problem does not come from the existence of the mechanism itself, but rather the gap between the image and substance of the person utilizing it that the problem actually roots.

Perhaps to go about this in a more personal way, the biggest problem I have with charisma is the excessive relational value that it contains. In this sense, I mean the inherent nature of charisma allows its user to get themselves out of heavy, difficult situations when what they should do is sit with it and try to emotionally engage with the very factors that make those situations hard - because through these predicaments they can develop their own identity and internal values. I believe this is so because emotionally difficult situations by nature forces the individual to confront their deeper discomfort, whether it be unresolved trauma or past emotional pressures. And so these individuals never actually face the emotional problems because they can escape so easily with charisma. Furthermore, this prompts them to habitually avoid similar social confrontations that can have the same effect. Conclusively, charisma works against personal development not only by allowing the person to have an out to a challenging situation but also encouraging them to do so in all future instances.

The second point that I want to shortly address is the amount of social power it possesses - which is constitutive of the moral problem that charisma presents. Coming back to the aforementioned example of the couple, or now, ex-couple. In said situation, it is highly unlikely that the charismatic individual would have ever been seen as what caused the relationship to fall out despite the severe lack of objective information that anyone can have. Perhaps the girl might even be shunned and rumored to be what she is not (as I have argued elsewhere). This subsequently comes round to the points about morality made in said essay: that people are morally responsible for wrongly interpreting someone and harming them, even if the ways they do so are invisible. But to extend on that point in this situation, the responsibility now also stands in the other individual - the charismatic dude. Now, why does that person have said accountability ? After all, they are in no way responsible for the observers’ harmful and downright morally wrong views on their ex-partner. I agree with the point made in the previous sentence, but I still believe that the individual now bears moral responsibility due to how their charismatic simulation has conjured a false social image of reality onto the girl that others interpreted as real. Due to the manufactured personality that he has differing so much from who he fundamentally is, he is now morally obliged to disclose said gap because of how people are making real judgements on the basis of it.

For the reasons stated above, it is, from my own belief, enough as a criticism towards charisma and how society interprets it. Of course, the essay is in no way extensive in its philosophizing, defending, or clarifying and connecting the points as the aim is only to bring up certain issues that charisma as a social concept presents and maybe push some to think about it more deeply.
