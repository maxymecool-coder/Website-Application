# Perception and Recognizing Patterns
**Details**
Our perception of how bright an object is does not depend solely on the intensity of the light it reflects. Instead, the brain determines brightness through comparison with the surrounding environment. This phenomenon is known as **brightness contrast**. An object can appear lighter or darker depending on the brightness of the objects or background it is compared against.

For example, two bars of the exact same color and light intensity will appear to have different brightness levels if one is placed against a dark background and the other against a light one. This demonstrates that perception is an active calculation based on context, not a passive reception of raw data. The brain interprets the pattern of light by considering past experiences and contextual clues to infer the most likely reality.
