# The Limbic System and Sex
**Details**
In mating, the many parts of the limbic brain have a playing role in it. In the early 1930s and 1940s, scientists did lesion studies of the brain of animals to see how differently they would act when having sex. Damaging certain aspects of the limbic system made it so that they would start mating with inanimate objects. This shows us that there is a certain ability that the limbic system has to turn mating from just smell (as pheromones and all attract the rhinocephalone).

The Amygdala, for instance, is usually assigned to the danger reaction of the brain and body to escape situations, but in sex, it is associated with aggression. This aggression is more prevalent in men rather than in women due to the fact that there is simply an aspect of “competition” existing in between men.

Most prevalent areas are in the hypothalamus, which contains the sex functions depending on the genders. For the female, its the ventral medial hypothalamus (VMH), this part contains estrogen and such so its related to the ovulation cycle. For males, its the medial preoptic area (MPOA) and it contains testosterone.
