# The Central Ingredient of many Phobias
**Details**
Many people can have a wide variety of phobias, which may seem to have little or no correlation with each other. It is possible for us to find a central theme in all of these phobias that may seem disparate. It is usually concerned with a specific fear of the consequences of being in the apparent dissimilar situations.

A woman had a fear of flying, lying on the beach on a hot day, standing still in crowded places, riding in an open car on a windy day, riding in a closed car, elevators, tunnels, hills, and flying. After determining what the woman was afraid of in each of these situations, it was quite possible to find a common denominator. 

She had the notion, based on certain superstitions or folklore or actual possibility, that she could suffocate under each of these conditions. The central fear, in this case, was being deprived of air. For example, the feared outcome of being in enclosed places was lack of sufficient air. She had also heard, as a child, that “The wind was strong enough to blow the air out of your mouth.” Also, she had been impressed by the notion, “It was so hot, you couldn’t breathe.” The patient’s fear of flying was based on the notion that the pressurized cabin might be punctured accidentally and that there would be a loss of oxygen.

It was also discovered that she had a latent fear of water, which she managed to handle by having someone going with her when she was swimming. If she did not, she would be afraid as she thinks she might drone and there would be no one to save her.

This shows us how important it is not to make a priori judgement regarding the ideational content of phobias. Just as how the meaning of a particular object or situation may vary from patient to patient, so a varied assortment of fears experienced by one may have a common underlying meaning.
