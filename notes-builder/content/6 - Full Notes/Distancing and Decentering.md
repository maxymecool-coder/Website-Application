# Distancing and Decentering
**Details**
Now to continue with what to do after they have recognized these automatic thoughts. The process of regarding these thoughts objectively is called distancing. This derives from the Rorschach's ink-blot test, which is to maintain the distinction between the ink-blot configurations and the association of fantasy evoked by it from the patient. People who found the ink-blot to represent, literally, the scene or situation that were going inside their head are the ones who have automatic thoughts. On the other hand, those who can perceive it as merely an ink-blot perceives it as....well merely a blot.

This relates heavily back to epistemology as the patient is regarding his own perception of something to the fact of it. The ability to make these distinctions are of critical importance in helping the patient change his reactions to his distortions in reality.

Moreover, in various psychological disorders, the patient can also personalize the event to himself. For example, a depressed man may blame himself for the fact that a picnic trip was cancelled due to rain as it was scheduled by him. Same with an anxious women who sees a burned building and thinks that its her house that was burnt. And so same as distancing, we help our patients to **decenter** themselves from the focal point of these events.
