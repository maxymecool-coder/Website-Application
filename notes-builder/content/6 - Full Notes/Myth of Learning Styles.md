# Non-existence of learning styles
**Details**
Many people believe that there are learning styles which corresponds to their efficiency in learning. More often than not, although there are learning preferences, people should not be persuaded to choose a preference and to learn their materials in that way. Rather than that, all the previously mentioned principals matter more, the fact that you learn actively, consolidate and build mental models truly matter.

In 2008, cognitive psychologists Harold Pashler, Mark McDaniel, Doug Rohrer, and Bob Bjork were commissioned to experiment and prove this claim and see if it was true. They set out to answer two questions. First, what forms of evidence are needed to justify basing their styles on students or employees. They did this by seeing how much more or less effective different people and different learning methods would do. Second question was if the evidence for this all existed, they found that very few studies were designed to test the validity of learning styles theory in education, and of those they found that virtually none validate it and few even contradicted it. In fact, more importantly is the method of instruction to the subject.

This does not mean that these do not exist, it is mostly cause of the fact that they have not done many rigorous studies to confirm it all and the cost to do so would cost way too much.
