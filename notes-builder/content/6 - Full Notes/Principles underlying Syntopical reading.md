# Principles underlying Syntopical reading
**Details**
Some argue that we need to treat authors’ terminologies as sacrosanct, as books should never be read “out of context”, and also it is dangerous when we translate terms to other of other books as we can lose meaning in translation. They support this further by expressing the fact that these works are radically different in style and in space and time, and so it would be hard to make it so that they are discussing together. Finally, they say that the subjects that they discuss are not as important as the ways they do so.

Of course, we disagree with literally everything. First, we can translate that of course, and also treating something as sacrosanct is merely saying that it is difficult to translate to others. Sure it may be hard, but it is not impossible.

Second, let’s take an example. For example, if Aristotle, walked into our office right now, and accompanied by an interpreter who knew both of our languages, we would be able to understand him and his points. There may be recurring difficulties about us discussing philosophical questions but it would still be possible.

Finally, to the point about manner or style. This is like saying we communicate at the same level, all of us, no differences whenever, whoever, whatever. Like even if someone tells us to “fuck off” in a nice tone, he’s still telling us to fuck off, and maybe we should leave him alone.

Believing, then, that translation is possible (because it is done all the time), that books can “talk” to one another (because human beings do so), and that there is an objective, rational content of communication between human beings when they are trying to be rational (because we can and do learn from each other), we believe that syntopical reading is possible.
