# Polarized Thinking
**Details**
The neurotic patient may be prone to thinking in extremes in situations that impinge on his sensitive areas, which is in his domain. Events are labeled very simply, it is all in black and white, Good or Bad. This characteristic has been termed "dichotomous thinking" or "bipolar thinking". But in these patients, the absolute terms that couch this kind of thinking are "always" and "never."

An example is of a Valorant player, in a game, if he scores less than 500 points on his tracker score or even less, he might scold himself for being so bad and that he cannot rank up, but if he scores 600 or more consistently, he will regard himself as having the ability to climb through the ranks and that he is not deserving of the current predicament he is in. This is like a loop, as he will inevitably have bad and good games, but he will never label them as mildly good, mildly bad, or just OK.

Sometimes this kind of thinking may even be unipolar: for example, events may be perceived as totally bad or neutral or irrelevant. "Catastrophizing," a common characteristic of anxious patients, illustrates anticipation of extreme adverse outcomes. Regardless of evidence, of aspects, the patient will always think of the worst possible situation.

As this way of thinking impinge on the patient's sensitive areas, it will apply so as to tangible objects as well. For example, a man seeing a mere scratch of his wooden table, he might dwell on it for hours, thinking that it is a permanent defacement, it cannot be repaired, it ruins the whole aesthetic of the whole room. But as he gives it time and view the damage more objectively (that is, as trivial). Also the degree of his exaggerations may even present in his self-criticisms.

**Supporting Content**
Moreover, other types of thinking can also lead to distortions and misinterpretations, such as selective abstraction, arbitrary inference, overgeneralizations. These examples illustrate how aberrant thinking is aroused in situations that impinge on specific vulnerabilities of the patient's domain.
