# The nature of psychology
**Details**
In psychology, the lack of solid knowledge and indisputable treatments have allowed competing ideologies, movements and fads to be in it. Their very own history shows that even when major figures assume that certain concepts are incontrovertible, later they would be discarded as mere myths or superstitions. We have to acknowledge the fact in this field is that nothing rests on any proven theorems or assumptions. And the field is dominated by a few durable establishments and clusters of smaller sects of more tenuous standing. The major schools popularity usually depends on the charisma and the single-mindedness of the originator rather than how soundness it actually is. They also instead of working with each other, they convict others, having a disdain on others. 

And this is no different, classical psychoanalysis in general regards thoughts of the patients as disguises for their unconscious conflicts and are presumably causing the problem. Basically anything that they even attempt to do in response to their problems are recognized as defenses, as rationalizations rather than his ideas, his reasoning, his self-thought solutions, they are not taken at face value and are treated as stepping-stones to deeper, concealed components of the mind. 

On the other hand, the behavior therapists, similarly, tend to downgrade the importance of thinking. This is their attempt to emulate physical sciences, they reject data and concepts derived from man’s reflections on his own experiences and instead depend on of outsiders, of those who don’t really have a grasp on how the patient’s privacies fully. 

Lastly, traditional neuropsychiatry. These guys inquire the patients thoughts and feelings to form their diagnosis. Abnormal ideation and feeling states are simply manifestations of underlying physical process or clues to a disturbance in neurochemistry rather than to the abnormal psychological states themselves. 

And their solutions follow this, they never take into account the thoughts of the patients, by changing the external stimuli, by uncovering unconscious thoughts, administration of drugs or electroconvulsive therapy. This makes the patient thinks that they cannot help themselves, they must seek out someone who can assist them to do so. His confidence in the “obvious” techniques that he has customarily used in solving such problems slowly goes away as he accepts the view that emotional disturbances arise from forces beyond his comprehension. He cannot hope to understand it because such things are dismissed as insubstantial. This attitude deters the patient from getting out of those situations himself even when he clearly has the tools to do so.
