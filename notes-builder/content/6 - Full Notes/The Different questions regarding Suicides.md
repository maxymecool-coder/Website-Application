# The Different questions regarding Suicides
**Details**
Usually in the matter of morality and rationality, people conflate the two together and assume that they are relatively correlated. That, of course, does not apply to many situations, and suicide is one of them. Though that can also be debated as well.

The cultural default treats contemplating the act as evidence of either madness or wickedness, which means the question never gets asked as a question. Separating rationality from morality is what makes it askable at all. The two standards can diverge in both directions. Cheating on taxes may be prudentially rational — audit rates are low, penalties mild — and still impermissible: a moral requirement with no rational one behind it. Choosing a much worse university over a much better one violates no duty but may be irrational: a rational requirement with no moral one. Inside rationality, then, there's a separate set of questions:
- Could it ever be **true** that a person is better off dead?
- Could a person ever be **warranted** in believing their own case is one of those?
Of course these questions are separate and can have separate answers.
