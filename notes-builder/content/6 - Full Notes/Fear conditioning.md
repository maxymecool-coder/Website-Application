# Fear conditioning
**Details**
From the Behaviorist view, the fears that are in anxiety disorders come from conditioning. Mowrer's two-factor model of anxiety disorders influence thinking in this area, he suggests there are two steps in the development of an anxiety disorder:

1. Through classical conditioning, a person learns to fear a neutral stimulus (CS) that is paired with an intrinsically aversive stimulus (UCS).
2. Through operant conditioning, a person gains relief by avoiding the CS. It is then maintained because it it a reinforcement.

This may further extend to other people who has a somewhat built-in fear of something, it is a potentiation for them to develop a phobia of that thing. I say may because seeing someone afraid of flowers does not mean you will be scared of flowers.
