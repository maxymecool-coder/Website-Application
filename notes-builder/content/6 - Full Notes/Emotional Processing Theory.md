# Emotional Processing Theory
**Details**
This theory is the main model that covers PTSD. It posits two necessary conditions for emotional processing: the fear structure must be activated to modify it, and new info must be incorporated into the fear structure. This new information is encoded during the exposure and leading to a general pattern of reduction in negative effects as similar stimuli are encountered yet results are positive instead of negative.

The way one can get access to this emotional processing and be able to reduce their trauma symptoms include activation of the fear, within-session extinction (symptoms reducing in sessions), or between-session extinction. All of the improvement in the degree of fear activation, reduction, and peak responses with positively correlate with the improvement in PTSD symptom severity. Of course, there are also the reduction in negative cognitions, which proves the improvement of emotional processing and the resultant recovery.

In more recent times, the role of within-session extinction has been discouraged mostly, for it has been ineffective whilst the other, between-session extinction has. This has made it so current treatment directions are more towards that outcome, with emphasis on emotional engagement, disconfirmation.

In fact, research has even shown that higher fear expression shows superior treatment outcome after PE (Prolonged Exposure).
