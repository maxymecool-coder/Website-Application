# Justification as Public, Practical, and Distinct from Moral Truth
**Details**
Similar to Scanlon's [[Scanlon Contractualism]], this is a feature of Rawls's philosophy that addresses the justification of the citizens about their own selves and institutions.

There are three features to this account: the first is addressed to disagreement - "If there is no disagreement, then offhand there isn't anything to justify." One must convince or reconcile through reason, not rhetoric, manipulation, or coercion. Second, justification is not just a valid deductive argument. It must holds an internal role, it must also work in the public consensus of it. Third and most distinctively is the aim of it, it must be practical: "to preserve the conditions of social cooperation on a basis of mutual respect." Realistically, one cannot use moral frameworks to inherently justify and apply it to politics or society, because there would simply be too many disagreements. The task of political philosophy then is to narrow said range of disagreement by constituting partial convergence.
