# The Efficacy of Psychotherapies
**Details**
Usually, people are recommended to go to therapy despite not knowing exactly how effective it will be. This is a problem that is inherent in society, and it likely prevents many people from picking up therapy as they think that it might not be effective or as effective as medications.

And most of the time, these organizations, the APA, or WHO give quite arbitrary statistics on how effective it actually is, they just say that it is recommended then leave it at that. However, statistics for these areas do exist, though they are in research papers with academic jargon and paywalls, basically places that your average daily citizen will never check out. This directly hurts the reputation of therapies as well as prevent people from getting necessary treatment.

For example, in multiple meta-analyses, the most popular mental disorders, depression, PTSD, BPD, Bipolar,... all have a Hedge's g range of 0.5 to above 1, which means that 50% of people or more see better results and a decrease in symptoms rather than those who do nothing about it. Not only that but the efficacy is also higher than medications, which ranges at about 0.4-0.5 for most mental disorders, that is without considering the practical effects of dependency on drugs and so on.
