# Dark adaptation
**Details**
The eyes have this ability to be able to see faint light in the dark, as time passes of course, at first you will hardly see anything, but it will adapt.

Exposure to light chemically alters molecules called retinaldehydes, stimulating the visual receptors. In moderate light, these receptors regenerate the molecules as fast as the light alters them for the person to maintain that visual sensitivity. In darkness or dim light, these receptors regenerate without interruption (as they are not constantly altered) and so they can detect faint light.

Now cones and rods are also important in this, as they adapt at different rates, cones adapt faster than rods, but rods are more sensitive to faint light then cones, and so when rods finish their regeneration, we mostly see with rods.
