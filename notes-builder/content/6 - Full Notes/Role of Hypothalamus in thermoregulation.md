# Role of Hypothalamus in thermoregulation
**Details**
This small (pea-sized) compartment of the brain resides in the base of the forebrain. It contains many groups of neurons, one of which is **thermoreceptors** which of course, acts as a thermostat for the body's temperature. Some research has shown that the hypothalamus is also responsible for some actions for the host to maintain their regular temperature, though other researches have shown that there are two ways in which the hypothalamus does this, and different parts of the body triggers different mechanisms.

Interestingly though, the hypothalamus is also responsible for causing fever. In response to bacterial or viral infections, the hypothalamus release what's called pyrogens into the bloodstream. These chemicals then stimulate special receptors in the anterior part of the hypothalamus, leading to a temporary change in the body's temperature setpoint, killing the infections by temporarily overheating the body.
