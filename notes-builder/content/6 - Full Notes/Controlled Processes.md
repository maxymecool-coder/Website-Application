# Controlled Processes
**Details**
Controlled processes are intentional, require awareness, interfere with other controlled processes, demand effort, and tend to be linguistic. You will never accidentally find yourself counting backwards by sevens from 16,358. These properties are the exact inverse of automatic processes.

They are also simultaneously the reason that we have buildings, electricity, architecture, science, these fields all combine the understanding and perception of multiple fields that require well, knowledge on multiple things to build it.

Yet, they are also the reason that people can feel safe in their certain logical system when the logic itself is GARBAGE. Two extended examples:

**The elevator.** The lecturer has elaborate rules about which floors it's acceptable to take the elevator between, modulated by time of day and direction. He presents this as a rational system — but then admits the entire logic reduces to "I don't want you on my elevator when I'm trying to go somewhere." If he worked on the 8th floor instead of the 4th, every rule would shift accordingly. He catches himself judging a person in a suit taking the elevator one floor (probably a visitor who doesn't know the building) and even after having gout himself and taking the elevator one floor, he _still_ doesn't extend that charitable interpretation to others. The logic feels valid because it's complex and reportable. It isn't.

**The Monty Python witch.** Witches burn → wood burns → wood floats → ducks float → if she weighs the same as a duck, she's a witch. Each step sounds locally reasonable. The woman herself agrees it seems fair. The whole chain is insane. Hyperbolic, but it demonstrates how step-by-step conscious reasoning can feel compelling while being completely detached from reality.

The point is, when the logical chain is linguistical and reproducible, we tend to value it much higher even if it is completely horseshit. What is not on the other hand, like gut feeling, gets dismissed way more.
