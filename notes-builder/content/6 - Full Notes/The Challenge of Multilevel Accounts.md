# The Challenge of Multilevel Accounts
**Details**
The ultimate goal of cognitive psychology and neuroscience is to create a grand, unified theory of behavior—one that can explain how we get from the most fundamental biological processes to the complexity of human experience. This requires generating **multilevel accounts** that bridge the enormous gaps between vastly different scales of analysis. The challenge lies in connecting the dots from the expression of DNA into genes, from genes to the production of proteins and molecules, from those molecules to the firing of single neurons, from single neurons to the coordinated activity of large neural populations, and from those populations to the function of gross brain structures, which finally results in observable behavior.

As our knowledge at each of these individual levels increases at a staggering rate, the primary challenge is no longer just about discovery within a single level, but about integration _across_ them. How does a change in a single gene cascade up through the system to alter the function of a neural network and ultimately influence a personality trait or a decision? Answering these questions requires a deeply interdisciplinary approach, combining genetics, molecular biology, electrophysiology, systems neuroscience, and psychology. Creating these comprehensive, multilevel explanations represents the major frontier of brain science, promising to unravel the deepest mysteries of who we are and why we do what we do.
