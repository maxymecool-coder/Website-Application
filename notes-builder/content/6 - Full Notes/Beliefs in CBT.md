# Adaptive Beliefs
**Details**
According to developmental psychopathology, and the cognitive model, we can understand that people develop adaptive beliefs about the world and themselves to well, socially function. They would have a healthy and balanced view of the world, not overly too optimistic but not depressive either. They understand how unpredictable and how evil they can be.

The opposite of these are negative, which results when one interprets a setback related to their effectiveness, an interpersonal problem, or action that was contrary to their moral code. But they would be able to come back to the baseline after some time. In the case that they aren’t able to do this for any reason, weak beliefs for example, they would then need treatment.

There would be worksheets or graphs to allow people to conceptualize their patients, behaviors, thoughts, and so on. Though, one should remember to not ask the clients for the meanings of these negative thoughts due to the distress they can evoke. Try to hypothesize, that’s the most you can do, but remind yourself it’s just a hypothesis. Some tips would be that the meaning must connect to their core beliefs.
