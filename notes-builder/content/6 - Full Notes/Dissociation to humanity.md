# Dissociation to humanity
**Details**
Trauma response are irrational and not in people’s control, it makes people feel crazy, like they do not belong to the human race. And therefore leading to then mostly feeling shame and hiding the truth of why they would feel so. Most patients don’t actually realize what makes them feel so alienated and this is where therapy helps, by bringing out the emotions that trauma generates, and getting back the human capacity to observe oneself. Their usual reactions to this is to find groups that are similar to them, which may be able to give solace but not the flexibility of mental needed to function in life, to open to other, accepting the individual differences and acknowledging humanity. 

The trauma that once came from outside is now being fought in our own body and we don’t know if its in the past or the present. The goal is not to get us to accept the truths that has happened to us but to get the internal systems of the body to listen to us. And for it to understand that what happened then was in the past and now is current.

**Supporting Content**
This can even be classified as a subtype of PTSD, the dissociative subtype. The neurobiology behind this subtype is actually different from PTSD, where patients are rather prone to being behaviorally inactive even though their prefrontal cortex is working. As the prefrontal cortex is overworking, it effectively inhibits the amygdala, causing dissociation.

Moreover, the **frontotemporal network**, responsible for problem solving and logical reasoning in the brain, as well as the default mode network are both hyperactive during dissociation, and they also are proportional.
