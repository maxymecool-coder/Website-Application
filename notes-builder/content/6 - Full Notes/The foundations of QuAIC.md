# The foundations of QuAIC
**Details**
The development of the QuAIC was not an abstract academic exercise but a direct response to "organizational demands that sought a standardized approach to early reporting". Institutions required a practical method for front-line personnel and potential targets of concerning behavior to report their concerns in a consistent and quantifiable manner. The primary function of the QuAIC is to serve as a screening tool that can be "used easily by non-clinicians to quantify their emotional responses to problem behavior". This design principle is critical, as it effectively democratizes the initial stage of the threat assessment process, empowering individuals without specialized clinical or forensic training to contribute meaningful data.  

The core objective of the tool is to provide an "objective measurement and quantification of the 'gut feeling'" that often accompanies experiences of concerning behavior. By translating subjective intuition into a structured output, the QuAIC aims to remove the "reliance on personal thresholds and biases for both targets and responders". This addresses a fundamental vulnerability in many reporting systems. Without a standardized framework, the decision to escalate a concern can be highly variable, depending on the reporting individual's temperament, cultural background, or tolerance for conflict, as well as the personal biases of the staff member receiving the report. A stoic individual might under-report a serious pattern of harassment, while a more anxious person might frame a minor issue in alarming terms. This inconsistency creates systemic risk, as genuinely dangerous situations may be overlooked if they are not articulated in a way that triggers an institutional response.  

The QuAIC's intervention is therefore systemic in nature. It is designed to correct this flaw by creating a more reliable and equitable "front door" to the university's safety and support apparatus. By prompting users to answer four specific questions about the behavior—its level of aggression, frequency, persistence, and disruptive impact—it channels subjective experience into objective data points. This structured data can then be processed, triaged, and acted upon with a degree of consistency that is impossible to achieve with purely narrative or ad-hoc reporting. This ensures that cases are evaluated based on the intrinsic characteristics of the behavior in question, rather than on the communication style of the person reporting it.
