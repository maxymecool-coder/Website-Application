# Automaticity and Donald
**Details**
In the hallmark experiments on automaticity, most of the human subjects were Donald, not in the sense that the participants had that name but the object of the experiment was Donald. Now actually explaining the experiments, basically people were put through variations of priming, which is basically showing them words in one way or the other, some were negative, others were positive, and they would relate or not relate to the object.

After that, they were then shown a passage of Donald doing neutral things, and then asked to evaluate him. For the words that were related, people were much more likely to say he was reckless or something if that was the word that they were primed with in a "separate" experiment minutes ago. And of course, these people did not realize that they were in the experiment or anything of the sorts.

There were many other experiments that tested if automaticity was real, like the John Bargh experiments to show that flashing hostility-related words influenced participants' judgments of Donald, leading them to perceive him as more hostile.

There was also the Bob the priest experiment, testing that if people could realize the inconsistency of the priest's kindness (not assuming that all priests are kind) through his actions. And yes, they could if they weren't in a cognitive task, but if they are, they would be more likely to just follow the normal understanding.
