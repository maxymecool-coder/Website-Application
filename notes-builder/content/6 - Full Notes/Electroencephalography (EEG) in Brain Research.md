# Electroencephalography (EEG) in Brain Research
**Details**
- **Methodology**: Records electrical activity of neuron groups via scalp electrodes; amplifies and digitizes signals. Typically measures responses to stimuli (e.g., viewing images or sounds) over multiple trials to average out noise and correlate activations to tasks. Applicable to healthy/unhealthy subjects in labs or hospitals. 
- **Advantages**: Excellent temporal resolution. Detects unconscious processing and brain abnormalities (e.g., depression, schizophrenia). Inexpensive, widely available, minimally invasive. Allows long experiments with many trials. 
- **Disadvantages**: Poor spatial resolution (only gross brain areas). Noisy signals (e.g., from eye blinks, muscles, or room electricity) require multiple trials to average out noise, but noise isn't fully eliminated.
