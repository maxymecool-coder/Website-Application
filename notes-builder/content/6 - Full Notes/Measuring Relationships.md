# Measuring Relationships
**Details**
Something as abstract as relationships is objectively difficult to measure. After all it is a concept, and to measure it, we would need to operationalize (finding variables that we can measure the "relationship" that reliably predicts it). And the answer here is interdependence: the ability between two people to affect each other's situations.

The definition is embarrassingly overinclusive on purpose. Lecturer and class are interdependent. Students competing for parking spaces are. Through global warming, you and a stranger in Australia are. The move here is that once everything is on one continuum, narrowing can be done by specifying coordinates rather than by fiat.

The coordinates we are talking about will come from Kelley and colleagues — five dimensions along which interdependence varies: **frequency** of contact; **duration**, meaning how long a given level of contact persists; **diversity** of the interactions shared; **direction of influence**, from unidirectional (my relationship with a film star) to fully mutual; and **strength** of influence. Any relationship in the world is a point in that five-dimensional space.
