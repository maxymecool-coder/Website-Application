# Externalist Against Skepticism
**Details**
Internalism against skepticism would only result in a Cartesian circle, as one would have to constantly wonder about "do I know that I know" again and again infinitely. This is why many philosophers turn to Externalism, which instead proposes that we need not to "know" about the mechanics behind something but rather just that it works.

Millican uses the example of a dog knowing its master is approaching. The dog relies on its nose. The dog cannot write a philosophical treatise proving its nose is accurate, but if the nose _is_ in fact reliable, the dog knows. This perspective rehabilitates G.E. Moore’s famous anti-sceptical argument ("Here is one hand, and here is another"). From an internalist view, Moore proves nothing. But from an externalist view, if Moore’s eyes are working, he _does_ know he has hands, effectively ignoring the sceptic’s demand for proof.

Externalism though, still fails when it doesn't address the specific anxiety of the inductive sceptic. Let's say a sceptic asks, "How do you know the sun will rise tomorrow?", the Externalist replies, "If the universe is uniform, then my belief is justified." The sceptic is asking if the world will be the same, telling that person that "if it does, you'll be fine" is a tautology, not comfort.
