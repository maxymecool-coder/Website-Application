# The Limits of Feature Detectors
**Details**
While feature detectors are fundamental to vision, they cannot fully explain perception on their own. They represent a **bottom-up process**, where simple elements are combined to form a larger perception. However, this model fails to account for how context and expectation influence what we see.

For instance, an ambiguous symbol can be perceived as the letter 'H' in the word 'THE' and as the letter 'A' in the word 'CAT', even though the visual stimulus and the feature detectors being activated are identical. Similarly, reversible figures, which can be perceived in more than one way, show that perception is not a fixed output based on a given input. This indicates that a **top-down process**, where experience and expectations are applied to interpret sensory information, is also essential for perception.
