# The Passive Learner
**Details**
As stated in [[Childhood Socialization]], there are two groups about how children understand the socialization around them and adapt to it, the paragraphs below concerns the first group.

The effectiveness of simple conditioning can be overstated. Children often conform to societal roles not because they have been conditioned, but because they understand social expectations and are motivated by a fear of negative backlash. Consequently, when a child's genuine preferences deviate from the norm, conditioning alone is rarely enough to force a change in their behavior.

Indoctrination is when new ideals are introduced into the child, usually at middle school, when they start questioning and when they start finding out new things about the world, about their own ideas and all. They still challenge these views though, and there is quite a bit of personal bias involved in it sometimes. So it does not do much for socialization as well.

Imitation is what children usually do, viewing others who are involved around them and try to imitate the way they act and such to learn from it and socialize the way that they do. In normal families where there are both parents, the child can learn from them, but in households with only one parent or both parents are the same gender, do they act like them instead ? The answer is no, the influence does not exactly come from them. When they get pass the age where they learn from their parents, they will instead start imitating the society and community around them.
