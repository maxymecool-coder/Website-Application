# The kinds of practical books
**Details**
Any book that contains rules, prescriptions or maxims, or directions are practical books. But furthermore, it may contain principles that underlie those rules to explain why they work and how they do so. 

From this insight, we can divide practical books into two main groups. One is primarily presentations of rules, the other is concerned with the principles that generate said rules. The distinction is not obvious as both of these things can be found in the same book. Thing is, the point is one of relative emphasis. The book of rules in any field will be recognizable as practical. The book of principles deals with theory of practicing rules, and so you can tell if it’s practical. All of the fields that practical books are about are usually fields which men can do better or worse. 

The major propositions to look for in a rulebook are, of course, rules. It is expressed by an imperative rather than a declarative sentence since it is a little more emphatic, but not necessarily more memorably. It recommends you to do something as worth to gain a certain end. The book of principles on the other hand will try to show that rules are sound. They have to appeal and persuade you that they are good, in any way they can. 

In the other kind of practical books, principles underlying rules will look exactly like those in a purely theoretical book. The propositions will state that something is the case, then the arguments will try to show that it is so.
