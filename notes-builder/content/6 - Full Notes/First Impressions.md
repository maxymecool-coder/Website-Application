# First Impressions
**Details**
In both folk psychology and social psychology, we often regard this as the most important aspect of an interaction between people as it often will dictate how they treat each other further down the line. This is known as the primacy effect (same as the one we studied in [[Primacy Effect]]). Though it may come as a surprise, we actually form first impressions with amazing speed and more accuracy through many researches and experiments. This concept can dictate and influence our decisions throughout our daily lives as we encounter many situations requiring the use of it.

These first impressions can manifest themselves into something worse though, referred to as self-fulfilling prophecies, which are the expectations that change the behavior of a person to increase the probability of those expectations becoming true. Suppose a psychologist hands you a cellphone and asks you to talk with someone, while showing you a photo supposedly of that person. Unknown to the person you are talking to, the psychologist might hand you a photo of a very attractive member of the opposite sex or a much less attractive photo. Not surprisingly, you act friendlier to someone you regard as attractive. Furthermore, if you think you are talking to someone attractive, that person reacts by becoming more cheerful and talkative. In short, your first impression changes how you act and influences the other person to live up to (or down to) your expectations (M. Snyder, Tanke, & Berscheid, 1977).
