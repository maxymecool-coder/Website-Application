# I, Me, and Our Selves
**Details**
James divided the self into **the Me** (self-as-object: all listable attributes, roles, possessions, relationships — the thumbnail sketch we draw of ourselves by watching our own behavior) and **the I** (self-as-subject: the active, experiencing, doing self in the present moment). believed that the latter was temporary, circumstantial perhaps but some of the things that exist in the I actually is reflected into the Me. The main problem here is that we are quite unable to differentiate between our values and our traits, and often project what we value to what we are (which makes sense) but yeah.
