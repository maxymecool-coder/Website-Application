# Freud's Stages of Psychosexual Development
**Details**
Sigmund Freud proposed that personality develops through a series of five **psychosexual stages**. During each stage, a child's psychosexual energy, or libido, is focused on a different erogenous zone. Freud believed that if a child's needs at a particular stage were either over-gratified or not met, they could become **fixated**, leading to personality traits and behaviors that persist into adulthood.

**1. The Oral Stage (Birth to ~1.5 years)**

- **Erogenous Zone:** Mouth.
    
- **Focus:** The infant derives intense pleasure from oral activities like sucking, biting, and breastfeeding.
    
- **Potential Fixation:** An unresolved conflict at this stage could lead to adult behaviors centered on the mouth (e.g., smoking, overeating, excessive talking) or issues related to dependency and independence.
    

**2. The Anal Stage (~1.5 to 3 years)**

- **Erogenous Zone:** Bowel and bladder control.
    
- **Focus:** The child gets psychosexual pleasure from the sensations of controlling and eliminating feces. The major conflict is toilet training.
    
- **Potential Fixation:** Freud suggested that overly strict or lenient toilet training could lead to an "anal-retentive" personality (orderly, stingy, stubborn) or an "anal-expulsive" personality (messy, wasteful).
    

**3. The Phallic Stage (~3 to 6 years)**

- **Erogenous Zone:** Genitals.
    
- **Focus:** Children become aware of their genitals and, according to Freud, develop a sexual attraction to the opposite-sex parent (the Oedipus complex for boys and the Electra complex for girls). This stage is marked by controversial concepts like "penis envy" in girls.
    
- **Potential Fixation:** Fixation at this stage could lead to long-term difficulties with relationships and authority.
    

**4. The Latent Period (~6 years to puberty)**

- **Focus:** Sexual impulses are suppressed and dormant. The child's energy is redirected toward social interactions, schoolwork, and developing friendships, primarily with same-sex peers. This is not a psychosexual stage in the same way as the others.
    

**5. The Genital Stage (Puberty onwards)**

- **Erogenous Zone:** Maturing sexual interests.
    
- **Focus:** The individual develops a strong sexual interest in people outside the family. If the previous stages have been successfully navigated, the person can form healthy, mature sexual relationships.
    
- **Potential Fixation:** Freud believed that individuals with significant fixations in earlier stages would have less libido available for this stage, leading to difficulties in forming satisfying relationships.
    

**Evaluation of the Stages** While the basic observations are undeniable (infants suck, toddlers are toilet-trained), the core Freudian concept of fixation is very difficult to test scientifically. When researchers have attempted to test these ideas, such as the link between toilet training and an "anal" personality, they have found little to no supporting evidence.
