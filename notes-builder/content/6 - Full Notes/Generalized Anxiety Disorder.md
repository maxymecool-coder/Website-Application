# Anxiety Disorders
**Details**
Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) is characterized by a persistent and excessive state of anxiety that is not tied to any specific object or situation. Unlike other anxiety disorders that are triggered by particular stimuli, individuals with GAD experience a "free-floating" sense of tension and worry that permeates all aspects of their lives. From academic performance to social interactions, everything becomes a source of apprehension. This constant state of heightened arousal means there are no moments of relief, distinguishing it from the episodic nature of panic attacks or phobias.

Diagnostically, GAD is identified through intake interviews that assess the breadth of a person's worries and through physiological measures like a heightened startle response. Affecting a significant portion of the population (around 5-6%), it is often treated with a combination of medication (antidepressants or tranquilizers) and therapy. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is particularly effective, as it helps individuals reinterpret situations and manage their anxious responses, providing them with tools to cope with the pervasive sense of worry.
