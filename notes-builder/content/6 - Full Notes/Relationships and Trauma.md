# The Effects of Relationships
**Details**
"Having a good support network constitutes the single most powerful protection against becoming traumatized" - Van der Kolk.

When we are terrified, nothing calms us down more than the soothing voice of someone or the firm embrace of someone that we trust. Any humans, adults or children respond the same to the assurance of someone, they always feel safer, feel protected. Studies has proven this to be true numerous times before. For example, in WW2 in England, children who lived in London during the Blitz and were sent away to the countryside fared much worse than those who stayed with their parents and endured nights upon nights in bomb shelters and frightening images of war (assuming they're not dead).

But even in treatment it is present, traumatized human beings recover in the arms of relationships (sounds weird just stay with me). Those who are treated usually improve their relationships with families, loved ones, or their therapists. The role of them are to provide physical and emotional safety, as well as safety from the judgement of their reactions of excessive emotions caused by their illnesses.

As discussed before, the reason why DTD patients act how they do is because of this lack of relationship or betrayal that they experienced early in their lives. This causes them to find alternative ways with dealing with their intense emotions, having no one to guide them gives rise to another set of problems: dissociation, despair, addictions, and so on.
