# DNA's Unique Causal Status in Behavioral Science
**Details**
The paper argues that DNA variation has a unique causal status compared to other biological correlates of behavior. While a correlation between brain activity and a behavior is ambiguous (does the brain state cause the behavior, or does the behavior cause the brain state?), the relationship with DNA is unidirectional. An individual's behavior does not alter their inherited DNA sequence. Therefore, a confirmed association between a DNA variant and a behavioral trait can be interpreted causally: the DNA difference contributes to causing the behavioral difference, not the other way around. This provides a firm anchor for causal inference in the notoriously difficult "direction of effects" problem in psychology.
