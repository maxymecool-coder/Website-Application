# Problem of Vitality and Decadence
**Details**
According to his philosophy, Nietzsche believed that there was a certain problem in the late 19th century being Nihilism, or a catastrophic loss of vitality. Because of the lost of hope and a set "framework", humanity risks losing the "will" to continue. Of course, now looking back, people would scold him for being so wrong, but in the context of the time, it would be logical why he would think that. Nonetheless, Nietzsche adopts "Vitality" as his primary axiomatic criterion for judgement, rather than Justice, Happiness, or Holiness.

Initially, Nietzsche believe that health, reproductive power, and physical energy was the definition for Vitality, traits where he didn't have as he had syphilis. However, as his thoughts matured, he moved away from purely biological definitions and toward Self-Affirmation. Self-Affirmation is basically the concept of one being able to judge one's life as good and meaningful without needing external justification. 

**Supporting Content**
Interestingly, Nietzsche complicates this dichotomy by acknowledging that vitality and decadence are often implicated in one another; great cultural breakthroughs (like his own insights) may arise from states of physiological sickness, suggesting a complex relationship between biological decay and cultural potency. He also argued that the levels of Vitality could differ in the structure, a society of Vitality may include citizens who aren't all vital and so can the opposite exist.
