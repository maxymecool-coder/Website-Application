# Stereotypes, Prejudice, and Discrimination
**Details**
These three terms are usually conflated alongside each other but they all refer to separable psychological phenomena along the cognitive, affective, and behavioral dimensions. Stereotype is easy to define, prejudice is affective: they are negative feelings or attitudes toward individuals because of their group membership. Discrimination is behavioral: they are actions that people take against others group memberships. These often co-occur but are logically independent—one can hold a stereotype without feeling prejudice, or feel prejudice without discriminating. A critical distinction regarding stereotypes: they can be statistically accurate when applied to groups (NBA players are indeed tall) but become problematic when applied to individuals, because overlapping distributions mean many individual members of the "lower" group exceed many members of the "higher" group. The distributions' means may differ while their ranges largely overlap. A second problem arises when people make causal attributions for group differences, citing "nature" when confounding variables (socioeconomic status, diet, culture, historical oppression) make such claims scientifically unsupportable.
