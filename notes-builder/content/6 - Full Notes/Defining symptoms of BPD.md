# Defining symptoms of BPD
**Details**
It has been notoriously difficult to evaluate if someone has BPD or not. Not only that but the symptoms are also quite interesting/weird from a psychological perspective. They have the normal which may include cognitive distortions (rigidity,…) as well as unstable relationships and mood swings, attachment issues,… but then there’s one other symptom, parasuicidal behaviors. 

Parasuicide was first coined by Marsha Linehen in the 1970s. Basically, the definition for the term is behaviors that pushes the individual close to death, but not actually death as they stop before fully committing (eg. putting pills in mouth but not consuming). The reason people decided on such a term is because other names would have caused stigma or negative opinions on the behavior, thinking its manipulative and so on, which may worsen the reputation of people who have BPD even more.

The mood swings in more detail include a sensitivity to it, intensity, and slow return to the baseline. Simply, the sensitivity means the person reacts quickly and cannot control their emotional reactions, they can easily be less understandable of the situation and let their emotions take over. Continuing, intensity, it means that these people are emotionally intense, it may make them experience extreme grief or extreme happiness. Lastly, the slow return is of course just how they cannot return to normal after experiencing these intense emotional states as a part of their cognitive delusions.

Because of all this emotional dysregulation, people might even dissociate themselves to get away from these situations and refuse to feel it all because it is just simply too overwhelming. They then will lose their sense of self, goals, and so on.
