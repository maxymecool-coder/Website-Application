# Auditory Cortex
**Details**
The **primary auditory cortex** (A1) is the region of the cerebral cortex responsible for processing auditory information. It is located in the superior temporal gyrus of the temporal lobe.

Its main function is to perceive and interpret the fundamental aspects of sound, including pitch (frequency), loudness (amplitude), and the location of the sound source. Information from the ears is relayed through the thalamus to the auditory cortex for conscious perception.

The auditory cortex is organized **tonotopically**. This means that neurons are arranged according to the sound frequency they respond to, creating a "frequency map" across the cortical surface. Lower frequencies are processed at one end, and higher frequencies at the other. This organization is critical for distinguishing between different sounds.
