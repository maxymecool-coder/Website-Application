# The sign to stop eating
**Details**
The liver is a major source of information in this mechanic, as its job is to monitor and control the major nutrient used as short-term energy: glucose (blood sugar).

When a meal is done, there's usually a lot of glucose, some of it is stored, some is converted into glycogen and various fatty acids which are used for later. When energy is required, these substances are used and burnt to be used as glucose (energy).

The liver manages this conversion process and informs other organs of where this process is going. If the balance tips toward storage (when we are eating too much), it will send a satiety signal to stop the animal from eating. Same for the opposite. The evidence of this came from injecting glucose into hungry dogs, only into the vein that was leading to the liver though.

Now though, we recognize that the process in between announcing that the body is low on glucose and when we actually get supply is really slow. To counter this, the liver must anticipate the body's future needs, so that eating has already initiated.

It does this by responding to a characteristic drop and rise to the glucose level. Simply, when we're low on glucose, glycogen turns into glucose, this indicates a drop and rise, characteristically, and that is what the liver responds to.
