# Developmental Psychopathology
**Details**
This field of psychopathology is quite new due to its nature. For the most of history, children have been seen as miniaturized forms of adults, not completely different type of humans that have different functions, systems than adults.

This utilitarian view meant there was little societal or scientific framework for recognizing or studying the unique mental and emotional struggles of the young. The first academic text on children wasn't written until the mid-16th century, and concepts like adolescence were not formally defined until the early 20th century. The recognition of child abuse as a specific societal problem, with the coining of the "battered child syndrome" by radiologists in 1962, is shockingly recent. It took the observation of physical injuries that could only be intentionally inflicted to formally acknowledge children's vulnerability.

The first edition of DSM even only had 2 conditions related to developmental psychopathology, but nowadays there are hundreds. The reasons for this is not that all of these disorders are new, but rather because now we can see all of the problems that children have that concerns them rather than adults.
