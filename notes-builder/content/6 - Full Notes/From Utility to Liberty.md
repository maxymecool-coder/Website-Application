# From Utility to Liberty
**Details**
[[Mill's Utility]] has posited that happiness is the higher pleasure and should be, above all, desired and try to be achieved. But how does this connect to liberty and equal, near-absolute rights ? Well, he grounds it in that liberty basically allows a person the faculties, knowledge, and intellect that is necessary for them to understand and choose the path to happiness and pleasure for themselves.

**Supporting Content**
The main psychological assumption for this is: the particular form of life best suited to any individual can only be ascertained by that individual themselves, under conditions where the decided preference criterion is operative. Each individual has a peculiar bent, and the best mode of existence adapted to their nature must be discovered by them through experience and reflection.

The other assumption he makes is that everyone is possible of experiencing this pleasure, as long as they have higher faculties and are given suitable opportunities, which is a finely large claim. You cannot maximize what you cannot measure, and the only way to generate the relevant information is to allow individuals freedom to experiment, choose, and reflect.
