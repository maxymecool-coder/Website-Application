# Solubility Principles
**Details**
How a drugs behave is usually accredited to its interactions with the human body. Molecules are generally classified as hydrophilic or hydrophobic, which simultaneously means water-loving and water-hating. Chemically, that means hydrophilic substances are polar or charged, allowing them to dissolve easily in blood and urine, excreted quickly. On the other hand, hydrophobic means they prefer oily or fatty environments, and will dissolve slower.

This is critical in drug testing as **THC** (the active compound in cannabis), dissolve into the body's fatty tissues. Because they are stored in fat rather than staying in the water-based blood, they are eliminated from the body very slowly. This all explains how a user of cannabis may still test positive weeks after use whilst those who use water-based substances won't.
