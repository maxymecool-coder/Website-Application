# The Yuck Factor
**Details**
Humans pride themselves on being rational creatures who use logic and social rules to determine kinship. But beneath this cognitive surface lies a powerful, non-rational mechanism that shapes one of our most critical decisions: who we choose as a mate. Studies of individuals raised in Israeli kibbutzes and traditional Taiwanese households revealed a profound rule: if you are raised in close, domestic proximity with someone during the first six years of your life, you will not develop a sexual attraction to them later. They feel, irrevocably, like a sibling. This is known as the **Westermarck effect**. It's not a conscious decision or a cultural taboo; it's a deep-seated "yuck factor," an imprinted sexual aversion. This shows that despite our large cortexes, we are still guided by ancient, subliminal rules.
