# A Society Beyond Justice
**Details**
Marx famously stated that "all science would be superfluous if the appearance of things and the essence of things were to directly coincide." In a capitalist system, economic science (like the Labor Theory of Value) is necessary to pierce the ideological illusions of the free market and reveal the hidden essence of exploitation. A "full communist society," therefore, is structurally defined as a society where appearance and essence finally coincide. It is a society completely devoid of ideological consciousness, false delusions, and hidden surplus value extraction.

In a fully communistic society, the means in production are held in common and social labor is applied consciously according to a visible, democratic plan. The means of production here is merely a metric to balance the needs of the community and determine individual consumption shares. Because the economy is consciously planned and administered by everyone, there are no hidden forces ruling over the populace. Because class domination is abolished, the very need for juridical superstructures—like political "justice" and liberal "rights"—evaporates. Instead of fighting over rights to mitigate exploitation, the society operates organically on principles of the "good," emphasizing autonomy, self-development, and meaningful work.
