# The Hippocampus in Depression
**Details**
The **hippocampus** is a brain region primarily associated with learning and memory. It also plays a vital role in regulating the body's stress response; specifically, it is one of the main structures that helps to **turn off** the release of stress hormones like cortisol.

Under the chronic stress of major depression, the hippocampus is known to be vulnerable. The constant exposure to high levels of glucocorticoids can cause neurons in the hippocampus to shrink and can inhibit the birth of new neurons, leading to a measurable **atrophy or volume loss** in the region. This damage likely contributes to the memory problems often seen in depression and further impairs the brain's ability to shut down the stress response.

However, Sapolsky notes in his updated lecture that while this link is important, he now considers the hippocampus less central to the _core_ symptoms of depression (anhedonia and rumination) compared to the dysregulation in the prefrontal cortex and mesolimbic circuits.
