# Clarifying Addiction
**Details**
Even though people attribute addiction to simply the overall symptoms like having physical or psychological reactions to a drug, it is in fact much more complicated than that. We are actually able to split the generally accepted meaning of addiction into three much more detailed terms.

Firstly, addiction, still, basically means the inability to control drug use ([[The Drug Abuse Potential]]), regardless of whether physical dependence withdrawal symptoms are present or not. Secondly is the termed just mentioned, physical dependence, which are symptoms that a person physically present when they are "off" the drug. And lastly psychological dependence which manifests in cravings or need.

But to exemplify this further, we will consider the example of a patient who is on opiates due to chronic pain or medical conditions, this individual may have physical dependence symptoms but is not actually addicted to the medication, simply using it to function. An addict of something like morphine on the other hand will have more signs, like looking different, or psychological dependence.

**Supporting Content**
The DSM on the other hand... it does have a diagnostic for addiction but is rather named "Substance Dependence". To be diagnosed, a patient must exhibit three of seven criteria, which include tolerance and withdrawal, but also behavioral markers like time spent obtaining the drug and giving up social activities.
