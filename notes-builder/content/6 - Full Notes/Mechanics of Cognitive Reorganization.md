# Mechanics of Cognitive Reorganization
**Details**
As we have talked about, in depression the patient may act on a "closed system." A system where they reject any and all information that contradicts what they believe is true currently. And so we just question these assumptions, which consists of: eliciting the patient's reasons for believing the depressogenic assumption, and then marshaling the evidence in favor of or contradictory to the assumption, as if an argument, to help them find the truth.

Another important approach to testing these beliefs is through empirical demonstration. It is possible for us and the patient, as collaborators, to set up an experimental situation in which our assumptions can be tested and an answer can be obtained. Beck has carried out analog studies of this procedure with particular tasks and even has a clinical derivation of these experimental paradigms, to name is the Graded Task Assignment. Success improved the performance of the depressed group, and failure improved the nondepressed group (Loeb et al., 1971).

Further in his experiments, the patient was seen to react positively to tangible evidence of his superior performance, which can further provide clues for cognitive restructuring. Furthermore, to see the patient overgeneralize their own successes will mean that their cognitive distortions are malleable.
