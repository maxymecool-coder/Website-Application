# The Limitations of the Free Will Defense
**Details**
Basically, this defense asserts that God allows evil because human freedom is more important than that of the costs of sin. However, this defense is logically contingent upon a libertarian (incompatibilist) view of free will. If compatibilism is true—as many philosophers and neuroscientists argue—then a free action is simply one that flows from the agent's desires, even if those desires are determined. A compatibilist God could therefore create agents who are determined to _always_ freely choose the good. The refusal to do so implies that God is either not omnipotent or not benevolent. Furthermore, the Free Will Defense fails to account for natural evil (suffering caused by disease, tectonic shifts, or predation) that existed for millions of years prior to human agency. To bridge this gap, theists must postulate "fallen angels" affecting physics, a violation of parsimony that complicates the God hypothesis significantly.
