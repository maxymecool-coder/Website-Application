# Manage Afternoon Dips with naps and such
**Details**
Most people experience a natural dip in energy and alertness in the afternoon, often tied to the circadian rhythm of body temperature. To manage this, one can use traditional napping or protocols like Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR), which are designed to induce states of deep relaxation without necessarily causing sleep.

**Napping** is a valid recovery strategy, but its effectiveness depends on timing and duration. To avoid disrupting nighttime sleep, naps should be kept under 90 minutes and not be taken too late in the day. It's crucial to note that napping is not a requirement for optimal performance.

For individuals who wake up groggy from naps or prefer a non-sleep alternative, **NSDR** is a highly effective tool. This category includes several practices:

- **Yoga Nidra & NSDR Scripts:** These are typically zero-cost, guided audio meditations found on platforms like YouTube. They systematically guide the body and mind into a state of profound rest, which can replenish energy in 10-30 minutes.
    
- **Self-Hypnosis Apps (e.g., Reveri):** Specific, science-backed applications have been developed to facilitate deep relaxation and improve sleep. The Reveri app, for instance, was developed based on Stanford research and uses self-hypnosis scripts to help users fall asleep, return to sleep if awakened, and generally improve sleep quality. While these tools often carry a cost, they provide structured, goal-directed protocols.
    

The key advantage of NSDR-based tools is that they help replenish mental and physical energy without the risk of sleep inertia (grogginess). Furthermore, consistent practice can improve one's overall ability to control their state of relaxation, effectively training the brain for better sleep.
