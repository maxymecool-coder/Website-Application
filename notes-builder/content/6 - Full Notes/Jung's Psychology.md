# Jung's Psychology
**Details**
Carl Jung, once seen as Sigmund Freud's heir apparent, broke away to develop his own influential theory of personality. While he agreed with Freud on the importance of the unconscious mind, he envisioned it differently. Jung proposed that beyond our personal unconscious, there exists a deeper, universal layer he called the **collective unconscious**.

This collective unconscious is not shaped by personal experience but is inherited from our ancestors. It is a repository of shared human memories, instincts, and predispositions that manifest as **archetypes**.

**Archetypes** are universal symbols, themes, or patterns that appear in myths, art, literature, and dreams across all cultures. They are innate tendencies that shape how we perceive the world and react to it. Jung identified numerous archetypes, including:

- **The Good Mother:** A universally understood figure representing nurturing, warmth, and unconditional love.
    
- **The Shadow:** The dark, repressed, and sometimes creative side of our personality.
    
- **The Animus/Anima:** The masculine aspect within a woman and the feminine aspect within a man.
    
- **The Faithful Companion:** Often represented by an animal (like a loyal dog), symbolizing guidance and unwavering support.
    

According to Jung, we are born with these archetypal frameworks, and they cause us to unconsciously seek out and project these roles onto people and situations in our lives. This idea represented a significant departure from Freud's sexually-driven model, expanding the unconscious to include a spiritual and ancestral dimension.
