# Scanlon Contractualism
**Details**
An act is wrong if its performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any set of principles for the general regulation of behavior that no one could reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced, general agreement. (Scanlon 1998, p. 153).

So, what does this quote mean in light of Scanlon’s moral philosophy ? Scanlon’s framework is what I would say is based on a certain rationality, which may bear similarities to Rawls’s necessary person. What may be different in this sense is the standard that Scanlon actually holds people at: the moral authority standard, or what is “mutual recognition.” In this way, contractualism guides our substantive reflection about wrongness. Wrong is the primary moral predicate; right is defined as “not wrong”. One reason for focusing on wrong is to draw attention to the domain that contractualism is concerned to map, concerning what it is for one person to have been wronged by another. In this situation, it would also then require us to treat others as ends: as beings who can reasonably think rationally (very Kantian yes).
