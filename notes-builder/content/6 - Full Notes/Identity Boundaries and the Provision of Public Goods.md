# Identity Boundaries and the Provision of Public Goods
**Details**
One of the most significant consequences of identity politics is its profound effect on social cooperation. Compelling research from across the globe demonstrates a powerful inverse correlation: the sharper and more salient the identity boundaries within a society—whether racial, ethnic, or religious—the lower its provision of public goods. This pattern arises because people are systematically less willing to invest in collective resources like education, healthcare, and infrastructure if they perceive that the benefits will flow to a rival "out-group."

And so in countries that have their own agendas and try to manage so that everyone shares one identity, the public amenities are generally better and more well-maintained than countries where the government supports to "out-group." Like how in Kenya, they promoted tribal languages and culture, resulting in bad public goods.

An even more specific example is the AIDS epidemics in Brazil and South Africa. By uniting every culture and diversity in their place, Brazil successfully managed AIDS as everyone was cooperating and helping each other. Whilst in South Africa, AIDS turned into a "Black" disease and so the public health response was negative and so on.
