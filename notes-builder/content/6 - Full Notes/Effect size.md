# Effect size
**Details**
A raw difference between two means tells you almost nothing on its own. Bay Area kids scoring 60 against a US mean of 50 is a gap of 10, but this 10 might be meaningless. If US scores cluster at 49-51 and Bay Area scores at 59 to 61, that gap is enormous; if scores scatter all over the place, it's nothing. Effect sizes solve this problem by introducing scaling the gap to the spread: d = (μ₁ − μ₂)/σ. The trap worth flagging in red is that this σ is the standard deviation of scores, not of the distribution of sample means. The result is unitless, it says how many standard deviations the two means are apart.  

**Supporting Content**
A lot of times effect size is confused with statistical significance. However, the latter asks "could this be chance?" while effect size asks "how big is it?". Push n to 10000 and the distribution of sample means narrows a hundredfold, so even a trivially small true effect lands far out in the tail and returns a microscopic p.
