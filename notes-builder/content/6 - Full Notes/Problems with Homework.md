# Problems with Homework
**Details**
If problems are reported about using the skills, we then would use problem-solving strategies to help the client analyze what happened, what went wrong and how to use the skills better. It is important to lead them through an examination of what happened. Though it can be torturous since they are afraid of the trainers' judgements. Many cannot come to the conclusion of what objectively stopped them, rather, they automatically just blame their own inability or saying that it will just not work for them.

Another problem one may encounter is difficulty with homework (as with not practicing). Do not take this statement at face value, most of the time, they did practice, its that they just didn't solve the problem necessarily. Then the conversation after this, turns to discussing to help the client have a fuller understanding of the skills. For example, a client may enter a situation where they are going to practice relaxing, but it doesn't give them anything, no reduction in anxiety or tension. In these situations, both sides must be understanding and try to tackle the problems to see if its truly because it isn't working or that they are doing it wrong. At any point, the trainer should be thinking about how to solve a problem rather than what would work.

The last problem is that of noncompliance and of refusal or failure to engage in skills training activities. Reject anything (excuses, such like that), and try to tackle why they would not practice, like if they don't want to, don't remember, not having an occasion to do so. Try to help them to tackle their problems in a gentle way, don't punish or anything like that, maybe one can even take advantage of the situation to practice skills as clients usually are ashamed in these situations (emotional regulation).

Here are some questions to start and deal with these problems:
1. Did doing your homework get into short-term memory: if no, then non-awareness must be dealt with. This may stem from inattention in the session, absence from it, and/or not reading or getting the reminder of the homework.
2. Were you willing to do the needed or expected effective behavior: if no, ask what got in the way.
3. Did the thought of doing homework get into mind: if no, find ideas to get it into mind.
4. What got in the way: Continue to problem solve.

Something to keep in mind is motivation. Now, most of the time we do not need to be judgmental with the client, they are already judgmental enough about themselves. Instead, we have to go ahead and try to see what negative thoughts would be making them having negative reactions. Though it is fine for them to have these thoughts, it is probably not effective to withhold them, and so one should try to tackle them.
