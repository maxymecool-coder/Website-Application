# Context, Hidden Premises, and the Three Levels
**Details**
In our society, we reason logically a lot, and we do so mostly with suppressing the premises instead of stating them outright and rationalizing them completely so. But this could also prove to be conspicuous, because bad reasoning can hide in those hidden premises. Real-world arguments routinely leave premises unstated. "It's Friday, so Marianne's wearing jeans" omits "Marianne always wears jeans on Friday." We suppress premises when context makes them obvious. But hidden premises are also where bad reasoning hides — a dubious assumption left unexamined because it was never made explicit. Surfacing suppressed premises is a core skill.

There are three levels to talking about arguments and about how the premises are hidden. Firstly, language, the word red has three letters; secondly, the thought red is a concept of it; and lastly, the property of it. They are three factors that we as human can comprehend and understand yet no one else can.
