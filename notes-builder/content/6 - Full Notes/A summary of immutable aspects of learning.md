# A summary of immutable aspects
**Details**
These are some aspects that we probably could agree on about learning:
First, to be useful, learning requires memory, so that we can make use of the information later down the line.
Second, we need to keep learning and remembering all our lives. From middle school where we have to gain mastery of something to get a job. Then in jobs where learning better might give us a chance at promotion, and so on and so forth.
Third, learning is an acquired skill, yet the most effective strategies go against the norms (counterintuitive).

# Claims that are made
Learning is deeper and more durable when it takes effort.
We are really bad at recognizing whether we're learning effectively or not. When it becomes harder and it does not feel productive, we may turn to other strategies that do not provide any merits, or temporary ones, that feel more fruitful.
Rereading text and massed practice are the preferred methods of learning by most learners, but they are also among the least productive. This means just repetition or rote memorization as we say it. It may be useful before you are taking a test, but for mastery or durability, its a no.
Retrieval practice is a more effective strategy. Flashcards are an example of this type of learning, it strengthens the memory and interrupts forgetting. A single, simple quiz after we finish a lesson or a lecture is better than merely rereading the lecture notes. This is related to neuroplasticity, when the brain neural pathways strengthen, and the memory is reinforced.
Space out practice may make you a little bit rusty in between sessions, or you interleave the practice of two or more subjects, retrieval may become harder and feel less productive, but this is the opposite, as it actually enables long lasting learning and allows you to be more versatile in future application.
Try to solve a problem before checking the solution, resist the urge, its much better this way even if errors are made.
Do not limit yourself to any form of learning, empirical evidence does not support the existence of learning styles. Go wide, utilize everything you have at your disposal.
When you become adept at analyzing the underlying rules to differentiate between types of problems, you will become more successful at picking the correct solutions for unfamiliar situations. This can be acquired through varied practice and interleaved practice.
As humans, we can make illusions that hijack our judgement of what we know and what we do. Testing helps calibrate this.
All new learning requires us to have a foundation of prior knowledge about the topic.

A method called Zettelkasten can exemplify the key ideas extraction from new material and organizing them and connecting them into a master mental model will show an advantage in learning complex mastery.
