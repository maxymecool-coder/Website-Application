# Different flavors of depression
**Details**
Depression is not a single, uniform illness but a family of related disorders with distinct biological profiles. The existence of these different "flavors" of depression underscores that it is a biological phenomenon, not a singular emotional state. Each subtype has a different presentation and likely a different underlying neurochemical signature.

Key subtypes include:

- **Reactive vs. Endogenous Depression:** This is a classic distinction. **Reactive depression** is triggered by a major external stressor or negative life event. In contrast, **endogenous depression** appears to arise from internal factors, without an obvious external cause. After several reactive episodes, the brain can become "kindled," and the depression can begin to run on its own endogenous rhythm.
    
- **Atypical Depression:** This subtype is dominated by vegetative symptoms, particularly **psychomotor retardation** and a feeling of "leaden paralysis." The intense exhaustion and physical slowing are more prominent than the anhedonia or rumination. Sapolsky notes its biochemical similarities to chronic fatigue syndrome.
    
- **Psychotic Depression:** A severe form where the individual experiences delusions and thought disorder. These delusions are not random but are typically extensions of the depressive mood, often centered on extreme, irrational guilt. The case of Andrea Yates, who believed she was saving her children from damnation, is a tragic example.
    
- **Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD):** A rhythmic depression where episodes are tied to a specific time of year, most commonly the winter months. A person with SAD can be perfectly fine in the summer but fall into a major depression each winter, regardless of external circumstances. This is strongly linked to biology, specifically light exposure and the hormone melatonin.
    

The fact that a person can cope with a major stressor in July but become incapacitated by an internal rhythm in January demonstrates that depression is a collection of distinct biological diseases.
