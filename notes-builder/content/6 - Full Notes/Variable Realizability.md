# Fallacy of the Identity Theory
**Details**
Following the rejection of Behaviorism, philosophers briefly turned to **Identity Theory** (or Physicalism), which claimed that mental states are strictly identical to specific physical brain states (e.g., "Pain _is_ C-fibers firing"). This view relies on scientific identities, arguing that just as "Lightning _is_ electrical discharge" (regardless of how it looks), pain _is_ a specific biological event. However, Hilary Putnam dismantled this view with the argument of **Multiple Realizability**.

Putnam pointed out that creatures with vastly different physiologies—such as octopuses (which have very different evolutionary histories and neural structures) or hypothetical silicon-based Martians—can clearly experience mental states like pain or hunger. If Identity Theory were true, and pain _were_ identical to "C-fibers firing," then an octopus without C-fibers _could not_ be in pain. Because it is absurd to deny pain to these creatures simply based on their specific chemistry (a bias Putnam calls "speciesism"), Identity Theory must be false. Mental states are "variably realizable," meaning the exact same mental state can be implemented by completely different physical substances.
