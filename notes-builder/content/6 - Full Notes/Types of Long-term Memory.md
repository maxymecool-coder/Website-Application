# Types of Long-term Memory
**Details**
Long-term memory is not a single, monolithic entity but is broadly divided into two fundamental categories: declarative and non-declarative memory. The distinction hinges on conscious awareness.

**Declarative memory** encompasses all the information that we can consciously recall and "declare." It is further subdivided into two types. **Episodic memory** is autobiographical, consisting of personal events and experiences tied to specific times and places, such as recalling your high school graduation or where you were during a major world event. **Semantic memory**, on the other hand, consists of general knowledge and facts about the world, like knowing the capital of Indiana or the function of a laser pointer. These memories are not tied to personal experience.

In contrast, **non-declarative memory** (or procedural memory) operates unconsciously. It includes motor skills like playing tennis or riding a bike, which are performed automatically without conscious thought about the individual steps. This category also includes priming, where prior exposure to a stimulus influences a later response without conscious awareness, and classical conditioning, like Pavlov's dogs salivating at the ring of a bell. These two systems of memory are anatomically distinct and can be affected differently by brain injury, explaining why a person with severe amnesia might forget their own life story but still retain the ability to play the piano flawlessly.
