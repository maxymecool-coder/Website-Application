# Omega-3s and the Crucial Distinction Between Modulation and Mediation
**Details**
Omega-3 fatty acids, particularly EPA and DHA, are well-known for their broad health benefits, and they also play a significant role in brain function related to focus and mood. For ADHD, studies show that their effect is modest but significant. The data point to a key threshold of consuming over **300mg of DHA** per day, which is typically achieved when taking a higher dose of fish oil (over 1g of total EPA+DHA) for its mood-elevating effects. This supplementation can help individuals function well on lower doses of medication and, in some cases, may help eliminate the need for it entirely.

This highlights a critical concept in biology: the difference between **mediation** and **modulation**. A process is _mediated_ by a substance that directly causes it to happen; for instance, dopamine directly mediates motivation and focus. In contrast, a process is _modulated_ by a substance that influences or adjusts the primary process. Omega-3s, like proper sleep or a healthy diet, are powerful _modulators_. They don't directly cause focus, but they create a biological environment where the brain's focus circuits can function more efficiently. They might make dopamine more available or help receptors work better. Understanding this distinction is vital for setting realistic expectations for treatments. While a drug like Adderall _mediates_ focus, supplements and lifestyle changes _modulate_ it, playing a crucial supportive role in the overall treatment plan.
