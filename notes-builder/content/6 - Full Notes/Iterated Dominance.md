# Iterated Dominance
**Details**
In a game about choosing the number that is two-thirds of the class average, a natural first thought is, “Since it's random, the average would be about 50, so pick 33.” Now here’s the problem: you’re not assuming that the others are trying to win as well… and if they picked 33, well, now 22 is the two-thirds number.

Iterated deletion of dominated strategies makes this rigorous. Each round of deletion needs one more layer of knowledge:

- Above 67. Even if everyone picked 100, the target would be 66⅔, so every number above 67 is weakly dominated by 67. Ruling those out needs only your own rationality, meaning you're trying to maximize your payoff.
    
- 46–67. With 68–100 gone, the target can't go above about 45, so 46–67 are now dominated. They weren't dominated in the original game, only after the first round. Ruling them out also needs you to know that the others are rational (Polak's "in shoes" step).
    
- 31–45. This slice needs you to know that they know it too ("in shoes in shoes").
    

Getting all the way to 1 would need common knowledge of rationality: everyone is rational, they know it, so everyone knows it, and so on.

So, depending on the population, the number might be much lower than one might think because of the sophistication of the people around them.
