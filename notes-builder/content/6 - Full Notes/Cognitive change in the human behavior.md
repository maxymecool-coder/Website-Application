# Cognitive change in the human behavior
**Details**
The cognitive method can be applied for any patient, but certainly it will work most to the people who have the ability to introspect and reflect about their own thoughts. As this method to therapy is just an extension and refinement of what people have been doing to various degrees since the earliest stages of their intellectual development. They have always been making, building up hypotheses then testing, weeding out, which then will get sorted into the skills that they unconsciously use.

This type of function is actually analogous to the formation of speech in which how the rules of pronunciation and grammatical construction are applied without us consciously thinking about the rules. When learning a new language or having problem speaking a language which one has already supposedly "mastered" they must focus on forming the sentence by seeing the rules and such. Same goes for cognitive therapy, one must focus on the rules which he is applying to make false judgements, and in so he will find that such rule is incorrect to change it.

As this can easily become a habit that is hard to get rid of (as the patient is not aware of it), we must take several steps to correct it. Firstly, we have to make him aware of it, secondly, he needs to recognize which thoughts are awry. Then he has to substitute such thoughts. Finally, he needs feedback to inform him if he was correct in changing it. The same kind of sequence is necessary in correcting faults like playing an instrument, or sports.

**Supporting Content**
To illustrate the process of cognitive change, let us take as a rather gross example a person who is afraid of all strangers. When we explore his reactions, we may find that he is operating under the rule, “All strangers are unfriendly or hostile.” In this case, the rule is wrong. On the other hand, he may realize that strangers vary, but he may not have learned to discriminate among friendly strangers, neutral strangers, and unfriendly strangers. In such a case, his trouble is in applying the rule, that is, in converting the available information in a given situation into an appropriate judgment. 

It is obvious that not all people who think erroneously need or want to get their thinking straightened out. When a person’s erroneous ideation disrupts his life or makes him feel miserable, then he becomes a candidate for some form of help.
