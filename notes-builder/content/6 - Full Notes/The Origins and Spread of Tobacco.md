# The Origins and Spread of Tobacco
**Details**
Tobacco is a member of the _Solanaceae_ (nightshade) family, sharing lineage with both potent poisons and common food crops. Whilst there are over 50 species of Nicotiana, the main crop that we cultivate and use is Nicotiana tabacum.

Following Colombus's arrival, tobacco spread the world with unprecedented speed. Within a century, it has spread from mere rituals to sailors, and then the world. Figures like Jean Nicot, for whom the genus and the molecule nicotine are named, popularized the plant in French courts as a medicine for migraines.

For the United States, this plant can be viewed as its parent plant. It was the cash crop par excellence for the English colonies, letting the early settlements have enough money to settle. Due to this, King James I of England hated the plant due to its economic and political dynamics, but even he declared it a royal monopoly.
