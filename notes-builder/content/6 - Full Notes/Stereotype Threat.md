# Stereotype Threat
**Details**
Stereotyping doesn't just have social effects, it also has cognitive effects that can impair performance on tasks relative to stereotypes. Claude Steele demonstrated this through an observational study pertaining to SAT scores between races. The mechanism is quite elegant, when Black and White students were told a test measured intelligence, Black students' performance dropped dramatically. Plotted against a control group where they were told it was just some piloting items, no racial gap appeared. The mechanism operates through distraction and anxiety: fearing that one's performance might confirm the group stereotype consumes working memory resources. This mechanism is entirely culture-dependent as the relevant stereotypes vary by society. But the implication is sound: if everyone believes that the SAT measures intelligence, and there exists a stereotype linking group memberships to said intelligence, the act of taking the test under those beliefs may bring systematic disadvantages to the stereotyped group, creating the gap it describes.
