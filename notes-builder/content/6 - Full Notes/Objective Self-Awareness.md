# Objective Self-Awareness
**Details**
Proposed by Robert Wicklund, this theory suggests that whenever we are thinking inward, we are inherently evaluating ourselves as a social object meant to be judged by others. This can be exemplified by the actor-observer effect, which constitutes that people usually judge themselves depending on external situations, but when rewatching footage of themselves they instead treat themselves as something to be scrutinized personally and emotionally. Environmental triggers, such as sitting in front of a mirror or hearing a recording of one's own heartbeat, force this objective perspective, activating the generalized other and compelling the individual to evaluate themselves against societal standards (so its not just rewatching footage but perhaps whatever increases self-awareness).

This comparison usually creates extensive aversion and anxiety, which people adapt with conformity or escape. On the Apollonian side of things, we will conform to morals and society, whilst individuals cannot conform to the standard, they will actively seek Dionysian escape from the self. This explains why people generally report higher self-esteem when engrossed in a television show rather than looking in a mirror; the television absorbs their attention outward, shutting down the reflective, critical voice of the generalized other and providing profound psychological relief.
