# Theories
**Details**
Kelly proposed that accurate attribution requires observing behavior across multiple instances, tracking three types: distinctiveness, consensus, and consistency. Basically they are trying to draw the best inference by eliminating other factors that may affect said inference. This is quite actually impractical and useless as no one does this, no one ever tries to track and follow somebody through their day to understand how they react in situations and then infer who they are from there. Furthermore, I believe that it is also a randomized, perhaps luck-based method, as a person can have a particular day or week that they just look like something they are not. Another theory addresses this gap - Jone’s Correspondent Inference Theory. It addresses this by arguing that we can also infer from situations, it is quite simple to understand: Behavior = Disposition + Situation. The critical insight here is that we already have situational norms so we can accurately understand who someone is, and it only works for situations that are justified by counternormative behavior.
