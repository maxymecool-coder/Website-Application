# The Temporal Nature of Depression
**Details**
Depression is highly defined by its temporal nature, meaning its trait to reoccur in another period of the person's life, often illustrated by the Kupfer's curve or the "Five R's": Response, Remission, Relapse, Recovery, and Recurrence. Depression can occur within 6 to 9 months and when treated with medication, the acute response (feeling 50% better) in the first few weeks and remission makes the patient feel like they are cured, even though that's not what actually happened.

And so antidepressants may have to be used for a long period of time until recovery actually happens, but it does not lead to a full recovery as it does not teach the person how to deal with the aftermath, causing them to fall into depression once more.

**Supporting Content**
The "Kindling Hypothesis" is one that states how depression may destroy the brain by resetting its serotonin receptors or shrink the hippocampus. Through this, they can effectively lower the threshold for when the person experiences depression. It comes to a point where people can experience it out of no where without any environmental factors pushing them to, which is why therapy and intervention is recommended after a third episode.
