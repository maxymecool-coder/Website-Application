# The Difference of the 19th Century Philosophy
**Details**
The general model for philosophy is one of philosophers presenting their arguments to the public and having said arguments be challenged and fought over by other philosophers again and again-Kant reads Hume, Hegel reads Kant, and so on. Raymond Geuss argues that this model fails when applied to the 19th century of Central Europe. Because of the political revolutions happening at the time, philosophers were no longer responding to each other in dialogue but rather to the evens that were happening at that time, like the French Revolution. And so the period was split into two distinct periods where different ideologies dominated—the era of Idealism (ending in 1848) and the era of Neo-Kantianism (beginning around 1890)—separated by a chaotic "interregnum" where the philosophical chain was broken.
