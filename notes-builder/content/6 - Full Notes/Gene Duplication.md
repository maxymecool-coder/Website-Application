# Gene Duplication
**Details**
In the process of evolution, an entire gene or a large stretch of DNA can be duplicated. This creates redundancy, which allows for evolutionary experimentation. One copy of the gene can continue to perform its essential, original function, while the second copy is can mutate and change itself and combine with other copies whilst it does not ruin any function in the organism. Over time, this second copy can evolve a completely new function. This helps explain how complex features (the "what good is half an eye?" problem) can evolve, as the necessary components can be built and refined in a duplicated gene without breaking the existing system (Basically people who opposed the theory of evolution thought that since the body could not just accept half an eye because it is useless, evolution just could not just happen).
