# The law of the rules of the psyche
**Details**
As mentioned before, there exists a set of rules within a person to help him decipher and evaluate his experiences so that he can act accordingly to regulate his own behavior and others as well. The nature of these rules is that they are applied unconsciously. He completely just screens selectively, integrates, and sorts the flow of stimuli to form his responses. But this process is far from perfect.

When someone has not have enough experiences and such to prepare for a situation, they might infer incorrect meanings from the other person's behavior; their underlying intentions, attitudes, their probable future towards him.

In anxiety, the rules are generally conditional: "If something occurs, it will probably have adverse results." Hence, when it does, there is still a possibility of an innocuous outcome. In contrast, the rules in depression are absolute and unconditional: "My present deficiencies mean I shall always be a failure."

In phobias, the rules are also conditional; which applies to situations the patient is successfully able to avoid. Though similar to anxiety in which the reactions are high probability, in phobia it can be fortified when a helpful person is with them.

In depression, the rules as formulated derive negative meanings and negative predictions from a present or past circumstance. There is no 'escape clause' as in anxiety and phobias.

In manic conditions, the content of the assumptions is opposite to that in depression. The rules are framed in such a way to exaggerate the gain and elevate self-esteem: "When people look at me, they admire me."

In conclusion, when we question a patient about his ideas, he generally does not volunteer the rule that shapes his interpretation of events. Instead, he states his conclusion. We then have to work back to derive the rule, sometimes the patient is able to do this without difficulty.
