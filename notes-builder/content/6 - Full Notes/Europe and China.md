# Europe and China
**Details**
Revisionist historians, most notably those associated with the "California School" such as Kenneth Pomeranz and R. Bin Wong, fiercely challenge the notion of an inherent Western miracle or deep-seated European exceptionalism. Historians actually argued that these colonies could be equal to each other economically and technologically, and that they only really diverged in the 19th century onward. Europe with England’s abundance of coal and the massive influx of wealth and resources extracted from European overseas colonies. From this perspective, modernity is less a profound, localized structural shift and more a toolkit of surface-level economic and technological changes that spread across a globally level playing field.

England’s role in all of this is actually quite profound, it isn’t coincidental that it is perhaps marked as the origin of the modern world. It did not have strong academic prowess or innovative power as other nations combined but it was the main distributor. It would bring back inventions from certain areas of the world and formalize and distribute it to other nations, consequently allowing everyone to grow alongside.
