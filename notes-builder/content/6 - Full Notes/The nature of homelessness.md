# The nature of homelessness
**Details**
Homelessness can be understood as an acute form of **anomie**, as defined by Émile Durkheim. It represents a state where the social and economic regulations that once provided a stable path to housing and self-sufficiency have broken down for a segment of the population. This breakdown is not an individual failing but a result of rapid societal changes—such as deindustrialization, the erosion of social safety nets, and escalating housing costs—that have outpaced the ability of many to adapt. Left without a clear or legitimate means to secure their place, these individuals are cast into a state of normlessness.

This condition is exacerbated in a modern context, which Guy Debord termed **"The Spectacle."** A society oriented around consumption and appearance actively marginalizes those who cannot participate. The homeless are rendered invisible or are stigmatized because they do not fulfill the roles of producer or consumer that the Spectacle demands. This severs the bonds of social solidarity, replacing them with indifference. As Durkheim's theory predicts, this state of anomie places individuals at a significantly higher risk of suicide. Therefore, homelessness is a stark indicator of a society that fails to adequately regulate its economic life and integrate its most vulnerable members.
