# Gender difference in psychopathology
**Details**
There is a striking developmental reversal in the gender ratio of psychopathology. In the first decade of life, boys are significantly more vulnerable, acting as "canaries in the coal mine" for neurodevelopmental disorders like Autism, ADHD, and early-onset Conduct Disorder. Being male is a risk factor during this period, potentially due to the vulnerability of the Y chromosome or slower maturation. However, in the second decade of life, the pattern flips. Starting around puberty, which occurs earlier for girls, females show a surge in internalizing disorders. By age 15-16, rates of depression, anxiety, and eating disorders skyrocket in girls, becoming 2-3 times higher than in boys. Thus, being female shifts from a protective factor in childhood (where girls are more verbal and socially connected) to a significant risk factor in adolescence.
