# Micromutations
**Details**
Genes are basically a sequence of letters, and these micromutations are the singular changes of the letters in the sequence. There are three primary types of micromutations, each with different consequences for the resulting protein:

1. **Point Mutation:** A single DNA letter is changed to another.
    
    - **Neutral:** The change results in a triplet that codes for the _same_ amino acid (due to redundancy in the code). The protein is unchanged.
        
    - **Minor Consequence:** Codes for a _different but similar_ amino acid. The protein's shape and function are only slightly altered.
        
    - **Major Consequence:** Codes for a _very different_ amino acid, dramatically changing the protein's shape and function.
        
2. **Deletion:** A single DNA letter is lost. This causes a **frameshift**, where the three-letter reading frame is shifted for the rest of the gene. The result is a completely garbled sequence of amino acids and a non-functional protein.
    
3. **Insertion:** A single DNA letter is added or duplicated. This also causes a **frameshift**, with the same catastrophic result as a deletion.
    

The key takeaway is that micromutations typically alter the _efficacy_ of a single protein, making it work slightly better, slightly worse, or not at all. And this is the basis for gradualism, the slight incremental changes of genes for it to be better.
