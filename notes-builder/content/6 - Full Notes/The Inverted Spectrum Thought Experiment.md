# The Inverted Spectrum Thought Experiment
**Details**
One day you wake up and realize that everything is of a different color from previously before you were asleep, but even weirder than that, everyone else's sense of color seemed to remain the same. So how would you respond to this ? Continue responding and talking to the others as if nothing changed so that you get used to calling everything by their "perceived" color ? Then would you "forget" what the color is from your perspective ?

The question is, do YOU still see the same qualia, the same difference from others ? Philosophers argue that if this is true, then Functionalism is false, because different processes has yielded different results (they should have been the same).

**Supporting Content**
- This does not matter though, as the function is the same, identifying color, regardless of the result (counter-argument from Functionalism ?). Our mental states could be the same even if we get different experience. But nonetheless, the point is the same, the experience/qualitative aspect is missed in Functionalism. 

- **Relative vs. Absolute:** We can compare relative states (e.g., "I see this as larger than that"), but absolute comparisons between minds might be meaningless. It is like saying "My desire for a cookie is absolutely stronger than your desire for world peace." We can only rank preferences relative to each other, not on an absolute scale between people.
    
- **Asymmetry:** Colors have emotional associations (red is "hot" or "angry"). If you swapped red and blue qualia, you would likely feel "cold" when looking at fire, which would change your functional role (behavior). A perfect inversion that preserves all function might be impossible because color space is not perfectly symmetrical.
