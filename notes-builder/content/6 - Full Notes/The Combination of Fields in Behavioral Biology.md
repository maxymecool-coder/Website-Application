# The Combination of Fields in Behavioral Biology
**Details**
The study of behavior is not one field but at least three warring tribes, each with its own language, tools, and definition of truth. The **evolutionary biologists** tell grand stories of selection and adaptation. The **molecular geneticists** hunt for the precise mutations in the DNA. And the **behavior geneticists** look for patterns of inheritance in families and twins. From within one camp, the others look naive or misguided. The molecular biologist scoffs at the evolutionary theorist's story, demanding to see the specific gene. The theorist finds the geneticist's focus on a single molecule laughably reductive. This isn't a sign that one field is "wrong." It's a demonstration of different levels of analysis. One discipline's satisfying answer—"culture affects biology"—is another's starting point: "Yes, but _how_? Which genes are turned on? What is the epigenetic mechanism?" The whole point is, all of this can exist with each other and none necessarily disproves the other, is just that they have to have a different view of it.
