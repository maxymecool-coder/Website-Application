# The Social Cognitive Perspective
**Details**
The Social Cognitive perspective marked a shift toward a more scientific and empirical approach to personality. It posits that personality is not just an internal force, but is shaped through a dynamic interaction between our thoughts (cognition), our environment (social context), and our own behaviors. A key element of this theory is that we learn by observing others.

**Albert Bandura and Modeling** Albert Bandura was a pioneer of this view, arguing that much of our personality is learned through **modeling**—observing and imitating the actions of others. His famous **Bobo doll experiment** provided powerful evidence for this concept.

- **The Experiment:** Children watched a video of an adult interacting with a large, inflatable "Bobo" doll. One group saw the adult act aggressively toward the doll—punching, kicking, and yelling at it—and then get rewarded. The other group saw the adult play non-aggressively.
    
- **The Results:** When the children were later allowed to play in a room with the Bobo doll and other toys, those who had witnessed the aggressive model were overwhelmingly more likely to attack the doll themselves. They not only mimicked the adult's specific actions but also devised new ways to be aggressive.
    

Bandura's work demonstrated that personality traits like aggression are not just innate but can be learned through social observation. We learn not only _how_ to behave but also _what_ behaviors are likely to be rewarded by watching the people around us.
