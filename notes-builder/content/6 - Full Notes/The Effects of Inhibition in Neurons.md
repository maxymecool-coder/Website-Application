# The Effects of Inhibition in Neurons
**Details**
As mentioned in [[Listening between Neurons]], the presynaptic neurons can be louder in numerous ways, one of them is by silencing the other neurons around it or sending a more distinct signal using inhibition. The first type of inhibition is **temporal inhibition**, a neuron can inhibit itself to create a sharp, precise end to its own signal, like a musician using silence for dramatic effect. Through **spatial inhibition** (or lateral inhibition), a neuron silences its immediate neighbors, effectively shouting, "Pay attention to me, not them!" This is not an abstract concept; you can see it in the Hermann grid illusion, where your brain creates illusory dark dots at the intersections because the neurons there are being heavily inhibited. This process of active ignoring is what allows our brain to separate signal from noise, to detect edges, and to construct a coherent reality out of a chaotic flood of sensory information.
