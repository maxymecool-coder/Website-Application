# The Brain in Depression
**Details**
Functional brain-imaging studies (fMRIs, MRIs,...) have shown that episodes of MDD are associated with many systems of the brain being affected in their usage. The four most studied structures in the brain are: the amygdala, the subgenual anterior cingulate, the hippocampus, and the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.

Beginning with the amygdala, it is the part of the brain that helps us to see how emotionally important something is, or how dangerous it is. It has been shown that in depression, this area of the brain functions more. Further studies have shown that this does not correlate with anything really, people even have this effect when they are not in depression, which made psychologists came to the conclusion that it is a genetic vulnerability. The sensitivity that these depressed people show when observing simply sad or angriness shows that their amygdala is also considering sadness as dangerous now.

The other parts mention are on the opposite end of the spectrum, they regulate emotions, and in depression, these areas' activities are decreased, combining that with the amygdala, and we can understand why people suffer from so many negative emotions.
