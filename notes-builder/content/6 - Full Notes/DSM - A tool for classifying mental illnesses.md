# DSM - A tool for classifying mental illnesses
**Details**
To create a standardized language for diagnosing mental health conditions, the American Psychiatric Association developed the **Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM)**. The DSM provides a common set of criteria and classifications, allowing clinicians and researchers to communicate more effectively about specific disorders.

The manual (specifically the fourth edition discussed in the lecture) organizes its diagnoses across five distinct categories, or **axes**, to provide a comprehensive picture of a client's condition:

- **Axis I: Clinical Disorders:** This is for the primary psychological disorders that are the main focus of treatment, such as depression, anxiety disorders, or ADHD. These are generally seen as conditions that can be treated or managed.
    
- **Axis II: Personality Disorders and Mental Retardation:** This axis is for long-lasting, pervasive conditions that are less responsive to treatment. This includes personality disorders (e.g., antisocial personality disorder) and intellectual disabilities.
    
- **Axis III: General Medical Conditions:** This includes any physical health problems that could be relevant to the client's mental health, such as a heart condition that might be affected by medication or a recent injury causing stress.
    
- **Axis IV: Psychosocial and Environmental Problems:** This axis documents social or environmental stressors that could be impacting the client's condition, such as a recent divorce, job loss, or lack of a social support system.
    
- **Axis V: Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF) Score:** This is a single score from 1 to 100 that provides an overall assessment of the person's ability to function in daily life. A high score (90-100) indicates superior functioning, while a very low score indicates severe impairment and potential danger to self or others.
