# The minds of children
**Details**
Usually children ask very magnificent questions. These things are of finding wisdom, and it mostly exists within our childhood, even if most of it stops there, too.

The child is a natural questioner. It is not the number of questions he asks but their character that distinguishes him from the adult. Adults do not lose the curiosity that seems to be a native human trait, but their curiosity deteriorates in quality. They want to know whether something is so, not why. But children’s questions are not limited to the sort that can be answered by an encyclopedia.

Why should we try to develop our minds like this despite being born with relatively the same brain ? Well, that is because somewhere along the line, we lost the ability to maintain our curiosity at such depth. This can be attributed to many things, we being scolded for asking such questions, rote learning of schools, and so many more. But this doesn’t destroy it, it only debases it, and so we can gain it back.

I am not asking you to think as children, but to be able to see as they do, wonder as they wonder, ask what they ask. We must see through the complexities of our adult lives to be able to ask questions of simple distinctions.

So we must be childlike in our questions but mature in our answers.
