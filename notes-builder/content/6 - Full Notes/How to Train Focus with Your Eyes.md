# How to Train Focus with Your Eyes
**Details**
The simple act of blinking is profoundly connected to our state of attention and our perception of time, all of which is regulated by dopamine. Each eye blink serves as a neurological "reset," segmenting the continuous flow of our experience into discrete moments, much like the shutter of a camera. The rate at which we blink is a direct reflection of our internal dopamine levels. When dopamine is high, we are aroused and focused, and our blink rate decreases. This causes our brain to process more "frames" of information per second, making time feel as if it's slowing down. Conversely, when dopamine is low, as in ADHD, our blink rate increases, and our perception of time speeds up, causing us to underestimate how long things take.

This mechanical link provides a direct, behavioral tool for training focus. Studies show that a practice called **fixation-focused training** can significantly improve attention in both children and adults. The exercise is simple: you fix your visual attention on a single object for a short period (30-60 seconds) and consciously try to inhibit the desire to blink. This act of overriding the impulse to blink and maintaining a steady gaze directly engages and strengthens the neural circuits that control both the physical "shutters" of your eyes and the mental "aperture" of your attention. It's a way of using a physical action to directly train the cognitive skill of sustained focus.
