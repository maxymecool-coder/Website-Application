# The Basic Structure as the Primary Subject of Justice
**Details**
Concerning the third fundamental intuitive idea in justice as fairness is the notion of basic structure. The question is simple: what subjects should we apply the idea of society as a fair system of cooperation on ? The answer Rawls come to is in the way that the main political, legal, and social institutions hang together into one system and determine the division of benefits from cooperation. This includes the constitution, the judiciary, the legal system, the forms of property permitted (including private property in means of production), and the organization of the economy.

There are some limits to this though. First, the principles of justice discussed in TJ do not apply directly to particular institutions like churches or universities. Though they operate within the framework, they do not actually fulfill the requirements of the principle of toleration, which prevents them to use state power to engage politically. Second, the inquiry does not apply directly to the law of nations or relations between states. If the view works for the basic structure, one might later attempt to extend it (perhaps imagining a well-ordered international society composed of well-ordered constitutional states), but that extension is a separate project.

Rawls offers two reasons for this focus. Practically, it is to ensure that we have a view for the basic structure for it would be significant regardless of its extension. The deeper reason is to reemphasize how people's identities are built upon this basic structure.
