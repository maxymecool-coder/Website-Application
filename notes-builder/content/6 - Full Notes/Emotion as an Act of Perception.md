# Emotion as an Act of Perception
**Details**
Understanding another person's emotional state is fundamentally an act of perception; we observe their behavior and infer their internal world. More surprisingly, understanding our _own_ emotions is also an act of self-perception that requires interpretation. This ability is not innate but develops over time. A young child, for example, may not correctly perceive their own bodily signals (like the need to use the bathroom), while their parents can perceive it clearly from external cues. This leads to a fascinating developmental shift: before early adolescence, a majority of children will assert that their parents know their inner emotional states better than they do themselves. It is only as they mature that they come to believe they are the primary authority on their own feelings. This process highlights that even our most intimate feelings are not simply "had," but are actively perceived and understood through a developing cognitive lens.
