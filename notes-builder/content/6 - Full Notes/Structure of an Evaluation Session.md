# Structure of an Evaluation Session
**Details**
Firstly, you should try to set the agenda and tell the patient what is going to happen, walk them through the generals of it, this may include: conducting the psychosocial assessment, set goals, relate to the treatment plan, set an Action Plan, set expectations for treatment, and then summarize everything.

Something you can start with is to ask them how they spend their day, understand their perspective and then work out from there. For example, if they are already doing well taking care of themselves, validate and encourage them to do further.

At times, there would be hopelessness and skepticism due to many reasons, like a previous therapy not working. When that happens, try to validate, understand, encourage them for that matter. Try to discredit their skepticism by proving your care and competence, also understand why it failed for the previous therapist and resolve it with them. When the period does come to an end, ask them important stuff you might have missed or haven’t got a chance to talk about.

Action Plan example:

“ACTION PLAN May 6
Put this Action Plan next to the coffee maker and read it every morning and again later in the day.

Therapy Notes: When I start to feel more depressed, remind myself that the therapy plan makes sense. With Judy’s help, I’ll be working toward goals every week, step by step. I’ll learn how to evaluate my thinking, which may be 100% true, or 0% true, or somewhere in the middle. The way I’ll get better is by making small changes in my thinking and behavior every day.
Take grandchildren out for ice cream.
Give myself credit for doing all of the above and for doing anything that’s even a little difficult—because I do it anyway.”
