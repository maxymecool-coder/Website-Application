# Targeting Painful Effects
**Details**
This is extremely intense, and so it is important to alleviate the patient's sadness and other affective disturbances as soon as possible. Depressed patients frequently report that none of their usual sources brings them pleasure anymore.

There are a variety of techniques we can use to solve this. Firstly, it is possible to make the patient feel sorry him self, be it crying or something else. This will make him thaw out of his frozen effect, it will make them feel more alive, and more like a "whole person" as Beck says. (Of course, some may feel worse after something like this, so it is advised to be used with caution.) The patient will feel sympathy for themselves in this,. and so his cognitive set will change from rejecting or derogatory to sympathetic.

Other methods are alike this, in which they also stimulate the patient and change them in a way so that they become more sympathetic for themselves.

**Supporting Content**
Feeling sorry for oneself can be analogized to the kind of empathy one experiences with characters who are portrayed as wronged or unfortunate in a movie. The people in the audience become dewy-eyed or sob because they care about the unfortunate character. Similarly, by feeling genuine pity for themselves, depressed patients are likely to be less self-critical (Efran, 1973). The process can be accelerated by a number of techniques. Telling the patient a story of another unfortunate person with a similar problem and with whom the patient can identify frequently evokes sympathy in the patient. Feeling sympathy for another person in the same situation helps the patient to regard himself sympathetically. Dramatic techniques such as role-playing, in which the therapist assumes the role of his depressed patient, may also help the patient to change his cognitive set from critical to sympathetic.
