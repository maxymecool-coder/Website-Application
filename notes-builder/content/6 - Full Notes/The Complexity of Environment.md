# The Complexity of Environment
**Details**
While it's easy to think of the "environment" as a single entity, its influence is far more nuanced. Siblings growing up in the same family do not experience the exact same world. To account for this, scholars distinguish between **shared** and **non-shared** environments. A shared environment includes all the factors that siblings have in common as a result of growing up in the same family, which theoretically should make them more similar to one another. This includes things like the family's socioeconomic status, the house and neighborhood they live in, their parents' child-rearing style, family activities and diet, and the language and culture of the household.

However, the **non-shared environment** consists of all the unique experiences that individuals living together do not share, and these factors are what cause them to become dissimilar. This can include having different friends or teachers, participating in different activities outside the home, or even experiencing unique family events that affect one sibling differently than another. Counterintuitively, research consistently shows that these non-shared environmental factors tend to have a _more significant_ influence on shaping an individual's personality and traits than the shared factors. This finding complicates the simple nature-nurture equation, highlighting that even within the same family, individual experience is paramount.
