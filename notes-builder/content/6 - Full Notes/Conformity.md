# Conformity
**Details**
**Conformity** is the act of altering one’s behavior or opinions to match those of other people or to align with social expectations. While many people believe they are non-conformists, the pressure to go along with the group is a powerful and often underestimated social force.

**Asch's Classic Experiment** Psychologist Solomon Asch conducted a famous experiment to see if people would conform to a majority view, even if it was obviously incorrect.

- **The Setup:** A participant was placed in a group with several other people (who were secretly confederates of the experimenter). The group was shown a standard line and then asked to identify which of three comparison lines was the same length. The task was simple, and the correct answer was clear.
    
- **The Deception:** On 12 out of 18 trials, the confederates were instructed to unanimously give the same wrong answer. The real participant was positioned to answer only after hearing most of the others give their incorrect responses.
    
- **The Surprising Results:** Asch found that the pressure to conform was remarkably strong. 74% of participants conformed to the incorrect majority at least once. On average, participants conformed on about one-third (33%) of the trials where the group was wrong. When interviewed later, some participants said they conformed because they thought the group must be right, while others admitted they knew the group was wrong but went along out of fear of ridicule.
