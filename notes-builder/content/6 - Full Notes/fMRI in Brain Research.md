# fMRI in Brain Research
**Details**
- **Methodology**: Measures brain activity indirectly by detecting changes in blood flow and oxygenation. Active neurons consume more oxygen, increasing the ratio of deoxygenated to oxygenated hemoglobin. The fMRI machine detects the magnetic properties of deoxygenated hemoglobin, creating a map of brain activity known as the BOLD (Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent) signal.
    
- **Advantages**: Good spatial resolution, allowing for precise localization of brain activity. Non-invasive, as it does not use radiation or require injections. Relatively affordable and widely accessible in research settings.
    
- **Disadvantages**: Poor temporal resolution, with a 4-6 second lag between neural activity and its detection, making it unsuitable for studying fast processes. Provides only an indirect, correlational measure of brain activity, not causation. Interpretation is complex due to individual differences, and the BOLD signal can be affected by age, health, and medication.
