# Price Changes and Different Goods
**Details**
We have already established how the price change may affect demand through the mechanisms of substitution effect and the income effect. The substitution effect comes from the change in relative prices. If cookies become more expensive relative to pizza, cookies are now a worse deal, so the consumer substitutes away from cookies and toward pizza. This effect always moves opposite the price change: when a good becomes more expensive, substitution pushes demand down; when it becomes cheaper, substitution pushes demand up.

The income is on the purchasing power. If the price of cookies rises while income stays the same, the consumer is effectively poorer because the same money buys a smaller opportunity set. For normal goods, this reinforces the substitution effect. Cookies are both relatively less attractive and less affordable, so demand falls. For inferior goods, however, the income effect goes the other way. As an example, if potatoes price increases, you'd still need to purchase potatoes, but you cannot purchase as much steak... so what does one do ? They would have to abandon the steak money to use it for the potatoes.

**Supporting Content**
This creates the possibility of a Giffen good: an inferior good that has an income effect so powerful that it overwhelms the substitution effect, causing demand to rise when the price rises. But this effect can also be skewed by data, like how rich people leaving and not purchasing the inferior goods anymore may superficially increase the income effect.
