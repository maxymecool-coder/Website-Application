# Comparative Analysis of QuAIC vs Triage Risk Levels
**Details**
A primary test for a screening tool like the QuAIC is its ability to produce results consistent with those of a trained expert. The study found a "high degree of overlap between levels of concern determined by triage and levels of concern determined by the QuAIC". This finding, which speaks to the tool's concurrent validity, is the study's most direct piece of evidence supporting its use. The paper concludes that the QuAIC levels "closely matched the overall triage risk level, demonstrating a high degree of consistency with professional judgment".  

The specific data supporting this conclusion are presented in Table 3 of the source document and summarized below in Table 2 for clarity.

The data in Table 2 reveal a remarkable degree of alignment, particularly at the upper end of the risk spectrum where accuracy is most critical. For the "Highest" concern level, there is a perfect match, with both methods identifying exactly 5 cases (2.0%). For the "High" concern level, the numbers are nearly identical (82 cases for QuAIC vs. 84 for Triage Risk). This strong concordance indicates that the QuAIC, when used by non-clinicians, can effectively identify and flag high-priority cases in a manner that aligns with a more holistic professional assessment. The minor discrepancies in the "Moderate" and "Low" categories are also informative, likely reflecting the SPJ process in action, where the practitioner might use information from the warning signs checklist or other contextual factors to slightly adjust a final rating up or down from the QuAIC's initial output.
