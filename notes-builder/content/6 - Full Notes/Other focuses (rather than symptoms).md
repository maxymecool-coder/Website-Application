# Other focuses (rather than symptoms)
**Details**
Up until recent times, the main focus of therapy has always been by reducing symptoms, and the majority of therapists these day still focuses on that. This view first derived from the field of biomedicine, back when clinical psychology was created, and so it focuses on the symptoms. Different from biomedicine however, reducing symptoms does not mean the mental disorder will never bother the person again, relapse chances are high, proving the approach not necessarily effective. And so in recent times, as researchers have realized said fact, the field has diverted its ways to focusing on the mental wellbeing of the person, their connections, their mental capacity,... so that they can maintain their lives and know what to do after treating their mental illness.

With that, the focus has somewhat shifted to lifestyle factors, including access to natural environments, mindfulness practices, resources to allow individuals to work on themselves and build skills. More than just the above mentioned benefits, this approach even reduces stigma, widens accessibility,... For example, NBIs (Nature-based interventions) can range from full contact with natural environments, like forest bathing (Shinrin Yoku).
