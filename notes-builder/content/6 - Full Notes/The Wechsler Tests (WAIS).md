# The Wechsler Tests (WAIS)
**Details**
The **Wechsler intelligence scales** (WAIS for adults, WISC for children) are the most widely used IQ tests today. Like the Stanford-Binet, they produce an overall IQ score with a mean of 100.

Their defining feature is the emphasis on **subscores**, which provide a more detailed cognitive profile. The test is structured into four main indices:

1. **Verbal Comprehension Index:** Measures verbal reasoning and knowledge.
    
2. **Perceptual Reasoning Index:** Measures non-verbal, fluid reasoning.
    
3. **Working Memory Index:** Measures attention and short-term memory.
    
4. **Processing Speed Index:** Measures the speed and accuracy of simple visual scanning and discrimination.
    

This structure is clinically useful for identifying specific cognitive strengths and weaknesses, which can help diagnose learning disabilities or account for cultural factors affecting verbal scores.
