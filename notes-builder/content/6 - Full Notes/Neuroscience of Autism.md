# Neuroscience of Autism
**Details**
Autism is quite a heritable disease, genetic factors explaining a vast majority of variance in the condition. While severe autism is rare, a "broad autism phenotype" consisting of mild social awkwardness or rigidity can run in families with autism. A huge finding is that a subset of children with autism exhibit head circumferences significantly larger than norms. This macrocephaly is linked to a failure of synaptic pruning. Typically, the brain sheds half its neurons between ages one and two to become efficient; in autism, this pruning process may fail, leaving the brain with an overabundance of noisy, inefficient connections, which correlates with the clinical phenomenon of developmental regression often seen around that age.

Simon Baron-Cohen proposes the "Extreme Male Brain" theory, suggesting that autism represents an extreme version of "systemizing" behaviors-traits typically associated with males-such as logic, analysis, and order, at the expense of "empathizing." This aligns with the "Silicon Valley Syndrome" hypothesis, which posits that the mating of highly analytical, technically minded individuals may concentrate these genes, increasing the prevalence of autism. This may additionally explain why autism abundance hasn't decreased, because these traits may contribute to evolution.
