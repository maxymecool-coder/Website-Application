# Nothing else matters other than writing
**Details**
Studying is not preparing for independent research, it does not teach the student ways that he can come up with ideas from information that he researches but that the things that he is studying is his own research.

Since nobody really starts from scratch because they can think for themselves. Studying, when done properly, is research, because at the end of the day it is learning something to then present to others, opening it up to public scrutiny, and what the public cares about is the truth, not the writer, not the process, but the validity and relevancy of the writing. And so we come to the conclusion that the presentation and the production of knowledge are two sides of the same coin.

Having this mindset allows us to do everything differently, but not necessarily worse. Going to lectures suddenly has a reason to get an idea on the current state of the research, as it is the main source of information for that research. Because then one would not be focusing on what they "should learn" but how they can learn to get to the position when they can get open questions, as they are the only things worth writing about (because they are problems that are unanswered). 

Another side effect is increasing focus, as one has less time to get the information as he has to write more, he will soon learn to focus better to get the gist of the information as well as distinguishing, connecting ideas and so on with his previous knowledge. For example, when reading, they will read the piece of writing in a more engaged way as they understand that they will have to rewrite it in their own understanding and they cannot do so if they don't understand.
