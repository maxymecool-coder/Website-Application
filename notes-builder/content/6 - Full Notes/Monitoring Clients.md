# Monitoring Clients
**Details**
Now, we do not mean that you have to know their every move and mood everyday, but rather their emotional reactions throughout the session, like how you would naturally read their body language, choice of words, tone of voice. After that, you would want to intervene them by asking gently about it, make sure to reinforce them if they tell you about it.

Then you will conceptualize the problem and plan something to solve it, an action plan. Tell them if you're sure or not and such, roleplay with your supervisor and other therapists to be able to respond to them well. Asking questions about how the session went is also a strong suit in improving the relationship between the client and practitioner as well.

**Supporting Content**
You can also, at times and in certain situations, you can ignore their negative reaction if it doesn't exactly intervene in the therapy session.

When they show you clearly that they are dissatisfied or something has gone wrong, it is time to enter this position and try to evaluate if you are wrong or not. After understanding if you were wrong (or not) you can express empathy, seek additional information and then see if they agree with the thought. For example, a client is insecure with the relationship of them and their therapist, perhaps its because of a past belief they harbor about how people are. Because of this, as their therapist do not respond to one of their calls, the client regards the therapist as uncaring. Now, this is a clear cognitive distortion, and so the therapist must then prove and allow the client to recognize that such thought is false.

The same situation can happen to the client's other relationships so it is important to help them generalize that as well.
