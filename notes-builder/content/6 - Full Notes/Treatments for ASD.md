# Treatments for ASD
**Details**
Although the research for treatment in ASD has been quite difficult due to the sheer amount of time and effort required, there are still promising efforts in the treatment of ASD, mainly psychological. Early landmark studies, like Ivar Lovaas's intensive behavioral program in the 1980s, demonstrated that dramatic gains in IQ and functioning were possible by applying operant conditioning for over 40 hours a week. This established that intensive intervention could work, but the model's rigor was questioned as it wasn't a randomized controlled trial. This methodological challenge persists; a later meta-analysis confirmed that while intensive treatments show large positive effects, the quality of most studies remains low.

In response, research has evolved beyond sheer intensity towards more targeted and sustainable approaches. A key development is parent-led intervention, which leverages the family's constant presence to generalize gains. This led to innovations like **Pivotal Response Treatment (PRT)**. Instead of sequentially targeting individual problem behaviors, PRT focuses on core "pivotal" areas like a child's motivation and responsiveness, using natural reinforcers like play and praise. This not only proves effective but also improves the overall family dynamic. Furthermore, other interventions have successfully targeted foundational skills like joint attention (JA) and symbolic play (SP), where short, daily sessions have shown lasting improvements in expressive language. This suggests the frontier of ASD treatment lies in identifying these key leverage points rather than just maximizing therapeutic hours.
