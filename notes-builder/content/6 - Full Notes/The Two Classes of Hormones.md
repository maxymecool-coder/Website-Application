# The Two Classes of Hormones
**Details**
Basically these two classes of hormones that have different natures, characteristics, that's really their only differences. **Peptide Hormones** (like Insulin) are built from amino acids that are hydrophilic. This allows them to travel freely in the watery bloodstream, but it does not allow them to pass through the membrane, meaning their receptors will be on the surface, these hormones usually have a part in modifying proteins. In contrast, **steroid hormones** (like cortisol and testosterone) are built from cholesterol and are **hydrophobic** ("water-hating"). They need a chaperone protein to travel through the blood, but once they arrive at a target cell, they can slip right through the membrane. Their receptors are located _inside_ the cell, and their primary job is to march into the nucleus and directly change gene transcription. This makes their action slower to start but far more long-lasting.
