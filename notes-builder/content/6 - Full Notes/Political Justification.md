# Political Justification
**Details**
Rawlsian justice aims to develop a critical, normative conception of justice. It does not describe people as they are, but interprets them as representatives of free and equal citizens. Therefore, the arguments do not rely on predictive psychology, but on structurally embedded moral premises.

The central premise of this theory is that people have incommensurable and irreconcilable conceptions of the good, and so we cannot prove one conception clearly enough for it to secure universal public agreement. Consequently, it should be known that a political conception of justice cannot be based on a single comprehensive doctrine. Even if fulfilling these conditions, it would also need to be able to be recognized by the public and mutually accepted by all members of society. If a political system relies on keeping its foundational principles a secret from the masses to function—resembling the Marxist concept of ideological or "false consciousness"—it fails this essential test of rightness. Finally, the "Primary Goods" distributed in this framework are not merely things people psychologically desire. Instead, they are objective, fundamental needs derived directly from the conception of citizens as free and equal persons (which basically are reasons that they will be reasonable people).
