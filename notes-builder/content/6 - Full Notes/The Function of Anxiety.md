# The Function of Anxiety
**Details**
Although many scientists have tried to attribute a useful function to anxiety, Beck personally believed that it is not an important stimulus for preparing an individual against combatting a threat. This is due to us having no solid evidence that it is something essential for emergency responses or that it is even helpful. Take for example, an athlete, who can push himself and his body over the edge in a competition even when there is no risk involved in it, there is no danger in such situations, yet he is capable of emergency responses.

Moreover, it can be argued that anxiety may even get in the way of one's response to danger, as they may "freeze" in the face of a threat that they themselves cannot deal with or get out of, as said of the bridge worker who falls as a result of dizziness and trembling from anxiety.

One theory suggested by Leventhal (1969) proposed that a "parallel response paradigm" which outlines the sequence from events of the danger to the actions: external threat -> appraisal of it -> appropriate coping behavior. Which means that anxiety appears in the second phase, as the person identify if the threat is too much or not.

**Supporting Content**
This raises questions such as why do we not assign more crucial functions to other emotions such as sadness, embarrassment, or joy ? Even though they all are other faces of our own human experience.
