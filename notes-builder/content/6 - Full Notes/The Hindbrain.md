# The Hindbrain
**Details**
The hindbrain is the visible part of the brain that is at the back of it, connecting to the spine. At the bottom of it, there's the medulla, which lies above the spinal cord. It is crucial in controlling many basic biological functions like the cardiovascular and respiratory systems, determining the functions of it. Moreover, it also incorporates functions like swallowing, coughing and sneezing. It also helps us to maintain balance and orientation of ourself.

Above it is pons. This part consists of special regions that will integrate movements of, and sensations from, the facial muscles, tongues, eyes, and ears. Other regions of it may also control the level of attention and initiate sleep and dreams.

The cerebellum, a large, cauliflower-like part of the hindbrain, acts as a specialized computer for the body. It processes information from balance sensors and your muscles and joints to coordinate movement. Different parts of the cerebellum have specific jobs:

- One part controls **overall bodily balance**. Damage to this area results in a wide, staggering walk, which is why police check for this in suspected drunk drivers.
    
- Another part manages the **timing and precision of skilled movements**, like a tennis swing or playing the piano. Damage here can cause tremors and an inability to perform rapid, alternating motions.
    

Studies on cats with their hindbrain surgically separated from the rest of the brain show that the hindbrain alone can produce basic movements for standing or walking. However, the animal cannot coordinate these movements to perform a complete action and will collapse without support.

In short, an animal with only its hindbrain "can move, but it cannot act."
