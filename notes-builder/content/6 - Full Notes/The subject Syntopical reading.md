# The subject Syntopical reading
**Details**
The first requirement of this level of reading is to know the books that are relevant to a particular question that you are trying to answer. Next is to know which books should be read, which is greatly harder as how would you know if you haven’t read them ?

This makes us question what is the “subject” of syntopical reading, what is the question that we are reading to answer ? It may be easy to answer if the subject is a particular event in history, but almost in any other sphere, it just isn’t. Like in fiction, they do not communicate like an expository writer, and vice versa.

Suppose, for example, that you are interested in reading about the idea of love. Since the literature of love is vast, you would have relatively little difficulty in creating a bibliography of books to read. Suppose that you have done that, by asking advisors, by searching through the card catalogue of a good library, and by examining the bibliography in a good scholarly treatise on the subject. And suppose in addition that you have confined yourself to expository works, despite the undoubted interest of novelists and poets in the subject. (We will explain why it would be advisable to do this later.) You now begin to examine the books in your bibliography. What do you find?

Absolutely nothing, because anything can be labeled as love, like how one loves eating, a certain food, person, etc… but all those love are different even if they are related to “love”. And we haven’t even touched writers whose opinions on love are that it is merely a function of humans, biologically or cognitively rather than something more than that.

And so the conclusion comes back to the start, you cannot classify what you’ve been reading about (the very question you sought out to answer) unless you’ve read hundreds of works. But even when you do, you may regard half of them as being useless and off-topic !
