# Traumatic memory
**Details**
The nature of memories change and are constantly revised and updated. It is not base on reflections of reality but rather stories we tell to convey our personal take on such experiences. These memories are not integrated within the storages of our autobiographical memory, creating a dual system. Traumatic memories on the other hand are cut-off, they are frozen in time, barely comprehensible fragments. The goal of treatment would be association, which is making these elements of trauma into the ongoing narrative of life so that the brain can recognize the timelines of when these events happened and move on from it.

The accuracy of our memories largely depends on how meaningful it was and how emotional we felt about it at the time it happened. The key factor is our level of arousal. I mean things associated with people, smells, sounds, and places that will stay with us for a long time. Although usually the more adrenaline is pumped, the more precise our memories are. But it is only true until confronted with horror, especially “inescapable shock” that this system becomes overwhelmed and breaks down entirely, leading to PTSD.

The major differences between how people talked about normal memories and traumatic experiences is the fact that they are organized differently and reacted differently. Weddings, births, graduations were recalled as events with clear structures, and people usually said there had never been a period that they had completely forgotten any of these events. In contrast, the memories of traumatized patient were more disorganized; details were too clearly but the thing is the sequence and other vital details was completely fragmented.
