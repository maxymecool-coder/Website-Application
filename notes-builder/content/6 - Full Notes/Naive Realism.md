# Naive Realism
**Details**
As understood in social psychology, we usually interpret things subjectively from our point of view but think of it as the objective situation. This can then lead to Naive Realism, which states that we would then misjudge the difficulty and the state of the situation and everything alongside that. For example, in the [[Game Theory and the Prisoner's Dilemma]], observers of the experiment had a Reasonable Person Bias of themselves that they would have stopped at 150-volt when the person in the other room started showing reactions rather than be the 255-volt "killers" and considering those people as stupid, evil, etc., not realizing that they would have likely done the same in said situation (68%).

This extends to interpersonal situations as well, when someone who doesn't/hasn't gotten through the context of a situation, the person who has perceives that person as stupid or naive rather than attributing it to the situation.
