# Polyvagal Theory
**Details**
Neuroception, the term describing the capacity to evaluate one’s danger and safety in an environment. Traumatized patients usually have a faulty neuroception, the challenge is the find ways to reset this so that their survival mechanisms stop going against them. There is a spectrum of responses: focused, collapsed and frantic. Porge’s theory suggests that there are 3 fundamental physiological states. When we feel threatened, the first level is social engagement, we try to find help from others, this is the work of the “ventral vagal complex”. But if no one tries to help us, or we’re in danger, we revert to fight or flight, the second stage, fighting off, running to a safe place. And when that also stops working, we preserve ourselves by shutting down and using the least amount of energy as possible, also known as freeze or collapse, this is of the “dorsal vagal complex”.
