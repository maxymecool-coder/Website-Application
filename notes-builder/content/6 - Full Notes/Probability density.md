# Probability density
**Details**
Arguably the most difficult thing you can put on a histogram. Probability density is represented by the area of the graph, of each bar, so to say. It may sound simple and purely cosmetic, but it actually has quite the defect.

The defect: with relative frequency, a bar's height depends on how wide you made the bins. Take a graduate-income bin of $10–15k holding 30 of 200 students = 15%. Split it into $1k sub-bins and those same 30 students spread out as (say) 7.5%, 5%, 1.5%, 1%, 0% — still summing to 15%, but every bar is now far shorter. Keep subdividing and the bars shrink toward zero. So you cannot compare two histograms drawn with different bin widths, and you certainly cannot lay a smooth theoretical curve (which effectively has infinitely small bins) on top of relative-frequency bars.

To fix this, before dividing, just make sure equals the actual probability by separating the two variables before you divide them to get a clearer picture.
