# The SAT
**Details**
Within Victoria University's intricate framework for managing student safety, the Safety and Triage (SAT) team operates as a critical, high-level body for addressing the most serious behavioral concerns. The SAT team's work is distinct and highly specialized, functioning not as a primary investigative unit, but as a multi-disciplinary panel convened to make determinative judgments about student risk and recommend consequential sanctions.

The composition of the SAT team is one of its defining features, bringing together key stakeholders from across the university. It includes representatives from Human Resources, Occupational Health and Safety, Security, Legal Services, Student Counseling, and Student Misconduct, among others as needed, such as faculty or accommodation staff. This multi-disciplinary structure ensures that any student case referred to it is evaluated from a comprehensive range of professional perspectives—legal, clinical, safety, and academic. The team convenes on an ad-hoc basis, assembled specifically when a referral is made, indicating its role as a responsive, rather than a standing, committee.

Crucially, the text delineates what the SAT team does and, perhaps more importantly, what it does not do. Unlike similar "threat assessment teams" found in U.S. campuses, the SAT team at Victoria University does not conduct its own risk assessments or formulate threat management plans. Its function is more focused and adjudicative. Its primary purpose is to evaluate the evidence presented—including comprehensive reports from external threat assessors referred by the Safer Community Unit (SCU)—and determine whether a student meets the university's specific definition of a "high risk person."

The outcome of the SAT team's deliberation carries significant weight. Based on its assessment, the team makes direct recommendations to the university's leadership regarding appropriate management and sanctions. These are not minor disciplinary actions; they can include severe outcomes such as the suspension or complete exclusion of a student from the university. In essence, the SAT team serves as the university's ultimate internal tribunal for cases where student behavior is deemed to pose a significant threat, acting on expert information to enforce policies and protect the wider university community.
