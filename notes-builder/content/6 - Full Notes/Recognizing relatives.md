# Recognizing relatives
**Details**
In the realms of identifying who is similar to you, animals have two ways of going at this, either innate or imprinted. The innate mechanism includes a unique protein that is called the Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC). Every individual has a different MHC, and they let it out in pheromones like sweat or pee. The brain, then, has olfactory receptors that can recognize these scents. In fact, we all have these abilities to smell and identify MHC, but when giving birth, the olfactory part of our brains are actually being renovated by the body increasing hormones like prolactin and such through neurogenesis. The increase in oxytocin and vasopressin also makes the new system more sensitive to recognizing the baby's smell.

The other way of recognizing relative is imprinting. This is quite simple, if a child smells like my vaginal fluids, its mouth smells like my milk, etc. that would mean that it is my child. Same for the siblings, if the siblings smell like mom, then they are my relative. The innate part here is the learning process, the recognition is still imprinted.

There is actually a third method for doing this, and it is cognitive, and of course it is most prevalent in humans. We recognize who our mother is because she is holding us at birth or taking care of us and such, not because we have some innate ability or imprinted stuff. Same as how the mother recognizes the father, "I had sex with this dude in my ovulating stage."... God that sounds weird. Of course there also exists this system in primates although their abilities are quite weak from that of the humans.
