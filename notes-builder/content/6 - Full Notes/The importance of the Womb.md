# The importance of the Womb
**Details**
From the time that they are in the womb, fetuses are already keeping track of everything that is happening from their mother and indirectly from the environment (as the mother is reacting to it) and change adaptably to it. For example, the "Dutch Hunger Winter" provides a chilling case study. Fetuses whose mothers endured a sudden famine during WWII had their metabolisms permanently programmed for scarcity. When they grew up in a world of abundance, this "thrifty phenotype" led to soaring rates of obesity and diabetes. This was an environmental effect so powerful it was passed to the next generation, masquerading as a genetic legacy. This principle extends beyond nutrition. A mother's stress levels can sculpt the developing brain of her fetus, predisposing it to anxiety. A fetus can even learn to recognize the rhythm of a story like _The Cat in the Hat_ read aloud. To ignore the prenatal world is to ignore the first and perhaps most formative chapter of our lives.
