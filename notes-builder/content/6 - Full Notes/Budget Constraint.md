# Budget Constraint
**Details**
The practical implications of preferences lie in this variable as it is what breads scarcity and in turn makes people try to optimize for the best economical decision. A consumer may prefer many possible bundles of pizza slices and cookies, but only some of those bundles are affordable. The budget constraint is the line separating what the consumer can afford from what lies outside their resources.

The basic budget constraint says that income equals spending, here we are ignoring the possibility of saving and only focusing on the situation where the person spends every single cent of their income. Mathematically, we will mark income by I, cookies by C, pizza by S, and the price of cookies by PC, and the price of pizza slices by PS, then the consumer’s total spending is PC times C plus PS times S. It is also not an absurd approximation for many real households, because people with little savings often do live close to paycheck-to-paycheck, where income effectively bounds immediate consumption.

Graphically, the budget constraint has cookies on one axis and pizza slices on the other. The cookie intercept shows how many cookies the consumer could buy if they spent all income on cookies. The pizza intercept shows how many slices they could buy if they spent all income on pizza. The slope of the budget constraint is the negative price ratio, minus the price of cookies divided by the price of pizza. This slope expresses opportunity cost. If cookies cost two dollars and pizza slices cost four dollars, then each cookie costs half a slice of pizza. Buying one more cookie effectively means giving up half a pizza slice, because the same money cannot be spent twice. This is what Gruber calls the marginal rate of transformation: the rate at which the market allows the consumer to transform one good into another through spending decisions.

And so this is the other factor that relates to the [[Marginal Rate of Substitution]], the amount the market allows the person to trade these goods off each other.
