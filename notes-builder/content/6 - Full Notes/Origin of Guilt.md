# Origin of Guilt
**Details**
Nietzsche further challenges the assumption that "Guilt" is a fundamentally moral or religious concept. Instead, through etymological analysis, he asserts that it originates from the material concept of Debt, which originated from the relationship of the buyer and seller. Nietzsche posited that the most primordial, aboriginal human relationship is commercial exchange, which is an empirical mistake, referenced from David Graeber's book: Debt: The First 5000 Years, where people mostly engaged in unequal exchanges.

As mentioned in [[The Phenomenology of Bad Conscience]], we as humans are able to make deals and bind ourselves to the future, and this arises in the relationship between creditor and debtor, if the latter cannot pay the former, how is the balance restored ? Nietzsche argues that early justice was based on an equivalence principle: **Pain is a currency.** If the debtor is insolvent, the creditor is granted the right to inflict a specific quantum of pain on the debtor's body (e.g., cutting off a pound of flesh, torture, imprisonment). This pain provides the creditor with a psychological "repayment"—the pleasure of cruelty, the "festival" of exercising power over another.
