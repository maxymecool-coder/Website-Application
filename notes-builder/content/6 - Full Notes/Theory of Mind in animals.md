# Theory of Mind in animals
**Details**
For decades, the idea of studying an animal's inner life—its thoughts, feelings, or awareness—was dismissed as unscientific sentimentality. **Cognitive ethology** shattered that taboo. Through elegant experiments, scientists have revealed that the lights are on inside many animal minds. Chimps can pass the mirror test, recognizing a dot of paint on their own forehead, demonstrating **self-awareness**. They can also demonstrate a **theory of mind**, knowing that another chimp has different information than they do—a skill they use to out-compete rivals for a hidden banana. This cognitive sophistication is not limited to primates. Crows, some of the smartest birds, will hide food for the next day, demonstrating an ability to **plan for the future**. They will even re-hide their stash if they know another crow was watching, showing they understand what others know. From chimps that can distinguish intentional acts from accidental ones to bees that ignore information about a food source in an impossible location, the evidence is overwhelming. The animal mind is not a simple black box; for many species, it is a complex, strategic, and aware consciousness.
