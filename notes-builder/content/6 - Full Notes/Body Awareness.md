# Body Awareness
**Details**
As talked before, the brain and body are strongly connected with each other, which means that breathing calmly and remaining in a state of relaxation, even while accessing painful and horrifying memories, is essential to treatment. When taking slow breaths, the parasympathetic helps pump chemicals to calm the body down. This is even more effective the more you focus on hour breathing, particularly the very end of the out breath and waiting a moment before inhaling again. It may even help to think about how oxygen nourishes the body and bathing the tissues with energy whilst doing so. 

The overarching idea of this is also to put us in touch with our inner world, and landscape of our organism. Noticing how we are feeling can immediately open up our perspective and options to how to react rather than the automatic and habitual reactions. Mindfulness puts us in touch with the transitory nature of our feelings and perceptions. When we focus on these sensations, we can recognize the ebb and flow of our emotions, with that, increasing our control over them. 

Once you have noticed these emotions and reactions, you must label them, like when you get mad, what tenses up, how your chest, arms,… feel and focusing on that sensation then focusing on how relieving it is when it is released practices mindfulness. If one cannot tolerate what they are feeling in the present, opening up to the past will only bring them more pain and retraumatize them further. (This is so often ignored in traditional treatments and is why the method of reintroducing trauma so often fails).
