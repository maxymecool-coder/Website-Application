# Rejection of Philosophical Social Roles
**Details**
The traditional view of philosophy entails a fixed social role for philosophers who address a permanent set of questions, alike a doctor, or a cook, or whatever, as they don't have a set social role throughout the times but rather a fluid function that changes based on historical context. He takes this from the example of philosophers throughout the time-a king's advisor, a doctor of culture, a theologian, or a free thinker. Furthermore, there are no eternal philosophical questions, only questions arising from specific historical conflicts or events during those times. The Greeks asked about rhetoric because of the Sophists; Kant asked about determinism because of Newtonian science, and the Stoics were the one who created Will. Nietzsche derides the belief in eternal questions as "Egyptianism"—the fallacy of treating historically contingent concepts as immutable pyramids.
