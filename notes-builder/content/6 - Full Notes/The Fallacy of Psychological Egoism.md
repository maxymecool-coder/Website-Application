# The Fallacy of Psychological Egoism
**Details**
Psychological Egoism is quite a popular philosophy in the space for certain groups of people, even if it isn't considered an exactly "strong philosophy." Basically, it states that people, human beings, are completely and utterly selfish, they only act in their self-interest, whatever can lead to their pleasures. This view is often supported by a seductive "a priori" argument: "When I act, I act to satisfy my desire. Satisfying my desire brings me pleasure. Therefore, I act to get pleasure."

But this actually exposes certain flaws about the philosophy. For example, if a father buys life insurance to protect his children after his death, his desire is that "my children are provided for." This desire is _semantically_ satisfied if the children are actually provided for, even though the father will be dead and feel no _psychological_ pleasure at that moment. To claim the father only pays the premium to avoid the "unease" of worry is circular; he would only feel unease if he _already_ cared about his children's future welfare independently of his own state. Thus, the argument that all actions are selfish is based on a linguistic trick, not psychological reality.

Furthermore, even as those who believe in this philosophy argues from an evolutionary perspective, it still falls apart. The gene itself that propagates us can be selfish while we can be altruistic. Not only that, the best way for a gene that wants to be passed on further is if it just jumped to empathy and love and care rather than a series of calculations running in our head to think about if we are "logically" deducting that we should do this. It would be an absurd view of psychology if we did !
