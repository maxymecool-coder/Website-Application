# Reliability
**Details**
The aspect of reliability in these formal assignments have a wide range of sub-aspects. Some of them are interrater reliability which refer to the agreement between two professionals on what they are observing, so a psychologist who is taking care of a patient gets a second opinion from another psychologist and they agree. 

Test-retest reliability is the type to concern a person's results not alternating when they took the test twice, preferably with some time apart. Of course this test is only useful when we consider that the person will not change enough for the test results to change suddenly. 

Some other considered reliability forms are alternate-form reliability, where the test is instead of being the same test, is actually different so that the taker cannot artificially increase their scores or something like that, but still retaining the nature of the test.

Finally, the internal consistency reliability, which assess how the items on a test may relate to each other.
