# The Triune Brain Model and Depression
**Details**
To understand the circuitry of depression, Sapolsky uses Paul MacLean's **triune brain model**. This is not a literal anatomical map but a conceptual framework for understanding how different brain functions interact. It organizes the brain into three layers:

1. **Layer 1: The Reptilian Brain (Hypothalamus, Brain Stem):** This is the most primitive layer, responsible for automatic, regulatory functions essential for survival: body temperature, blood pressure, hunger, etc. It handles the body's basic housekeeping.
    
2. **Layer 2: The Limbic System (Amygdala, Hippocampus):** This layer, more developed in mammals, is the seat of **emotion**. It processes feelings like fear, anger, anxiety, and pleasure.
    
3. **Layer 3: The Cortex (especially the Prefrontal Cortex):** This is the most recently evolved layer, responsible for **cognition**: thought, memory, planning, and abstract reasoning.
    

The critical insight from this model is that these layers are in constant communication, both top-down and bottom-up.

- **Top-Down Influence:** A thought can trigger an emotion, which in turn triggers a physical response. For example, simply _thinking_ about a past failure (Layer 3) can activate feelings of sadness and guilt (Layer 2), which then elevates stress hormones and heart rate (Layer 1). This is the mechanism by which psychological stress becomes a biological event, and it's central to how rumination drives the physical symptoms of depression.
    
- **Bottom-Up Influence:** Physical states can influence emotion and thought. For example, being hungry (Layer 1) can make a person more irritable (Layer 2) and lead to poorer, more impulsive decisions (Layer 3).
    

In depression, this system becomes pathological. The cortex (Layer 3) gets caught in a loop of negative thoughts, constantly sending "sad" signals to the limbic system (Layer 2), which then keeps the body's stress systems (Layer 1) on high alert.
