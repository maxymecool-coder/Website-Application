# Trauma leading to actions leading to more trauma
**Details**
Trauma does not only come from what happens to us. It often comes from what we do, or fail to do, when it happens.

Many survivors look back and feel shame about how they acted. They wish they had fought harder, spoken louder, moved faster. They regret freezing, staying silent, or giving in. Even if their reactions were natural survival instincts, they see them later as weakness or betrayal. This regret becomes a second wound, deeper than the first.

This self-blame can be more painful than the original event. It can grow into self-hatred. Survivors do not only grieve what was done to them. They grieve who they were in that moment. They despise the fear they felt. They despise the helplessness. They despise themselves.

When someone feels broken inside, it changes how they act later. Shame makes people pull away from others. Anger makes them lash out. Fear makes them avoid things that might heal them. Without meaning to, they create new situations where they get hurt again. Trauma leads to actions that cause more trauma. It becomes a loop that tightens every time they fall.

The only way to break the loop is to forgive the past self. To understand that reactions under trauma are not moral failures. They are survival. They are not weakness. They are what kept the person alive. Healing starts by facing the feelings that were too hard to face before. Healing grows when there is compassion instead of punishment.
