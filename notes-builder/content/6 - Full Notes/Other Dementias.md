# Other Dementias
**Details**
Other than Alzheimer's, there are some other types of dementia out there such as frontotemporal dementia, vascular dementia, and dementia with Lewy Bodies. The following paragraphs below will offer a quick explanation for each of them.

First off, the frontotemporal dementia, or just FTD. It is caused by a loss of neurons in the frontal and temporal regions of the brain, this causes the deterioration to mainly be in those areas. It can start to as early as in the 30s, to the mid-late 50s, and unlike Alzheimer's which patients can live for a couple decades, people with FTD only live 5-10 years, though the effects are much less annoying in this type of dementia. Unlike Alzheimer's, people with this dementia only experience deterioration in their empathy, executive function, ability to inhibit behavior, hyperorality, and apathy. Usually, this type of dementia damages others relationship and emotional processes more than Alzheimer's. Physical illnesses and diseases can cause this type of dementia.

Vascular dementia, similar to FTD, is caused by diseases as well, specifically cerebrovascular disease. Most commonly, the person has had a sequence of strokes forming a clot, impairing circulation and causing cell death. And then with the strokes repeating, the person gets more and more chances of getting vascular dementia.

Last but not least, dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), which can be divided further into two subtypes, depending on the presence of Parkinson's disease in the patient. About 80 percent of people with it will develop DLB, but some people without it can develop DLB as well. The symptoms are difficult to differentiate from Parkinson's and Alzheimer's.
