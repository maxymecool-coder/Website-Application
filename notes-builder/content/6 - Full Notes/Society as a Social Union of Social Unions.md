# Society as a Social Union of Social Unions
**Details**
A social union differs from [[The Private Society and Human Nature]] by two features. The first is that its participants have identically the same end and that the social institutions and cooperative activities are ends in themselves, not just means.

And so a well-ordered society governed on this philosophy would govern the smaller social unions of families, friendships, and so on. The end that they all share is the basic structure of a society to be just and that everyone be treated justly and do their part accordingly.

Crucially, acting from a sense of justice is not merely an obligation imposed from outside. Given the moral psychology Rawls relies on, the exercise and realization of one's moral powers — including the capacity for justice — is part of one's good. The good of exercising complex, subtle, and discriminating powers is greater than simpler exercises of capacity. The sense of justice is among citizens' most important powers, and its realization in a successful cooperative life organized around just principles is part of what makes that life go well for the person living it.
