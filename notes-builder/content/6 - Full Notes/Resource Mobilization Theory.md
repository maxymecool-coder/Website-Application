# Resource Mobilization Theory
**Details**
The contemporary understanding of how social movements emerge is dominated by **Resource Mobilization Theory**, a framework pioneered by sociologists like Charles Tilly. This theory is radically different from the [[The Collective Behavior Theory]] in that instead of these protests just being collective behavior that happen because people are so out of touch with society, it is instead led by those who are educated and intelligent.

The people involved in these protests are not the alienated and helpless members of society but often those who believe that they have political efficacy, that they have the power to change things, which motivates them psychologically. Leaders of these protests are educated because they need to be able to handle many things that a leader is required to do such as financial, or public speaking to incite people to fight, and prior organization. **Prior organization** is extremely important because these uprisings do not just appear out of a vacuum, but rather from existing political institution. They are almost always built upon pre-existing organizational structures—churches, unions, student groups, community associations—that can be repurposed for political action. This existing network provides the communication channels, leadership, and trust necessary to mobilize people effectively. In history, there have been so many examples of this, the French Revolution is one of the most obvious.
