# Stigma in Asian Cultures
**Details**
In today's society, scholars have argued that the impact that stigma has on PWLES (people with lived experiences of mental illnesses) are far worse than the symptoms of the conditions itself. Particularly in Asia where mental illnesses tend to be highly moralized and connected with the person rather than with the condition. This has been proven throughout thousands of scientific studies, proving that stigma against PWLEs is much stronger in Asia rather than Western contexts.

Theories have been made to explain why this is the case, with results showing how historical context of governmental and religious institutions and country-specific power elites and individuals shape these conceptions of cultural stigma. They then continue to permeate through society by word-of-mouth (facilitated by collectivism) and media turning into public stigma, which causes distrust between the public and PWLEs. Individuals in society further perpetuate stigma by shunning, interpretating and discriminating PWLEs based on the preconceived cultural prejudices about the stigma, activating during social interactions, further reinforcing the public stigma. Finally, PWLEs turn this into learned helplessness as they anticipate stigma and then self-stigmatize due to internalization of cultural and public stigma.

**Supporting Content**
Though this does account for the majority of PWLEs, there are those who contest stigma due to holding socially valued roles.
