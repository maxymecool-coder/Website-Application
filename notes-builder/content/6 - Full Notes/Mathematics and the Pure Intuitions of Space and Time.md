# Mathematics and the Pure Intuitions of Space and Time
**Details**
Kant argues that math itself is also dependent on synthetic propositions: alike how arithmetic additions, subtractions, so on and so forth presupposes an existent of time so that the calculations can actually happen and change: the same also applies for geometry, which depends on space.

Kant illustrates this with the story of Thales (or whoever first demonstrated the properties of the isosceles triangle). The discoverer did not simply meditate on a figure in front of his eyes or in his mind. He constructed the properties through a positive act — producing them "in accordance with his own concept." He attributed to the object only those properties that necessarily followed from what he himself had placed in the object conceptually. This is the model of mathematical knowledge for Kant: the mind constructs the object according to its own rational resources, and then discovers necessary truths about it — truths that are both synthetic (genuinely informative) and a priori (knowable independently of particular experience).
