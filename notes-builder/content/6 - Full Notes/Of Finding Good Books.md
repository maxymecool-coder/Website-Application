# On finding good books
**Details**
"Means". As told before means can be the method in which one becomes a better reader, they are the set of rules that one must follow to do so. But it can also refer to the things we read, the books that we apply such rules too. These two go hand in hand with each other as good methods lacking good books to apply to is just as useless as vice versa.

Even though the methods that we have taught can seem to be able to be applied to any kind of book, in most cases, it can and should only be applied to books that truly requires it to. These kind of books are the kinds that will challenge your mind and stretch it, eventually helping you to expend your knowledge and mind.

And the kinds of books that merely entertain or inform you of facts cannot do this. Perhaps it has made you more knowledgeable, but it has not made you better at reading. The books that you would want to practice these skills on, particularly analytical reading, must also make demands on you. They must seem to go beyond your capacity. This includes genres and books outside of expository books, which may seem to be harder to read, which is true as the author is not actively pursuing the task of helping the reader understand their work.

**Supporting Content**
Good books can do more than just improving your reading skills, it can also teach you about the world and yourself. You learn more than how to read better but you learn about life. You become wiser.
