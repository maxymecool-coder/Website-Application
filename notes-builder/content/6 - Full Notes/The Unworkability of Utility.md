# The Unworkability of Utility
**Details**
Rawls believe that utilitarianism cannot be used as a foundational political philosophy for a modern democratic society because "utility" or "happiness" cannot be objectively measured and compared across an entire population. This is due to ideological pluralism, as people will naturally hold various frameworks of the "good life" that are conflicting or even incommensurable to each other. A devout religious ascetic, a hyper-competitive entrepreneur, and an introverted artist have radically different ultimate ends.

And because a comprehensive conception of the good is impossible to enforce without violating liberty, one must then rely on a partial conception of it, which is the framework of Primary Goods. Instead of attempting to maximize subjective psychological happiness, the state’s role is to ensure the fair distribution of objective, "all-purpose means." Primary goods include basic rights and liberties, freedom of movement, free choice of occupation, income, wealth, and the social bases of self-respect. This is highly relative to [[From Utility to Liberty]], which states that liberty is a necessity for people to find pleasure and happiness.
