# Validity in Diagnosis and Assessments
**Details**
Validity is something that is closely related to reliability, though they do not ensure the other level of correlation. This means even if you have high reliability that does not mean you have high validity, as the topic that you are testing may be completely displaced with each other.

Content validity would refer to how related the area of information given by the test is to the goal of it. For example, later in this chapter we will describe an interview that is often used to make an Axis I diagnosis. It has excellent content validity because it contains questions about all the symptoms that are involved in most Axis I diagnoses. For certain uses, though, the interview might have poor content validity. The interview doesn’t cover questions about kleptomania (a disorder characterized by a compulsive need to steal). If one were trying to assess kleptomania, this interview would have poor content validity.

Criterion validity is how related a measure of test is to the other measures of the test. If both variables are measured at the same point in time, the resulting validity is referred to as concurrent validity. For example, below we will describe a measure of the overly negative thoughts that are believed to play an important role in depression. Criterion validity for this measure of negative thoughts could be established by showing that people with depression score higher on the test than do people without depression. One means of establishing reliability is to determine whether different judges agree, as happens when two umpires witness the same event in a baseball game. A measure to predict some other variable that is measured at some point in the future, often referred to as predictive validity. For example, IQ tests were originally developed to predict future school performance. Similarly, a measure of negative thinking  could be used to predict the development of depression in the future. In summary, concurrent and predictive validity are both types of criterion validity.

Lastly is construct validity, which is somewhat complex as it measures the extent of the predicted value and states that the taker would be in accurate with the level of the test.
