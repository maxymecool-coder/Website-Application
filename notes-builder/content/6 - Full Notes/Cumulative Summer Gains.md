# Cumulative Summer Gains
**Details**
Sociologist Barbara Heyns research on schools came to a wall, which led her to start examining the summer activities that different classes of students do and how that helps them or form the difference between academic achievement gap.

During the school year, it is pretty clear that their growth and progress are remarkably similar, in fact, the low-income students may gain a bit of ground on their high or middle-income counterparts. The divergence happens from June to September.

- **High-Income Students:** Continue to learn and gain skills over the summer, benefiting from enrichment activities like camps, museum visits, travel, and literacy-rich home environments.
    
- **Middle-Income Students:** Tend to tread water, retaining most of what they learned but not making significant gains.
    
- **Low-Income Students:** Often experience a "summer slide," losing a significant portion of the skills and knowledge they gained during the previous school year.
    

This pattern of course repeats every single year, meaning the gap here only becomes wider and wider between the classes.
