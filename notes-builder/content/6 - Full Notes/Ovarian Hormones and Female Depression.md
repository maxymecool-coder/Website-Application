# Ovarian Hormones and Female Depression
**Details**
Fluctuations in ovarian hormones, specifically **estrogen and progesterone**, are strongly linked to the higher incidence of depression in women. Women are roughly twice as likely as men to be diagnosed with major depression, and this risk is not constant throughout life. Instead, it spikes during periods of significant hormonal change.

Key periods of vulnerability include:

- **Puberty**
    
- **The perimenstrual phase** (the time before and during menstruation)
    
- **Perimenopause**
    
- **Postpartum** (after childbirth)
    

The link is most stark in **postpartum depression**. The dramatic crash in progesterone levels immediately following childbirth is a major biological trigger for a depressive episode in susceptible individuals. Sapolsky highlights that this connection is so clear that new antidepressant medications have been developed to specifically target this hormonal pathway.

This hormonal link provides powerful evidence that the sex difference in depression rates is not merely a social or psychological phenomenon, but is rooted in fundamental biological differences.
