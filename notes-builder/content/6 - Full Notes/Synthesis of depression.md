# Synthesis of depression
**Details**
According to Aaron T. Beck's seminal work, depression is not a spontaneous wave of sadness but a structured "chain reaction" of thought, initiated by a profound sense of loss. This cognitive model reframes the disorder, moving from a mysterious affliction to an understandable, albeit painful, process of distorted interpretation. The entire structure of depression, from self-loathing to a bleak view of the future, is built upon this initial foundation of perceived loss.

The triggering loss is not always obvious or dramatic, like the death of a spouse. While such events are clear catalysts, Beck highlights a more subtle and insidious form of deprivation: an internal imbalance. This "upset in his 'give-get balance'" occurs when an individual perceives a chronic deficit between what they contribute emotionally and the gratification they receive, or when they consistently fail to meet their own demanding standards. This persistent sense of falling short or being emotionally short-changed creates a powerful feeling of diminishment.

Following this experience of loss, the depression-prone individual undergoes a critical cognitive shift. They begin to appraise all their experiences through a negative lens, overinterpreting neutral or even positive events as further proof of defeat or deprivation. This negativity pervades their entire worldview, creating the classic depressive triad: a negative view of themselves, their world, and their future. The initial sense of loss becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy; by viewing themselves as "deficient" and "inadequate," they filter all incoming information to confirm this belief, locking themselves into the cognitive architecture of despair.
