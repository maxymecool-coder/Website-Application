# Authenticating Conclusions
**Details**
A primary function of therapy is to help a patient move from distorted or externally-imposed beliefs about themselves to conclusions that are authenticated by their own lived experience. Patients often arrive with rigid narratives—"I am a failure," "I am unlovable"—that feel true but are often unexamined generalizations based on painful events. These are inauthentic conclusions because they ignore contradictory evidence and deny the patient's own agency.

The therapist's role is not to provide new, "correct" conclusions, but to act as a co-investigator, helping the patient critically examine their own life. This is achieved through several methods:

1. **Socratic Questioning:** The therapist asks guiding questions that challenge the patient's rigid beliefs. ("What is the evidence for that thought? What is the evidence against it? Is there another way to interpret what happened?") This prompts the patient to become a detective of their own experience, seeking data rather than accepting assumptions.
    
2. **Narrative Externalization:** The therapist helps the patient separate themselves from their problems. Instead of "I am a depressed person," it becomes "How has depression been influencing your life?" This creates space for the patient to identify "unique outcomes"—moments, however small, where they resisted the problem's influence.
    
3. **Focus on Embodied Truth:** An authenticated conclusion is not just an intellectual realization; it must feel true emotionally and physically. The therapist pays attention to the patient's somatic responses and affect, helping them connect a new, more balanced thought with a genuine feeling of relief or rightness.
    

Through this process, the patient learns to construct a new, more complex, and resilient narrative. The resulting conclusions—"I have failed at times, but I have also succeeded," "I have felt unloved, but I am capable of giving and receiving love"—are "authenticated" because the patient has built them from the full, nuanced evidence of their own life. They are not just positive affirmations; they are earned truths.
