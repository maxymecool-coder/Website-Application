# To finally draft and write
**Details**
These are the last steps to getting a manuscript done. We need to structure the text and keep it flexible. After experimenting and making new ideas, we now need to make it into a linear order. Instead of deciding what to write in it, we have to decide what isn’t needed to be written in particular parts of the manuscript. By looking at the structure, we can see if the info will be mentioned in another part. 

Another vital aspect is to have a separate, project-specific place to sort out notes for projects. An outliner might help with developing a rough structure, but also allows us to keep it flexible. The structure of an argument is a part of it and therefore will change during the process of development. When it no longer changes much, it will turn into a “table of contents”. But even then, it helps to see it as a structural guideline and not a prescription. It is not unusual to change the order of chapters at the very end. 

The other key point is to work on different manuscripts at the same time, as it will help us produce by-products which becomes the resource for another, and repeating so on, until it becomes so intertwined that an isolated factory can’t compete with it anymore. This also comes with the benefit of us not losing motivation ever, as when we get bored with working on one of the projects, we can just switch to another to do instead, which is still productive.
