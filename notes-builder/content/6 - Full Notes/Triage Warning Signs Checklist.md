# Triage Warning Signs Checklist
**Details**
The triage risk assessment for all cases reported to the SCU was conducted by a practitioner with a background in psychology, utilizing a **Structured Professional Judgment (SPJ)** approach. SPJ is a well-established analytical method in risk assessment, defined as a process that is "discretionary but relies on evidence-based guidelines to systematize the exercise of discretion". This approach seeks to combine the strengths of structured, data-driven tools with the nuanced understanding of a trained professional, avoiding the pitfalls of purely mechanistic or purely subjective assessments.

The checklist is divided into two key categories, ensuring a comprehensive view of the situation: Behavioral Red Flags for the **Individual of Concern (IoC)** and Behavioral Red Flags for **Targets**. The IoC flags are designed to identify acute risk factors that might not be fully captured by the QuAIC's broader dimensions. These include high-lethality indicators such as "Homicide threats," "Access to or affinity with weapons," and psychological indicators like "Last-resort thinking" (comments suggesting a person has nothing left to lose) and evidence of severe "Mental illness". The inclusion of flags for Targets, such as "Significant distress" and "Adherence to protective measures," is equally important. It ensures that the assessment process remains victim-centered and accounts for the impact on the person being targeted, recognizing that a target's high level of fear may itself be a critical risk factor requiring immediate intervention.
