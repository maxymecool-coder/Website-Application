# Karl Popper and the Myth of Deductive Science
**Details**
Karl Popper was one of the most influential philosopher of the 20th century, and his approach to [[Hume's Fork]] was quite different. He did not try to argue that it was wrong or unagreeable, he accepted completely that there was no argument you could realistically make against it. However, Popper argued that this didn't matter because science does not actually rely on induction. Instead, he proposed "Falsificationism": science proceeds by making bold conjectures and then trying to refute them deductively. If a theory survives a test, it is "corroborated," but never confirmed.

Peter Millican argues that this point of view is also inductive due to how it functions. Of course, according to Popper, we would prefer something that has withstood 1000 tests rather than a new theory or model. But that would be inductive, because we are assuming that the previous model will guide/dictate the future model, while it doesn't.
