# Self-Esteem
**Details**
In the non-clinical populations, rate of self-esteem is nearly universally high. On a 1-7 scale, almost nobody scores below 4. "Low self-esteem" in research typically means moderate self-esteem (4-5), not genuine self-dislike. Though one would have to distinguish self-esteem between underlying cognitive-emotional mechanisms versus as a cause of good outcomes. Self-esteem may act as a gauge and reads how full the tank is (which is the actual cognitive and social resources a person has). If there are already mechanisms that already produce resilience, mood repair, and social competence are present, self-esteem will be high as a byproduct.

This matters enormously because a 1980s government task force promoted self-esteem enhancement as a solution to crime, drug abuse, and educational failure. Subsequent research found no lasting benefits from interventions that targeted self-esteem directly.

Mark Leary proposed a sociometer hypothesis that turns self-esteem from a personal evaluation tool to one determined by the [[Generalized Other]], this subsequently means it would be dependent on how we view how others view us (weird phrasing I know). This is quite counterintuitive because people regard self-esteem as purely personal, not as a social barometer, yet; evolutionarily, it makes sense as we know about who we are and how that might affect our standing in the population.
