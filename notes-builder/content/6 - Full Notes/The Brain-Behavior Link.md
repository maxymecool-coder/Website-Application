# The Brain-Behavior Link
**Details**
While functional brain imaging provides an unprecedented map of neural activity, the relationship between this activity and our actual cognitive processes is not straightforward. A brain scan is not a mind reading. For example, while the fusiform gyrus is strongly associated with face perception, we know that damage to this area is not the only cause of prosopagnosia (face blindness), indicating that cognition is the product of a distributed network, not a single isolated area. This tells us that behavioral measures—the end product of cognition—remain the gold standard for validating our theories. A brain imaging study without a clear behavioral outcome is less informative, as it's difficult to know what the observed brain activity truly signifies.

A useful way to think about this is the **artificial intelligence metaphor**. One cannot understand the function of a computer program simply by looking at the electricity flowing through its circuit board. The operations are performed by algorithms (the software), which exist as relationships between the physical circuits (the hardware). Similarly, our cognition can be thought of as the "software" that runs on the "hardware" of the brain. The patterns of neural firing are the substrate, but the cognitive functions themselves—our thoughts, memories, and decisions—are the complex operations that this hardware performs. This distinction highlights why simply mapping active brain regions is only part of the story; we must also understand the computational rules and relationships that constitute the mind itself.
