# How to read lyric poetry
**Details**
Many people believe that they cannot read lyric poetry because it is obscure and complex. But often, it is very much worth it and not as hard as you may think if you read it in the right way (bias to attract attention haha).

And so we start with the first rule, same as others, read continuously, do not stop under any conditions, this is even more important in this than others medias we have talked about.

Why ? You may ask, and it is because when you encounter a line or a part that is particularly difficult, it can be resolved after you’ve understood the unity of the piece, never because you extensively researched it like a puzzle. This process makes poetry unenjoyable and would demotivate you rather than anything else.

The meaning of these things can only be found in unity, when all layers are eventually connected, we cannot expect from a single line or a stanza that the meaning be present.

The second rule is this: Read the poem through again - but out loud, as said before in Shakespeare’s poetic dramas. This is essential compared to the helpful nature of it in those. Here, speaking those words forces you to understand them better. You cannot glide over such things if you are speaking it, and so you are forced to confront it. All of the context clues derived from the piece will also help with this as well. Last rule is immersion, which won’t be elaborated further.

The questions you ask of an expository work are grammatical and logical. The questions you ask of a lyric are usually rhetorical, though they may also be syntactical. You do not come to terms with a poem; but you must discover the key words. You discover them not primarily by an act of grammatical discernment, however, but by an act of rhetorical discernment. Why do certain words pop out of the poem and stare you in the face? Is it because the rhythm marks them? Or the rhyme? Or are the words repeated? Do several stanzas seem to be about the same ideas; if so, do these ideas form any kind of sequence? Anything of this sort that you can discover will help your understanding.

Also try to find the conflict in good lyrics as they are part of the other helpful clues.

The final advice is to understand the author, as poetry reflects him more than any other genre, and so it will help you understand the work further knowing the time period it was in and what kind of man the author was. Knowing extensively all this does not necessarily guarantee you would read the piece better, only rereading and putting effort in it will ensure that.
