# Weber's Historical Context of the Class Conflict
**Details**
According to Weber, the conflict relationship between certain classes that determine the history of the times usually depended on what market was dominant at that time. He came up with the existence of three markets: credit, commodity, labor.

1. Credit Market: This is most prevalent in the ancient societies, where the conflict were between the creditors and the indebted. In those times, peasants and farmers had to borrow money to purchase seeds and plant so that they would have food to survive. These people sometimes had to sell their land, their own children, and eventually themselves (giving service).

2. Commodity Market: This market was in between the buyers and the sellers, usually in medieval times or early modern periods. The grain riots during the French Revolution are a classic example. When a bad harvest drove up the price of bread, the urban poor, as consumers, rioted against what they saw as hoarding and profiteering by sellers and aristocrats. This type of conflict arises when access to essential commodities becomes a point of social friction.

3. Labor Market: This is the most relevant to us, as we are actively living in it, pitting workers against owners. It is quite a usual sight for us to see workers go on strike nowadays and revolutionize in the 19th century, as those are clear examples of the conflict. In 19th-century America, for instance, radical small farmers (debtors) were hostile to big banks (creditors), but as employers of farm labor, they were opposed to the goals of urban workers (laborers). This fragmentation prevented the formation of a single, unified radical movement. The example also show how different markets can clash together and prevent revolutions.
