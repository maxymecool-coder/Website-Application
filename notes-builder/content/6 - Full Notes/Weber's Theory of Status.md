# Weber's Theory of Status
**Details**
Unlike [[The Weber's Class theory]], the status groups have clear boundaries with lower status groups. They have different etiquettes, behaviors, their style of life would be different from a group that has the status of peasants, as an example.

The most popular example is the Medieval European nobility, which included families who were noble, graceful, just overall separated from the peasants of their property.  These brutal warriors transformed themselves into a refined aristocracy with ornate dress, exquisite manners, and claims of superior "blood" and "breeding." Stories like "The Princess and the Pea" are mythologies created to assert that their privilege was not merely won by force, but was a natural result of their inherent superiority.

It is simple to see that this creates a hard boundary, a hard border so that it is extremely difficult for people to come into status, they would almost always have to be born in it rather than work their way up to it like how class does.
