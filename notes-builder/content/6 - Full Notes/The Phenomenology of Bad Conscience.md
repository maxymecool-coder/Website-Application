# The Phenomenology of Bad Conscience
**Details**
Before going into the history and function of Bad Conscience, Nietzsche defines it not just as a simple feeling, but as a complex compound of a raw physiological fact and a specific cultural interpretation. The lecturer breaks it down into four components that must all be present for it to exist. First is the somatic fact, which is the real, pre-moral state of internal psychic pain or "malaise." Then it is the subjective interpretation of it as guilt. Then the guilt would turn into the cognitive awareness of having done something "wrong." Lastly the transgression would have to be interpreted metaphysically, the subject would have to believe that they have Free Will and could have acted otherwise yet they weren't strong enough to do so. Nietzsche argues that while the first step (internal pain) might be a universal result of civilization (internalization), the subsequent steps are not. The connection of pain to "Guilt" and "Free Will" is a specific Christian/Western interpretation of that pain. Therefore, one cannot simply "cure" bad conscience by changing one's beliefs, because the underlying somatic pain (Step 1) remains even if the interpretation is removed.

Nietzsche then tries to exemplify this by projecting a possible future-the Sovereign Individual. Basically, they are an individual that is the most capable of humanity, they are autonomous, predictable, and possess the "right to make promises."

Nietzsche believed that the ability to make promises is not a natural human trait but rather a hard-won artificial power that allows one to bind themselves to the future and be able to control both internally and externally to ensure they can perform the function they promised to do.

This continuity, as Nietzsche posits, which was something revolutionary for his time, was the "Mnemonics of Pain." which is basically modern cognitive psychology about how memories get more integrated through strong feelings.
