# The central limit theorem
**Details**
When the normal curve first arrived, people wondered why the bell curve existed in so much nature. And so this theorem was found out, the statement being: if a measured variable is the sum of many other random variables, that sum will be approximately normal. The pieces can be skewed, flat, exponential, anything; add enough of them and the total comes out normal.

This is exactly the case that happens in psychology. After all, in any situation, there are infinitely many factors contributing to how one acts. "How happy I am" is not driven by one thing but by _many_ contributing factors — friends who called, cups of coffee, roses spotted, hours slept — each with its own odd distribution. Because your happiness score is their sum, happiness across people lands on a normal curve. A simulation makes it vivid: sum 50 skewed factors per person, repeat for hundreds of people, and the histogram of totals traces the bell curve.

However, for certain exceptions where the chance of something happening is so low, this theorem does not apply, e.g. "number of presidents in your family," which is almost always 0. As the summing factors never yields a normal shape, we cannot apply the theorem.
