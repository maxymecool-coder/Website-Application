# Definition
Mini Essays are based on the Feynman Technique. When you find something that resonates with you, you should try to write on it and produce a writing as if you are teaching it to someone. It allows you to see if you fully know and understand a piece of information or a concept. When you re-read the material and fill up those holes of knowledge, you can fully understand everything you try to learn. 

It should be kept in only one idea to not bleach too much into thinking about linking ideas together. Every mini-essays should be short and in a small amount of time, preferably enough to be seen without scrolling on a note.

The intro should be short and interesting to draw the reader in. Or make a strong statement to give the reader some idea and food for thoughts. Ask a question, a hard hitting one that will help interest the reader to continue and find out the answer. The last method is using a story, a relevant story that you think can resonate with the reader and you can remember immediately on the spot. Blending story and formal writing together. Writing back the essay multiple times help you find out problems that may make them harder to understand and fix them on the road to ensure the clearest level of the point you are getting across. Writing a powerful conclusion that resonates with the reader also helps to close the piece out.
