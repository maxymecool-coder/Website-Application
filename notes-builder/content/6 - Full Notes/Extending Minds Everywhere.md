# Extending Minds Everywhere
**Details**
Fritz Heider's 1944 video of geometric shapes — triangles and a circle moving around a rectangle — is perhaps the most elegant demonstration of a cognitive tendency so deep it feels like perception rather than inference. When audiences watch the video, they don't see shapes moving. They see a bully chasing a victim, a romantic rivalry, parents protecting children. They attribute intentions, desires, emotions, and personality traits to pieces of felt that have never met each other.

We are quite literally designed to interpret the movement of patterns as mental states. We see and we interpret, not that we actively figure and assign roles and stories to it. But of course there are exceptions to this. Children and adults with **severe autism** describe the video in purely physical terms: "The triangle moved left, then the circle moved right." People with early **amygdala damage** do the same. For everyone else — including very young neurotypical children — the automatic attribution of mental states to moving objects is inescapable.

This is where psychology lies, it is one of, if not the most important leg that social psychology stands on; we need to explain things that cannot be reducible to the physical, which are what we interpret from whatever situations that we are in as hard sciences do not have a good explanation for it.
