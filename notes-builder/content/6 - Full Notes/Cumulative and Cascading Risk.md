# Cumulative and Cascading Risk
**Details**
Most of the time, one singular risk factors changing does not affect much to the outcome of having mental disorders, but when you take multiple into the equation, it starts to become much much more risky.

Cumulative risk, developed by Michael Rutter, is the idea that the accumulation of multiple risk factors may drastically increase the chance of a negative outcome. Two risk factors are often twice as bad as one, even if one gets worse. Another idea is of Cascading risk, which states that a risk factor appearing may facilitate or create the environment for other risk factors at later stages in life to develop. For example, insecure attachment may lead to poor academic relationships and then poor academics, then it just constantly builds, like a cascading waterfall.
