# Love as an Evolutionary Exaptation
**Details**
The profound feeling of adult romantic love, according to Lisa Diamond's model, has an evolutionary origin that is both surprising and deeply logical. The neurochemical system that creates pair-bonds between partners did not originally evolve for the purpose of mating. Instead, it is a remarkable example of an **exaptation**: a biological feature that evolved for one purpose and was later co-opted for an entirely new function.

The original function of this attachment system was to ensure the survival of helpless mammalian infants. Nature forged a powerful, neurochemically-driven system to create an unbreakable bond between an infant and its caregiver. This bond, characterized by a desire for proximity, distress upon separation, and feelings of profound comfort and security, is mediated by hormones like oxytocin and vasopressin. It is a fundamental mechanism for survival.

Later in our evolutionary history, this same ancient system was repurposed to also create long-term bonds between adult reproductive partners. This new function was highly advantageous, as it encouraged parents to remain together to collaborate in the difficult, long-term project of raising their vulnerable offspring. The love you feel for a romantic partner is, in essence, running on the same neurochemical hardware that first evolved to bond you to a parent, a testament to nature's ability to adapt existing tools for new and complex purposes.
