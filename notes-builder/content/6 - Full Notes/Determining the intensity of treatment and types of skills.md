# Determining the intensity of treatment and types of skills
**Details**
Another assessment necessary to be done is to see if the person needs more than just DBT. There are many options and intensities of care such as standard outpatient DBT; DBT skills training plus intensive case management; DBT skills integrated into individual DBT; DBT added to non-DBT case management or psychotherapy; and inpatient, residential, and day treatment DBT. This will depend on the patient’s “level of disorder,” which is defined by characteristics of the patient’s current conditions. Each stage relates to different symptoms and different goals.

The basic rundown for Stage 1 are a current severe mental disorder, behavioral dyscontrol, and/or imminent threat to life. Therapists will have to work on these conditions before others to ensure the client has suitable control. In the subsequent stages, the treatment are to replace “quiet desperation” with nontraumatic emotional experiencing; to achieve “ordinary” happiness and unhappiness, and to reduce ongoing disorders and problems in living; and to resolve a sense of incompleteness, to achieve a sense of freedom and joy. The stages can be jumped between each other, and there is no requirement for a person to go through all of them.

There is also the problem of evaluating progress. Many uses the DBT diary card to track the problem behaviors, changes in mood, self-efficacy, use of skills, beliefs that they are improving. However, it isn’t optimal for data analysis as it is a diary, so one must transfer it to a data list or something instead to keep easier track of.
