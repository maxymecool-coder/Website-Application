# The Outflow Table and Its Findings
**Details**
This is the central tool in the research of Blau and Duncan's studies. This table shows the occupational destinations (outcomes) for sons originating from various paternal occupational categories. By reading across a row, one can see the percentage of sons from a specific background (e.g., fathers were professionals) who "flowed out" into different occupations. These statistics show us the difference between the social inheritance and hierarchies inheritance between each class so to say.

- Top occupations of family meant that children usually ended up there as well, at the class of the jobs that their father occupied, they would also have the highest chance to inherit their fathers' occupations as well (direct occupational inheritance). The same can be seen from the bottom as well, this showed that at these places, there are little mobility in the occupational hierarchy.

- At the middle though, there is much higher mobility, they scattered around the middle to the high-status groups, not on the lower tiers though.
