# The Humanistic Perspective
**Details**
The Humanistic approach to personality, championed by theorists like Carl Rogers and Abraham Maslow, offered a more optimistic alternative to the deterministic views of Freud. This perspective emphasizes conscious experience, free will, and the innate human potential for growth.

**Carl Rogers and the Self** Rogers believed that the core motive of personality is the drive toward **self-actualization**—the fulfillment of one's unique potential. He proposed that our personality is structured around our concept of "self":

- **The Actual Self:** Our perception of who we are right now.
    
- **The Ideal Self:** The person we aspire to become.
    

According to Rogers, psychological health and growth occur when we work to close the gap between our actual and ideal selves. When these two concepts align, we achieve a state of congruence and move closer to self-actualization.

**Abraham Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs** Maslow expanded on this idea by proposing that our ability to pursue self-actualization depends on the fulfillment of more fundamental needs. He organized these needs into a **hierarchy**:

1. **Physiological Needs:** Food, water, air, basic survival.
    
2. **Safety Needs:** Security, stability, a roof over one's head.
    
3. **Love and Belonging Needs:** Intimacy, friendship, a sense of connection.
    
4. **Esteem Needs:** Self-respect, achievement, recognition from others.
    
5. **Self-Actualization:** Reaching one's full potential, creativity, morality.
    

Maslow argued that if our lower-level needs are unmet, our personality and behavior will be dominated by the struggle to satisfy them. Only when these foundational needs are secure can we fully dedicate ourselves to the higher-level pursuit of self-actualization.
