# Dopamine in relationships
**Details**
Dopamine reward systems also plays a part in this, reinstating the fact that it is the anticipation of pleasure rather than pleasure itself. And so we can get a melancholic saying from this: “The price of a relationship is the loss of all anticipation from it.”, roughly translates to the price of love is the loss of reward from chasing it. When someone is trying to get their feelings reciprocated, they always have a huge maybe connected to the person that they are trying to have their feelings reciprocated. The larger that maybe is, the more dopamine they get from it, and so when it eventually flourishes, they come to the height of the pleasure, as it slowly dips down (according to scientific studies). Instead, it turns into feelings/chemicals of comfort, of care, as the person is now that rather than something to be chased or desired.

There are also different types of dopamine receptors that serves different purposes. In monogamous species, two receptors are key: D1 and D2.

• D2 receptors are associated with the rewarding, anticipatory aspects of forming a new pair bond. The initial mating and attachment process requires their activation.

• D1 receptors appear to mediate the maintenance and pleasure of an established monogamous relationship.

Immediately after a pair bond is formed in monogamous voles, D2 receptor levels are down-regulated while D1 receptor levels are up-regulated. If this chemical shift is blocked, the pair bond does not last. This suggests that the brain has distinct mechanisms for the excitement of forming an attachment versus the contentment of maintaining one.
