# Cultural Relativity of Attachment Behaviors
**Details**
When tested for the "gold standards" in the US, results in other countries actually yield quite radically different. In Japan for example, there were higher rates of Insecure-Resistant attachment due to them being separated from their mothers a lot, but that separation actually inflates the "resistance" stats. In Germany, higher rates of Insecure-Avoidant attachment. This may reflect cultural values regarding independence and structured schedules rather than rejection. Jerome Kagan’s observations in Guatemala suggest that despite radically different child-rearing practices (e.g., infants left alone in huts while parents work), infants show similar rates of secure attachment by age one as US infants.
