# The Types of Biases
**Details**
Explicit stereotyping—what people willingly self-report—has been the dominant research paradigm since the 1930s. Princeton studies tracking white Americans' trait attributions about Black Americans from 1933 to 1993 show dramatic declines in endorsement of traits like "superstitious" and "lazy." This could reflect genuine attitude change or merely increased awareness that such endorsements are socially unacceptable. There are problems with this methodology though: people may conceal their biases, or lack awareness about their own biases. The IAT (Implicit Association Test) addresses both problems by measuring reaction time differences when categorizing stimuli under compatible versus incompatible pairings (white+pleasant vs. black+pleasant). The classroom demonstration reveals the phenomenon experientially: pairing black names with pleasant words and white names with unpleasant words produces measurably slower responses for most participants, regardless of their conscious beliefs. Over 10 million people have taken online IATs, and the effects are robust across hundreds of studies controlling for order, side assignment, and stimulus type. Most strikingly, African Americans show attenuated but still present pro-white implicit bias—evidence that implicit associations are shaped by cultural exposure (shared media, shared teachers, shared imagery) rather than by group membership or personal endorsement.
