# The Background pillar in traits
**Details**
Other than just genetics or the environment, there also exists a chance factor in it. Biology is not a perfectly deterministic machine. Randomness, or stochasticity, is woven into its very fabric. Consider the mitochondria in a single cell. Because they have their own DNA and replicate independently, a cell contains a mosaic of slightly different mitochondrial genes. When that cell divides, which mitochondria end up in which daughter cell is a matter of random chance—where they happened to be floating at the moment of the split. Two genetically identical cells can thus give rise to genetically different offspring in a single generation, not because of a grand blueprint, but because of a cosmic dice roll. This principle applies to all the crucial molecules of life, like transcription factors. This inherent randomness is a fundamental source of variation, ensuring that even with identical genes and an identical environment, life will always retain an element of unpredictability.
