# Hypothetical Transgressions
**Details**
Now we step into the territory of the transgressions that happens when someone does not actually experience anything. In these situations, their reactions may be as strong as if it did happen to them. But why ? The common denominator is that the offender violated a rule regarded as important to the offended. For example, a motorcyclist passing a red light, a pedestrian gets mad, they might explain it as if they WERE walking or that they could've been walking there.

Although these situations are subtle, they account for a large proportion of the discord in our relations. The imposition of a value judgement points to the existence of an implicit code of laws, rules, principles, and standards. These rules are mostly applied to protect the protagonist from physical and psychological harm even though he and his domain are in no way involved with the interaction of the offender.

To justify and be the outer wall of this are the principles of fair play, justice. Anything that goes against these principles are regarded as a threat to the wall, and violate the rules. We can notice that these things are being affected thanks to the angry complaints that the offended may say such as: "They have no right to act that way." "He should not have done that."

Moreso, they themselves do not realize that their anger may seem inappropriate and unhealthy to others, but to the individual it seems appropriate as he has his own standards of right and wrong. He only cares that it violates his own code (may be idiosyncratic). 

Moreover, social conventions can play a great role in specifying in which conditions is this anger justified, expected, or even demanded, but also set the limits when it becomes excessive. And of course those implications are present in statements such as "You're over-reacting", or "Why the temper tantrum?".
