# The All-Or-Nothing Rule
**Details**
A neuron's action potential operates on an **all-or-none** basis. This means that if a stimulus is strong enough to reach the **threshold of excitation**, the neuron will fire with its full, characteristic strength. If the stimulus is weaker than the threshold, the neuron will not fire at all.

There is no "half-fire" or "weak fire." The intensity of the initial stimulus does _not_ change the amplitude (strength) or velocity (speed) of the resulting action potential. The action potential is a binary, digital event: it is either on (1) or off (0).

**Analogy:** This is like firing a gun or flushing a toilet. Pressing the trigger harder does not make the bullet fly faster. Pushing the flush handle gently versus slamming it down both result in the same standard flush. The mechanism either engages completely or not at all.

This raises a question: If all action potentials are the same size, how does the nervous system encode the _intensity_ of a stimulus? The answer lies in the **frequency** of firing. A strong stimulus (e.g., intense pain) will cause a neuron to fire action potentials more rapidly in succession compared to a weak stimulus (e.g., a light touch).
