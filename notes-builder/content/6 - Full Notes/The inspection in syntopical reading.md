# The inspection in syntopical reading
**Details**
This is practically the saving grace of syntopical reading. Having said as a basic premise, all levels of reading relates to one another in some way. Both inspectional and analytical reading can be considered anticipations and preparations for syntopical reading.

This is where inspectional reading comes into play, as there are perhaps hundreds of works to read. Analytical reading all of those works may take years, and so we must cut it down, which inspectional reading can do.

When you have amassed your bibliography, you must inspect all the books on your list to see which ones are truly worth reading, and give you a clear enough idea of the subject. This leads you to cutting down the whole index into something more manageable. You must understand that books should be read at different paces, speeds, as some demands and deserves more attention than the other.

This advice, though effective, is often unheeded. The reason for this is that people treat the inspection of all the books as concurrent with the analytical reading. This is impossible, no matter how skillful one is. Thinking they can combine these steps together, they read slower, and they read wrongly. (In the case of syntopical reading it isn’t even necessary to read analytically, it’s different.)
