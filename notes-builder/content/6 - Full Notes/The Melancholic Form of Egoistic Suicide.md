# The Melancholic Form of Egoistic Suicide
**Details**
Durkheim identifies an ideal type of egoistic suicide characterized by a state of melancholic languor that relaxes all springs to action. In revulsion from the external world, the individual's consciousness becomes self-preoccupied, taking itself as its sole object of study. This extreme concentration, however, only deepens the chasm separating the individual from others and from the very sources of life's meaning. As the person detaches radically from the world, their inner life is deprived of its primary material, and it can find nothing to reflect upon but its own "wretched misery" and "inner nothingness." The individual becomes addicted to this state with a kind of morbid joy, finding a perverse pleasure in the contemplation of non-existence. This condition is the direct individual expression of the over-individuation that defines egoistic suicide; it is the result of the slackening of social ties. The final act is not violent or hasty but is instead a calm, meditative process, where the sufferer analyzes their own state to the very end.
