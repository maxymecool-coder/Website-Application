# Frameworks for differences
**Details**
The statistical framework of psychopathology defines abnormality in a logical way, meaning that a deviation from the norm. A behavior or trait is considered disordered if it falls in the extreme tails of a normal distribution, typically two or more standard deviations from the mean. It is clear about how this functions due to the amount of tests that exists nowadays, DASS, K-12, so on and so forth. They all are dependent on the person taking the test or someone else that knows them well. However, this model has two glaring drawbacks, one is about how easy it is to change the norm and deviation and how much it can affect; the other is that it is value-neutral, meaning we cannot understand if it is a positive or negative deviation. How we deal with the latter is that we apply our own social values and understandings into it so we can distinguish them.

The social deviance model on the other hand depends on what is regarded as a societal (not mathematical) norm and then identifying abnormalities from that norm. This model can be and is highly hurtful, as it can be used to make people feel "left out" and "isolated" from society. In fact, it has been used like this before, like how people with mental illnesses are considered weak and pathetic in Eastern societies or how mental disorders were known as possessions in the 20th century. Even worse, it was used as a political tool by the Soviet Union by defining those who does not believe in communism to have a mental illness.

Moreover, this model even includes the morals of the human beings, it stops being political or cultural and rather goes into the morals. When a person does activities to cope with or are symptoms of their mental disorders, it can be regarded as a moral failing.
