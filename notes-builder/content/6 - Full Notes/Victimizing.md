# Victimizing
**Details**
Another type of phobia that exists is the type where the person is not experiencing, or in the face of any danger but they are seeing, being involved in it. Like surgeons, doctors, nurses, they are forced into such situations where they must watch or observe someone being worked on. At first, most experience typical symptoms of anxiety as described, and although most seem to become more resistant to it overtime, there are some who retain the phobia despite repeated exposures.

I believe this is because they have a high degree of identification with the "occupied." which Beck seems to agree with. The person who observe or is involved in it is able to recall either visual or sensory of some kind of cognition that indicates he is reacting to the situation as if he was the one on the operating table. A nurse may observe a patient bleeding from a laceration and think, "I wonder what it would be like if I were bleeding," and then felt dizzy and faint (just as though she was losing blood).
