# Only to criticize after understanding
**Details**
After the first two stages which were interpenetrated to each other. The reader should have combined both of these stages completely. And now he can move on to Rule 9: You must be able to say with reasonable certainty, “I understand,” before you can say “I agree” or “I disagree”, or “I suspend judgement”. 

Yes, suspending judgement is also an act of criticism, it is taking the position of something that has not been shown in the conversation. You weren’t convinced by either sides of it. 

To narrow down to one example of good readers experiencing a good book, they would hesitate a lot to say “I understand” as they have a lot of work to do before declaring such statement with ease and honesty. But the opposite can be true, for bad books that is, when you have tried everything to your fullest ability to understand a book. Most of the time it’s not the book fault but the reader’s fault, but keep an eye out for such situations. 

But this rule should be exercise under cautions, such as being sure you’re not reading only a part of it, since you’d be lacking context, and so you should be hesitant to criticize. And sometimes it can be related to other books of the author, and so you’d need knowledge of the other books to do so.
