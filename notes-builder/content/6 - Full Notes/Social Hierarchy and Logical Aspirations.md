# Social Hierarchy and Logical Aspirations
**Details**
According to Durkheim, a healthy society provides a "regimen" that regulates desires. This happens through the "moral consciousness of societies," which establishes a hierarchy of social functions and assigns a corresponding degree of well-being that each class may legitimately aspire to. They will be assigned to needs that are fair for what they were born as, their inheriting, their abilities, their statuses.

This creates a scale, though not immutable, that sets an upper limit on the ambitions for each person within their sphere. If an individual has a "wholesome moral constitution," they accept this collective authority and feel "that it is not well to ask more."

This limitation is what allows for contentment. It sets a defined end and goal for the passions, which "make men contented with their lot while stimulating them moderately to improve it." This creates a "calm, active happiness" because one's happiness is secure and defined, not dependent on an infinite and unattainable goal.

For this system to work, however, individuals must not only accept the hierarchy of functions but also believe the _distribution_ of those functions is just.
