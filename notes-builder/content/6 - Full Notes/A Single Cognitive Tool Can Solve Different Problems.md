# The reusage of cognitive solutions
**Details**
Shapiro and Epstein (1998) argue against the idea that every adaptive problem requires a unique cognitive mechanism. Their position rests on two main points: They claim the error argument mistakenly identifies the cognitive process (the tool) with the task it performs. A single screwdriver can tighten (turn right) and loosen (turn left). The tool is the same, but the use is different. Therefore, a single cognitive process could solve different problems. Natural selection is economical. It is more likely to recruit or co-opt existing cognitive abilities for new purposes than to evolve entirely new ones from scratch for every novel problem. Their conclusion is that there isn't a one-to-one relationship between adaptive problems and cognitive solutions.
