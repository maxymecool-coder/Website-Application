# Society as a System of Social Cooperation
**Details**
John Rawls posits that the most fundamental intuitive idea in justice as fairness is the concept that society itself is a system of social cooperation between citizens as free and equal persons. He also does not force this concept to be the definitive idea or that it is required, but rather that it is a model that will be used and reflected upon.

This idea of course has to include some prerequisites from the relationships: Firstly, citizens do not see their social order as something fixed but rather something that can change and be justified depending on the context of discussion.

From this, Rawls distinguishes three features of cooperation. First is the separation of cooperation from mutual coordinated activity to an unquestionable central authority as this authority cannot be questioned and people would be obligatory to follow. Secondly, he states that cooperation would involve fair times that are publicly recognized and embody some sense of reciprocity or mutuality. Lastly, he believes that both parties engaged in this act would be looking to increase their own wellbeing and achieve personal benefits.

And so justice acts as a definition, it has the role of setting the grounds and the principles on which cooperation happens in a certain way with the purpose of that way leading to fairness.
