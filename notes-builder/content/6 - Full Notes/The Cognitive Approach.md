# The Cognitive Approach
**Details**
What this approach differs from other psychological schools is that it uses the person's reports of his ideas, feelings and wishes as the base of the data, different from how others regard these thoughts as merely unimportant, or a "screen" to hide deeper meanings. At times we will need to examine these data to change the intricate pattern of meanings. This must then be tried in real situations and reworked until it fits the patient's conception of reality.

To understand how an emotion connects to an event, one must identify the difference between the dictionary meaning of something and its personal or private meaning. This is basically the objective meaning, without any personal interpretation and such. Whilst the other is the personal meaning, like how one thinks "They hate me" when they get bullied and such. Although they are aware of this, they keep it hidden because revealing it would only bring more displeasure. Special meanings happen when the event touches an important of someone's life, but of course are frequently kept hidden.

These are often unrealistic because they normally don't check the authenticity of these thoughts. In fact, when patients reveal such meanings to therapists, it is usually the first time they get to examine these thoughts and consider their validity. And most of the time, their interpretations are misinterpretations, which depart from reality and yet they constitute the cognitive distortions that are the core of emotional disorders.
