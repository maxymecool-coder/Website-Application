# Slave's Evilness
**Details**
Nietzsche furthermore argued about the reversal of attribute of slaves. We have established in [[Slave Morality]] how they were considered "Good." Now, this goodness doesn't address the useless suffering that these slaves have to endure without ending, and so the priests have two solutions to this. They either do therapy to the slaves by making them confess and such and so on (Innocent Means) which numbs the pain, or they give the slaves' suffering meaning (Guilty Means). To give them meaning, the Priest fuses the psychophysical pain of Bad Conscience with the moral concept of Evil in Nietzsche previous essays and turns it into the Slave interpreting their own instincts as evil. This saves the Slave from suicidal nihilism (the suffering now makes sense), but it traps them in a pathological narrative where their own natural instincts are the enemy.

Furthermore, as mentioned in [[The Phenomenology of Bad Conscience]], us humans have the ability to perform "Ascetic Practices" which are activities that we do to refrain ourselves from making it more difficult for us to reach a higher goal (this is a theory similar or primitive to [[Deterministic Autonomy and Second-Order Desires]]).

The Priest's idea pushes this to the extreme, by forcing an Ascetic Ideal instead, universally claiming every turn of the will to be extremely evil and a failure of morals. But in conclusion, this does not solve the problem fully, it instead introduces a descending spiral of Vitality.

The "cure" the Priest prescribes for the "sin" of willfulness is to crush the will—to engage in self-abnegation, fasting, guilt, and penance. However, the Slave is already sick and weak; that is why they are suffering in the first place. The Priest’s cure (attacking the self) attacks the very reservoirs of energy the Slave needs to get better. By turning aggression against their own remaining instincts, the Slave becomes sicker and weaker. As they become sicker, their suffering increases. As their suffering increases, the Priest interprets this as a need for _more_ penance and self-flagellation. Thus, the remedy aggravates the disease. Christianity, in Nietzsche’s view, is a systematic machine for maximizing degeneration by turning the human animal against its own biological foundations.
