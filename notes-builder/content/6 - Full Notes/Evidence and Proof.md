# Evidence and Proof
**Details**
When distinguishing between evidence and proof that is central to rhetorical practice, we have to understand what these two terms entail: Evidence is raw material — it exists, but it doesn't yet mean anything. It requires interpretation, assembly, arrangement. It must be made to fit an argument. Proof, by contrast, implies something settled, something that compels assent by its very nature. Evidence can be interpreted in many ways, which is why data is sent to many analysts. Even though this is the case, not every interpretation is good or accurate. Nonetheless, the fact that this exists is enough for rhetoricians, as they only need to understand how the same set of facts can support radically different conclusions depending on how they are framed, sequenced, and contextualized.
