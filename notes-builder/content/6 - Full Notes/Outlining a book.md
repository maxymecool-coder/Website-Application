# Outlining a book
**Details**
This is another part where the art of outlining a book will be discussed. Firstly we must divide the author plan into major parts, talk about what they are, and then move on. Secondly we divide these parts into sections, which considers X, the second considers Y, and so on and so forth, then we go to points,… 

This is long sure, but it is merely a formula. It may look like an impossible amount of work, but it is merely a sort of thing good reader does habitually, and hence easily and naturally. He won’t need to write it out, verbalize it even, but when asked he can say something close to the formula talked about. 

A good rule always describes the ideal performance. But one doesn’t need to be ideal to be skilled, he can merely approximate the rule and still consider himself good, great even. And so one should be satisfied to make a rough approximation of the rule above. Even the best readers evade expending their skills all on only some books, they make a fairly close approximation to the requirements of this rule for a few only. And they are satisfied with that. The detailedness and such will also vary with books.
