# The development of the Limbic Brain
**Details**
From the earlier days, neuroanatomists researched the brain of rodents and found that 40% of it was dedicated to olfactory functions, and so they called it the rhinencephalon, or the "nose-brain." The more modern approach however, concluded that this part was the emotions of the brain, and so it would be the limbic system.

The perspective that settled this came from neither of the fields above, but of course ethology. Ethology proved that the reason the rat's brain was so dedicated to olfactory functions is because that is its world of inciting emotions through processing the cues of its activities. Therefore, for a rat, the "nose-brain" _is_ the emotional brain. This insight unified the two perspectives, showing that the limbic system's primary inputs are whatever sensory information is most relevant to the emotional life of that particular species. In an auditory species like a songbird, the limbic system is more of an "ear-brain," and in electric fish, it receives powerful inputs from electroreceptors. The limbic system is not fundamentally a "nose-brain," but rather the brain's hub for processing the most emotionally salient sensory information, whatever that may be.
