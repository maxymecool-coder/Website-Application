# The Dandelion Theory of Democracy
**Details**
Building upon the work of Huntington, sociologists believed that the economy and political constitutions of a country mattered a lot together. Only if the political aspect of the country is evolved and worked on can the economic factor then follow. 

Huntington critiqued the common American belief that good, democratic institutions will spontaneously emerge once a population is given "freedom"—as if they were weeds in a garden. This was the flawed model applied in post-invasion Iraq, where the U.S. focused on writing a constitution but neglected to build the actual machinery of a functioning state. The same situation can be seen in Mexico (although one can argue how the US is tolerating Mexico and so on).

A few more examples would be the difference between Cuba and South Vietnam. Of course Cuba was the enemy of the US back then and South Vietnam was their ally. The US tried hard to get Fidel Castro to be assassinated, but they failed due to how the people in Cuba were so supervised (and also treated really nicely). At the end though, they had a rock solid foundation on their political landscape.

On the other hand, South Vietnam was the opposite, they did not have good politics, no structure, their political institution was practically fighting themselves for power. North Vietnam though, as authoritarian as it is, it still was a tighter political system which allowed them to win.
