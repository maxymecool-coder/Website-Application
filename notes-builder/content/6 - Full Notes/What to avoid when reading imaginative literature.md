# What to avoid when reading imaginative literature
**Details**
First of all, it is necessary to grasp the basic differences between expository and imaginative literature as it will explain why we read them differently. 

The most prevalent is their purposes, expository works convey knowledge, imaginative ones try to communicate an experience itself. And because they differ from their purposes, they also differ between practically everything else. Our experiences are through senses and imagination, whilst our judgement and reasoning are intellectual. This doesn’t mean we must separate the two of these things when reading, but rather, we emphasize one more when reading that specific type of literature. And so we come to the most important point. Don’t try to resist the effect that a work of imaginative literature has on you. 

In reading expository works, we had to be vigilant, ready to spot, catch anything that is important and maintain a neutral stance. In imaginative literature, we must allow ourselves to be immersed into the story, only then can we truly understand it. 

We owe much to the expository literature—the philosophy, science, mathematics—that has shaped the real world in which we live. But we could not live in this world if we were not able, from time to time, to get away from it. We do not mean that imaginative literature is always, or essentially, escapist. In the ordinary sense of that term, the idea is contemptible. If we must escape from reality, it should be to a deeper, or greater, reality. This is the reality of our inner life, of our own unique vision of the world. To discover this reality makes us happy; the experience is deeply satisfying to some part of ourselves we do not ordinarily touch. In any event, the rules of reading a great work of literary art should have as an end or goal just such a profound experience. The rules should clear away all that stops us from feeling as deeply as we possibly can. 

Now we come to their differences in words, the imaginative writer tries to maximize the latent ambiguities of words, in order thereby to gain all the richness and force in their multiple meanings. He uses metaphors to construct, different from how the logical writer uses concise words to construct a singular meaning. 

Finally, we must not criticize such books by the standards of truth and consistency of general knowledge, we must do so by its logical progression. We want a story that could’ve happened in the world that was built.
