# Genealogy as Entanglement
**Details**
Nietzsche’s Genealogy of Morality radically departs from the 19th-century tendency to write "Origin Stories." Traditional histories (like Christian or Marxist narratives) trace a phenomenon back to a single, pure source—such as the teachings of Jesus or primitive communism—and view history as a linear development or degeneration from that point. Accordingly to his account of [[The Myth of Moral Coherence]], Nietzsche argued that history is rather a contingent convergence of distinct, unrelated causal lines.

This explains the structure of the book itself. The three essays in _Genealogy_ do not tell a continuous story.

- **Essay 1** traces the origin of "Good and Evil" to **Aristocratic/Slave conflict** (Resentment).
    
- **Essay 2** traces the origin of "Guilt" (_Schuld_) to **commercial contract law** and debt (_Schulden_).
    
- **Essay 3** traces the origin of "Ascetic Ideals." Crucially, the concept of "Guilt" (Essay 2) did not arise from the concept of "Evil" (Essay 1). They have completely different origins—one in class warfare, the other in marketplace economics. The genius of Christianity was to accidentally or forcefully entangle these separate strands, fusing "debt" with "moral badness" to create the "Bad Conscience." Therefore, genealogy is the study of how disparate historical accidents knot together to form the present, rather than a search for a singular "meaningful origin."
