# Framing Effects
**Details**
Kahneman and Tversky demonstrated that mathematically identical choices produce opposite preferences depending on whether outcomes are framed as gains or losses. Given 600 people threatened by an earthquake: "200 will be saved" (gain frame) versus "400 will die" (loss frame) yield identical outcomes, yet 72% choose the sure option under gain framing while 78% choose the risky option under loss framing. This shows that for gains, we are risk-averse; for losses, we are risk seeking, we would prefer the gamble that might prevent the bad thing from happening. This shows that anyone, and anyone at all, can change the way they frame their words and propositions to reverse public preference. The deeper principle from prospect theory is that psychological distance is nonlinear: the subjective difference between 0 and 1 death is far greater than between 100 and 101 deaths, explaining Stalin's observation that "one death is a tragedy, a hundred deaths is a statistic." Even when people see both framings simultaneously and recognize they're identical, the _feeling_ differs—gain and loss frames activate different emotional responses that resist logical override.
