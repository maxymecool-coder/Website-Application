# The specifics of QuAIC
**Details**
The paper draws a sharp and important contrast between the campus threat management model prevalent in Australia and the model developed in the United States. The US model, born from tragedies like the Virginia Tech shooting, is primarily focused on the assessment and prevention of "targeted violence" and mass casualty incidents. The Australian model, as exemplified by the SCU at Victoria University, has a much broader remit. It focuses on "risk screening, primary prevention, early intervention case management, and psychological support" for a wide spectrum of problem behaviors, most of which fall short of targeted violence.  

The QuAIC is presented as an instrument perfectly adapted to this Australian context. Its dual-axis framework is designed to effectively triage the very behaviors—bullying, stalking, cyber-abuse, and harassment—that constitute the bulk of the SCU's caseload. The paper also highlights structural differences in the Australian model, such as the "separation of threat assessment and management functions" between the front-line SCU and the higher-level SAT team. While this can sometimes "compromise case management cohesion" due to case hand-offs, it also allows for specialized roles, with the SCU focusing on broad intake and support and the SAT team focusing on high-risk adjudication. The QuAIC is the ideal engine for this kind of separated model, as its primary function is to reliably sort cases and direct them to the appropriate channel.
