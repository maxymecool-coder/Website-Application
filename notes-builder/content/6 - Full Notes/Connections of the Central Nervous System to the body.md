# Connections of the Central Nervous System to the body
**Details**
The CNS connects with the body through nerve fibers that conduct excitation towards the brain and spinal cord or away from them. These fibers that transmit info from the sense organs to the brain and spinal cord are called afferent nerves. The fibers that transmit the messages from the CNS to the effectors, the muscles and glands are the organs of actions, or efferent nerves.

The spinal cord is a trunk line of the fibers: some are efferent, some are afferent, with still others just making local internal connections within the cord. These connections can initiate or regulate movements on their own and modulate the sensory info as it gets to the brain.

Not all nerves between the body and brain are through the spinal cord though. Separate nerves, twelve pairs in all, enter and exit from the hindbrain (the parts that connects with the spinal cord) and poke through holes in the skull. These are cranial nerves, and they have both functions of the above. They control the movements of the head and neck, carry sensations and regulate various glandular secretions in the head, control life-sustaining visceral functions, like digestion and excretion.

All this culminate to the peripheral nervous system, with two divisions, called the somatic, which controls the skeleton and transmission from the sense organs, and autonomic, set of spinal and cranial nerves that regulate and inform the brain about the viscera.
