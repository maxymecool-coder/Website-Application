# ACE Study
**Details**
Felitti-chief of the Kaiser Permanente's Department of Preventive Medicine in San Diego (1985) conducted a large study with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). The result was the monumental investigation of Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE).

More than 50000 Kaiser patients came through for an evaluation, filling out a questionnaire. Felitti and Anda spent more than a year developing 10 new questions to define categories of adverse childhood experiences, including physical, sexual, emotional neglect, and family dysfunction, such as having had parents who were divorced, mentally ill, addicted, or in prison.

Each yes answer was scored as one point, so possible scores ranged from 0 to 10. For example, someone who experienced frequent verbal abuse, alcoholic mother, and whose parents divorced would have an ACE score of three. Of the 2/3 of the respondents, 87% scored 2 or more. 1/6 had 4 or higher. The respondents were mostly white, middle class, middle aged, well educated, and financially secure, yet 1/3 reported having none of the adverse experiences.

And in short, Felitti and his team found that adverse experiences are interrelated, even though they're usually studied separately. A family whose brother is in prison but everything else is fine doesn't happen. Incidents of abuse never stand-alone (they have cause and effect).

The important thing out of this study is that when children matured, they didn't "outgrow" the effects of their childhood trauma, but rather it gets lost in time and concealed by shame, secrecy and social taboo (or their own fear due to fear of people getting mad at them for saying something so supposedly crazy).
