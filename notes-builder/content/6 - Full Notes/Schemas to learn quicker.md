# Schemas to learn quicker
**Details**
Learning is not always a slow, trial-and-error process. The brain dramatically accelerates the acquisition of new information when it can fit that information into a pre-existing knowledge structure, or **schema**. A schema is a mental framework or map for a particular concept or activity. Schemas can be big or tin depending on things that one interacts with the world.

This was demonstrated in an experiment where rats learned the location of a food reward in a complex environment. The first time, it took many trials. However, when presented with a new food in a new location days later, the rats learned the new layout in a fraction of the time. They had developed a schema for "how this game works," which made subsequent learning far more efficient.

This explains why an expert golfer can remember every shot of a round while a novice remembers almost none. The expert has a rich, detailed schema for the game of golf, providing a mental scaffold upon which to hang each new memory. The novice has no such framework, and the experiences remain a disorganized blur. Building these schemas—through education, practice, and experience—is fundamental to developing expertise and allows for the rapid, almost effortless, encoding of new, related knowledge.
