# Liberalism and Theory of Tolerance
**Details**
When talking about mode of politics or the method of operation of a country, it particularly falls into the grounds of politics and everything related to it. Furthermore, in this sense, it is not just a constitution of policies, but rather the very philosophical stance that concerns with the nature and justification of constitutional democratic institutions. All the writers examined — Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Hume, Kant, Mill, Marx — belong to the modern democratic tradition of thought, and all except Marx count as liberal.

Rawls grounded this in historical examples: the wars of religion in the 16th and 17th centuries. These conflicts could not be resolved through military forces or theological arguments alone. Instead, they needed some principle of toleration, to cope with religious pluralism (coinciding with the [[The Epistemic Explosion of The 16th Century]]).
