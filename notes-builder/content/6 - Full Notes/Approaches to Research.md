# Approaches to Research
**Details**
Firstly is the case study method, which is incredibly good for rare things, something that is hard to find a lot of people to test on. This could then set the stage for future research, it can be used for things like trying a new treatment, finding out a new mental disorder and so on. It usually has a very small samples, down to one person. The drawback is that it is quite prone to generalization, as one person or multiple can expand the symptoms of such patients to everyone. This shows that it cannot prove a hypothesis, but it can prove it. Still, it is used because of the rich information that it can give us.

Another method is correlation, which is incredibly popular in academics. As stated in psychopathology, a truly random experiment is quite difficult, as the methodologies just take a lot of effort and often is not worth it. Correlation coefficient is the value for us to determine if two variables correlate with each other synonymously or autonomously. It is denoted by the symbol r, which range between -1.00 and +1.00, and it measures the magnitude and relationship of the two variables. For example, some dumbass correlation like if you're dumber, your girlfriend is more pretty (I don't actually know if this is true). Maybe the correlation will be negative, which mean if you're smarter then your girl is more pretty. But we do not know that, one causes the other, causation does not mean correlation.

A form of correlation is epidemiological studies, which studies the distribution of disorders in a population. That means data are gathered about the rates of a disorder and correlates in a large sample. This helps us see the following factors of a disorder:

1. Prevalence: proportion of people that has the disorder during their lifetime.
2. Incidence: proportion of people who develop new cases of the disorder in some period, usually in a year.
3. Risk factors: likelihood of developing the disorder (variables).
   
These studies only represent the sample of the population being studied - meaning if you test the population of Berkeley for a certain mental disorder for example, that would only represent the population in Berkeley, not other states.

Lastly, experiments, these are the the most powerful tools for determining causal relationships. It will include a **random assignment** of participants to conditions, meaning the participants will be split into different groups randomly, one group will manipulate the **independent variable**, and the measurement of a **dependent variable**. The group that does not have the dependent variable changed is called the control group.
