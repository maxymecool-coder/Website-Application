# Other books as extrinsic aids
**Details**
This advice is applied to “great books”. Which people usually excitingly embark on conquering and fairly soon, finding a hopeless inadequacy in reading it (because they couldn’t). One reason is that they didn’t know how to read a book well, but more than that, is that they haven’t read the books surrounding or closely related to it. 

Many great books are related, even more so they are written in certain orders of the author, or other authors. For example, a later author being influenced by an earlier one and proceeding to make books that have the ideas of that before him. 

This is similar to terms and propositions as we are gaining value from the context of reading books as if we were interpreting the terms and propositions from words and sentences. It is also often that the great books are prolonged conversation… As great authors are also great readers, they conversed with other authors as well, through books. We can join into this conversation obviously, but we must read the great books together and respecting their order (timely as conversations are in real time and the natural chronological order helps immensely with that).
