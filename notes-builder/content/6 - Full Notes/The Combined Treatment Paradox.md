# The Combined Treatment Paradox
**Details**
The logical clinical instinct is to combine treatments: "If CT works and Meds work, surely CT + Meds is best." However, studies actually show that this can result in the drugs undermining the effects of CT. In a long-term trial, patients receiving a combined treatment expressed no greater benefit after withdrawal compared to those only using medications.

The mechanism likely relates to "Hebb’s Dictum": _neurons that fire together, wire together._ Based on Steve Maier’s rat models, the cortex (vmPFC) needs to fire in response to the brainstem’s stress signal to build the inhibitory pathway. But because the medication dampens the brainstem's stress response system, they're not actually experiencing any stress. And as suggested in [[Why are treatments of Depression different]], we NEED stress to teach the brain. Another study that reinforced this is Dave Barlow's panic disorder study, in which patients using CT + placebo retained their gains whilst those with CT + medication lost them.
