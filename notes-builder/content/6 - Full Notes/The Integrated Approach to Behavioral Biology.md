# The Integrated Approach to Behavioral Biology
**Details**
To avoid the fallacies of categorical thinking, behavior must be analyzed as a seamless process through time. The proposed method is to start with a behavior and work backward in nested layers of explanation:

1. **A few seconds before:** What neural activity caused the behavior?
    
2. **Seconds to minutes before:** What environmental stimuli triggered that neural activity?
    
3. **Hours to days before:** What hormonal states changed the sensitivity to those stimuli?
    
4. **Weeks to months before:** How has experience and environment reshaped the brain's structure (neuroplasticity)?
    
5. **From conception onward:** How did genetics and fetal life shape the brain and body?
    
6. **Centuries to millennia before:** How did culture and ecology shape the pressures on the population?
    
7. **Millions of years before:** What evolutionary pressures shaped the genes?
    

In this model, there are no separate "buckets." Each level is simply the most convenient way of describing the sum total of all the influences that came before it. A "hormone" is the physical manifestation of a "gene" being expressed, which itself is a product of "evolution."
