# Sets and the Axiom of Extensionality
**Details**
A "set" is rock bottom basic modern mathematics; it is a primitive notion that cannot be defined by simpler terms, only described by synonyms such as class, collection, or totality. A defining feature of a set is its indifference to the relatedness of its members, for example something like Socrates' nose, the number 19, and Mark Twain's sense of humor without contradiction.

This identity is extended by the Axiom of Extensionality, which dictates that it is solely defined by its members, not by the method used to describe it. This distinguishes sets from properties. For instance, the property "creature with a heart" is conceptually distinct from "creature with kidneys." However, if every creature with a heart also has kidneys, the _set_ of creatures with hearts is identical to the _set_ of creatures with kidneys. Consequently, because identity is tied strictly to membership, the order of elements and the repetition of names in a list are irrelevant; $\{a, b\}$ is identical to $\{b, a, a\}$.
