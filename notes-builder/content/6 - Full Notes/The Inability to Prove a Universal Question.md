# The Inability to Prove a Universal Question
**Details**
A core idea in the functionalist argument is about "Why is there inequality?" Due to the nature of scientific experiments, we need variation, but in our world, we cannot just find a society that does not have inequality, as it is practically impossible. That does not mean that we give up on the idea entirely, rather we ask a question that is more practical, and actually can be verified.

For example, the incest taboo, one cannot get a firm explanation on why this taboo exists because there are so many reasons that can be said, maybe because it is a genetical nightmare from the view of someone who is educated, or that is refrains a family from expanding their grasp on the overall community and power from society because they are only allowed to care for the people in their family.

To change this, we must turn the universal idea into a variable one. We do not try to force or find a society where taboo does not exist, we find the varieties of it, like how many bloodlines, or how far bloodlines should be for them to marry each other, is it completely forbidden to the sense that people in a family should literally not interact with each other (African societies).

This variation allows us to ask testable questions. We can now investigate _why_ the scope of the taboo changes. Do societies with complex property inheritance rules have broader incest prohibitions to manage alliances? Does the taboo shrink as societies become more anonymous and less reliant on kinship? By focusing on the variation, we can test theories against evidence.

This same logic applies to inequality. Asking "Why is there inequality?" is a dead end. Instead, we should ask, "Under what conditions do societies become _more_ or _less_ unequal?" This reframing allows for real comparison and testing, moving beyond untestable stories and toward genuine social science.
