# BDNF and Memory
**Details**
BDNF, or Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor, is a neurochemical that the brain releases whenever we are doing a cardio-heavy exercise of some sort. This is the most important chemical to increasing our focus and memory in our neural circuits. Normally, if we move for just 10 minutes (which is already beneficial), the brain releases dopamine, serotonin, and noradrenaline, regulating our moods. BDNF works this by acting as a growth factor for the hippocampus, allowing it to grow new cells (neurogenesis), a process that occurs in humans well into the ninth decade of life.

There are two pathways that BDNF works through our body, the first being the muscles; when striated muscles work, they release proteins called myokines, which cross the blood-brain barrier and stimulate BDNF production. The second pathway is through the liver; exercise is a physical stressor that causes the liver to release a ketone called beta-hydroxybutyrate, which also crosses the blood-brain barrier to trigger BDNF.

**Supporting Content**
These effects can last for a maximum of 2 hours and is recommended to happen in a 30-45 minute period to truly get the benefits.
