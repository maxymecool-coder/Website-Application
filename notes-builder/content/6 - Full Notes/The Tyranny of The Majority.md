# The Tyranny of The Majority
**Details**
As society moved towards democratic, industrial systems, Mill realized that the primary threat to individual liberty was no longer from individuals who simply had too much power, but rather from the majority and the moral coercion of the masses. To draw a definitive boundary between the rights of the individual and the authority of society, Mill introduces the Principle of Liberty, widely known as the Harm Principle. It posits that the only legitimate reason society can interfere with the liberty of an individual, to prevent the harm of others, so to say.

Mill's principle functions by strictly excluding three specific categories of reasoning from public and legal justification. Paternalistic reasons (cannot coerce someone "for their own good"). You can't force them even if you can advise or persuade them (though this line is fairly thin so to say). Second, society cannot affect or restrict an individual based on ideals of excellence of perfectionism. Third, society cannot restrict individuals based on mere _preferences or disgust_ (repressing behavior simply because the majority does not like it). This effectively allows people to maintain their core liberties.
