# Graphing satisfaction and life
**Details**
Plotting time against how good a life is at that time, we would see that everything turns on where the curve sits relative to that line, not where it used to be or relative to the people nearby.

This would mean that a life can decline permanently and a lot, but still be worth living due to it not hitting negative values yet. Getting worse is not the same as being worse than nothing (though it can feel that way). Kagan is blunt that this is the ordinary structure of the error. Most people mistake a drop for permanence and sense that life isn't worth living anymore.
