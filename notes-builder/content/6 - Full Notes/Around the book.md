# Around the book
**Details**
The structure of a book and its story is the skeleton that is hidden between its covers, how it is pieced together to become a well-written book is the flesh, the clothes of it. The reader doesn’t have to tear through all this to get at the firm structure that underlies it, but they must see the book with x-ray eyes. 

This leads into the second rule of analytical reading. Rule 2: State the unity of the whole book in a single sentence, or at most a few sentences (a short paragraph). This means that one must summarize what the book is about as short as possible, not what kind of book it is (rule 1). This kind of “about” is colloquial, it is what the author is up to, what he is trying to do with the book. 

Continuously, this connects to Rule 3: Set forth the major parts of the book, show how these are organized into a whole, by being ordered to one another and to the unity of the whole. The only way one can grasp the complex unity of a book is if one knows how it is one. Considering many parts of it, how they are organized and so on. And so we come to the point that a book is like a house, and its parts are like rooms, they all have different furniture, purposes, but they are not independent and separate. They are all connected and missing neither of them will make the house unlivable. 

This also corresponds to the readability of books, the most readable books are like the most functional houses, they have the most intelligible structure, they are complex but in that is an even greater simplicity because they are organized, unified.
