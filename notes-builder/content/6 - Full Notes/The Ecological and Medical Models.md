# The Ecological and Medical Models
**Details**
The impairment model, aka an ecological model, argues that behavior is not inherently disordered but rather it is significantly impairing an individual's life. This makes it so that the abnormality dependent on how good of a fit the behaviors are to the person rather than social norms or statistical norms. The model works profoundly correct in how it personalizes the feelings and personal suffering rather than just the data.

Next up is the medical model, which argues that mental illnesses are like biomedical illnesses, that it is a problem of the brain. The ultimate goal is to identify objective biological markers—lesions, neurotransmitter imbalances, or faulty neural circuits—that cause the disorder. This approach promises scientific objectivity and holds the hope of reducing stigma by framing these conditions as legitimate illnesses. However, it faces significant hurdles. First, for most mental disorders, we lack a definitive "lab test" like a blood test or a simple brain scan. Second, even in medicine, complex illnesses like heart disease are multifactorial, resulting from an interplay of genetics, lifestyle, and environment, not a single cause. And lastly, even if it is defined, it does not prevent stigma, it just changes from a moral one to a fundamental one.
