# Sternberg's Triarchic Theory of Intelligence
**Details**
Spearman's [[The Psychometric Approach]] has been criticized to be "monarchic" as it proposes "g" as a monarch above all the specialized ability. In this department, Robert Sternberg attempted to go beyond it by proposing a triarchic theory with three processes in mind: (a) cognitive processes, (b) identifying situations that require intelligence, and (c) using that intelligence in practical ways. 

This model improves upon the [[Gardner's theory of multiple intelligences]] by actually having empirical research to back it up. In the studies, it was found out that children in rural Kenya were found out to have incredibly informal knowledge of herbal medicines. The children who performed best on these tests of knowledge of their world did worst relative to the tests of formal academic subjects. This is because their families probably valued these knowledge and skills much more than the formalized knowledge. Moreover, since tests did not account for this type intelligence or the fact that the mastery of the academic intelligence was simply undeveloped compared to the others.

And from that example, Robert Sternberg introduced his theory of intelligence. Analytical intelligence, which is the ability to complete problem-solving tasks such as those in tests; creative intelligence is the ability to synthesize and apply existing knowledge to deal with unusual and new situations; practical intelligence is our ability to adapt to everyday life, or streets smarts.
