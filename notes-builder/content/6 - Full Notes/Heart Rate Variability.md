# Heart Rate Variability
**Details**
Every time we breathe, our body balances between two nervous systems. When we inhale, the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) speeds up the heart. When we exhale, the parasympathetic nervous system (PNS) slows it down. These small, steady fluctuations in heart rate are called heart rate variability (HRV).

Good HRV means the heart adjusts easily to changes. It helps us stay calm when faced with frustrations and small setbacks. With good HRV, we can assess situations clearly without panicking or rushing into impulsive reactions. It keeps both mind and body flexible and stable.

Poor HRV shows up when these fluctuations are missing. People with poor HRV often feel mentally scattered, overreact emotionally, and make poor decisions under pressure. Studies have found that low HRV is linked to greater risks of anxiety, depression, and physical illness.

The good news is that HRV can be improved. Simple practices like slow breathing, meditation, or mindfulness training help strengthen the natural rhythms between the SNS and PNS. In time, this builds a more stable emotional core—and a stronger, healthier heart.
