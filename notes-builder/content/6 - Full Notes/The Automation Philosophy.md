# The Automation Philosophy
**Details**
We can continue on and on about how this amazing aircraft manages and executes the maneuvers, how simple and elegant its cockpit, displays, and mechanisms are. Frankly, the system of the jet is extremely interesting cause of how it basically interacts with everything the aircraft does. But also because of this software, there exists a possible failure mode: coding errors. A mismatch between the software’s model of the aircraft and its actual physical state can lead to PIO or Pilot Induced Oscillation.  In the example of the F-22 crash discussed, the software did not correctly account for the "gains" (sensitivity) required when the aircraft was in afterburner versus military power. This caused the pilot's inputs to become out of phase with the aircraft's response; the pilot would correct a pitch motion, but the computer would overreact, leading to a divergent oscillation and eventually a ground impact.

This philosophy exists due to how machines are capable of executing and pushing the plane to far higher limits than human can, as it doesn’t have restrictions like glocs and such. The only reason humans are still controlling is because of their decision making skills, which AIs cannot replicate just yet.
