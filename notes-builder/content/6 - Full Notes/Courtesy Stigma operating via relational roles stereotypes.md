# Courtesy Stigma operating via relational roles stereotypes
**Details**
In his theory, Goffman’s courtesy stigma also extends prejudice and discrimination towards individuals affiliated with a stigmatized person. The relational effects correlate with how closely affiliated the person is to the stigmatized individual. The effects differ for each role.

Parents would bear the brunt of onset blame, stereotyped as incompetent caregivers or historically labeled as causing psychiatric disorders. Whether through psychoanalytic frameworks or updated biomedical models, the blame still lies with the parents.

Siblings and spouses would then suffer offset blame, meaning they would be held responsible for the symptom relapse, treatment non-adherence, etc. For offspring, they would be at risk of contamination stereotypes, where peers and clinicians assume the child is tainted developmentally and psychologically.

Courtesy stigma operates along two axes: public avoidance and self-directed shame. Family members frequently report higher distress concerning the vicarious stigma borne by the patient than the direct discrimination directed at themselves.
