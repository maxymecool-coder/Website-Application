# The Forebrain
**Details**
The human being has a forebrain that covers all of our midbrain and half of our hindbrain. The part that is on the surface and all wrinkled as we see in models and such is called the mammalian cortex, and it is extremely important.

Like all the other parts, the forebrain is bilaterally symmetrical. It is obvious on the surface of it as it shows a deep front-to-back cleavage called the longitudinal fissure. On either side of this fissure, and underneath the cortex, there is the rest of the forebrain, including numerous subcortical structures. There are four that should be mentioned here:

- The thalamus, buried deep inside the forebrain and shaped like a fist atop the midbrain. It contains a number of centers that act as relay stations for nearly all the sensory information going into the cortex.

- Secondly, the hypothalamus, which is just beneath it, it is intimately involved in controlling behaviors like feeding, drinking, body temperature, sexual activities.

- Astride the thalamus in each hemisphere is the basal ganglia. It is crucial in regulating muscular contractions during movement. There are many disorders that come from these cells dying and just not being sufficient like Parkinson's and Huntington's.

- The last party that covers literally everything and lies beneath the cortex is the limbic system, which contains the amygdala and hippocampus, it has close ties to many of the parts of the brain. It is primarily involved in emotional and motivational activities, some aspects of learning and memory.
