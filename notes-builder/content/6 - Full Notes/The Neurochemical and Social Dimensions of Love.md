# The Neurochemical and Social Dimensions of Love
**Details**
The motivational drive of love is not monolithic and can be distinguished from lust at a neurochemical level. Scientific evidence suggests that the brain pathways involved in romantic love are closely related to those involved in parent-child attachment, utilizing opioid chemicals that the brain naturally generates. In contrast, lust is more directly linked to sex hormones like testosterone and androgens. This distinction supports the idea that romantic love may have evolved as a social mechanism to channel the more primal sex drive in a way that is compatible with civilization. Unchecked lust, as seen in the reproductive behaviors of animals like spiders, is not conducive to building complex, stable social structures. Romantic love, by co-opting the powerful bonding mechanism seen between parent and child, provides a framework for the long-term, cooperative relationships that are foundational to human society. This echoes Freudian concepts where civilization is built upon the sublimation and channeling of basic drives.
