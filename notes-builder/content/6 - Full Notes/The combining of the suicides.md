# The combining of the suicides
**Details**
Durkheim asserts that while the egoistic, altruistic, and anomic types of suicide are analytically distinct, they are not always found in a state of purity in actual experience. The different social causes of suicide can simultaneously affect the same individual, leading to composite varieties where the characteristics of several types are united in a single act. There is a peculiar affinity between egoism and anomy, as they are often two different aspects of the same social state; an individual detached from society (egoism) lacks the social hold needed for regulation (anomy). This can produce a mixed state where reflective sadness alternates with transports of desire. Similarly, anomy can be associated with altruism, as a single crisis may both disrupt an individual's equilibrium and intensify their devotion to a collective cause, blending exasperation with a sense of duty. Even the contraries of egoism and altruism can combine, as seen in Stoic suicide, which blends the apathy of the egoist with the sense of duty of the altruist.
