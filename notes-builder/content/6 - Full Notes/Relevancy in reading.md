# Relevancy in reading
**Details**
There are two types of this that will help you read difficult books. As said before, common experience and special experience. These are either available to all because they are alive, special experience must be sought after and available to those who do so. 

Though common experience does not mean universal. The experience of being a child of parents, for example, is not universal, for some are orphans from birth. However, family life is nonetheless common experience,… 

Common experience is mostly relevant to the reading of fiction or philosophy as both of those kinds of books depends on the own experience of the reader. Whilst special experience is to the scientific works. You must have this to understand and judge the arguments in those books. 

Both of the experiences are relevant to the reading of history books. This is because it partakes in both. It has characters and a plot like fiction, but it is like science in the sense that the information the historian bases on is special. To test if you’re utilizing your experiences correctly is a test of understanding: ask yourself if you’re utilizing your can give a concrete example of a point you understand.
