# Marx's Dialectic of Power and Alienation
**Details**
At the core of Marx's theory, there is a profound dialectical contradiction: as human history progresses, humankind becomes stronger, while the individual human being become progressively more powerless. This is the central idea, and tragedy of his model.

We can easily see this from examples of the feudalism to the capitalism society. The feudalism was constraint, we as humans function in our own countries, we were basically just locked, chained down by our connections, our relationships with our family, our language, nationality. But in capitalism, by cooperating, we have built marvelous things, invented numerous generational inventions all because of capitalism.

But on the other side, other problems arise, the alienation of the individual human, we do not have a choice for labor anymore, and that choice is not enforced by some knight or some government, instead, it is enforced by death. If we do not work, we will die, and our family does not have to help us legally, its just out a sense of pity, of moralities, perhaps. And our tasks at times are meaningless, repetitive, not worth our effort. We get manipulated, used, everybody tries to find loopholes to abuse their workers.

Thus, every advance in the power of humankind has been achieved through the increasing oppression and alienation of individual human beings. This growing tension between our collective strength and our individual powerlessness is the contradiction that Marx believed would ultimately lead to revolution.

**Supporting Content**
Moreover, Marx also argues that this integration of the human being into society and the group takes away their ability to produce the conditions of their own social life over time and takes away their ability for self-realization.
