# Learning of new knowledge
**Details**
Assuming the rhetorician point of view, learning would not be something simple but rather extremely difficult because of how it functions under these circumstances. This goes against Socrates' method in the sense that a person is trying to not teach their students about their point of view and such, but rather trying to get students to imitate different styles of thinking entirely from master thinkers like Nietzsche or  Foucault or Nabokov. This is because the style is not decoration, but rather the thought itself.

The rejection of Socrates also entails a rejection of the classroom drama of contradiction — the student who stands up, argues with the professor, and earns respect for their boldness. In a Socratic classroom, this dialectical opposition is the engine of learning. But contradiction, the lecturer insists, is "not interesting. It gets us nowhere." Saying "you're wrong" simply reverses polarity within the same circuit. What is interesting is making new things, being different — not negating the existing argument but escaping it entirely, taking the line of flight. The classroom should produce novelty, not opposition.
