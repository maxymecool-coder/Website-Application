# The Power of Others
**Details**
When placed into difficult moral situations, people may look for social proof - how people around them act - to then act. If others act, you should act, if others don't, then you shouldn't. We look at them not to find if they are doing or not, but to find out if its right to do so or not (since if it is then the majority would do ?). Also, something that also heavily relates to this is the Diffusion of Responsibility, which states that well, the more people in the room who has to make the decision, the less each will bear the responsibility. This encourages people who you normally wouldn't think would act a certain way to act that way because they carry less responsibility now.

For example, in a study done in the Reagan era by social psychologists to find out the effects of crowd cheering, they separated a recording of the presidential debate with the claps and laugh of the audience and one without. Now those who view the former version were significantly affected, believing Reagan to be suitable as a president because he is a funny person whilst the latter was not affected by his arguments/jokes without the audience (70% vs 45%). This shows that even if we don't realize it, our mind is still being affected by these things nonetheless.
