# Choice In Determinism and Free Will
**Details**
While thinking about this problem, it may be productive first to try and define the meaning of "choice" as it is one of the main terms involved throughout the discussion. People often semantically hijack this term to use to debate, even though it is quite unproductive to do so. Incompatibilists frequently argue that if a brain state is determined by prior causes, a "choice" is an illusion. This though, ignores the standard acquisition and utility of the world. We learn the concept of choices through daily cases and context, not from the deterministic probability of the world. This is to say that the word "choice" is inherently BUILT on grounds not considering determinism, and so we cannot use determinism to try and redefine the function of the word. The compatibilists, on the other hand, think that our choices are simply a process guided by internal desires and purposes, regardless of if it was predetermined or not. And so, causal determinism does not negate existence of choice, provided we do not require acausal origins.
