# Concepts of humane and inhumane
**Details**
Russell and Burch sought out to understood and basically defined what it meant for us as people experimenting on animals to be humane or inhumane. And so the concepts of humanity and inhumanity that they came up with is purely descriptive and empirical, meaning they will refer only to the objectively verifiable and measurable aspects of the process, not the ethical or psychological reasons of it all.

Inhumanity can be defined in many ways, and most of the time it is the ethical and philosophical aspects of it that matters in these discussions. But that is subjective, so due to that fact, we will instead define it like a metric, like the amount of distress that an animal can be subjected to, its mental states. The first of these states is the state of conflict, which is characterized to be when an animal is beset by 2 or more external forces, making them feel *fear*. This derives from the fact that their primary response to danger, flight, is neutralized, which is some of the same of humans and so it becomes a neuros or an acutely unpleasant state, similarly to anxiety. Although we cannot exactly quantify the levels of these states, we know they exist and there is a metric defining which is worse. Prolonged time in these states can make the animal have *physiologic stress syndrome*, affecting their body and systems.

But further than that, they also define inhumanity into categories as well, direct and contingent. Direct is defined by the fact that certain amounts of distress are completely unavoidable in the process of experimenting, or “the infliction of distress as an unavoidable consequence of the procedure employed, as such, even if it is conducted with perfect efficiency and completely freed of operations irrelevant to the object in view”. In contrast, contingent inhumanity is defined as “the infliction of distress as an incidental and inadvertent by-product of the use of the procedure, which is not necessary for its success.”

Finally, they also define it by the sum of the amount of distress in a group, like how the decreased amount of pain inflicted on one animal will reduce the sum total of pain that animals as a whole would have sustained. And so in certain situations, we would have choices to decide whether or not we want to inflict all the pain onto one or spread the pain out onto multiple (which can be applicable to overall to reduce the sum total distress).
