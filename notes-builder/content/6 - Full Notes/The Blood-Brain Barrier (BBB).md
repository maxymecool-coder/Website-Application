# The Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB)
**Details**
In the general circulatory system, capillaries have small pores or gaps in between them, allowing most small molecules to flow freely between blood and tissue. However, in the CNS, these blood vessels have no pores.

For a drug to be psychoactive, it has to be able to pass through this barrier. Meaning it has to pass by being actively transported or it has to be hydrophobic enough ([[The Lipid Bilayer Membrane]]). This principle explains the potency difference between **Morphine** and **Heroin**. Heroin is chemically modified morphine; the modification makes it more hydrophobic (oil-soluble), allowing it to cross the BBB much faster and in higher concentrations, creating a more intense "rush." Conversely, antibiotics like penicillin are hydrophilic and cannot cross the BBB, making them useless for treating brain infections.
