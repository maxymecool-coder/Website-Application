# Prefrontal Cortex in Toggling Stress Perception and Hierarchies
**Details**
The prefrontal cortex (PFC) is a marvel, toggling stress from harmful to beneficial via context and choice, influencing primitive systems like the amygdala and hypothalamus. It can rewire autonomic responses—type-A personalities dilate blood vessels in high-stress scenarios due to motivation, reversing typical constriction. This "switch" changes event polarity: voluntary challenges energize, involuntary ones drain.

Humans uniquely juggle hierarchies, using PFC to prioritize (e.g., excelling in softball despite a lowly job). It excuses our flaws situationally ("I was stressed") while judging others constitutionally, enabling self-deception. Individual differences are vast: optimal stress ranges from birdwatching to mercenaries. You can judge someone who is doing something rotten, then assume that they will always be rotten, has always been. Yet when you do the same thing, you may excuse yourself as it being situational, under the circumstances, so that you don't merge lanes with the person that you perceive as a rotten and bad person.

Toggling potential is enormous—reframe threats as opportunities, but it requires effort. The PFC abstracts over space/time, unlike animals, allowing infinite fear/love plug-ins. Harness via therapy or mindfulness to direct this power wisely.
