# The engineering argument of EP
**Details**
Since adaptive problems require different solutions and in most cases can only be implied by distinct mechanisms. This would mean that there are certain characteristics that would be necessary for each one. In contrast, a jack of all trades would be a master of none because they would have to sacrifice effectiveness for generality. So when there are multiple adaptive problems, one solution would be inferior to two specialized ones.

Be aware that this does not mean there is a specific solution for every problem because of the cost of this, the principles of self-organization (Kauffman, 1995), and physiological constraints. It only entails that within these constraints, engineering would consider specialized solutions to general ones as it would be less costly.

Moreover, we would have had to encountered many problems of this nature throughout generations that it is necessary that we create a module to solve it by cognitive mechanisms.
