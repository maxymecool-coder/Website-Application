# Snowballing of sadness and apathy
**Details**
Although the onset of depression may be sudden, its full development spreads over a certain timeframe. The symptoms of the depressed patient may gradually increase in intensity until he “hits rock bottom.” This gradual development is due to the repeat of the sense of loss being repeated and constituting a fresh experience which is added more.

The depressed person tends to extract elements suggestive of loss and to gloss over other features that are not consonant with, or are contradictory to, this interpretation. As a result of such “selective abstraction,” the patient overinterprets daily events in terms of loss and is oblivious to more positive interpretations; he is hypersensitive to stimuli suggestive of loss and is blind to stimuli representing gain. He shows the same type of selectivity in recalling past experiences. He is facile in recalling unpleasant experiences, but may “draw a blank” when questioned about positive experiences. This selectivity in memory has been demonstrated experimentally by Lishman (1972).

This “tunnel vision” makes it so that the patient becomes impermeable to stimuli that can arouse pleasure. Though he might be able to recognize his achievements, he might still not feel happy, or angry, or any emotions other than sadness. The same applies to any other distortion in thinking  from depression.

Whilst this usually results in sadness, the passive resignation may lead to apathy, as they feel like there is no point in trying so they stop trying.
