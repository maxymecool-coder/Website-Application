# The g and s factors of Spearman
**Details**
Based on his psychometric research, Charles Spearman proposed a two-factor theory of intelligence. He observed that performance across a wide range of seemingly unrelated mental tasks was positively correlated, leading him to infer the existence of a single, unifying intellectual capacity.

- **The g factor (general intelligence):** Spearman argued that a "general" ability, which he labeled **g**, underlies all intelligent behavior. It represents a common mental energy or power that is required for any cognitive task. A person's level of _g_ is seen as the primary determinant of their overall intellectual ability.
    
- **The s factor (specific abilities):** To account for the fact that correlations between tasks were not perfect, Spearman also proposed that each task requires a set of **s** factors, or "specific" abilities. These are abilities unique to a particular task, such as mechanical, musical, or logical skills.
    

In this "monarchic" theory, intelligence is a combination of one's general ability (_g_) and various specific abilities (_s_). While _g_ rules over all cognitive functions, the _s_ factors explain why an individual might excel in one area but not another, despite having the same level of general intelligence.
