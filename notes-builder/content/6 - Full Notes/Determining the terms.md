# Determining the terms
**Details**
From marking these words, you now have two main possibilities. Either the words are in a single sense throughout or two or more, shifting the meaning depending on the context. 

Your first instinct should be to try and determine if they have one or many meanings, then to see how they are related. At last, you can note the contexts of how they are used to determine the reason for the shift in meaning and so on, equalizing the flexibility of the author. 

But, I hear you ask, or not. How does one find out what the meanings are ? The answer, though simple, may appear unsatisfactory, it is that you have to discover the meaning by using the meanings of all the other words in the context that you do understand. (The words surrounding the special words are the contexts.). 

To illustrate this, consider definitions, stated in words. If you do not understand any of the words used in the definition, you will not be able to understand the word. But huh ? How does this help ? We know that a complex sentence has many parts, and the opposite of complex is simple, when we understand that a sentence has many parts of complex words and such, we know that that is what the author had intended. The more parts of a sentence, piece of writing you can put together, the easier it is to find the remaining parts, because there are fewer of them. (Like a jigsaw puzzle) 

Usually, a good puzzle is one that always fits everything. The same should be for a perfect book, but such book doesn’t exist. The better a book is, the more fruitfully the reader can interpret it. The opposite applies for bad books.
