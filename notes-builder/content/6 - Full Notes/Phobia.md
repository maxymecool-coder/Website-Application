# Phobia
**Details**
The anticipation of physical or psychological harm is confined to definable situations. If the patient can avoid such situations, then he does not feel threatened and may be tranquil. But if he may enter these situations perhaps out of necessity or their own desire to overcome it, he will experience the general effects of the anxiety-neurotic. The symptoms may include: intense anxiety, rapid breathing, palpitations, abdominal pains, difficulty in concentration and recall. Yet some find that their distress is milder than they expected - much less than what their avoidance warranted. Despite this very bland expression, they may still try to avoid it nonetheless. And reintegration or attempts to face the phobia does not help reduce any of the symptoms nor avoidance.

As said before of the anxiety-neurotic, a patient can experience these symptoms anywhere from mere verbal form or in some form of imagery. For example, a man entering a tunnel, and because he has a phobia of it, may imagine the tunnel collapsing upon itself and killing him even though it was an external event.
