# Internal Stressor
**Details**
These stresses are the kind that comes internally and consists of psychological phenomena as the demands the person places on themselves, their fears, and their self-reproaches. This self-stressing mechanism may be detected by one tapping into the "internal communication system." In some respects it is analogous to Freud's concept of the superego.

Most patients who consult a physician for a psychosomatic disorder do not present any evidence of specific external stress. They frequently appear “nervous” and often acquire the label “neurotic” or “psycho” (first in the physician’s eyes and then in their own). The physician often tells them they worry too much and take things too seriously. Such statements, instead of providing insight, generally reinforce the patient’s self-concept of being unstable and inferior.

The hard-driving businessman who develops a peptic ulcer provides a stereotypical example of proneness to psychosomatic illness. Although the same psychological configuration may lead to other psychosomatic syndromes such as hypertension or dermatitis, it is useful to consider this case as illustrative of psychosomatic disorders in general. The “executive ulcer” type sets high goals and drives himself and others in order to achieve them. His outward behavior reflects his system of goals and beliefs that lead to a constant state of tension. The momentum behind his work drive is his chronic concern that he will not reach his goals, or that he or his subordinates will make costly errors. He reacts to each new task with strong doubts. He usually exaggerates the importance and the difficulty of said task, and underestimates his capacity to deal with it.

Although as we can see there are absolutely no external pressure from the outside, his mere occupation is causing a detrimental amount of stress because of the way he is perceiving his work. As he interprets every tasks as a major confrontation with the result being failure, he is under continuous stress, causing his physiological systems to overload.

Even more so, these stresses do not just make the consequences much worse, but also increases the probability of them happening. There is no idea on why some develop these disorders whilst other only suffer psychologically.
