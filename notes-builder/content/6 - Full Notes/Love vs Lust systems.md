# Love vs Lust systems
**Details**
In our common understanding of intimacy, romantic love and sexual desire are often treated as a single, unified experience. However, the influential framework proposed by psychologist Lisa M. Diamond argues for a crucial distinction: love and lust are governed by two functionally independent biobehavioral systems, each with its own unique evolutionary purpose and neurochemical foundation.

The first, the sexual desire system, is the more straightforward of the two. Mediated primarily by gonadal hormones such as estrogens and androgens, its evolutionary function is to create a powerful drive to seek out sexual partners and engage in reproductive acts. This is the system of lust.

The second, far more intricate system is that of romantic love, or attachment. This system is not driven by sex hormones but by neuropeptides like oxytocin and vasopressin, which act upon the brain's fundamental reward circuitry. Its function is to forge a powerful emotional bond, creating feelings of infatuation, security, and lasting comfort with a specific partner. The functional independence of these systems is evident throughout human experience; it is what allows for the existence of deep, "platonic" love devoid of desire, and for casual sexual encounters devoid of romantic attachment. Recognizing that these two systems can and do operate separately is the first step to understanding the full spectrum of human connection.
