# Marginal Rate of Substitution
**Details**
This is the MRS, basically the algebraic equivalent of marginal utility, where we can calculate the rate at which a consumer is willing to trade his good for another while remaining on the same indifference curve.  In the lecture’s setup, it is the rate at which the consumer is willing to give up the good on the y-axis, pizza slices, for the good on the x-axis, cookies. Even if no literal barter is happening, the idea still applies because of opportunity cost. If the consumer has a limited budget, spending money on pizza means not spending that same money on cookies, and vice versa. A purchase is therefore an implicit substitution. The MRS captures the consumer’s subjective willingness to make that substitution.

Mathematically, the slope of the indifference curve will give the MRS. If the consumer gives up a little pizza, they lose utility to the marginal utility of pizza. Accordingly, the same applies for them gaining cookies. And so these changes offset each other and stay on the curve.
