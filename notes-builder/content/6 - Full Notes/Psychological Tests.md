# Psychological Tests
**Details**
These are some other methods of assessing if a person has a mental illness or not. For example, an IQ test may help a diagnostician understand and see the symptoms of a child who has ADHD, as they can see the child has a learning disability or something of the sorts. Another is schizophrenia, which we can apply the same IQ test to see if they have symptoms of it, learning disability. It may of course help further stuff like determining neurological injuries.

Another is a projective test where the patient is incredibly guarded in telling the diagnostician about the things they believe, and it would help them gauge out those hidden beliefs and such. An example of this is the Rorsarch’s test (Thematic Apperception Test) which are LITERALLY INK BLOTS, there’s nothing more to it, and it helps to find out stuff that are hidden behind the patient’s wariness. They will then evaluate the way they say and what they are saying to determine the above mentioned items. We can also gauge out stories from them and see the theme in their responses to determine the hidden information.

More are the personality tests, an example is the MMPI, or the Minnesota Multi-phasic Personality Inventory, which can be used to detect a number of psychological problems. These are also known as a personality inventory, where the person is asked to complete a self-report questionnaire indicating whether the questions conform to him or her. Something of worry is that people may lie about these things, and of course there is a built-in items to see if people are faking it.

Talking about some cognitive-behavioral assessments, which are observing people activities when they are at home, or at school to see what they might do that may be related to the diagnosis. There may also be interviews and self-report inventories like the other assessments.

There are of course also neurobiological assessments which are just looking at the brain to see what is happening or if their brain is healthy to diagnose the effects.

Lastly, assessments of course can also get affected by cultural and sociological biases. Since a lot of these psychological assessments were developed for the western populations, it is quite hard to get accurate information in other cultures. Forms that this can come in are differences in religions, beliefs, alienation or timidity, and so on.
