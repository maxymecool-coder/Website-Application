# Targeting Self-criticisms and Self-blame
**Details**
The depressed patient, most of the time tries to find a reason for his problems. And due to his causality, he is prone to regard his own self as the problem of his difficulties, maybe deriving from a social stigma as "take a look at your own mistakes before judging others." His rules can then make him carry this notion to absurd extremes, like how when it is pointed out that his self-blame is maladaptive, he might blame himself for blaming himself, which can only make him feel even worse.

And so the cognitive approach to this would be to make the patient first be aware of the stream of self-criticisms. This is easy as the patient will feel worse when the criticism happens; thus when he experiences a spurt of dysphoria, he need only to "play back" his preceding thoughts to identify it.

The next is for us to increase his objectivity to his self-hate. This is crucial as the patient commonly believes his assumptions are justified (as always). One method is for us to ask something like, "Suppose a friend made the same mistakes that you did? Would you hate or encourage them and try to get them to feel better?" And this would make him realize his own exaggeratedness of his self-criticisms. 

This process may sometimes be boosted by role playing. For example, the therapist would play the role of the person that the patient sees in themself. And the patient is then coached to become a critic to harshly judge and attack the therapist. The therapist would act accordingly to demonstrate the patient's own conditions, his cognitive distortions. If the patient understands the role of the critic, he can then get out of that role and see its extravagance.

Same as we mentioned before, recognizing automatic thoughts here will also help, and the "three-column" task will help as well as we assign the patients homework.
