# Anxiety
**Details**
This is a commonplace observation when someone considers themselves to be in imminent danger, in which they will be likely to feel anxious. Threats of any kind, be it physical, economically,... can have this effect. Moreover, it can even be a threat (even imaginary) to the safety, health, or psychological state of anyone else in his own domain may do this. A person may also become anxious if he perceives a danger to some institution or principle he values.

This anticipation is what we call fear, and people's reaction to it is what we call anxiety. But there is a way to alleviate this, it is that the person experiencing is confident they can cope an deal with it. But it will increase when he feels that this damage is unavoidable, extremely destructive and such, and it may also be increased by uncertainty with regard to when the harm may occur.

In identifying a situation as dangerous, a person usually makes two simultaneous judgements. The first is termed "primary appraisal" by Richard Lazarus, who identifies the situation as a threat and assesses all the factors of harm in it, the intensity, the probability, the imminence,... The second is "counter-harm" resources; that is, the ability to cope with the danger. The ratio between the negative in the primary with the positive of the secondary constitutes the perceived risk. The latter then determines the intensity of anxiety.

It is important to recognize that the same situation can have different effects on different people and even some situations perceived generally to be safe and normal can become dangerous for certain individuals.
