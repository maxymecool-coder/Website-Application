# The Fine-Tuning Argument
**Details**
Considered by many atheists to be possibly the strongest argument that the theists have, this argument basically that the creation of the universe rests on the observation that the fundamental constants of nature—such as the gravitational constant relative to the force of the Big Bang—fall within an exceedingly narrow range necessary for the emergence of complex structures.

Theists argue that the probability of this happening is so astronomically small that it is unlikely that it would have happened if there wasn't something like a divine creator taking part or actively having done it. Or that we inhabit a Multiverse where all parameter combinations are realized, and we simply observe the one capable of sustaining observers (the Anthropic Principle). Both hypotheses are metaphysically "expensive," requiring either a complex unobservable entity (God) or a complex unobservable system (infinite universes).

**Supporting Content**
Some standard arguments against this include questioning the nature of the fine-tuning like for what purpose was the fine-tuning for ? Because for that, science justifies that the universe was tuned for the existence of planets, black holes, and so on rather than human life. Another may be arguing about what kind of God would fine-tune the universe like this. If a God was omniperfect then they could just snap into existence life and everything, yet it took millions of years for the process to actually take place, and so the God would've been a "fine-tuning" God instead.
