# Reducing therapy-interfering behaviors
**Details**
Most of the time, these behaviors are at the bottom of the hierarchy. This is because they may be therapy interfering, but they are not dangerous as to kill the patients. And if they were to do this, it would require the whole session to just resolve said problems.

When it is occurring though, the effective strategies are to ignore the behavior if it is brief; or ask them to stop and focus on teaching the skills. Because of the limited amount of time, these behaviors cannot be dealt with by spending valuable time and rather by just pretending it doesn’t exist or something.

As discussed in [[The Comprehensive Guide to DBT]], there is a wind-down period at the end of sessions. This is the time to observe and improve these behaviors.
