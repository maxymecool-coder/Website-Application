# Self-Disclosure in Therapy
**Details**
In psychoanalytic therapy, therapists are usually advised not to use self-disclosure, but in CBT, you don't want to do that, you want them to think of you as a warm person who is trying to help them and is capable of helping them. Self-disclosure can be a powerful tool used to normalize the clients' difficulties, humanizing them, strengthening the therapeutic relationship, and demonstrating how CBT can help.

Sometimes, clients may push too far and try to ask about things that they shouldn't ask or is uncomfortable to you. In these situations, redirect it back to them rather like "Is it okay if we get back to____ ? I do want to tell you but if I spend time doing so we might not have time to help you.", and think of what you COULD tell them that may be related. Try to be mindful and do self-disclosures to help them with their negative beliefs and problems. Additionally, pay attention to your timing and how you say it as well, perhaps even their body language to understand if they prefer you telling them about yourself or not.
