# Questioning "The life after death"
**Details**
The cheap objection, which Rosenberg presses and Kagan takes seriously enough to answer: death is the end of life, so "life after death" unpacks as "life after the end of life," and "will I survive my death?" unpacks as "will I be alive after I stopped being alive?" Both answer themselves. On this reading the question that has occupied religion and philosophy for millennia is a grammatical accident — the same joke as asking which country buries the survivors of a crash on the border.

Kagan's response is not to dispute the definition but rather change the subject that is being questioned. Instead of "the life after death," we can simply restrict the subject to our body instead. Now, this doesn't entirely comicalize the question as no can still be an answer, but now it has to be earned rather than be assumed through language.

Though the reformulation is not perfect, due to how the question is now defined, it is stipulated that the answer is rooted in dualism as it is assuming the body and soul are now two different things.

From then on the question also enters the realm of philosophy of mind: the substance of someone and how to know they are the same version of themselves that lived.
