# Further than drives and emotions
**Details**
Moving beyond simple reward-seeking, a more complete model of motivation must account for the complexity of drives and the role of emotions. A key distinction is between **consummatory** and **appetitive** behaviors. Consummatory behaviors involve the direct satisfaction of a need (e.g., eating the food), while appetitive behaviors are the actions involved in seeking out that satisfaction (e.g., the hunt). For complex organisms, the seeking behavior must itself be motivating to ensure survival; one cannot wait until starvation is imminent to begin looking for food.

Emotions serve as a higher-level system to manage these drives. They act as a kind of internal shorthand that helps us mobilize resources, generalize from past experiences, and, crucially, **communicate** our motivational states to others. An expression of fear, for instance, can signal danger to the entire group. Emotions allow for a more flexible and social response to the world than simple, isolated drives would permit. They are the language through which we understand and express our internal motivational landscape.
