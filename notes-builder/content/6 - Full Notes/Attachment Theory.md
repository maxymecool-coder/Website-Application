# Attachment Theory
**Details**
Children usually take after their caregivers, this is not just a saying, it is scientifically proven. As babies get into this world, they automatically have someone to take care of them, to feed, to sleep with and so. When they grow up, they learn to take care of themselves, and the first lesson that they ever get to know how to do this is of how they are cared for. Children are inherently sensitive to facial expression, postures, tones, everything. This is theorized to be a trait of evolution to help them survive,  and that they also choose a few adults to learn from, attach to and develop healthy ways of living in life. Attachment is a secure base that would allow children to function without being afraid of being left behind, doing something wrong as such. This safe haven also allows them to develop empathy, self-awareness, impulse control and many other functions as they realize that people will have different feelings and thoughts from themselves. 

Now the physiological part, to describe the connection between these two, I'd say... "in sync". And not just emotionally, but physically as well, children don't just feel sad when they notice that others or sad or something along those lines, their bodies also do that, it tenses, releases hormones, and all that jazz. We can tell equilibrium has been restored when the physiology calms down.

Newborns are soothed, but their toleration are also taught, a role given to their fathers. Learning how to manage this is especially important in life, and parents must do this before babies can do it for themselves. If the baby shits itself, the parents are gonna clean it up, if the baby is hungry, the mother feeds,... Associating these intense feelings with safety, comfort is the foundation of self-regulation, self-soothing, and self-nurture. These securely-attached children knows what makes them feel good, what makes others feel bad and so on. But abused children don't, their cries, their needs are often responded with negligence from their caregivers. In effect they are being conditioned to give up whenever they face challenges in life.
