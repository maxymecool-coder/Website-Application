# Folk Psychology
**Details**
Folk psychology is the common-sense, intuitive understanding of the mind that people use in everyday life without formal training. It's the psychology that "folk know," encompassing a vocabulary of terms like memory, emotion, learning, and personality that we use to navigate our social world. Unlike many science courses where students begin with zero knowledge of the topics, an introductory psychology course starts with subjects about which everyone already has pre-existing beliefs and notions. The term was originally coined in the 19th century to distinguish this everyday knowledge from the psychology one would learn in an academic setting. The goal of academic psychology is to move beyond this surface-level, often anecdotal understanding. It seeks to scientifically investigate the mechanisms, structures, and true nature behind these familiar concepts, often revealing that our intuitive or "folk" theories are incomplete or incorrect.

This sub-subject of psychology is often what people use in their daily life. As we could see at times, it can be quite helpful as it connects people together and may even help them empathize and help each other, but at other times, when problems get more specific and academic, it can backfire and instead harm the person in need.
