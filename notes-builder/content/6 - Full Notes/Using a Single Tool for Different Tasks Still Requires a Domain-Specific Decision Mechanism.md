# The rebuttal to the reusage
**Details**
The text argues that the screwdriver analogy, intended to refute the need for specialization, actually demonstrates its necessity. The analogy ignores the "decision-making machinery" that determines how the screwdriver is used. Something must decide whether to turn the screwdriver left or right, when to turn it, and when to stop. This decision-making system is, by definition, domain-specific. It is activated by specific inputs (e.g., the goal to tighten or loosen) and produces different functional outcomes with different error definitions (turning left is an error for tightening, and vice-versa). A single physical structure (the mouth) serves different systems (digestion, respiration). However, separate functional machinery (e.g., brain signals for lack of oxygen vs. presence of food) must direct its actions. The mouth itself doesn't decide. Even if a single tool is used, it must be controlled by functionally specific elements that know when and how to apply it for different tasks.
