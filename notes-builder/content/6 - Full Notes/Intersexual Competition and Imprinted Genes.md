# Intersexual Competition and Imprinted Genes
**Details**
Due to the difference in reproductive interest of males and females, a lot of the time, there is intersexual competition, which exists in the form of imprinted genes - genes expressed differently depending on who it is inherited from. This is mostly seen in tournament-bonding species, as they are likely to just leave when they finish impregnating the female:

- **Paternally-derived genes** tend to _promote_ fetal growth (e.g., increasing placental invasion, producing more growth factor). The father's genetic interest is in this specific offspring surviving, even at a high metabolic cost to the mother, as he may not mate with her again.
    
- **Maternally-derived genes** tend to _counteract_ this, slowing fetal growth to conserve the mother's resources for her own survival and future reproductive opportunities.
