# Visual Cortex
**Details**
The **primary visual cortex** (V1) is the main cortical area for receiving, processing, and integrating visual information from the retinas. It is located in the most posterior part of the brain, within the occipital lobe.

V1's fundamental role is to deconstruct visual signals into basic components like lines, edges, orientation, color, and motion. It is organized in a **retinotopic map**, where adjacent points in the visual field correspond to adjacent points in the cortex, preserving the spatial layout of the retinal image.

From V1, visual information is sent to higher-order visual areas along two main pathways for further processing: the **dorsal stream** ("where/how" pathway), which processes spatial location and motion, and the **ventral stream** ("what" pathway), which is involved in object recognition.
