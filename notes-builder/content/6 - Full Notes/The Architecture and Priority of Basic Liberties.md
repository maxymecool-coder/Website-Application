# The Architecture and Priority of Basic Liberties
**Details**
But of course, Locke's framework is way too loose and is merely operating on the possibility of contractualism. In this sense, John Rawls tries to harden this framework to something that people would accept. This means that he would have to build a far more rigorous machinery into the thought experiment, which is why the [[The Original Position]] and [[The Necessary Person]] exist.

When Rawls finally constructs his First Principle of Justice, he is not just praising some vague, abstract concept of "Liberty" like a modern libertarian might. Instead, he lists specific points of basic liberties-such as freedom of thought, conscience, political association. All of these are the conditions that allow people to develop and use their fundamental moral powers. And Rawls also place them at the top of the chain, as a first principle, so they could never ever be touched by any other factors.

His second principles heavily correspond to [[Scanlon Contractualism]] which could be checked there. It is simple algebra about how capitalism functions and how he requires it to benefit the lower class to a certain degree. Furthermore, he argues that justice is dependent on the situation and the multitude of factors of said situation/generation and so it would change. For example, the people who have benefited from it the previous generation would hold somewhat of a monopoly of their benefits, and so it would have to change to divert the "pie" to another group, and so on and so forth.
