# Commentaries and handbooks guide
**Details**
This is the third category of extrinsic aids and a part of books that aren’t necessarily connected to each other but rather guides. These references should be use wisely, and sparingly. 

This is because that commentators are not always right, their works can be enormously useful, but this does not mean that it is always. These guides are used to support students and so it will contain the details and definitions necessary to pass a class, not understand a book deeply. 

The second reason is that to not be enslaved to them, you should try to discover the important meanings of a book yourself as reading a commentary can limit yourself to that realm of knowledge, even though there is more. And so the advice to read reference books are… to read it after one has read and tried to understand the book (like a conversation or questioning). 

The rule of extrinsic reading given here applies also to abstracts and plot digests. They are useful in two connections, but in those two only. First, they can help to jog your memory of a book’s contents, if you have already read it. Ideally, you made such an abstract yourself, in reading the book analytically, but if you have not done so, an abstract or digest can be an important aid. Second, abstracts are useful when you are engaged in syntopical reading, and wish to know whether a certain work is likely to be germane to your project. An abstract can never replace the reading of a book, but it can sometimes tell you whether you want or need to read the book or not.
