# The Cartesian Circle
**Details**
Descartes famously employed "Antecedent Skepticism" which means that he completely and entirely doubting everything before his philosophy to strap away any Aristotelianism. Which came to one thing, Cogito Ergo Sum ([[The arguments of Descartes]]). However though, Descartes ran into a brick wall when trying to extend his arguments to others rather than himself, because he doesn't have objectivity in such areas, and so he assumed that there existed a God so perfect that it could've made him what he is.

The problem with this argument is that, if he relied on his "clear and distinct" idea of God, he cannot then use that God to justify his "clear and distinct" ideas as it would create a circle. This circle would then subsequently break under any doubts at all, as the first "unclear" reasoning chain that one makes would then disallow any future reasonings, even ones that try to mend the first, to be unbroken.
