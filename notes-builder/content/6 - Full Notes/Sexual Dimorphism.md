# Sexual Dimorphism
**Details**
The evolutionary principles of selection can be used to predict a species' entire social structure based on a single trait: the degree of **sexual dimorphism** (physical difference between males and females, especially size).

- **Tournament Species** are species where males are much bigger than females. This makes it so that the female would prefer bigger males as they have better sexual selection and in a group most of the time there will only be a few big males who is gonna be mated to instead of having couples. The males are quite aggressive and will fight each other to mate.
    
- **Pair-Bonding Species** (low dimorphism, e.g., marmoset monkeys), in these species, the males and females are quite similar size to each other, and so they do not have to compete in tournament, but rather in ability of parenting. In this species, the female leave the male due to knowing her children can be taken care of and can just go mate with other males.
    

This dichotomy is remarkably consistent across primates, birds, and fish. One look at the skulls can tell you about their aggression, parenting, and mating strategies.
