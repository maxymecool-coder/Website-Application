# PET as a method
**Details**
Positron Emission Tomography records blood flow and brain metabolism, specifically glucose. A radioisotope is a chemical element that emits radiation, including positrons, as it decays into a more stable form. These emitted positrons combine with nearby electrons, resulting in the emission of gamma rays which can be detected by a machine. In a typical procedure, the subject's head is surrounded by gamma ray detectors. When two gamma rays are recorded at the same time by opposing detectors, the point of origin is pinpointed as the spot halfway between them. This method produces a multitude of observations, particularly in research experiments where numerous events are recorded. All of these responses are then averaged to acquire a single, consolidated dataset that is used for statistical analysis. Many PET research experiments involve many subjects or many trials to get more reliable results.
