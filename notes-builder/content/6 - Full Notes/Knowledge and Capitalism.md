# Knowledge and Capitalism
**Details**
Alongside the traditional factors of production (land, labor, and capital), **knowledge**—and its product, information—has become a dominant productive force. The class of knowledge workers are increasingly becoming more powerful but not only that they are going against the traditional capitalist way.

In other words, they are politically realigning themselves with the values so that knowledge can be pushed further rather than economically conservating their own wealth and power. This suggests a growing disparity in between the societal elites.

This could be explained by examining the nature of knowledge. Knowledge inherently thrives on freedom, the more people know about it, the more people will become somewhat educated and be able to push it forward using their own expertise which is the complete opposite of what capitalism is. In contrast, capitalism makes knowledge private property, because as long as people do not know what makes them rich and powerful, they will retain all that to themselves. All of the information can be copyrighted or patented so that they can legally keep it to themselves.

But this of course comes as a cost, as said before about how knowledge thrives, the fact that these methods are private is a barrier to the potential of knowledge expansion as they are stifled by the legal system and private companies.
