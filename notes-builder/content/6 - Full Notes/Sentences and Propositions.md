# Sentences and Propositions
**Details**
As we have said before about words and terms, these are basically its bigger brothers. Sentences and paragraphs are like grammatical units,. whilst propositions and arguments are logical units, of knowledge and thoughts.

And the same that went for words and terms can be said here as well. Not every sentence in a book expresses a proposition, and not all propositions are expressed by one singular sentences. A sentence can be interrogative, it can be asking questions for the author to set up and answer using his propositions. Other sentences can express wishes or intentions, they can give us knowledge to the author's purpose, thinking and emotions even, but they themselves do not provide the knowledge that we want to get.

And so we come to the conclusion of the sixth and seventh rule of analytical reading: Rule 6. Mark the most important sentences in a book and discover the propositions they contain. The seventh is: Rule 7. Locate or construct the basic arguments in the book by finding them in the connection of sentences.
