# What is history
**Details**
We usually class this under fiction more than science, that is if it must be affiliated with one or the other. Though it would sit in relatively the middle of both of these, leaning over to fiction of course.

As talked before, this is because the elusiveness of history and the necessity that the author guess, hypothecate,… and so a good author will try to find general patterns, logical ones to impose on these events. He may have a general theory or philosophy, such as that Providence rules human affairs, and make his history fit that. Or he may abjure any such pattern, imposed as it were from the outside or above, and instead insist that he is merely reporting the real events that have occurred. But in that case he is likely to be forced to assign causes for events and motivations for actions. It is essential to recognize which way the historian you are reading is operating.

The only way we can avoid taking either position is to assume men do not do things for a purpose or that purpose is undiscoverable, which means there would be no pattern to history. (Tolstoy had a theory like this, that the human motivation was so complex and hidden that it is impossible to know why anything ever happened.)

Since these theories differ and affects the historians’ accounts of events, it is necessary for us to read multiple account of history if we want to understand it. This is the first rule and it is very important if it relates to our significant being (ho we came to be, etc…). We must understand every narrative of history has to be written from some point pf view and that we have to look at it from more than one viewpoint.
