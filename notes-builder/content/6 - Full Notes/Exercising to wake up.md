# Exercising to wake up
**Details**
Physical exercise is a powerful, non-photic (non-light-based) tool for influencing the body's circadian clock and promoting alertness. Its primary mechanism of action is the elevation of core body temperature. An increase in body temperature serves as a potent biological signal for wakefulness and activity, directly counteracting the state of sleep, which requires a temperature drop. However, the ultimate effect of exercise on the sleep-wake cycle is critically dependent on its timing.

When performed in the **morning** (within the first few hours of waking), exercise acts to _advance_ the circadian clock. It amplifies the natural morning rise in cortisol and temperature, strongly anchoring the body's sense of "daytime." This not only enhances alertness and focus throughout the day but also makes it easier to fall asleep earlier that night, reinforcing a consistent schedule. This effect is magnified when combined with other levers, such as viewing morning sunlight during an outdoor walk or run.

Conversely, when performed in the **late afternoon or evening**, vigorous exercise can _delay_ the circadian clock. The significant increase in core body temperature close to one's typical bedtime can interfere with the natural cooling process required to initiate sleep. This pushes the entire sleep-wake cycle later, causing a desire to fall asleep later and wake up later. While this can be a useful tool for intentionally shifting one's schedule (e.g., for travel or social reasons), it can be disruptive for those trying to maintain a regular sleep pattern. Therefore, the timing of exercise determines whether it functions to solidify an existing schedule or to actively shift it.

**Supporting Content**
It could be done whilst getting some sunlight from going outside, in fact, it is advised to be combined to do so.
