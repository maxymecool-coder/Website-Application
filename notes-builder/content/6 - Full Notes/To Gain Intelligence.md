# To Gain Intelligence
**Details**
And so how to gain intelligence ? Emerson argues that we should find our own virtues, follow our own interests, and souls to do so.

Imagine intelligence not as a destination reached by rote learning or the dutiful ingestion of others' conclusions, but as a state of being. Emerson beckons us away from the dusty library shelves where knowledge lies inert, entombed in print, unless quickened by a living mind. The first act of gaining true intelligence is the radical act of listening to the subtle murmurs of your own intuition, that "aboriginal Self" which connects you to the universal flow, the Over-Soul. This inner voice, often drowned out by the clamor of convention and external authority, holds the kernel of genuine insight. To cultivate intelligence is first to cultivate the courage to hear and heed this inner oracle.  

Then, Emerson would point us outward, not to institutions, but to Nature. Not merely a scenic backdrop, but the great, silent teacher, the living embodiment of divine law and reason. Walk the woods, he'd urge. Observe the effortless cycle of growth and decay, the perfect geometry of a snowflake, the steadfastness of the oak, the fluidity of the stream. In Nature's intricate, harmonious design, the receptive mind perceives analogies, mirrors to its own processes. Intelligence blossoms not from memorizing botanical names, but from understanding what is that animates the leaf, the star, the very air we breathe. The universe speaks in symbols, and intelligence is the capacity to interpret this silent, profound language. See how the light falls, how the shadow shifts – these are lessons more potent than any lecture.  

As there exists creative writing, there must also exists creative reading. And so Emerson does not dismiss them entirely, but reframes their role. They are not masters, but stimulants. Read, yes, but read actively, critically, with the fierce independence of your own spirit. Use the thoughts of Plato, of Shakespeare, of Goethe, not as molds into which you must pour your own consciousness, but as flint striking steel, igniting your own fire. "Meek young men grow up in libraries," he warns, mistaking passive acceptance for learning. True intelligence engages, questions, wrestles with ideas, using the past as a springboard into the present, into the unique perspective only you can hold. The wise man reads a book and closes it to think his own thoughts, sparked but not dictated by the author.  

Furthermore, Emerson champions action and experience. Intelligence is not a cloistered virtue. It must be tested and tempered in the forge of life. To truly understand labor, one must labor. To grasp resilience, one must face adversity. Living itself is the primary text. Each encounter, each challenge, each simple act of navigating the day holds the potential for insight. The mind grows keen through engagement, through the application of thought to circumstance. It is in the doing, the trying, the living of principles, that understanding deepens from abstract notion to lived reality.
