# The Epicurean Form of Egoistic Suicide
**Details**
Beyond the melancholic intellectual, Durkheim describes a more commonplace form of egoistic suicide, which he terms "Epicurean." In this state, the individual does not reflect sadly upon their condition but instead accepts their egoism and its logical consequences cheerfully. They assign themselves the single, simple task of satisfying personal needs, living for sensual pleasure with a full and knowing awareness of their limited purpose. This philosophy, however, provides only a very slight link to life. The individual is always prepared to leave existence at the slightest stimulus, should the possibility of their easy, pleasurable life be threatened. The dreamy melancholy of the intellectual form is replaced by a sceptical, disillusioned matter-of-factness. The final act is passionless, performed with an "ironic tranquillity" and a matter-of-course mood, with the primary goal being simply to minimize pain. This, too, is a direct result of a lack of a higher social purpose, but it manifests as a pursuit of fleeting pleasure rather than an absorption in inner emptiness.
