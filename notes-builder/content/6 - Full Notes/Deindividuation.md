# Deindividuation
**Details**
This concept is quite relative to the [[The Selves of Schopenhauer and Nietzsche]] as it constitutes the Will of Schopenhauer. And by Will I do mean the fact that these individuals that are in a group who experience deindividuation tends to do less responsible things rather than the opposite, as they are less accountable and are able to reason less.

Originating with Le Bon's 1895 concept of the "group mind," it describes what happens when a person stops experiencing themselves as a distinct, identifiable individual.

Zimbardo identified key inputs: anonymity, lack of accountability, physiological arousal, large groups, sensory overload, and altered consciousness (drugs, alcohol). These inputs produce specific psychological consequences: minimized self-awareness, reduced concern for social evaluation, impulsivity, time distortion (present dominates past/future), diminished differentiation from others, and poorer subsequent memory.

But how does this actually come into practice ? When experimenting on the differences between individuated and deindividuated people, it was recognized that people are to be less caring about how they shock an unidentifiable, unknown individual even if they know partially who they are and individuals who they can identify and that have shown them who they are. But Gergen's dark room study showed deindividuated participants becoming more affectionate, intimate, and connected — and evaluating the experience positively afterward.

And so its not necessarily that deindividuation would cause us to act one way over the other, rather, it strengthens the situational context of whatever was happening (as the groups all believed it was an activity that they believed others would do).
