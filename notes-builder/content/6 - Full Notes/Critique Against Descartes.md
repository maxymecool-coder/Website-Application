# Critique Against Descartes
**Details**
While the initial premise ("thinking is occurring") is widely accepted, Descartes' move to posit a substantial "Self" behind the thinking faces significant challenges. For example, the "Hume's Bundle Theory" which posits that Descartes proposition only meant that there is thinking rather than I am thinking. When we look inward, we do not find a stable form of a continuous soul or "thinker" but rather something that changes by time or by situation, and so perhaps we are just a "bundle" of souls having our perceptions transfer from one to another.

Georg Lichtenberg attacked the grammar of Descartes' deduction. He argued that saying "I think" assumes the very conclusion it tries to prove (that there is an "I"). A more scientifically rigorous observation would simply be "It thinks" or "There is thinking," much like we say "It rains." We observe the event (rain/thought) without necessarily observing an agent (rain-god/thinker). The "I" is a grammatical convenience, not a proven fact.

Additionally, there may be also arguments on our subjective belief, even if we believe that something is true, it might not even be so !
