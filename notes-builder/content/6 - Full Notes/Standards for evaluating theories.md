# Standards for evaluating theories
**Details**
1. As it is scientific, the theory should satisfy the requirements of any good scientific theory, namely, that is should explain the phenomena within its domain with ease. According to the law of parsimony, the best theory has the most data but uses the simplest of concepts. Moreover, it should logically have no internal contradictions and its basic assumptions and hypotheses should be consistent.
2. The theory should be closely related to its allied psychotherapy so that it is obvious how the methods derived from it.
3. The theory should provide the basis for understanding why the methods are effective. The rationale and mode of operation should be implicit, it should provide reasons for why it is more effective than other theories.
4. The theory should also be flexible enough for it to be the foundations of developing new techniques without being so loose or complex that it can obligingly dispenses a justification for any procedure a therapist might want to improvise.
5. The last thing is the fact it must have sufficient evidence, tests and such, or axioms, hypotheses, and assumptions that can be verified through experiments and investigations.
