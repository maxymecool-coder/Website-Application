# The reason for Phobia
**Details**
Although many schools of psychology has taken a jab at trying to find the cause of phobias, most of them have failed to come up with an explanation that truly is verifiable and can be used to help the patient. This is where Beck comes in, as he questions the phobic, he recognized that they have experienced a situation that ended badly in their childhood, prompting them to be scared, hyper fixate on that into adulthood. 

For example, a child who refuses to eat, when his parents demand an explanation, he just says that he will die if he does so, which they of course think is irrational. But when asked more clearly about a past experience, the psychologist finds that the child once choked on a large piece of potato, was scared to death by that experience and now immediately thinks about it whenever he is eating such types of food. This shows us that what the phobic is afraid of is not the situation, but the consequences of such situations.

The types of phobias that Beck mentioned in his works are: 
- **Physical/Violent Threat:** This is the most primal fear—fear of physical harm, injury, illness, or death. A person with panic disorder might misinterpret their racing heart as an imminent heart attack. Someone with a phobia of dogs fears being bitten.
- **Psychosocial Threat:** This is the fear of social harm—humiliation, rejection, criticism, or loss of status. This is the core of Social Anxiety Disorder, where the perceived "danger" is being judged negatively by others.
- **Supernatural Threat:** This is a category that is often overlooked but is crucial. It involves fears of evil, demonic possession, divine retribution, or other threats that are not of the physical or social world. Depending on a person's cultural and religious beliefs, these can be incredibly powerful drivers of anxiety.
The first and second one usually insisted on into adulthood more than the latter.
