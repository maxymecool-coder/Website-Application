# Panic Disorder
**Details**
Panic Disorder is defined by the experience of recurrent, unexpected panic attacks—sudden, intense episodes of terror that manifest in severe physical symptoms like shortness of breath, chest pain, and a feeling of impending death. Initially, these attacks are not directed at a specific trigger. However, the debilitating and frightening nature of the attacks often leads to a secondary, learned fear. There are physical symptoms such as labored breathing, heart palpitations, nausea, upset stomach, chest pain, feelings of choking and smothering, dizziness, lightheadedness, sweating, chills, heat sensations, and trembling. Other psychological symptoms may include depersonalization and derealization; and fears of losing control, going crazy. All of these symptoms mean that people will likely try to escape the situation they are in. They also come rapidly and reach a peak of intensity in about 10 minutes of the panic attack.

This is due to a misunderstanding of the fear system, it misidentifies that it is in danger and causes the sympathetic nervous system to arouse and makes the whole body go into panic mode. There are also things called cues, which are signs that triggers the panic attacks, cued panic attacks usually relate back to a phobia.

The complication of the panic disorder can bring phobias into the equation, they will become fearful of the situations where they get panic attacks, like situations of being seen, like being in public when it happens, which is called Agoraphobia. These fears only gauge the feeling of panic more, creating a debilitating cycle where the person avoids public life to prevent a potential panic attack. Treatment often involves antidepressants to reduce the frequency of attacks and cognitive therapy to break the association between public spaces and the experience of panic.
