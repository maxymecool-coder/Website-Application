# The Midbrain
**Details**
This portion of the brain is crucial for targeting auditory (sound) and visual stimuli. It seems to control the eye movements, and perhaps in floppy-eared animals, to prick up their ears when encountering novel or important events. Birds have proportionately large midbrains, means that it might take lots of fast brain machinery for them to do what they do. Mammals on the other hand are the opposite, having small midbrains, shifting the control of vision and hearing into the forebrains (which is the largest part of our brains). Other areas of the midbrain helps us regulate body temperature and pain perception, they cooperate with the pons to control the sleeping-waking cycle.

The midbrain also coordinates with the simple movements that the hindbrain creates to make larger wholes. Again, when a transection is performed, creating a high-decerebrate animal, they can do everything without support like standing, walking, shivering, chewing. But whilst this happen, they cannot do as much as an intact animal, they cannot act with purpose. This suggests that the midbrain contains circuitry that coordinates lower level building blocks and forming them into our normal acts.
