# Experimental and Observational Designs
**Details**
In scientific research, experimental design and observational studies serve distinct, essential, and sometimes conflicting purposes. Experimental design includes random sampling, control groups, and identical exposure; it is also the only methodology that can definitively prove a cause-and-effect relationship. Observational studies on the other hand may yield correlational data but are frequently required due to ethical constraints, practical limitations, or the need for naturalistic validity.

The dangers could be easily exemplified by the medical history of Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT). Prior to 2002, observational data suggested that HRT was mostly beneficial, reducing hear disease and improved longevity in postmenopausal women. However, later, as the Women's Health Initiative performed an experimental design, they had to halt the study due to participants experiencing increases in heart attacks, strokes, and breast cancer. Eventually, it was found out that this discrepancy was caused by the category of participants: in the observational study, the main group was women who tended to be healthier, wealthier, exercise more and was advised by their doctors.
