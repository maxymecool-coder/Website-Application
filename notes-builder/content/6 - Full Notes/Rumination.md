# Rumination
**Details**
Rumination is the state of being cognitively "stuck" in a loop of negative thoughts. It's a defining feature of depression where the mind compulsively replays sad memories, perceived failures, and feelings of guilt, unable to break free. This is not simply "thinking sad thoughts"; it is a pathological process where negative cognitions become self-perpetuating, like a cow endlessly chewing its cud.

The biological basis for rumination involves a critical imbalance in brain circuitry. The **Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC)**, a part of the brain's executive control center, is responsible for cognitive flexibility and suppressing unwanted thoughts. In major depression, the DLPFC is often underactive and can even atrophy over time. It loses its ability to say "stop" to the negative thought patterns.

Simultaneously, brain regions that generate these thoughts become overactive. The **Default Mode Network (DMN)**, which is active during self-referential thought and mind-wandering, gets hijacked by negativity. The **amygdala** (fear center) and **anterior cingulate cortex** (emotional pain) feed this network with a steady stream of distressing signals.

The result is a vicious cycle: the overactive DMN generates a negative thought, and the underactive DLPFC fails to suppress it. The thought then resonates through the network, reinforcing the negative emotional state, which in turn fuels more rumination. This cognitive quicksand is exhausting and makes it nearly impossible to focus on neutral or positive information, directly contributing to the cognitive deficits and persistent low mood seen in depression.
