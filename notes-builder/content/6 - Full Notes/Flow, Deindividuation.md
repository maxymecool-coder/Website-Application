# Flow, Deindividuation
**Details**
Csikszentmihalyi's concept of flow (1974) describes complete immersion in an activity where the self disappears. Its psychological features — time distortion, absent self-evaluation, poor subsequent memory, present-moment dominance, effortless-feeling control — are virtually identical to deindividuation's.

And the difference is rooted in the evaluative nature of the flow, if it produces socially condemned behavior (riots, aggression), we call it deindividuation and pathologize it. The underlying mental state is likely the same.

Zen meditative states represent a third framing: deliberate, disciplined self-dissolution pursued as a spiritual ideal. Herrigel described the Zen state as "purposeless and egoless" — language that Western culture would treat as pathological in any non-spiritual context. This reveals that society's evaluation of self-loss depends entirely on the cultural framework applied to it, not on the phenomenology of the experience itself.
