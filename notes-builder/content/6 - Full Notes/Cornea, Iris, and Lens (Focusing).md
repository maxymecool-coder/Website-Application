# Cornea, Iris, and Lens (Focusing)
**Details**
Before the brain can interpret an image, the eye must first act as a sophisticated optical instrument, capturing and focusing light onto the retina. This journey begins at the **cornea**, the eye's transparent and rigid outer layer. It performs the initial and most significant bending of light, starting the process of creating a sharp image. From the cornea, light passes through the **pupil**, an adjustable opening whose size is meticulously controlled by the **iris**, the colored muscle that we see. The iris is a dynamic structure, constricting the pupil in bright sunlight to prevent overexposure and dilating it in dim conditions to let in as much light as possible. Finally, the light reaches the **lens**, a remarkable, flexible structure that fine-tunes the focus. Through a process called **accommodation**, the ciliary muscle alters the lens's shape—making it thicker for nearby objects and thinner for those far away. Once perfectly focused, the light travels through the clear, jelly-like **vitreous humor** to its final destination at the back of the eye: the retina.
