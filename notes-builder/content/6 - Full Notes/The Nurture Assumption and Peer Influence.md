# The Nurture Assumption and Peer Influence
**Details**
In the late 1990s, Judith Harris challenged the hegemony of parenting research with _The Nurture Assumption_. She argued that parental influence is vastly overrated, particularly after age 5. Her thesis suggests that **shared environment** (parenting) accounts for very little variance in adult outcomes, while **genes** and **non-shared environments** (specifically peer groups and school culture) are the primary drivers of personality and behavior.

While controversial, this perspective forced a re-evaluation of developmental psychology, moving away from a "parent-centric" model to one that acknowledges that parents may influence children indirectly (by choosing neighborhoods/schools) rather than directly through day-to-day discipline.
