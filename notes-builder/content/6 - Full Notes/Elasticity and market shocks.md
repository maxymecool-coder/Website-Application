# Elasticity and market shocks
**Details**
So what decides how these two variables split? Elasticity. Take a supply shift that raises costs:

- **Normal demand:** the price rises and the quantity falls (Figure 9-3).
- **Perfectly inelastic, vertical demand:** quantity can't change, so the price rises by the whole shift (Figure 9-4). Insulin is the example, for those who can afford it.
- **Perfectly elastic, horizontal demand:** the price can't change, because charging a penny more drives everyone away, so all the adjustment comes through quantity (in this case mostly dropping). Evaluating McDonalds vs Burger King. If McDonalds raised its price, it would lose a ton of customers because they’d just go to Burger King.
