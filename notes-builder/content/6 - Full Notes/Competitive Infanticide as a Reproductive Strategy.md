# Competitive Infanticide as a Reproductive Strategy
**Details**
The killing of infants by adults of the same species, particularly by males, is not pathological behavior. It is a predictable, adaptive strategy under specific conditions. **Competitive infanticide** occurs when a new male takes over a breeding group and the average time a female spends nursing (and thus not ovulating) is _longer_ than the average time the new male can expect to remain dominant. This concept would support the individual selection theory. The killings of the infant ensures that other males' genes will not be passing on and that the female would start ovulating again so that they can mate.
