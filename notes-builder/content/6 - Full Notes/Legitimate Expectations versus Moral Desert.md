# Legitimate Expectations versus Moral Desert
**Details**
A critical and often misunderstood element of Rawlsian justice is the complete decoupling of economic reward from the concept of moral desert. In ordinary, everyday life, humans have a natural tendency to conflate a person's moral virtue with what they deserve to earn or achieve. However, Rawls argues that in a pluralistic democratic society, citizens hold fundamentally divergent, comprehensive moral and religious doctrines. Because we cannot reach a universal consensus on what constitutes a morally good character, basing a political economy on a disputed concept of moral worth is entirely unworkable.

Basically, in such a system, it is not that the morals or religious or philosophy of a person that determines their "deservedness" but rather the legitimate expectations that they have to the institutions and the entitlements that they subsequently deserve.
