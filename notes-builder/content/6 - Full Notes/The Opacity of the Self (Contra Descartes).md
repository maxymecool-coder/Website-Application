# The Opacity of the Self (Contra Descartes)
**Details**
Descartes argued that our mind is transparent, meaning we can always look inward to our mind and easily identify what we are actually thinking, but common sense and psychology actually disproves this. Most of the time, it is the other people that are able to understand and know what we are thinking before us.

For example, take into account a jealous person, they would rather contradict their own physical reactions and words when questioned about their true thoughts behind such reactions to protect their own mind and ego from admitting that they are indeed jealous. This suggests that the "Cartesian Theater"—where the self views its own thoughts infallibly—is a myth. We are often strangers to ourselves, and self-knowledge is a hard-won achievement, not a starting guarantee.

**Supporting Content**
We actually get to know ourselves by using others as a "social mirror" to help us understand.
