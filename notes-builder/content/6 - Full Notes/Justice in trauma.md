# Justice in trauma
**Details**
A lot of people believe in a just world, because such a world gives them comfort and relief that what they do will reflect and have certain conclusions and effects in the future. Same as in trauma, people who experience trauma sometimes do not get the retribution they deserve because of the unfairness of laws and corruption in said countries. But if that process of getting ahold of the wrongdoers and punishing them for what they have done does go through, it can validate the survivor's suffering, going against the denial and silence of society that they have to go through after those events.

Furthermore, the lecture expands the concept of reparations beyond symbolic gestures, like memorials or days of remembrance, to include tangible, practical support for healing. The Cambodian tribunal's decision to award $2 million to fund mental health programs is a groundbreaking example of this. This decision acknowledges the psychological harm as a core injury of the crime and provides direct resources for treatment. It forges a direct link between the legal process of accountability and the long-term well-being of the survivor community, making the pursuit of justice an active part of the therapeutic journey.
