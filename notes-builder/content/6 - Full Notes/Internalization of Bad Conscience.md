# Internalization of Bad Conscience
**Details**
Accordingly to his methodology, Nietzsche locates the origin of "Bad Conscience" not in a divine command or a metaphysical realization of sin, but in a historical rupture, not in religions like Christianity and such, but rather from the transition from wilderness to civilization of humanity.

He posits that in their pre-civilized state, humans were aggressive, semi-nomadic predators, we had powerful instincts for hunting, fighting, and releasing aggression outwardly toward enemies. And so when we were forced into society and peace, either by conquest or urbanization. Suddenly, the external discharge of aggression was prohibited by law and social pressure.

From this, Nietzsche argued that this tension did not go away (a very Freudian idea) and instead internalized. People didn't self mutilate, instead they pained themselves with negative thoughts and such rather. Before it becomes moral guilt, bad conscience is simply this structural stress—the suffering of an animal that must now rely on its "weakest organ" (consciousness) rather than its instincts.
