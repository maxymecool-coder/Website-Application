# Dialetics and Therapy
**Details**
When developing DBT, Marsha Linehan referred to her personal experience about how BPD consists of two distinctly different parts of a person, and how they needed to accept each other to live finely. And so using the world “dialectical” to describe how it is like a push and pull game to lead them to the middle, she would incorporate it into the name of the method she created as well.

Different from CBT, DBT instead emphasizes on the behavioral part more, the skill building such as stress tolerance, problem solving, mindfulness, and so on. Still the cognitive modification to think differently and from another perspective may be helpful, it is not the focused part. When the environment becomes unsafe though, therapists may directly intervene rather than encourage to prevent the patient from endangering themselves, which is also another focus of DBT. Another part that becomes more important is the bond between the therapists and their patients, since BPD is so heavy on relationships, it is generally advised for therapists to become even more mindful and vigilant when helping the patient. Moreover, reinforcing this relationship may even encourage the patient to work on themselves to get better.
