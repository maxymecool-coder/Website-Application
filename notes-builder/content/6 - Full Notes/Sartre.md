# Sartre
**Details**
Sartre had the view that the self is not continuously present, that there is no "I" and that there is only consciousness of the streetcar-to-be-caught. The self is like a refrigerator light: on when you open the door, off when you close it. We assume it persists, but during unreflective living there's no evidence it does. This directly challenges Descartes: _cogito ergo sum_ works only because Descartes was actively engaged in reflective doubt. Had he been busy chasing a streetcar, would he still exist as a self ? Sartre thinks no, or that there wouldn't be any evidence of that happening.

What this extends to is the objectiveness of our identities, because we never are able to exactly perceive ourselves and rather only understand that perception from other people. Each person is a "drain hole" through which the universe flows into a unique, private experience. With billions of such drains, almost everything knowable is unknowable to any one person. And so this develops anxiety, as it develops selfhood: us thinking about who we are, how we will be judged, and so on and so forth.
