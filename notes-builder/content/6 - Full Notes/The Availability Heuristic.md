# The Availability Heuristic
**Details**
When estimating how common something is, we usually use the question "how easily can I think of examples?" which is based in availability heuristic. More words have K as their third letter than as their first, but nobody believes this because there's an easy mental procedure for generating K-first words (king, kitchen, ketchup) and no corresponding procedure for third-letter-K words. Similarly, people estimate shark attacks as more lethal than failing airplane parts when it is 30 times less likely. Notably, this estimate reversed after 9/11, when falling airplane debris became the more available mental image. The heuristic works adequately when the items being compared are equally vivid or accessed through equivalent procedures (more S-words than J-words? Yes, and availability gives the right answer). The brain doesn't distinguish between "I've encountered this many times" and "I've encountered this few times but it was unforgettable."
