# The Reality of Partners in relationships
**Details**
When we love someone, we can put them on a pedestal, a pedestal of worshipping or believing the person that we love is the perfect fit, the perfect person for us. We project "The Good Mother" (Carl Jung's Psychology) onto them. This inherently prepares us for failure as these unrealistic expectations cannot be truly fulfilled, and so when we eventually come to the point that these people fail to live up to what we had believed in them, we lash out. As our projection is broken in the person, we feel betrayed, and all kinds of negative emotions that encourages us to lash out and criticize the person, forgetting that they are human too.

And how do we avoid this ? We do not avoid it by avoiding love entirely, but rather loving differently, loving someone's true self, understanding, seeing them in and out, rather than what our unconscious, our "shadow" wants us to believe them to be. This is a much better way to love someone, as the love serves both parties rather than one or the other.

**Supporting Content**
Moreover, in relationship, we tend to attack the person's character with criticism, maybe stonewalling by disengaging from the problem itself, refusing to answer. These are not ways to solve the problem, rather just avoiding or worsening it. Though one might still be allowed to spend time for themselves to be able to then come back to deal with the problem.
