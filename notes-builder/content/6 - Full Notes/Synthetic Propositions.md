# Synthetic Propositions
**Details**
Hume argued that there were only analytical reasoning or synthetic reasonings, which in both situations are incredibly lacking in one sense while fulfilling the other. For analytical reasoning, it holds up without a priori but does not bring any actual knowledge to the table, whilst the opposite is true for synthetic propositions (more like they’re logically correct but requires a priori).

Kant argued that synthetic propositions without a priori existed. His examples: "there is no number so large that one cannot be added to it" — you don't discover this by counting, yet it says something real about numbers. "Every effect has a cause" — Newton's laws, the principle of sufficient reason. These are substantive claims about the world, yet their truth doesn't depend on checking every case empirically. The radical empiricist who denies this is, as the lecturer quotes, "one who believes the series of positive integers was discovered one at a time."
