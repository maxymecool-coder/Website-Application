# Persuasion in practicality
**Details**
Propaganda exists in every book, this is because your judgement of if a practical book is good or not is if it solves your problems. And so the author must be able to persuade you that his method is achieving the goal that you are trying to get to. This is not wrong, although it is not optimal in your reading, it isn’t wrong. But we still are trying to read the best as possible, and so we must recognize it. 

The method to not fall for propaganda is to recognize it, as usually it is made for you to not even know that you’ve swallowed it. Now comes to the propositions and terms, if someone who has good use of these, he will know which passages that make an “emotive use of words”. And so he can do something about those appeals. This is good, but the opposite can also be detrimental, being too harsh and removing all emotive context of a piece of writing is being deaf to all appeals, which is the whole point of practical books. 

A further point (still relative), is that these “emotive use of words” also projects the author’s personality. It allows us to see what kind of man he is through his moral treatise, a political tract,…
