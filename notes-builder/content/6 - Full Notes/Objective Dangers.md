# Objective Dangers
**Details**
It is quite difficult to differentiate rational and normal fears from phobias. But there is a general rule which states that "The greater the anxiety and disability compared to the actual risk, the easier it is to justify applying the term." This means that it should be easy to make the diagnosis when the content of the fear is far-fetched. Like the person who avoids all outdoor activities as he is afraid a thunderstorm would happen and he'd get stricken and die.

In clinical practice, it has also been shown that phobias are quite easily distinguishable, and so there is often no need for us to analyze. In most cases, the phobic seeks help due to him not being able to live with the conditions that his phobia is preventing him from anymore, as the pain of such things are too much.

**Supporting Content**
But furthermore, a lot of people, specialists so to say, have been able to buffer or extinguish their fears of situations that are said to be dangerous by the normal human being. Experimental studies by Epstein has also showed us how parachutists have reduced anxiety as they become more experienced. As well as troops who experience less anxiety than fresh recruits. They use the method that these specialists do to help phobic patients deal with their own situations.

We also know that phobic patients may be fearless in a situation that would make others anxious, whilst doing the opposite with their phobia. This shows that even if a person seems to deal with a situation better than the normal person, it does not mean it is the same for every other.
