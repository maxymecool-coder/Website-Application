# Some other pain
**Details**
3.2 extends the rejection mechanism beyond welfare: my grounds for rejecting a principle include not just how much it burdens me but what it implies about me. Two principles can impose identical welfare costs yet one remains rejectable — because the basis of treatment, not just its magnitude, is morally relevant.

The racist distribution example makes this clean: I cannot reject the racist principle on welfare grounds alone (the random principle burdens someone equally). I reject it because it treats my race as a legitimate distributive criterion — which constitutes a failure to recognize my status as a person. The wrong is epistemic and relational before it is material.

This is where contractualism most sharply diverges from utilitarianism, which has no native vocabulary for “this wrongs me because of what it says about me” independent of welfare impact. Scanlon is quietly installing something like dignity as a moral primitive here — without naming it as such. The mechanism of reasonable rejection just allows it in through the back door.

Connection to my framework: “unnecessary intentional suffering” already gestures at this gap. The intentional component isn’t welfare-tracking — a racist distribution that hurts you identically to a random one still wrongs you more. The intention behind the principle, and what it implies about the person subjected to it, carries independent moral weight.
