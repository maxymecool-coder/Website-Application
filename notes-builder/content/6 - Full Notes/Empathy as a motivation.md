# Empathy as a motivation
**Details**
To understand the full scope of human behavior, we must move beyond basic drives and consider social emotions and the conflict between motivations. **Empathy**, or emotional contagion, is a prime example of a social emotion. When we see an emotion in another person, we tend to reflexively mimic it (e.g., via our facial expression), which in turn helps us to "feel" their state. This mechanism helps bind social groups, allowing for shared experiences of joy or fear.

However, motivations are often in conflict. A person is not just hungry _or_ thirsty _or_ tired; they are a composite of many competing drives at once. And so we use the metaphor of a **"vector field,"** where an individual is pulled in multiple directions by various motivations (to eat, to sleep, to socialize, to study). Our ultimate behavior is the resultant vector of these competing forces. This explains why we act in complex, sometimes seemingly irrational ways—our actions are a compromise negotiated between the many different, and often conflicting, demands of our internal motivational systems.
