# Toxic Effects of Tobacco
**Details**
There are three major ways that Tobacco affects us: the toxicity of nicotine, carbon monoxide, and the irritant action of tars and combustion byproducts.

Firstly, Nicotine acts as a cardiovascular poison. It causes chronic vasoconstriction-tightening of the blood vessels-increasing cardiac workload. For example, if we flex our muscles for a whole day, the fibers would tear and degrade eventually. This also applies to the cardiovascular system, leading to microscopic damage, atherosclerosis, and eventually heart attacks or strokes. This vasoconstriction is particularly damaging to fine capillaries, leading to peripheral vascular disease, a condition where blood flow to the extremities is compromised, sometimes necessitating amputation even in young athletes using smokeless tobacco.

Second, Carbon Monoxide, being a byproduct of the combustion binding in blood and of course, taking oxygen away from us. This creates chronic hypoxia (oxygen deprivation).

Lastly, Smoke and Tars contain carcinogenic and irritant compounds which destroys the lung tissue and leads to lung, larynx, and mouth cancer. Our body tries to get this out by coughing (smoker's cough).
