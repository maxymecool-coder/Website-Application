# The Structure of a Therapy Session
**Details**
In the first session of therapy, the main agenda is not to solve problems or figure out what to do and so on but rather elicit hope from the patient as well as psychoeducate them on anything that they haven’t understood or known about the process yet. First though, it always starts with a mood check.

Now, we can check by using public questionnaires such as the PHQ-9 or the GAD-9 or even using Beck’s item inventories to evaluate their hopelessness and so on. It may also be necessary to check on people’s medication use or other conditions that should be known. For this, it may be better to get in contact with the doctor, psychiatrist,… managing the patient.

Initially, we would obviously start by setting the overall agenda of the session, seeing what to do and everything. If anything arises during the session, we may discuss it collaboratively with the client to see if we should move to that idea instead.

After that, it’s a lot of psychoeducation as we reinforce the cognitive model, talking about automatic thoughts, making analogies for them to understand. We may even educate them on depression and why they should perceive it as a real and damaging illness like pneumonia or diabetes.

Moving on, we will try to understand and get what are the client’s values and aspirations to then be able to set goals from there. Then we will figure out how to achieve these goals with the given tools of CBT (and other therapies). Of course, all this should be documented in the action plan (summarized) to allow the client to have something to remind themselves about the process.

Last but not least, feedback is also important to know what has gone wrong and what needs to be changed from the process.
